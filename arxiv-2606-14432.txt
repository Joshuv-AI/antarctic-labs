The Brezis–Ekeland–Nayroles principle in metric spaces: time-continuous setting
Pierre–Cyril Aubin–Frankowski, Giacomo Enrico Sodini, Ulisse Stefanelli

Abstract
. Based on a variational characterization of the local slope of a functional, we extend the celebrated Brezis–Ekeland–Nayroles null-minimization principle to curves of maximal slope in metric spaces. In the time-continuous setting, we check that the ensuing global-in-time functionals admit minimizers.

1.
Introduction

The Brezis–Ekeland–Nayroles principle
[
10
,
11
,
36
,
37
]
is a variational
approach to dissipative evolution systems, characterizing solutions as null-minimizers of suitable
nonnegative functionals on trajectories. To introduce the principle, consider for simplicity a doubly nonlinear flow

\partial\hat{\psi}(u^{\prime})+\partial\phi(u)=0\quad\text{a.e. in}\ [0,T]

(1.1)

where
u:[0,T]\to V
is a trajectory in a reflexive Banach space
V
,
the prime denotes differentiation, and
\phi,\,\hat{\psi}:V\to[0,\infty)
are convex functionals with single-valued subdifferentials

\partial\phi,\,\partial\hat{\psi}:V\to V^{*}
(dual). By introducing a second
trajectory
a:[0,T]\to V^{*}
, equation
(
1.1
) can be equivalently written as the system
-a=\partial\hat{\psi}(u^{\prime})
and
a=\partial\phi(u)
almost everywhere in
[0,T]
.

One can then characterize
\partial\phi:V\to V^{*}
of
\phi
at a point
u
with

\phi(u)<\infty
as

a\in\partial\phi(u)
iff
-\left\langle a,u\right\rangle+\phi(u)+\phi^{*}(a)\leq 0

where
\phi^{*}(a)=\sup_{v\in V}\left\langle a,v\right\rangle-\phi(v)

is the classical
Fenchel conjugate
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the duality pairing between

V^{*}
and
V
. In the Banach case one hence
has

a\in\partial\phi(u)\ \Leftrightarrow\ -\left\langle a,u-v\right\rangle+\phi(u)-\phi(v)\leq 0\quad\forall v\in V.

(1.2)

Using this characterization, the Brezis–Ekeland-Nayroles principle for doubly nonlinear flows

[
50
, Thm. 1.2]
asserts that solutions
u
to (
1.1
) correspond
to null-minimizers of

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm Banach}(a,u)

\displaystyle=\left(\phi(u(T))-\phi(u(0))+\int_{0}^{T}\hat{\psi}(u^{\prime})\,{\rm d}r+\int_{0}^{T}\hat{\psi}^{*}(a)\,{\rm d}r\right)^{+}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{T}\sup_{v\in V}\big(-\langle a,u-v\rangle+\phi(u)-\phi(v)\big)\,{\rm d}r,

denoting
x^{+}=x\vee 0
. The second term in
{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm Banach}
is
0
iff
a=\partial\phi(u)
and, in this case, the first term is
0
iff
-a=\partial\hat{\psi}(u^{\prime})
.

We are interested in extending the Brezis–Ekeland–Nayroles
principle to evolution equations in metric spaces. Originating from the pioneering
contributions
[
28
,
39
]
, flows in metric spaces have
specifically received
a strong development, motivated by their wide range of possible
applications. The fundamental reference in the field is the monograph
by
Ambrosio, Gigli, & Savaré

[
2
]
. In a metric space, however, no
notion of derivative or duality is available
and one has to
resort to
scalar
fields: the
metric
derivative

|u^{\prime}|
of
u
and
u\mapsto|\partial\phi|(u)
the
local slope
of
\phi
.

A prominent notion of dissipative evolution in the complete metric
space
(U,d)
is that of
curve of maximal slope
. This is
a trajectory
u:[0,T]\to U
solving

\phi(u(t))-\phi(u(0))+\int_{0}^{t}\psi\big(|u^{\prime}|(r)\big)\,{\rm d}r+\int_{0}^{t}\psi^{*}\big(|\partial\phi|(u(r))\big)\,{\rm d}r=0\quad\forall t\in[0,T]

(1.3)

where the functional
\phi:U\to[0,\infty]
and the strictly convex,
smooth function
\psi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
with Fenchel conjugate
\psi^{*}
are
given.

In the
classical
geodesically-convex
case (a setting which we will nonetheless
soon abandon)
our extension of the classical Brezis–Ekeland–Nayroles principle is
based on the global variational characterization of the local slope (which in this case coincides with the global slope) given by

\alpha=|\partial\phi|(u)\ \Leftrightarrow\ \alpha=\argmin\big\{\overline{\alpha}\geq 0:-\overline{\alpha}d(u,v){+}\phi(u){-}\phi(v)\leq 0\ \forall v\in U\big\},

(1.4)

see Proposition

3.3
below.
By letting

E(\alpha,u)=\max_{v\in U}\big\{-\alpha d(u,v)+\phi(u)-\phi(v)\big\}

(see Lemma
3.1
below), we
define the analogue functional
{\mathcal{H}}
to
{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm Banach}
over entire trajectories
u:[0,T]\to U
and
\alpha:[0,T]\to[0,\infty)
as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}(\alpha,u)

\displaystyle=\left(\phi(u(T))-\phi(u(0))+\int_{0}^{T}\psi\big(|u^{\prime}|^{p}(r)\big)\,{\rm d}r+\int_{0}^{T}\psi^{*}\big(\alpha(r)\big)\,{\rm d}r\right)^{+}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{T}E(\alpha(r),u(r))\,{\rm d}r.

The
metric Brezis–Ekeland–Nayroles principle

ensues by checking in Theorem
4.1
that

\boxed{\text{$u$ is a curve of maximal slope}\ \Leftrightarrow{\mathcal{H}}(\alpha,u)=\min{\mathcal{H}}=0}

(1.5)

for some
\alpha
. In this case,
\alpha=|\partial\phi|(u)
almost
everywhere in
[0,T]
. This provides a variational characterization
of curves of maximal slope, opening the way to applying the methods
of the calculus of variations to the evolution problem. In particular,
the vectorial variable
a(t)\in V^{*}
in
{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm Banach}
is replaced by the scalar one

\alpha(t)\geq 0
in
{\mathcal{H}}
and the duality
-\langle a,u-v\rangle
is replaced by
-\alpha d(u,v)
. As an effect of this generalization, the
analysis of
{\mathcal{H}}
is new even in the Banach space case.

In
this paper, we analyze the above approach in the time-continuous
setting. After checking
characterization (
1.5
) (Theorem
4.1
),
we prove that
{\mathcal{H}}
is
lower semicontinuous (Proposition
5.2
) and that it
admits minimizers (Proposition
5.3
). In this paper, we do not explicitly prove that these minimizers are in fact null-minimizers, i.e.,
\min_{K(u^{0})}{\mathcal{H}}=0
. This is however true, as curves of maximal slope do exist, see also
[
3
]
.

Besides
{\mathcal{H}}
, other functionals may be used to obtain alternative metric
Brezis–Ekeland–Nayroles principles. In this paper, we also
investigate the
functional
{\mathcal{G}}
given by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}}(\alpha,u)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\phi(u(T))-\phi(u(0))+\int_{0}^{T}\psi\big(|u^{\prime}|(r)\big){\rm d}r+\int_{0}^{T}\psi^{*}\big(\alpha(r)\big)\,{\rm d}r\\[2.84526pt]
\qquad\text{if}\ \displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}E(\alpha(r),u(r))\,{\rm d}r=0,\\[8.53581pt]
\infty\quad\text{otherwise},\end{array}\right.

and prove that the characterization (
1.5
) holds for
{\mathcal{G}}

as well (Theorem
4.1
),
{\mathcal{G}}
is lower semicontinuous (Proposition
5.2
), and that it
admits minimizers (Proposition
5.3
). Moreover, we also
consider a penalized version of
{\mathcal{G}}
. For all
\delta>0
, we let
the approximating functional
{\mathcal{G}}_{\delta}
be given by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}}_{\delta}(\alpha,u)

\displaystyle=\phi(u(T))-\phi(u(0))+\int_{0}^{T}\psi\big(|u^{\prime}|(r)\big){\rm d}r+\int_{0}^{T}\psi^{*}\big(\alpha(r)\big)\,{\rm d}r

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{0}^{T}E(\alpha(r),u(r))\,{\rm d}r,

we
show that minimizers of
{\mathcal{G}}_{\delta}
converge as
\delta\to 0
to
a curve of maximal slope up to subsequences (Proposition
5.6
).

2.
Setting

In this section, we collect assumptions (
2.6
)-(
2.12
) and fix the notation. To
start with, we assume that

\displaystyle(U,d)\ \text{is a complete, separable, path-connected metric
space, and}\ T\in(0,\infty).

(2.6)

The driving functional
\phi
is requested to be

\displaystyle\quad\phi:U\to(-\infty,\infty]\ \text{proper and with compact
sublevels.}

(2.7)

A consequence of the compactness of the sublevels of
\phi
is
that
\phi
is lower semicontinuous and bounded from below.
In particular, (
2.7
) implies that the
effective domain

D(\phi)\coloneqq\{u\in U:\phi(u)<\infty\}
is nonempty and that
\phi
admits a global minimizer. With
no loss of generality, we henceforth
assume that

\min\phi=0.

(2.8)

For given
p\in(1,\infty)
, following the notation in
[
2
]
, the set
AC^{p}([0,T];U)

indicates
p
-absolutely continuous
curves, namely, trajectories

u:[0,T]\to U
such that there exists
\eta\in L^{p}(0,T)
with

d(u(s),u(t))\leq\int_{s}^{t}\eta(r)\,{\rm d}r\quad\forall 0\leq s\leq t<T.

If
u\in AC^{p}([0,T];U)
, the limit

|u^{\prime}|(t)\coloneqq\lim_{s\to t}\frac{d(u(s),u(t))}{|t-s|}

exists for almost all
t\in(0,T)
, see

[
2
, Thm. 1.1.2, p. 24]
, and is called the
metric
derivative
of
u
at
t
. In this case, the map

t\mapsto|u^{\prime}|(t)
is in
L^{p}(0,T)

and coincides with the minimal function
\eta\in L^{p}(0,T)
satisfying the above bound.

The
local slope

|\partial\phi|(u)
and the
global slope

\mathfrak{l}_{\phi}(u)

[
2
,
15
,
16
]
of
\phi
at
u\in U
are defined via

|\partial\phi|(u)\coloneqq\limsup_{v\to u}\frac{(\phi(u)-\phi(v))^{+}}{d(u,v)},\quad\mathfrak{l}_{\phi}(u)\coloneqq\sup_{v\not=u}\frac{(\phi(u)-\phi(v))^{+}}{d(u,v)},

respectively, and we indicate the corresponding domains as

D(|\partial\phi|)\coloneqq\{u\in D(\phi):|\partial\phi|(u)<\infty\}
and
D(\mathfrak{l}_{\phi})\coloneqq\{u\in D(\phi):\mathfrak{l}_{\phi}(u)<\infty\}
. Note that the definition
of
|\partial\phi|(u)
implicitly requires that
u
is not isolated which actually cannot occur as
U
is
path-connected by (
2.7
).

Being defined via a supremum,
the global slope
\mathfrak{l}_{\phi}
is lower semicontinuous. On the other hand, the local slope
|\partial\phi|
is defined via a
\limsup
and is not, in general, lower semicontinuous. In order to approximate
|\partial\phi|
, we introduce a penalized formulation of
\mathfrak{l}_{\phi}
through a
\mu\geq 0
. For some given
q>1
and for all
\mu\geq 0
, we define the
\mu
-slope

|\partial\phi|_{\mu}(u)
of
\phi
at
u
as

|\partial\phi|_{\mu}(u)\coloneqq\sup_{v\not=u}\left(\frac{(\phi(u)-\phi(v))^{+}}{d(u,v)}-\mu d^{q-1}(u,v)\right).

(2.9)

Note that
|\partial\phi|_{\mu}
is lower semicontinuous
[
46
, Prop. 2.7]
and that

|\partial\phi|_{0}=\mathfrak{l}_{\phi}
.

We say that a function
g:U\to[0,\infty]
is a
strong upper gradient
for
\phi

[
2
, Def. 1.2.1, p. 27]
if for all
u\in AC^{p}([0,T];U)
,
the map

t\mapsto g(u(t))
is Borel and

|\phi(u(t))-\phi(u(s))|\leq\int_{s}^{t}g(u(r))\,|u^{\prime}|(r)\,{\rm d}r\quad\forall 0\leq s\leq t\leq T.

If
t\mapsto g(u(t))|u^{\prime}|(t)\in L^{1}(0,T)
the
latter entails that
\phi\circ u\in W^{1,1}(0,T)
and
(\phi\circ u)^{\prime}\leq g(u)|u^{\prime}|
almost everywhere in
[0,T]
. Note that

|\partial\phi|_{\mu}
is a strong upper gradient for all
\mu\in[0,\infty)
based on
[
2
, proof of Corollary 2.4.10]
. In
particular,

\mathfrak{l}_{\phi}
is a strong upper gradient.

In what follows, we will assume that

\displaystyle\exists\mu_{0}\in[0,\infty)\ \text{such that}\ |\partial\phi|=|\partial\phi|_{\mu_{0}}.

(2.10)

The existence of such
\mu_{0}
entails in particular that
|\partial\phi|

is lower semicontinuous and a strong upper gradient for
\phi
. Assumption
(
2.10
) in particular holds if
\phi
is
(\lambda,q)
-generalized-geodesically convex
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}

[
46
]
(see also
[
38
,
49
]
for
alternative but comparable definitions). In this case, one can choose
\mu_{0}=\lambda^{-}/q
, where

\lambda=-\lambda\vee 0
. In Section
6
, we drop this assumption (
2.10
).

The initial value
u^{0}
is asked to fulfill

u^{0}\in D(\phi).

(2.11)

The dissipative character of the evolution is quantified via the function
\psi:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
satisfying

\displaystyle\psi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})\ \text{convex with}

\displaystyle\text{$\psi(0)=\min\psi=0$ such that there exist}\ p\in(1,\infty)\ \text{and}\ c_{\psi}\in(0,\infty)\ \text{with}

\displaystyle\psi(t)+\psi^{*}(t^{*})\geq c_{\psi}|t|^{p}+c_{\psi}|t^{*}|^{p^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{c_{\psi}}\quad\forall t,\,t_{*}\in\mathbb{R}

(2.12)

Here,
1/p+1/p^{\prime}=1
and
\psi^{*}
is the classical Fenchel conjugate
\psi^{*}(t^{*})=\sup_{t}(t^{*}t-\psi(t))
for
t^{*}\in\mathbb{R}
. The reference example is

\psi(t)=|t|^{p}/p
. By duality one has that

\psi(t)+\psi^{*}(t^{*})\leq C_{\psi}(1+|t|^{p}+|t^{*}|^{p^{\prime}})\quad\forall t,\,t_{*}\in\mathbb{R}

(2.13)

for some
C_{\psi}\in(0,\infty)
depending on
c_{\psi}
and
p
. Indeed, by (
2.12
) evaluated first at
t^{*}=0
and then at
t=0
, we have

\displaystyle\psi(t)\geq c_{\psi}|t|^{p}-\frac{1}{c_{\psi}},\quad\psi^{*}(t^{*})\geq c_{\psi}|t^{*}|^{p^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{c_{\psi}}\quad\forall t,t^{*}\in\mathbb{R},

so that

\displaystyle\psi(t)

\displaystyle=\psi^{**}(t)\leq\sup_{t^{*}}\{tt^{*}-c_{\psi}|t^{*}|^{p^{\prime}}\}+\frac{1}{c_{\psi}}=\frac{1}{p(c_{\psi}p^{\prime})^{p-1}}|t|^{p}+\frac{1}{c_{\psi}}\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle\psi^{*}(t^{*})

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{t}\{tt^{*}-c_{\psi}|t|^{p}\}+\frac{1}{c_{\psi}}=\frac{1}{p^{\prime}(c_{\psi}p)^{p^{\prime}-1}}|t|^{p^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{c_{\psi}}\quad\forall t^{*}\in\mathbb{R},

which give (
2.13
) with
C_{\psi}\coloneqq\max\{2c_{\psi}^{-1},(p^{\prime}(c_{\psi}p)^{p^{\prime}-1})^{-1},(p(c_{\psi}p^{\prime})^{p-1})^{-1}\}
. Note that the
convexity of
\psi
and the fact that
0=\psi(0)=\min\psi
imply that

\psi
is nondecreasing on
[0,\infty)
. On the other hand, the
differentiability of
\psi
ensures that
\psi^{*}
is strictly convex,
which, together with
0=\psi^{*}(0)=\min\psi^{*}
, gives that
\psi^{*}
is
strictly increasing on
[0,\infty)
.

Assumptions (
2.6
)–(
2.7
) and
(
2.10
)–(
2.12
) are tacitly assumed throughout the paper,
with the exception of Section
6
where only the assumption (

) is dropped.

In this paper, we focus on the following classical notion
[
35
]
in studying the evolution of gradient flows.

Definition 2.1

(Curve of maximal slope)
.

The trajectory
u\in AC^{p}([0,T];U)
is said to be a
curve of
maximal slope

if,
u(0)=u^{0}
,
\phi\circ u\in W^{1,1}(0,T)
, and

\phi(u(t))+\int_{0}^{t}\psi(|u^{\prime}|(r))\,{\rm d}r+\int_{0}^{t}\psi^{*}(|\partial\phi|(u(r)))\,{\rm d}r=\phi(u(0))\quad\forall t\in[0,T].

(2.14)

The latter is a slight generalization of the concept of
(p,q)
-curves
of maximal slope
from
[
2
, Def. 1.3.2, p. 32]
,
which indeed correspond to the choice
\psi(t)=|t|^{p}/p
.

To conclude this preliminary section, let us collect here some compactness tools,
which are used in the following.

Lemma 2.2

(Compactness tools)
.

Let
(u_{j})_{j}\subset AC^{p}([0,T];U)
have

|u_{j}^{\prime}|\to\eta
weakly in
L^{p}(0,T)
.

(a)

In case that

\sup_{j}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\phi(u_{j}(t))<\infty,

there exist
u\in AC^{p}([0,T];U)
with
|u^{\prime}|\leq\eta
almost everywhere in

[0,T]
and a not relabeled sequence such that
u_{j}(t)\to u(t)

for all
t\in[0,T]
.

(b)

In case that, for some
t_{0}\in[0,T]
,

(u_{j}(t_{0}))_{j}
is precompact and

\sup_{j}\int_{0}^{T}\phi(u_{j}(t))\,{\rm d}t<\infty,

there exist
u\in AC^{p}([0,T];U)
with
|u^{\prime}|\leq\eta
almost everywhere in

[0,T]
and a not relabeled sequence such that
u_{j}(t)\to u(t)

for almost all
t\in[0,T]
and
u_{j}(t_{0})\to u(t_{0})
.

The results (a) and (b) above correspond to
the Ascoli–Arzelà theorem and a metric version of the Aubin–Lions lemma, and are proved in

[
2
, Prop. 3.3.1]
and
[
43
, Thm. 2]
.
The only difference between the result (b) above and the one presented
in
[
43
, Thm. 2]
is the convergence at the additional
point
t_{0}
, which we briefly comment here. Since
(u_{j}(t_{0}))_{j}
is
assumed to be precompact, we can first extract a not
relabeled subsequence such that
u_{j}(t_{0})\to\overline{u}\in U
.
On the other hand, the result by
[
43
, Thm. 2]
gives the
existence of
u\in AC^{p}([0,T];U)
with
|u^{\prime}|\leq\eta
almost
everywhere in
[0,T]
and of a not relabeled sequence such that
u_{j}(t)\to u(t)
for almost all
t\in[0,T]
. In particular, we have

\displaystyle d(u_{j}(t_{0}),u(t_{0}))

\displaystyle\leq d(u_{j}(t_{0}),u_{j}(t))+d(u_{j}(t),u(t))+d(u(t),u(t_{0})),

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t_{0}\wedge t}^{t_{0}\vee t}|u^{\prime}_{j}|(r){\rm d}r+d(u_{j}(t),u(t))+d(u(t),u(t_{0}))

for every
t\in[0,T]
such that
u_{j}(t)\to u(t)
. Passing to the limit as
j\to\infty
, we get

d(\overline{u},u(t_{0}))\leq\int_{t_{0}\wedge t}^{t_{0}\vee t}\eta(r){\rm d}r+d(u(t),u(t_{0}))

and passing to the limit as
t\to t_{0}
, we obtain
u(t_{0})=\overline{u}
.

3.
Variational characterization of
|\partial\phi|(u)

This section pertains to some introductory discussion concerning
the variational characterization of the slope
|\partial\phi|
.
Let us start by defining the functional
\overline{E}:[0,\infty)\times U\times D(\phi)\to(-\infty,\infty]
as

\overline{E}(\alpha,u,v)\coloneqq-\alpha d(u,v)+\phi(u)-\phi(v)-\mu_{0}d^{q}(u,v).

We first observe the following.

Lemma 3.1

(Reduced functional)
.

For all
(\alpha,u)\in[0,\infty)\times U
there exists the maximum

E(\alpha,u)\coloneqq\max_{v\in D(\phi)}\overline{E}(\alpha,u,v)

(3.15)

which defines the reduced functional
E:[0,\infty)\times U\to[0,\infty]
.

Proof.

If
u\notin D(\phi)
, then
\overline{E}(\alpha,u,v)=\infty
for every
(\alpha,v)\in[0,\infty)\times D(\phi)
, so that the maximum in (
3.15
) is
\infty
and is achieved at any
v\in D(\phi)
. Assume now that
u\in D(\phi)
.
As
\phi
is
lower semicontinuous, for all
(\alpha,u)\in[0,\infty)\times D(\phi)
the map
v\in U\mapsto\overline{E}(\alpha,u,v)=-\alpha d(u,v)+\phi(u)-\phi(v)-\mu_{0}d^{q}(u,v)\in[-\infty,\infty)
is upper
semicontinuous. Moreover, it is
finite iff
v\in D(\phi)
. Letting
u_{*}\in\argmin\phi
and using
\phi(u_{*})=0
by (
2.8
), we can reduce the
maximization in (
3.15
) to those
v\in D(\phi)
such that

-\alpha d(u,v)-\phi(v)-\mu_{0}d^{q}(u,v)\geq-\alpha d(u,u_{*})-\mu_{0}d^{q}(u,u_{*}).

Hence, one can maximize
over
the compact set
\{v\in D(\phi):\phi(v)\leq\alpha d(u,u_{*})+{\mu_{0}}d^{q}(u,u_{*})\}
. The existence of a maximizer follows by the Direct Method.
∎

Note that we are not claiming uniqueness for problem
(
3.15
). This nonetheless follows if
v\mapsto\alpha d(u,v)+\phi(v)+\mu_{0}d^{q}(u,v)
is
strictly
geodesically
convex for all
(\alpha,u)
. This holds independently of
\alpha\geq 0
if
\phi
is
strictly geodesically convex and both
v\mapsto d(u,v)
and
v\mapsto\mu_{0}d^{q}(u,v)
are
convex along any geodesic.

Note that
E(\alpha,u)\geq\overline{E}(\alpha,u,u)=0
for all
\alpha\geq 0
and

u\in D(\phi)
. Moreover,
E
is convex with respect to
\alpha
, being the supremum of linear
functions in
\alpha
. We have
E(\alpha,u)=\infty
for all
\alpha\geq 0
iff
u\not\in D(\phi)
. We collect some properties of
E
in the following.

Proposition 3.2

(Properties of
E
)
.

The reduced functional
E:[0,\infty)\times U\to[0,\infty]
is lower semicontinuous and there exists a Borel map
\widehat{v}:[0,\infty)\times U\to U
such that
E(\alpha,u)=\overline{E}(\alpha,u,\widehat{v}(\alpha,u))
for all
(\alpha,u)\in[0,\infty)\times U
.

Proof.

The lower semicontinuity is immediate as the maps

(\alpha,u)\in[0,\infty)\times U\mapsto\overline{E}(\alpha,u,v)
are lower
semicontinuous for all
v\in D(\phi)
and
E(\alpha,u)

is defined as a maximum on
v
. This also implies that
E
is a
Borel map.

Let
\Gamma\subset[0,\infty)\times U\times U
be defined as

\Gamma\coloneqq\{(\alpha,u,v)\in[0,\infty)\times U\times D(\phi):\overline{E}(\alpha,u,v)=E(\alpha,u)\}.

Since
\overline{E}
and
E
are Borel maps, then
\Gamma
is a Borel
set. Its sections
\Gamma_{\alpha,u}
, given, for every
(\alpha,u)\in[0,\infty)\times U
, by

\Gamma_{\alpha,u}\coloneqq\{v\in U:(\alpha,u,v)\in\Gamma\}=\{v\in D(\phi):\overline{E}(\alpha,u,v)=E(\alpha,u)\},

are not empty by Lemma
3.1
and compact by the
upper semicontinuity of
D(\phi)\ni v\mapsto\overline{E}(\alpha,u,v)
and
by the compactness of the sublevels of
\phi
. By

[
8
, Theorem 6.9.6]
we obtain the existence of a Borel map
\widehat{v}:[0,\infty)\times U\to U
such that its graph is contained in
\Gamma
.
∎

Under assumption (
2.10
), for all
u\in U
one has that

\displaystyle|\partial\phi|(u)\leq\alpha\ \Leftrightarrow\ \overline{E}(\alpha,u,v)=-\alpha d(u,v)+\phi(u)-\phi(v)-{\mu_{0}}d^{q}(u,v)\leq 0\quad\forall v\in U.

(3.16)

As
E(\alpha,u)\geq 0
for all
u\in D(\phi)
, the latter yields

|\partial\phi|(u)\leq\alpha\ \Leftrightarrow\ E(\alpha,u)=0.

(3.17)

This implies the following variational
characterization, which corresponds to a metric version of the

Fenchel identity
in Banach spaces.

Proposition 3.3

(Characterization)
.

Given
u\in D(\phi)
, we have that

u\in D(|\partial\phi|)\ \Leftrightarrow\ A_{u}=\{\alpha\geq 0:E(\alpha,u)=0\}\not=\emptyset.

In this case,
|\partial\phi|(u)=\min A_{u}
.

Proof.

If
u\in D(|\partial\phi|)
one has that
E(|\partial\phi|(u),u)=0
from
(
3.17
), hence
|\partial\phi|(u)\in A_{u}\not=\emptyset
. Conversely, if

\alpha\in A_{u}
then
E(\alpha,u)=0
so that
|\partial\phi|(u)\leq\alpha<\infty
and
u\in D(|\partial\phi|)
. In
this case, again (
3.17
) guarantees that
|\partial\phi|(u)
is minimal
in
A_{u}
.
∎

Proposition
3.3
ensures that for all
u\in D(\phi)
one can variationally
characterize
|\partial\phi|(u)
as follows

|\partial\phi|(u)=\argmin_{E(\alpha,u)=0}\psi^{*}(\alpha),

(3.18)

where we recall that
\psi^{*}
from (
2.12
) is strictly
increasing on
[0,\infty)
. As
E
is nonnegative, this provides the possibility of penalizing the equation

\alpha=|\partial\phi|(u)
by considering an unconstrained minimization problem.

Proposition 3.4

(Penalization)
.

For all
u\in D(\phi)
and
\delta>0

one can find

\alpha_{\delta}\in\argmin_{\alpha\geq 0}\left\{\psi^{*}(\alpha)+\frac{1}{\delta}E(\alpha,u)\right\}.

(3.19)

If
u\in D(|\partial\phi|)
, we have that
\alpha_{\delta}\to|\partial\phi|(u)
as

\delta\to 0
.

Proof.

Let us first check that the minimization problem (
3.19
) can be solved.
As
E
is nonnegative and finite on
D(\phi)
and
\psi^{*}
is coercive, one has
that
\alpha\in[0,\infty)\mapsto\psi^{*}(\alpha)+E(\alpha,u)/\delta
is coercive, as well. The existence of
\alpha_{\delta}
for
all
\delta>0

follows then by the lower semicontinuity of
\psi^{*}
and by
Proposition
3.2
.

Assume now that
u\in D(|\partial\phi|)
. By comparing with
|\partial\phi|(u)
and
using the fact that
E(|\partial\phi|(u),u)=0

we obtain that

\psi^{*}(\alpha_{\delta})+\frac{1}{\delta}E(\alpha_{\delta},u)\leq\psi^{*}(|\partial\phi|(u))+\frac{1}{\delta}E(|\partial\phi|(u),u)=\psi^{*}(|\partial\phi|(u)).

(3.20)

On the one hand, as
E
is nonnegative this proves that
\alpha_{\delta}
is bounded
independently of
\delta
, so that
\alpha_{\delta}\to\alpha
(up to
some not relabeled subsequence) as

\delta\to 0
. On the other hand, as
\psi^{*}
is nonnegative, inequality (
3.20
) implies that

0\leq E(\alpha,u)\leq\liminf_{\delta\to 0}E(\alpha_{\delta},u)\leq\liminf_{\delta\to 0}\delta\,\psi^{*}(|\partial\phi|(u))=0

proving that
E(\alpha,u)=0
, or
|\partial\phi|(u)\leq\alpha
by (
3.17
). Hence, we get
that
\psi^{*}(|\partial\phi|(u))\leq\psi^{*}(\alpha)
since
\psi^{*}
is
(strictly) increasing on
[0,\infty)
. Again by
(
3.20
), we get

\psi^{*}(\alpha)\leq\liminf_{\delta\to 0}\psi^{*}(\alpha_{\delta})\leq\psi^{*}(|\partial\phi|(u))

which implies
\alpha=|\partial\phi|(u)
, as
\psi^{*}
is strictly
increasing on
[0,\infty)
. The above argument ensures that any subsequence of

(\alpha_{\delta})_{\delta}
has a converging subsequence to the same
limit. This proves that the whole sequence
(\alpha_{\delta})_{\delta}

converges to

|\partial\phi|(u)
and
no extraction is actually necessary.
∎

4.
Metric Brezis–Ekeland–Nayroles principle

In this section, we introduce the metric version of the
Brezis–Ekeland–Nayroles principle (Theorem
4.1
) and discuss some of its
properties. Let us endow
[0,T]
with the Lebesgue measure and indicate
by
M([0,T];U)
and
M([0,T];[0,\infty)\times U)
the sets of Borel
measurable maps with values in
U
and
[0,\infty)\times U
, respectively. Owing to
Proposition
3.2
, for all
(\alpha,u)\in M([0,T];[0,\infty)\times U)
we have that
t\in[0,T]\mapsto E(\alpha(t),u(t))
is measurable and
v\coloneqq\widehat{v}(\alpha,u)\in M([0,T];U)
is such that
E(\alpha(t),u(t))=\overline{E}(\alpha(t),u(t),v(t))
for all
t\in[0,T]
. Recalling that
E
is nonnegative, we define
{\mathcal{E}}:M([0,T];[0,\infty)\times U)\to[0,\infty]
as

{\mathcal{E}}(\alpha,u)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}E(\alpha(r),u(r))\,{\rm d}r.

Analogously to the static setting, we have that
{\mathcal{E}}
is
nonnegative and

{\mathcal{E}}(\alpha,u)<\infty
implies that
u\in D(\phi)
almost
everywhere in

[0,T]
.

By recalling that
u^{0}\in D(\phi)
from
(
2.11
), we indicate by

K(u^{0})\coloneqq\{(\alpha,u)\in L^{p^{\prime}}(0,T)\times AC^{p}([0,T];U):u(0)=u^{0}\}

(4.21)

the set of
admissible trajectories
. Moreover, we define the functionals
{\mathcal{F}}:L^{p^{\prime}}(0,T)\times AC^{p}([0,T];U)\to(-\infty,\infty]
and
{\mathcal{G}},\,{\mathcal{G}}_{\delta},{\mathcal{H}}:L^{p^{\prime}}(0,T)\times AC^{p}([0,T];U)\to(-\infty,\infty]
for
\delta>0
as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}(\alpha,u)=\phi(u(T))-\phi(u(0))+\int_{0}^{T}\psi(|u^{\prime}|(r))\,{\rm d}r+\int_{0}^{T}\psi^{*}(\alpha(r))\,{\rm d}r,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}}(\alpha,u)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\mathcal{F}}(\alpha,u)&\text{if}\ {\mathcal{E}}(\alpha,u)=0,\\[5.69054pt]
\infty&\text{otherwise},\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}}_{\delta}(\alpha,u)={\mathcal{F}}(\alpha,u)+\frac{1}{\delta}{\mathcal{E}}(\alpha,u),

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}(\alpha,u)=({\mathcal{F}}(\alpha,u))^{+}+{\mathcal{E}}(\alpha,u).

We are now ready to present our metric version of the Brezis–Ekeland–Nayroles principle
[
10
,
11
,
36
,
37
]
, delivering a
characterization of
curves of maximal slope.
As a preparation, let us remark that both
{\mathcal{H}}
and

{\mathcal{G}}
are nonnegative on

K(u^{0})
. Indeed,
{\mathcal{H}}\geq 0
trivially follows as
{\mathcal{E}}\geq 0
. Assume by contradiction that
{\mathcal{G}}(\alpha,u)<0
for some

(\alpha,u)\in K(u^{0})
. As
{\mathcal{E}}(\alpha,u)=0
, we have
that
|\partial\phi|(u)\leq\alpha
almost everywhere in
[0,T]
by (
3.17
), and

{\mathcal{F}}(\alpha,u)={\mathcal{G}}(\alpha,u)<0
. As
\psi^{*}(|\partial\phi|(u))\leq\psi^{*}(\alpha)
almost everywhere in

[0,T]
, we have
{\mathcal{F}}(|\partial\phi|(u),u)\leq{\mathcal{F}}(\alpha,u)<0
. On the other
hand, as
|\partial\phi|
is a strong upper gradient, one finds

\phi\circ u\in W^{1,1}(0,T)
and the chain rule yields

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}(|\partial\phi|(u),u)=\phi(u(T))-\phi(u(0))+\int_{0}^{T}\psi(|u^{\prime}|(r))\,{\rm d}r+\int_{0}^{T}\psi^{*}(|\partial\phi|(u(r)))\,{\rm d}r

\displaystyle\quad\geq\int_{0}^{T}\big(-|\partial\phi|(u(r))|u^{\prime}|(r)+\psi(|u^{\prime}|(r))+\psi^{*}(|\partial\phi|(u(r))\big){\rm d}r\geq 0

from Fenchel’s inequality, leading to a contradiction.

Theorem 4.1

(Null-minimization)
.

The following are equivalent:

(i)

u
is a curve of maximal slope,

(ii)

{\mathcal{G}}(\alpha,u)=\min_{K(u^{0})}{\mathcal{G}}=0
for some
\alpha\in L^{p^{\prime}}(0,T)
,

(iii)

{\mathcal{H}}(\alpha,u)=\min_{K(u^{0})}{\mathcal{H}}=0
for some
\alpha\in L^{p^{\prime}}(0,T)
.

Proof.

Let
u
be a curve of maximal slope. Then
u(0)=u^{0}
,
|\partial\phi|(u)\in L^{p^{\prime}}(0,T)
, and (
2.14
) for

t=T
gives
{\mathcal{F}}(|\partial\phi|(u),u)=0
. Since in particular
u\in D(|\partial\phi|)

almost everywhere in
[0,T]
, we have that
{\mathcal{E}}(|\partial\phi|(u),u)=0
and
{\mathcal{G}}(\alpha,u)={\mathcal{H}}(\alpha,u)=0
follows by choosing

\alpha=|\partial\phi|(u)
. Hence (i) implies both (ii) and (iii).

We now check (ii)
\Rightarrow
(i). Let
(\alpha,u)\in K(u^{0})
with

{\mathcal{G}}(\alpha,u)=0
. This implies
that
{\mathcal{E}}(\alpha,u)=0
, so that
{\mathcal{F}}(\alpha,u)={\mathcal{G}}(\alpha,u)=0
. As
|\partial\phi|(u)\leq\alpha
almost everywhere in
[0,T]
by (
3.17
), we have proved that
u\in D(|\partial\phi|)
almost everywhere in
[0,T]
and

|\partial\phi|(u)\in L^{p^{\prime}}(0,T)
. As
|\partial\phi|
is a strong upper gradient for

\phi
, this implies that
\phi\circ u\in W^{1,1}(0,T)
. In
particular, for all
t\in[0,T]
we have that

\displaystyle\phi(u^{0})-\phi(u(t))\leq|\phi(u(t))-\phi(u^{0})|\leq\int_{0}^{t}|\partial\phi|(u(r))\,|u^{\prime}|(r)\,{\rm d}r

\displaystyle\quad\leq\int_{0}^{t}\psi(|u^{\prime}|(r))\,{\rm d}r+\int_{0}^{t}\psi^{*}(|\partial\phi|(u(r)))\,{\rm d}r

giving the inequality

\phi(u(t))-\phi(u^{0})+\int_{0}^{t}\psi(|u^{\prime}|(r))\,{\rm d}r+\int_{0}^{t}\psi^{*}(|\partial\phi|(u(r)))\,{\rm d}r\geq 0.

(4.22)

On the other hand, from
|\partial\phi|(u)\leq\alpha
almost everywhere in

[0,T]
we deduce that
\psi^{*}(|\partial\phi|(u))\leq\psi^{*}(\alpha)
almost everywhere in

[0,T]
, hence
{\mathcal{F}}(|\partial\phi|(u),u)\leq{\mathcal{F}}(\alpha,u)=0
. By using the fact that

\displaystyle-\phi(u(T))+\phi(u(t))\leq|\phi(u(T))-\phi(u(t))|\leq\int_{t}^{T}|\partial\phi|(u(r))\,|u^{\prime}|(r)\,{\rm d}r

\displaystyle\quad\leq\int_{t}^{T}\psi(|u^{\prime}|(r))\,{\rm d}r+\int_{t}^{T}\psi^{*}(|\partial\phi|(u(r)))\,{\rm d}r,

(4.23)

we can check that

\displaystyle\phi(u(t))-\phi(u^{0})+\int_{0}^{t}\psi(|u^{\prime}|(r))\,{\rm d}r+\int_{0}^{t}\psi^{*}(|\partial\phi|(u(r)))\,{\rm d}r

\displaystyle\quad={\mathcal{F}}(|\partial\phi|(u),u)-\phi(u(T))+\phi(u(t))

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{t}^{T}\psi(|u^{\prime}|(r))\,{\rm d}r-\int_{t}^{T}\psi^{*}(|\partial\phi|(u(r)))\,{\rm d}r\leq 0.

where in the last inequality we have used
{\mathcal{F}}(|\partial\phi|(u),u)\leq{\mathcal{F}}(\alpha,u)=0
and
(
4.23
). This proves the converse inequality to
(
4.22
). As
t\in[0,T]
is arbitrary, (
2.14
)
follows and
u
is a curve of maximal slope.

Let us now prove that (iii)
\Rightarrow
(i). As

{\mathcal{H}}(\alpha,u)=0
we again have that
{\mathcal{E}}(\alpha,u)=0
, as well as

{\mathcal{F}}(\alpha,u)\leq 0
. As
|\partial\phi|(u)\leq\alpha
almost everywhere in
[0,T]
, this implies that

{\mathcal{F}}(|\partial\phi|(u),u)\leq{\mathcal{F}}(\alpha,u)\leq 0
. We can hence obtain
(
4.22
)–(
4.23
), proving again that
u
is a curve of
maximal slope.
∎

Remark 4.2

(
{\mathcal{G}}_{\delta}
may be negative)
.

Before moving on, let us remark that we cannot expect Theorem

4.1
to hold for
\delta>0
as
{\mathcal{G}}_{\delta}
may take
negative values. Indeed, if
\delta>0
it might be convenient to
choose some
\alpha<|\partial\phi|(u)
making
{\mathcal{F}}(\alpha,u)
negative, despite
having a positive
{\mathcal{E}}(\alpha,u)
. Let us provide an
elementary example in this direction. Set
U=[0,\infty)
,

\phi(u)=\lambda u^{2}/2
for some
\lambda>0
, and

\psi(t)=t^{2}/2
. In this case, we readily compute that

|\partial\phi|(u)=\lambda u
. For all
\alpha\geq 0
and
u\geq 0
we
have that

E(\alpha,u)=((\lambda u-\alpha)^{+})^{2}/(2\lambda)
. One then
considers

[0,\infty]\ni\alpha\mapsto g_{u}(\alpha)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{2}+\frac{1}{\delta}E(\alpha,u)=\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{2}+\frac{1}{2\delta\lambda}((\lambda u-\alpha)^{+})^{2}

which is
minimized at
\alpha_{u}\coloneqq\lambda u/(1+\lambda\delta)

with value

g_{u}(\alpha_{u})=\lambda^{2}u^{2}/(2(1+\lambda\delta))
. Hence,
for all
u\in AC^{2}([0,T];U)=H^{1}(0,T;U)
with
u>0
we compute

\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}}_{\delta}(\alpha_{u},u)={\mathcal{F}}(\alpha_{u},u)+\frac{1}{\delta}{\mathcal{E}}(\alpha_{u},u)

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{\lambda}{2}u^{2}(T)-\frac{\lambda}{2}u^{2}(0)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}|u^{\prime}(r)|^{2}\,{\rm d}r+\frac{1}{2(1+\lambda\delta)}\int_{0}^{T}|\lambda u(r)|^{2}\,{\rm d}r

\displaystyle\quad<\frac{\lambda}{2}u^{2}(T)-\frac{\lambda}{2}u^{2}(0)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}|u^{\prime}(r)|^{2}\,{\rm d}r+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}|\lambda u(r)|^{2}\,{\rm d}r

\displaystyle\quad={\mathcal{F}}(\lambda u,u)\leq{\mathcal{G}}_{\delta}(\lambda u,u).

By choosing
u_{0}=1
, setting

t\in[0,T]\mapsto\overline{u}(t)\coloneqq{\rm e}^{-\lambda t}
, and
observing that
\overline{u}^{\prime}+\lambda\overline{u}=0
on
[0,T]
and

(\lambda\overline{u},\overline{u})\in K(u^{0})
, one has that

{\mathcal{G}}_{\delta}(\lambda\overline{u},\overline{u})=0
. This implies that

{\mathcal{G}}_{\delta}(\alpha_{\overline{u}},\overline{u})<{\mathcal{G}}_{\delta}(\lambda\overline{u},\overline{u})=0
.

5.
Minimization

Theorem
4.1
links curves of maximal slope with
(
u
-components of) minimizers of
{\mathcal{G}}
and
{\mathcal{H}}

on
K(u^{0})
. This opens
the way to providing an alternative proof of the existence of curves
of maximal slope by minimization. We start by checking lower
semicontinuity.

Proposition 5.1

(Lower semicontinuity of
{\mathcal{E}}
)
.

Let
\alpha_{j}\to\alpha
weakly in
L^{p^{\prime}}(0,T)
,
u_{j},\,u\in M([0,T];U)
with
u_{j}\to u
almost everywhere in

[0,T]
, and

d(u_{j}(s),u_{j}(t))\leq\int_{s}^{t}\eta_{j}(r){\rm d}r\quad\forall 0\leq s\leq t\leq T,j\in\mathbb{N}

for some
\eta_{j}\in L^{1}(0,T)
converging to
\eta
weakly in
L^{1}(0,T)
. Then,

{\mathcal{E}}(\alpha,u)\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}(\alpha_{j},u_{j}).

Proof.

Let
v=\widehat{v}(\alpha,u)\in M([0,T];U)
from Proposition

3.2
, so that
E(\alpha,u)=\overline{E}(\alpha,u,v)
almost everywhere in
[0,T]
.
Using the triangle inequality
we readily check that

|d(u(t),v(t))-d(u_{j}(t),v(t))|\leq d(u(t),u_{j}(t))\to 0\quad\text{for a.e.}\ t\in[0,T].

Let
t_{0}\in[0,T]
be such that
u_{j}(t_{0})\to u(t_{0})
. For almost all
t\in[0,T]
we have that

d(u(t),u(t_{0}))=\lim_{j\to\infty}d(u_{j}(t),u_{j}(t_{0}))\leq\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{t\wedge t_{0}}^{t\vee t_{0}}\eta_{j}(r)\,{\rm d}r=\int_{t\wedge t_{0}}^{t\vee t_{0}}\eta(r)\,{\rm d}r.

Hence, we can bound

\displaystyle d(u(t),u_{j}(t))\leq d(u(t),u(t_{0}))+d(u(t_{0}),u_{j}(t_{0}))+d(u_{j}(t_{0}),u_{j}(t))

\displaystyle\quad\leq\int_{t\wedge t_{0}}^{t\vee t_{0}}\eta(r)\,{\rm d}r+d(u(t_{0}),u_{j}(t_{0}))+\int_{t\wedge t_{0}}^{t\vee t_{0}}\eta_{j}(r)\,{\rm d}r\quad\text{for a.e.}\ t\in[0,T].

We then use that
\eta_{j}
converges to
\eta
to further bound the right-hand side. It is thus bounded independently of
j
and
t\in[0,T]
, hence the
Dominated Convergence Theorem implies that
d(u_{j},v)\to d(u,v)
strongly in
L^{r}(0,T)
for every
r\in(1,\infty)
.
Using the lower semicontinuity of
\phi
and Fatou’s Lemma as

\phi
is nonnegative we can
hence deduce that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}(\alpha,u)=\int_{0}^{T}\left(-\alpha(r)d(u(r),v(r))+\phi(u(r))-\phi(v(r))-\mu_{0}d^{q}(u(r),v(r))\right)\,{\rm d}r

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\left(-\alpha_{j}(r)d(u_{j}(r),v(r)){+}\phi(u_{j}(r)){-}\phi(v(r)){-}\mu_{0}d^{q}(u_{j}(r),v(r))\right)\,{\rm d}r

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}E(\alpha_{j}(r),u_{j}(r))\,{\rm d}r

by taking the maximum. This proves the claim.
∎

Corollary 5.2

(Lower semicontinuity of
{\mathcal{G}}
and
{\mathcal{H}}
)
.

Let
(\alpha_{j},u_{j})_{j}\in K(u^{0})
be such that

\alpha_{j}\to\alpha
weakly in
L^{p^{\prime}}(0,T)
,
u_{j}(t)\to u(t)
for almost all
t\in[0,T]
with
u(0)=u^{0}
,
u\in AC^{p}([0,T];U)
,

d(u_{j}(s),u_{j}(t))\leq\int_{s}^{t}\eta_{j}(r)\,{\rm d}r\quad\forall 0\leq s\leq t\leq T,j\in\mathbb{N}

for some
\eta_{j}\in L^{1}(0,T)
converging to
\eta
weakly in

Remark 5.5

(Null-minimization)
.

Checking

Proposition 5.6

(Penalization via

\ |\partial\phi|\ \text{is a strong upper gradient

The first step to extend the results of the previous sections is to obtain an alternative
variational representation of the local slope

Let us start by proving the following properties of the

Proposition 6.1

(Properties of the
\mu
-slope)
.

For all

one has that

\displaystyle\text{the map}\ \mu\in[0,\infty)\mapsto|\partial\phi|_{\mu}(u)\ \text{is nonincreasing},

(6.30)

\displaystyle|\partial\phi|(u)\leq|\partial\phi|_{\mu}(u)\leq|\partial\phi|_{0}(u)=\mathfrak{l}_{\phi}(u),

(6.31)

\displaystyle\lim_{\mu\to\infty}|\partial\phi|_{\mu}(u)=|\partial\phi|(u),

(6.32)

\displaystyle\lim_{\mu\to 0}|\partial\phi|_{\mu}(u)=|\partial\phi|_{0}(u)=\mathfrak{l}_{\phi}(u).

(6.33)

In case
\mu_{0}<\infty
we have that
|\partial\phi|=|\partial\phi|_{\mu}
for all
\mu\geq\mu_{0}
.

Proof.

The monotonicity (
6.30

from the definition of the
\mu
-slope.

For all
v\in U
with

\frac{(\phi(u)-\phi(v))^{+}}{d(u,v)}-{\mu}d^{q-1}(u,v)\leq|\partial\phi|_{\mu}(u)\leq|\partial\phi|_{0}(u)=\mathfrak{l}_{\phi}(u).

Taking the

Fix

we can find

\frac{(\phi(u)-\phi(v))^{+}}{d(u,v)}\leq|\partial\phi|(u)+\varepsilon\quad\forall v\in B_{r}\coloneqq\{w\in U:0<d(u,w)<r\}.

Note that

is independent of
\mu
. We hence have that

\frac{(\phi(u)-\phi(v))^{+}}{d(u,v)}-{\mu}d^{q-1}(u,v)\leq|\partial\phi|(u)+\varepsilon\quad\forall v\in B_{r}.

On the other hand

\displaystyle\frac{(\phi(u)-\phi(v))^{+}}{d(u,v)}-{\mu}d^{q-1}(u,v)\leq\frac{(\phi(u)-\phi(v))^{+}}{r}-{\mu}r^{q-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{\phi(u)}{r}-\mu r^{q-1}\quad\forall v\in U\setminus B_{r}.

Let

\displaystyle\frac{(\phi(u)-\phi(v_{k}))^{+}}{d(u,v_{k})}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\phi(u)-\phi(v_{k}))^{+}}{d(u,v_{k})}-\mu d^{q-1}(u,v_{k})+\mu d^{q-1}(u,v_{k})

\displaystyle\ \leq|\partial\phi|_{\mu}(u)+\mu d^{q-1}(u,v_{k}).

.

In case

∎
