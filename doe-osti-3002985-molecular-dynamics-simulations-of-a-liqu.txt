ABSTRACT
Molecular dynamics simulations were performed of a liquid metal (Ag) interacting with various
zirconia suboxides to calculate the work of adhesion between the liquid metal and the suboxides.
These simulations were then used to assess the impact of oxygen content on wetting. The results
of this work show a wetting transition as oxygen content is reduced.

The phantom wall method was used to estimate the work of adhesion between different zirconia
sub-oxides and the braze filler material, approximated as liquid Ag. Four solid substrates were
considered: BCC Zr, Zr2 O (P-31m), ZrO (P-62m) and monoclinic ZrO2 . Zr and Zr-O structures
were taken from the Open Quantum Mechanics Database, lattice constants of Zr and Zr-O were
found using NPT ensemble MD simulations at 1600K. Structures were made initially as a layer of
Zr or Zr oxide with a layer of solid Ag, using the lattice constants from the NPT simulations. With
the substrate frozen, the Ag was heated to 3000 K and then cooled back down to 1600 K over
0.005 ns.

In these simulations we used molecular dynamics to evolve the Ag atoms in a canonical (i.e., fixed
NVT, where N is the number of atoms, V is the volume and T is the temperature) ensemble with a
Nose-Hoover thermostat, while the Zr and O atoms were allowed to move as a single rigid body.
A timestep of 0.5 × 10−6 ns was used to integrate Newton’s equations of motion with a damping
parameter of 0.5 × 10−4 ns. After equilibrating the liquid Ag atoms for 0.1 ns at 1600 K, we
introduced two walls, 𝑎 and 𝑏, with interactions configured such that wall 𝑎 has an initial position
in the Ag and only interacts with Zr and O atoms, while wall 𝑏 has an initial position in the oxide

The values for the work of separation as a function of oxygen content are given in Fig. 1-1. There
should be some care taken when interpreting these results. These simulations require the substrate
to be frozen, which will certainly affect the calculated work of separation. Additionally, we are
not considering the impact of adsorption on the work of separation, or surface tension for these
systems. Finally, we are only considering one termination surface for each compound considered.
With those assumptions in mind, the lower oxygen content oxides display much higher works of
separation compared to ZrO2 . Using Eq. (3), this results in complete wetting of liquid Ag on Zr,
Zr2 O and ZrO; while Ag appears to only partially wet ZrO2 . As Eq. 3 shows, wetting of a liquid
to a substrate is only possible when the work of separation of the solid and liquid is approximately
the same as the work of separation for the pure liquid (2𝜎). As described in Ref. [1], this
condition can be met when chemical bonding exists between the two phases. With that in mind, it
should be noted that the band gap of ZrO2 is over 5 eV [4], while density functional theory
calculations show ZrO and Zr2 O to have no band gap [3]. While there are only four data points
