TASTI-GRID: OVERVIEW OF THE STATE OF OREGON’S RESILIENCE AND RELIABILITY

This report is intended to help your state identify the most effective investments to improve grid resilience and reliability,
based on analysis of outage data, weather events, and the current state of the electric grid. The information presented is
derived from the best available data. The discussions and recommendations included provide context and valuable
insights into potential high returns on investment (ROI). Specifically, the report offers an overview of electric grid
resilience in Oregon, covering outage information, reasons for outages, calculated "resilience scores" across the state, and
recommendations for both immediate and long-term investments in grid infrastructure. The document provides a concise
overview of the analytical capabilities of the TASTI-GRID team. By utilizing additional data and resources, it synthesizes
insights from the platform to present a distinct perspective on investing in grid resilience throughout Oregon. This
document is not intended to influence or direct state-level decisions related to investments in grid resilience and
reliability. TASTI-GRID should not be used for real-time monitoring, supporting the ESF #12 emergency response
functions, or predicting the restoration times for outages.

OREGON’S ELECTRIC CRITICAL INFRASTRUCTURE RESILIENCE CHALLENGES

Across the state, Oregon faces unique challenges to critical infrastructure resilience – exacerbated by resource strained
counties experiencing fast population growth and decline, transmission system constraints, and vulnerabilities from
seismic activity, including tsunamis, as well as wildland fire and drought. In the Willamette Valley, Columbia River
Gorge, and Cascades, accelerated population growth for the last decade has increased stress on maintenance-backlogged
infrastructure. And, where assessments and upgrades for energy facilities are greatly needed, the urban growth boundaries
(UGB) for these counties create additional challenges to addressing the strain on infrastructure as the influx of people
moving to Oregon has tested land use capacity. Additionally, as communities try to adapt to the population increase in

these areas, a greater push for housing-issue solutions may reduce priorities and funding related to upgrading
infrastructure. As with other states in the West, adjacent land management further challenges mitigation for vegetation
and weather-related impacts– with federal and public land holdings abutting critical infrastructure assets and utility
jurisdictions. Added elements of environmental protection areas and Federally Recognized Tribal land creates further
complexities for maintaining and updating assets.

Energy and water resource challenges compound backlogs in maintenance, vegetation management, and demand for new
infrastructure across Deschutes, Crook, and Jefferson counties. Demands for housing across the Portland Metro Area
(parts of Columbia, Washington, Multnomah, Clackamas, and Yamhill counties, in addition to traffic and major
transportation congestion into Marion, Polk, Linn, Benton, and Lane Counties. Reduced funding for infrastructure
improvements among rural counties like Wasco, Sherman, and Wheeler Counties are at odds with the rapid proliferation
of data centers along the Gorge – even with high renewable generation and low numbers of utility customers.

Counties within the Columbia River Gorge are not well represented by EAGLE-I data. Due to the small populations and small
numbers of utility customers within these counties, many outages likely do not meet thresholds for event reporting. These
counties generate far more power via solar, wind, and hydropower than is used by utility customers. This major generation
capacity makes the Gorge an attractive area for data center operations. However, challenges related to load growth and
unprepared distribution capacity may be at odds with one another, in addition to existing and already-strained transmission
bottlenecks. Historically, these rural areas based in agriculture and tourism, and existing infrastructure may not be adequate to
support coexistence of historic communities, data centers, and adjacent industries to support the data center workforce.

NOTABLE WEATHER-BASED OUTAGES (2020-25)

Compared to states across the country, those in the Pacific Northwest experience infrequent, yet extreme weather-based
outages, with wildland fire and subsequent impacts as a constant across the state. Recent extreme drought has led to an
increase in fire risk and susceptibility, with low snowpack in the Cascades creating compounded challenges with drought
in the foothills and valleys. In 2021, the Willamette Valley heat dome affected PGE customers across the Portland Metro
Area and down into Lane County – in the City of Portland, extreme temperatures hit upwards of 115 degrees F, causing
outages across neighborhoods and boroughs. Extreme weather patterns in the winter from snow and ice, as well as
estuarine flooding during the rainy season, has stressed not only the electric grid, roadways, and access to remote areas,
but has follow-on impacts related to erosion and landslides, compound flooding, and vulnerability to future seismic
activity and wave events.

Oregon’s least resilient counties are the most populous in the state, with increasing population numbers, and
overburdened housing need, transportation, utility affordability, and strain on critical infrastructure. While
Oregon’s weather-based outages are from infrequent events with long durations, a system characterized by
approximately $12 billion in maintenance backlogs (ASCE 2025), an influx of data center activities, and
transmission bottlenecks is likely to produce ramifications for both customers and utilities alike. The consistent
wildland fire risk needed vegetation and land management schema, and vulnerabilities to tsunamis and wave
events, earthquakes, and volcanic activity necessitate state-level actions aimed at a coupled-systems approach to
resilience.

CRITICAL INFRASTRUCTURE RESILIENCE RECOMMENDATIONS USING TASTI-GRID

Using TASTI-GRID, it is recommended that the state integrate grid investments that prioritize not only
reduction in service disruption but also address weather-related emergency management and energy security
issues. Overall resilience in the state will require both top-down and bottom-up efforts aided by data and
resources for decision-making support, with hands-on stakeholder engagement and consideration of
multidimensional drivers behind localized resilience.
