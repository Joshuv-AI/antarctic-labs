Scaling Limits of Bivariate Nearly-Unstable Hawkes Processes and Applications to Rough Volatility
Sohaib El Karmi

Abstract
. We prove a functional limit theorem for a pair of nearly-unstable Hawkes processes interacting through a triangular cross-excitation mechanism with kernels with distinct heavy tails. To our knowledge this is the first functional limit theorem of this type in the heterogeneous case, where the two components have different degrees of roughness. As the system approaches criticality, the renormalized intensity processes converge weakly to the unique solution of a coupled stochastic Volterra system driven by two independent Brownian motions. The first component evolves autonomously as a rough fractional diffusion with Hurst index H 1 = α 1 − 1 / 2 H_{1}=\alpha_{1}-1/2 . The second component is driven both by its own noise and, through a convolution cross-kernel, by the noise of the first: it is this cross-kernel, the convolution of the two individual Mittag-Leffler kernels, that encodes both Hurst parameters simultaneously and distinguishes the bivariate limit from any product of independent univariate limits or any classical bivariate Brownian model. A key consequence is that the two components are decorrelated at short time scales : their functional correlation ϱ ⁡ ( t ) \varrho(t) vanishes as C ϱ ​ t α 1 C_{\varrho}\,t^{\alpha_{1}} as t ↓ 0 t\downarrow 0 , with the exponent governed exclusively by the rougher of the two components and the constant C ϱ C_{\varrho} given in closed form. We also show that hypothesis (A5) (scale matching) is not a structural restriction: dropping it replaces K 1 ∗ K 2 K_{1}*K_{2} by a time-rescaled version K 1 ∗ K 2 ( ρ 12 ) K_{1}*K_{2}^{(\rho_{12})} with an explicit correction factor. The proof is self-contained and combines four main ingredients: convergence of the renormalized kernels in the space of square-integrable functions, tightness of the joint intensity process in the space C ⁡ ( [ 0 , 1 ] , ℝ 2 ) C([0,1];\mathbb{R}^{2}) , identification of every limit point as a martingale solution via Rebolledo’s theorem, and uniqueness from the affine Volterra theory of Abi Jaber, Larsson, and Pulido, which we extend to a new theorem (Theorem 7.3 ) covering equations with random adapted drifts. We also identify precisely the three obstructions that prevent extending the proof to the fully symmetric case ψ 21 ≠ 0 \psi^{21}\neq 0 .

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

Hawkes processes
[
9
]
are self-exciting point processes
whose intensity is driven by the history of past events through a memory kernel.
When this kernel has heavy tails and the process operates near criticality
(branching ratio close to one), the macroscopic fluctuations of the intensity
exhibit long memory and fractional scaling.

The univariate limit theory is well developed.
Jaisson and Rosenbaum
[
16
]
proved that a nearly-unstable Hawkes
process with a power-law kernel of exponent
\alpha\in(1/2,1)
converges,
after appropriate renormalization, to a rough fractional diffusion
with Hurst parameter
H=\alpha-1/2\in(0,1/2)
.
This gives a microscopic derivation of rough volatility models,
which are empirically supported by high-frequency equity data
[
8
]
.
Subsequent work by Horst, Xu, and Zhang
[
15
]
and Horst and Xu
[
10
]

extended these results to more general kernels and established

C
-tightness of the intensity process in
C([0,1])
.

The present paper addresses what happens when
two
nearly-unstable
Hawkes components cross-excite each other.

1.2.
The bivariate problem and its difficulty

Bivariate Hawkes processes arise naturally in financial microstructure,
for instance as models of the joint order flow of two related assets
or the co-excitation structure of a bid-ask spread
[
5
,
6
,
4
]
.
In such applications the two components typically exhibit different degrees of
roughness, reflecting different autocorrelation structures in the underlying flows.
Empirical calibrations on high-frequency equity data indicate that the

nearly-unstable subcritical
regime is essential for reproducing the
clustering and autocorrelation patterns observed in order flow
[
5
,
8
]
;
the present paper provides a rigorous functional limit theorem underpinning
this observation in the bivariate heavy-tailed case.

The essential difficulty in the heterogeneous case is the following.
When the two self-exciting kernels have distinct heavy-tail exponents

1/2<\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}<1
, the two components must be renormalized
on
different time scales
simultaneously: the first by
T^{\alpha_{1}-1}
,
the second by
T^{\alpha_{2}-1}
.
Because these scales are different, the cross-excitation between the components
does not vanish in the limit nor reduce to a simple Brownian correlation.
Instead it contributes a genuinely functional dependence: the limiting
correlation structure is carried by a new kernel, the convolution

L_{12}=b_{\infty}^{12}\|\psi^{12}\|_{1}\cdot(K_{1}*K_{2})
,
which encodes both exponents simultaneously.
No existing limit theorem for bivariate Hawkes processes addresses this
simultaneous renormalization problem.
Multivariate Hawkes processes have been studied in the finite-dimensional
subcritical regime
[
2
,
9
]
,
and Bacry–Muzy
[
5
]
have calibrated bivariate models to
financial data, but neither paper addresses the near-critical scaling limit
with heterogeneous tail indices.

1.3.
Main novelty

To our knowledge, this is the first functional limit theorem for a bivariate
nearly-unstable Hawkes process with heterogeneous heavy-tail indices

1/2<\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}<1
.

The cross-kernel.

The cross-excitation survives renormalization and is captured in the limit
by the effective kernel
L_{12}=b_{\infty}^{12}\|\psi^{12}\|_{1}\cdot(K_{1}*K_{2})
,
where
K_{i}
are the individual Mittag-Leffler kernels of the two components
(Proposition
3.5
).
The convolution
K_{1}*K_{2}
has Laplace exponent
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}
,
strictly larger than either individual exponent, so the cross-kernel is

smoother
than either self-kernel.
The correlation between the two limit processes is therefore not a
constant Brownian correlation but a time-dependent functional dependence
whose strength decreases with the lag.

Short-time decorrelation.

A direct consequence is that the two components are decorrelated at short time scales
(i.e. as the time lag
t\downarrow 0
, not in the sense of trading frequency).
Their functional correlation satisfies

\varrho(t)\sim C_{\varrho}\,t^{\alpha_{1}}
as
t\downarrow 0
,
where
\alpha_{1}
is the exponent of the rougher component and
C_{\varrho}

is an explicit constant (Corollary
3.8
).
The rougher component governs the rate at which correlation is lost at fine scales.
This power-law decay of correlation at short lags is consistent with the
empirical
Epps effect

[
3
]
, which documents decreasing
cross-correlations between asset returns as the sampling frequency increases.

1.4.
Main contributions

The core of the paper (Sections
3
–
7
)
establishes the following results for the triangular case
(
\psi^{12}\neq 0
,
\psi^{21}=0
), in which component 1 excites component 2
but not conversely.

(1) Functional limit theorem
(Theorem
7.8
).
The bivariate renormalized intensity process
(V^{T,1},V^{T,2})
converges
weakly in
C([0,1];\mathbb{R}^{2})
to the unique weak solution of a coupled
stochastic Volterra system in which
V^{1}
is autonomous and
V^{2}
is driven
by both its own noise and the noise of
V^{1}
through the cross-kernel
L_{12}
.
The two components have distinct Hurst parameters
H_{i}=\alpha_{i}-1/2
.

(2) Cross-kernel identification and covariance formula

(Proposition
3.5
).
The effective cross-kernel is
L_{12}=b_{\infty}^{12}\|\psi^{12}\|_{1}\cdot(K_{1}*K_{2})
,
and the covariance between the two limit processes is given by an explicit
integral formula involving
K_{1}
and
K_{1}*K_{2}
.

(3) Short-time decorrelation law
(Corollary
3.8
).
The functional correlation satisfies
\varrho(t)\sim C_{\varrho}\,t^{\alpha_{1}}

as
t\downarrow 0
, with an explicit constant
C_{\varrho}
.

(4) Bivariate
C
-tightness
(Proposition
5.5
).
The cross-term in
V^{T,2}
has strictly better Hölder regularity
than the self-term, with exponent
2(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})-1>2\alpha_{2}-1
.
This is the key estimate showing that cross-excitation does not deteriorate
the path regularity of the limit.

(5) Uniqueness and ALP extension

(Theorems
7.7
and
7.3
).
The limiting system has a unique weak solution in law.
The proof rests on a new result (Theorem
7.3
):
the uniqueness theorem of Abi Jaber, Larsson, and Pulido
[
13
]

for scalar affine SVEs extends to equations whose drift is a random

\mathcal{G}
-measurable process, provided
\mathcal{G}\perp W
.
This extension (stated as an independent theorem applicable to any
admissible kernel, not just Mittag-Leffler) is used to close
the uniqueness argument for
V^{2}
conditionally on
V^{1}
,
and is the new technical ingredient in the bivariate setting.

Throughout this paper we restrict attention to the
triangular

case (
\psi^{12}\neq 0
,
\psi^{21}=0
): component 1 excites component 2
but not conversely.
We now identify precisely the three points at which the proof breaks
down when mutual cross-excitation
\psi^{21}\neq 0
is allowed,
so that this paper’s hypotheses are not merely a matter of convenience.

Obstruction 1: uniform moment bounds fail (C-tightness).

The
C
-tightness of the cross-term
\mathcal{C}^{T}

(Proposition
5.5
) uses the bound

\sup_{T,u}\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{u}^{T,1}]\leq\mu_{T}^{1}/(1-a_{T}^{1})

(Lemma
3.4
(i)), which under (A4) is
O(T^{2\alpha_{1}-1})
(growing with
T
, but controlled
uniformly in
u\in[0,T]
for fixed
T
, sufficient for C-tightness).
This bound follows because, in the triangular model, taking expectations
in the intensity equation for
\lambda^{T,1}
gives the scalar convolution
inequality
\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{t}^{T,1}]\leq\mu_{T}^{1}+a_{T}^{1}\int_{0}^{t}\phi^{1}(t-s)\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{s}^{T,1}]\,ds
,
solved by
\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{t}^{T,1}]\leq\mu_{T}^{1}/(1-a_{T}^{1})
.

When
\psi^{21}\neq 0
, the mean intensities satisfy the
2\times 2

convolution system

(1)

\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{t}^{T,i}]=\mu_{T}^{i}+a_{T}^{i}(\phi^{i}*\mathbb{E}[\lambda^{T,i}])(t)+b_{T}^{ji}(\psi^{ji}*\mathbb{E}[\lambda^{T,j}])(t),\quad i\neq j,\quad b_{T}^{ji}\to b_{\infty}^{ji}>0.

In Laplace space (
z>0
), writing
m_{i}(z):=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-zt}\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{t}^{T,i}]\,dt
,
system (
1
) becomes

(I-A_{T}(z))\mathbf{m}(z)=\boldsymbol{\mu}(z)

where
A_{T}(z)_{ii}=a_{T}^{i}\widehat{\phi^{i}}(z)
and

A_{T}(z)_{ij}=b_{T}^{ji}\widehat{\psi^{ji}}(z)
for
i\neq j
.
The solution is
\mathbf{m}(z)=(I-A_{T}(z))^{-1}\boldsymbol{\mu}(z)
,
which is finite if and only if
\det(I-A_{T}(z))\neq 0
.
At
z=0
:
A_{T}(0)_{ii}=a_{T}^{i}\to 1
and
A_{T}(0)_{ij}=b_{\infty}^{ji}>0
,
so

\det(I-A_{T}(0))=(1-a_{T}^{1})(1-a_{T}^{2})-b_{\infty}^{12}b_{\infty}^{21}\sim\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}T^{-(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})}-b_{\infty}^{12}b_{\infty}^{21}.

Since
b_{\infty}^{12}b_{\infty}^{21}>0
and
T^{-(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})}\to 0
,
there exists
T_{0}<\infty
such that for all
T\geq T_{0}
:

\det(I-A_{T}(0))<0
.
By continuity, the determinant changes sign at some
z^{*}(T)\to 0
,
meaning
(I-A_{T}(z))^{-1}
has a pole at
z^{*}(T)>0
that approaches
0
.
This pole causes
m_{i}(z)\to+\infty
as
z\to z^{*}(T)
,
which by a Tauberian theorem implies

\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{t}^{T,i}]\to+\infty
as
t\to\infty

at rate
e^{z^{*}(T)t}
, in particular,

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{t}^{T,i}]\to+\infty

as
T\to\infty
, making it impossible to satisfy
condition (H2) of Proposition
5.1
uniformly in
T
.

Obstruction 2: the off-diagonal quadratic covariation does not renormalize (identification).

The identification argument (Section
6
) uses
Rebolledo’s theorem to pass to the limit in the martingale problem:
the diagonal terms
\langle M^{T,i}\rangle
renormalize to

\int_{0}^{t}K_{i}^{2}*\bar{V}^{i}\,ds
under the scaling
T^{\alpha_{i}-1}
.
When
\psi^{21}\neq 0
, one must also handle the off-diagonal covariation.
The two compensated processes are

\widetilde{M}^{T,i}_{t}=T^{\alpha_{i}-1}(N^{T,i}_{Tt}-\Lambda^{T,i}_{Tt})
,
and their joint quadratic covariation satisfies

\langle\widetilde{M}^{T,1},\widetilde{M}^{T,2}\rangle_{t}=T^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-2}\int_{0}^{Tt}\lambda_{s}^{T,1}\,\mathbf{1}_{N^{T,1}=N^{T,2}}\,ds=0,

since
N^{T,1}
and
N^{T,2}
are
independent
Poisson processes
conditionally on the intensity; their jump times coincide only on a set
of measure zero, so
\langle\widetilde{M}^{T,1},\widetilde{M}^{T,2}\rangle\equiv 0
.
This means
\widetilde{M}^{T,1}\perp\widetilde{M}^{T,2}
as martingales,
and the limit driving noises are independent:
\beta^{1}\perp\beta^{2}
.
So far this is the same as in the triangular case.

The obstruction is different: in the limit system with
\psi^{21}\neq 0
,
the covariance structure of
V^{1}
and
V^{2}
is non-trivial because both
equations share the noise
\beta^{1}
.
Schematically, the covariance density of the two processes is governed by
cross-kernel terms of the form

\int_{0}^{t}L_{21}(t-s)L_{12}(t-s)\,V^{1}_{s}\,ds

(this expression is indicative of the structure; the precise covariance formula
involves a stochastic Fubini argument analogous to Section
3.3

and is not the quadratic covariation in the sense of semimartingale theory,
which is zero since
\beta^{1}\perp\!\!\!\perp\beta^{2}
).
The identification obstruction is the following.
But in Rebolledo’s martingale problem, the identification of a limit
point as a solution of the coupled SVE requires showing that the

cross-term integrand

L_{21}(t-s)\sqrt{V^{2}_{s}}
(resp.
L_{12}(t-s)\sqrt{V^{1}_{s}}
)
arises as the limit of the pre-limit cross-excitation kernel
\tilde{h}^{21}_{T}

(resp.
\tilde{h}^{12}_{T}
), evaluated against the
other
component’s
intensity process.
This requires the joint
L^{1}
convergence

\bigl\|g_{T}^{2}\cdot\tilde{h}_{T}^{21}-K_{2}\cdot L_{21}\bigr\|_{L^{1}([0,t])}\to 0\quad\text{and}\quad\bigl\|g_{T}^{1}\cdot\tilde{h}_{T}^{12}-K_{1}\cdot L_{12}\bigr\|_{L^{1}([0,t])}\to 0

simultaneously and uniformly
in the joint law of
(V^{T,1},V^{T,2})
,
which is not guaranteed by the separate scalar convergences of
Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
.
More precisely, the quadratic variation error involves the joint process

\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{s}^{T,1}\lambda_{s}^{T,2}]
, which is not controlled
by the individual bounds
\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{s}^{T,i}]\leq C
when the
two components are coupled; controlling
\mathbb{E}[\lambda^{T,1}\lambda^{T,2}]

would require a second-moment estimate for the coupled system,
which is not established in the present paper.

Obstruction 3: the independence
\mathcal{G}\perp\beta^{2}
fails (uniqueness).

This is the most fundamental obstruction.
In the uniqueness proof (Theorem
7.7
, Step 3),
the key hypothesis of Theorem
7.3
is

\mathcal{G}\perp W
, where
\mathcal{G}=\sigma(V^{1},\beta^{1})

and
W=\beta^{2}
.
This holds in the triangular case because

V^{1}
is a functional of
\beta^{1}
alone (the autonomous SVE (
49
)
involves only
\beta^{1}
), so
\sigma(V^{1})\subseteq\sigma(\beta^{1})
,
and
\beta^{1}\perp\beta^{2}
implies
\mathcal{G}\perp\beta^{2}
.

When
\psi^{21}\neq 0
, the limit equation for
V^{1}
becomes

V^{1}_{t}=b_{1}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}(t-s)\sqrt{V^{1}_{s}}\,d\beta^{1}_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}L_{21}(t-s)\sqrt{V^{2}_{s}}\,d\beta^{2}_{s}
,
so
V^{1}
depends on both
\beta^{1}

and

\beta^{2}
.
Therefore
\sigma(V^{1})\not\subseteq\sigma(\beta^{1})
, and in particular

\mathcal{G}=\sigma(V^{1},\beta^{1})
is
not
independent of
\beta^{2}
:
for any Borel set
A
,

\mathbb{P}(V^{1}\in A\,|\,\beta^{2})\neq\mathbb{P}(V^{1}\in A)\quad\text{in general,}

since
V^{1}
is constructed from
\beta^{2}
via the cross-term integral.
The hypothesis
\mathcal{G}\perp\beta^{2}
of
Theorem
7.3
is therefore violated, and the
Laplace-functional factorisation (
53
)
does not hold conditionally on
\mathcal{G}
.

The present uniqueness argument does not extend to the fully symmetric case.
To see why, consider the degenerate case
K_{1}=K_{2}=K
,
L_{21}=L_{12}=L
,

b_{1}=b_{2}=b
. Then
\mathcal{L}(V^{1})=\mathcal{L}(V^{2})
for any weak solution,
but establishing uniqueness of the joint law
\mathcal{L}(V^{1},V^{2})

would require ruling out solutions with the same marginals but different
joint laws, for instance those obtained by permuting the roles of

(\beta^{1},\beta^{2})
in the symmetric system.
The Laplace-functional argument of Theorem
7.3

achieves this in the triangular case via the independence

\mathcal{G}\perp\beta^{2}
, but no analogous argument is available
for the fully coupled bivariate system.

The three obstructions operate at different levels of the proof:
Obstruction 1 prevents establishing C-tightness of the pre-limit sequence;
Obstruction 2 prevents identifying limit points with solutions of the
coupled SVE; and Obstruction 3 prevents proving uniqueness of those solutions.
These are the points at which the present proof does not extend directly
to the fully symmetric case; a different approach would be needed at each step.

Relation to the literature

Jaisson–Rosenbaum
[

]
is the direct predecessor.
We borrow their renormalization scheme and extend it to the bivariate setting.
The new difficulty is the cross-term, which has no counterpart in the

Horst–Xu–Zhang
[

]
provide the
C
-tightness
criterion we apply (Proposition

).
We verify its hypotheses for the cross-term, which requires a separate

Abi Jaber–Larsson–Pulido
[
13
]
provide the uniqueness
theory for affine stochastic Volterra equations.
We use their Theorem 4.3 twice: once for
V^{1}
directly,
and once for
V^{2}
conditional on
V^{1}
after observing that

The extension of their uniqueness result to stochastic adapted drifts
(Theorem

) is new and is not covered by
[

]
.

]
and may find applications beyond

El Euch–Rosenbaum
[

]
identify the
rough Heston model as the limit of a univariate quadratic Hawkes process.
The bivariate limit obtained here can serve as a starting point for

2.
Model, assumptions, and main results

2.1.
The bivariate Hawkes model

Throughout this paper, we work in the asymmetric (triangular) case

\psi^{21}=0
: component 1 excites component 2 but not conversely.
The results below (Sections
3
–
7
)
apply in this setting.
The three specific obstructions that arise when
\psi^{21}\neq 0
are
identified in Section
1
above.

Assumption 2.1
.

The following five conditions hold.

(A1) Heavy-tail self-kernels.

For each
i\in\{1,2\}
, the self-exciting kernel satisfies

\phi^{i}\geq 0
,
\phi^{i}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
,
\|\phi^{i}\|_{1}=1
, and

(2)

1-\widehat{\phi^{i}}(z)=\delta_{i}z^{\alpha_{i}}+o(z^{\alpha_{i}}),\qquad z\downarrow 0,

for some
\alpha_{i}\in(1/2,1)
,
\delta_{i}>0
, with
\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}

(component
1
is the rougher of the two).

(A2) Near-criticality.

The self-exciting amplitudes satisfy
a_{T}^{i}\in(0,1)
,
a_{T}^{i}\uparrow 1
, and

(3)

T^{\alpha_{i}}(1-a_{T}^{i})\longrightarrow\lambda_{i}\in(0,\infty).

(A3) Cross-kernel.

The cross-exciting kernel satisfies
\psi^{12}\geq 0
,

\psi^{12}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
,
and
\phi_{T}^{12}=b_{T}^{12}\psi^{12}
with

(4)

T^{2\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}}\,b_{T}^{12}\longrightarrow b_{\infty}^{12}\in(0,\infty).

Equivalently,
b_{T}^{12}\sim b_{\infty}^{12}\,T^{\alpha_{2}-2\alpha_{1}}
.
This is the critical cross-excitation scaling for the present normalization:
it makes the deterministic cross-compensator in
V^{T,2}
finite and keeps
the stochastic inherited-noise term non-degenerate in the limit.

Remark 2.2

(Interpretation of (A3))
.

The condition (
4
) balances the growth of the first
component, the resolvent amplification of the second component, and the
renormalization of
V^{T,2}
. Indeed,

\mu_{T}^{1}/(1-a_{T}^{1})
is of order
T^{2\alpha_{1}-1}
, while the second
resolvent contributes a factor of order
T^{\alpha_{2}}
and the
normalization of
V^{T,2}
contributes
T^{1-2\alpha_{2}}
. Hence the
renormalized deterministic cross-contribution is of order

b_{T}^{12}T^{2\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}}
, which is finite precisely under
(
4
). The same balance gives a non-trivial stochastic
cross term.

When
\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}=\alpha
(homogeneous case), the condition reduces
to
T^{\alpha}b_{T}^{12}\to b_{\infty}^{12}
, i.e. the cross-amplitude is of
the same order as the distance to criticality
1-a_{T}^{i}
.
When
\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}
, the exponent
\alpha_{2}-2\alpha_{1}
is negative,
so the raw cross-amplitude tends to zero; the effective coupling survives
after the near-critical resolvent amplification.

(A4) Baseline scaling.

The baseline intensities satisfy
T^{1-\alpha_{i}}\mu_{T}^{i}\to m_{i}\geq 0
.
We assume
m_{i}>0
throughout.

(A5) Scale matching.

(5)

\lambda_{1}^{1/\alpha_{1}}=\lambda_{2}^{1/\alpha_{2}}=:\lambda^{*}.

Remark 2.3

(On condition (A1))
.

Condition (A1) is equivalent to saying that
1-\widehat{\phi^{i}}
is regularly
varying at zero with exponent
\alpha_{i}
, i.e.
\phi^{i}
belongs to the
domain of attraction of a positive
\alpha_{i}
-stable law
(Karamata representation theorem
[
19
]
).
The Laplace-domain condition (
2
) is the standard
form for the heavy-tail assumption in the Hawkes literature.

Proposition 2.4

(Necessity of (A5) and its effect on the limit)
.

Suppose Assumptions (A1)–(A4) hold but (A5) is dropped.
Define

(6)

\rho_{12}:=\frac{\lambda_{1}^{1/\alpha_{1}}}{\lambda_{2}^{1/\alpha_{2}}}\in(0,\infty),

so that
\varepsilon_{T}^{1}/\varepsilon_{T}^{2}\to\rho_{12}
as
T\to\infty
.
Then:

(i)

Modified cross-kernel.

The cross-kernel
\tilde{h}_{T}
defined in (
17
) converges in

L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
to

(7)

L_{12}^{(\rho)}:=b_{\infty}^{12}\|\psi^{12}\|_{1}\cdot(K_{1}*K_{2}^{(\rho_{12})}),

where
K_{2}^{(\rho_{12})}(t):=\rho_{12}^{-1}\,K_{2}(t/\rho_{12})
is the
Mittag-Leffler kernel of the second component time-rescaled by
\rho_{12}
.
Under (A5),
\rho_{12}=1
and
L_{12}^{(\rho)}=L_{12}
.

(ii)

C-tightness.

The bivariate C-tightness of Proposition
5.5
remains valid
without (A5), with

Remark 2.5

(The deterministic mean profiles)
.

The normalization (

from the

Theorem 2.7

(Main result)

\displaystyle V_{t}^{1}

\displaystyle=b_{1}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}(t-s)\sqrt{V_{s}^{1}}\,d\beta_{s}^{1},

(15)

\displaystyle V_{t}^{2}

\displaystyle=b_{2}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}K_{2}(t-s)\sqrt{V_{s}^{2}}\,d\beta_{s}^{2}+b_{\infty}^{12}\|\psi^{12}\|_{1}\int_{0}^{t}(K_{1}*K_{2})(t-s)\sqrt{V_{s}^{1}}\,d\beta_{s}^{1},

(i)

(ii)

Remark 2.9

(Interpretation)
.

The structure of (

The first component

the time-dependent, vanishing correlation found here is a distinctive

Lemma 3.1

(
L^{2}
convergence of the self-kernels)
.

(Purpose: the self-resolvent
g_{T}^{i}
converges in
L^{2}
to the
Mittag-Leffler kernel

; the uniform
L^{2}
bound on

is used

and in the tightness proof of

5.5

For each

(18)

g_{T}^{i}\longrightarrow K_{i}\quad\text{in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})

as
T\to\infty
.
In particular
\sup_{T\geq 1}\|g_{T}^{i}\|_{L^{2}}<\infty

Proof.

By the Plancherel identity on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
(via the Fourier extension
to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
of functions supported on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
),
it suffices to show

(19)

\widehat{g_{T}^{i}}(i\xi)\longrightarrow\widehat{K_{i}}(i\xi)\quad\text{for a.e.\ }\xi\in\mathbb{R},

together with a uniform
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
dominating function.

Step 1: Frequency-domain formula.

By the substitution
s\mapsto s/\varepsilon_{T}^{i}
in the Laplace transform:

\widehat{g_{T}^{i}}(i\xi)=(1-a_{T}^{i})\,\widehat{\psi_{T}^{i}}(i\varepsilon_{T}^{i}\xi).

Recall that
\psi_{T}^{i}
is the resolvent of
a_{T}^{i}\phi^{i}
, so

\widehat{\psi_{T}^{i}}(z)=\frac{a_{T}^{i}\widehat{\phi^{i}}(z)}{1-a_{T}^{i}\widehat{\phi^{i}}(z)},

and therefore

(20)

\widehat{g_{T}^{i}}(i\xi)=\frac{(1-a_{T}^{i})\,a_{T}^{i}\,\widehat{\phi^{i}}(i\varepsilon_{T}^{i}\xi)}{1-a_{T}^{i}\widehat{\phi^{i}}(i\varepsilon_{T}^{i}\xi)}.

Step 2: Pointwise convergence.

As
T\to\infty
we have
\varepsilon_{T}^{i}\to 0
and
a_{T}^{i}\to 1
.
Since
\phi^{i}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, the Riemann-Lebesgue lemma gives

\widehat{\phi^{i}}(i\varepsilon_{T}^{i}\xi)\to\widehat{\phi^{i}}(0)=\|\phi^{i}\|_{1}=1
.
By Assumption
2.1
(S2),
\|\phi^{i}\|_{1}=1
and the tail
expansion (
2
) gives

1-a_{T}^{i}\widehat{\phi^{i}}(i\varepsilon_{T}^{i}\xi)=(1-a_{T}^{i})+a_{T}^{i}\bigl(1-\widehat{\phi^{i}}(i\varepsilon_{T}^{i}\xi)\bigr)\sim(1-a_{T}^{i})+\tilde{\delta}_{i}(\varepsilon_{T}^{i}|\xi|)^{\alpha_{i}}\cdot e^{i\theta_{i,\xi}},

where
\theta_{i,\xi}
is a phase depending on the sign of
\xi
.
More precisely, writing
\eta:=\varepsilon_{T}^{i}\xi
and using

1-\widehat{\phi^{i}}(i\eta)\sim\tilde{\delta}_{i}|\eta|^{\alpha_{i}}\cdot e^{-i(\pi/2)\alpha_{i}\,\mathrm{sgn}(\eta)}

(which follows from the assumption that
\phi^{i}
is a probability density
supported on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
in the domain of attraction of a
one-sided stable law with index
\alpha_{i}
: see
[
17
]
,
Theorem 2.4.2, noting that the characteristic function of a positive
random variable has the minus sign in the stable phase):

\displaystyle 1-a_{T}^{i}\widehat{\phi^{i}}(i\varepsilon_{T}^{i}\xi)

\displaystyle\sim(\varepsilon_{T}^{i})^{\alpha_{i}}+\tilde{\delta}_{i}(\varepsilon_{T}^{i})^{\alpha_{i}}|\xi|^{\alpha_{i}}\cdot e^{-i(\pi/2)\alpha_{i}\,\mathrm{sgn}(\xi)}

\displaystyle=(\varepsilon_{T}^{i})^{\alpha_{i}}\bigl(1+\tilde{\delta}_{i}|\xi|^{\alpha_{i}}e^{-i(\pi/2)\alpha_{i}\,\mathrm{sgn}(\xi)}\bigr),

so that (using
(1-a_{T}^{i})=(\varepsilon_{T}^{i})^{\alpha_{i}}
and

):

\widehat{g_{T}^{i}}(i\xi)\to\frac{1}{1+\tilde{\delta}_{i}|\xi|^{\alpha_{i}}e^{-i(\pi/2)\alpha_{i}\,\mathrm{sgn}(\xi)}}=\widehat{K_{i}}(i\xi).

This is the Fourier transform of the Mittag-Leffler kernel
K_{i}

evaluated on the imaginary axis (see e.g.
[
13
]
, Appendix A).

Step 3: Uniform
L^{2}
domination.

From (
20
), the lower bound

|1-a_{T}^{i}\widehat{\phi^{i}}(i\varepsilon_{T}^{i}\xi)|\geq|1-a_{T}^{i}\,\mathrm{Re}(\widehat{\phi^{i}}(i\varepsilon_{T}^{i}\xi))|

and the real-part lower bound

(21)

1-\mathrm{Re}(\widehat{\phi^{i}}(i\xi))\geq c_{i}|\xi|^{\alpha_{i}}\quad\text{for all }|\xi|\leq 1

(which follows from the heavy-tail assumption (
2
)
applied to the real part of the characteristic function)
give, splitting into
|\varepsilon_{T}^{i}\xi|\leq 1
and
|\varepsilon_{T}^{i}\xi|>1
:

•

If
|\varepsilon_{T}^{i}\xi|\leq 1
: using (
21
),

|1-a_{T}^{i}\widehat{\phi^{i}}(i\varepsilon_{T}^{i}\xi)|\geq(1-a_{T}^{i})\bigl(1+c_{i}|\varepsilon_{T}^{i}\xi|^{\alpha_{i}}/(1-a_{T}^{i})\bigr)\geq(\varepsilon_{T}^{i})^{\alpha_{i}}\bigl(1+c_{i}^{\prime}|\xi|^{\alpha_{i}}\bigr).

•

If
|\varepsilon_{T}^{i}\xi|>1
: since
\phi^{i}\in L^{1}
, the Riemann-Lebesgue lemma gives

\widehat{\phi^{i}}(i\eta)\to 0
as

, so for

Combining both regimes with

and

(22)

for constants

\varepsilon_{T}^{2}
with argument
r_{T}\xi
:

1-a_{T}^{2}\widehat{\phi^{2}}(ir_{T}\varepsilon_{T}^{2}\xi)=(\varepsilon_{T}^{2})^{\alpha_{2}}\!\left(1+\tilde{\delta}_{2}|r_{T}\xi|^{\alpha_{2}}e^{-i(\pi/2)\alpha_{2}\,\mathrm{sgn}(\xi)}+o(1)\right).

Since

under (A5):

\frac{1-a_{T}^{2}}{1-a_{T}^{2}\widehat{\phi^{2}}(i\varepsilon_{T}^{1}\xi)}\to\widehat{K_{2}}(i\xi)

for a.e.
\xi
.
Without (A5), the limit is
\widehat{K_{2}}(i\rho_{12}\xi)
with

;
see Proposition
2.4
(i) for the precise statement.

Step 3: assembling the pointwise limit.

Since
T^{2\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}}b_{T}^{12}\to b_{\infty}^{12}
and

\widehat{\psi^{12}}(i\varepsilon_{T}^{1}\xi)\to\|\psi^{12}\|_{1}
(Riemann–Lebesgue):

\widehat{\tilde{h}_{T}}(i\xi)\longrightarrow b_{\infty}^{12}\|\psi^{12}\|_{1}\cdot\widehat{K_{1}}(i\xi)\cdot\widehat{K_{2}}(i\xi)=\widehat{L_{12}}(i\xi).

Step 4: uniform
L^{2}
domination.

From (
24
), the bound (
22
) for factor 1 gives

|\text{factor 1}|\leq C_{1}/(1+c_{1}^{\prime}|\xi|^{\alpha_{1}})
.
For factor 2 at scale
\varepsilon_{T}^{1}=r_{T}\varepsilon_{T}^{2}
with
r_{T}\geq\tfrac{1}{2}
,
Lemma
3.1
Step 3 yields

|1-a_{T}^{2}\widehat{\phi^{2}}(i\varepsilon_{T}^{1}\xi)|\geq c_{2}(\varepsilon_{T}^{2})^{\alpha_{2}}(1+c_{2}^{\prime\prime}|\xi|^{\alpha_{2}})

with

Hence:

(25)

|\widehat{\tilde{h}_{T}}(i\xi)|\leq\frac{C}{(1+c_{1}^{\prime}|\xi|^{\alpha_{1}})(1+c_{2}^{\prime\prime}|\xi|^{\alpha_{2}})}=:F(\xi),

uniformly in
T\geq T_{0}
.
Since

.
Dominated convergence in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and Plancherel
give
\tilde{h}_{T}\to L_{12}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.
∎

Lemma 3.3

(
L^{1}
convergence of the kernel product)
.

(Purpose:

is the integrand appearing in

to

For every

\bigl\|g_{T}^{1}\cdot\tilde{h}_{T}-K_{1}\cdot L_{12}\bigr\|_{L^{1}([0,t])}\longrightarrow 0

as

.

Proof.

Write

g_{T}^{1}\tilde{h}_{T}-K_{1}L_{12}=\underbrace{(g_{T}^{1}-K_{1})\tilde{h}_{T}}_{A_{T}}+\underbrace{K_{1}(\tilde{h}_{T}-L_{12})}_{B_{T}}.

By Cauchy-Schwarz:

\|A_{T}\|_{L^{1}([0,t])}\leq\|g_{T}^{1}-K_{1}\|_{L^{2}([0,t])}\cdot\|\tilde{h}_{T}\|_{L^{2}([0,t])}.

The first factor
\to 0
by Lemma
3.1
.
The second factor is

.
Similarly,

\|B_{T}\|_{L^{1}([0,t])}\leq\|K_{1}\|_{L^{2}([0,t])}\cdot\|\tilde{h}_{T}-L_{12}\|_{L^{2}([0,t])},

∎

3.2.
First-order moments of the pre-limit intensities

The covariance computation requires control on
\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{t}^{T,i}]

that is both uniform in
T
and explicit enough to be used as a bound,
not merely as an asymptotic.
The following lemma provides both.

Lemma 3.4

(First-moment bound and renormalized order)
.

Under Assumption
2.1
, the following estimates hold.

(i)

Component 1.
Since
N^{T,1}
is autonomous,
for all
T\geq 1
and
t\geq 0
,

(27)

\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{t}^{T,1}]\leq\frac{\mu_{T}^{1}}{1-a_{T}^{1}}.

Consequently
\sup_{T,t\in[0,1]}\mathbb{E}[V_{t}^{T,1}]<\infty
.

(ii)

Component 2.
For all
T\geq 1
and
t\geq 0
,

(28)

\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{t}^{T,2}]\leq\frac{\mu_{T}^{2}}{1-a_{T}^{2}}+\frac{b_{T}^{12}\|\psi^{12}\|_{1}}{1-a_{T}^{2}}\,\frac{\mu_{T}^{1}}{1-a_{T}^{1}}.

Under (A2)–(A4), both contributions are of the correct order after the
normalization (
10
). Indeed, the self term is of order

T^{2\alpha_{2}-1}
before normalization, while the cross term is of order

b_{T}^{12}T^{\alpha_{2}}T^{2\alpha_{1}-1}=O(T^{2\alpha_{2}-1})

by (
4
). Multiplication by

(1-a_{T}^{2})/(m_{2}T^{\alpha_{2}-1})=O(T^{1-2\alpha_{2}})
therefore gives a
uniform
O(1)
bound for
\mathbb{E}[V_{t}^{T,2}]
.

(iii)

Deterministic profiles.
The deterministic
parts of the renormalized intensities converge locally uniformly to the
profiles
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
defined in (
11
)–(
12
).
In particular the triangular cross-compensator does not disappear; it is the
last term in (
12
).

Proof.

For component 1, taking expectations in (
8
) gives the scalar
renewal equation

\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{t}^{T,1}]=\mu_{T}^{1}+\int_{0}^{t}\phi_{T}^{1}(t-s)\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{s}^{T,1}]\,ds.

The non-negative resolvent formula yields

\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{t}^{T,1}]=\mu_{T}^{1}\left(1+\int_{0}^{t}\psi_{T}^{1}(s)\,ds\right)\leq\mu_{T}^{1}(1+\|\psi_{T}^{1}\|_{1})=\frac{\mu_{T}^{1}}{1-a_{T}^{1}},

which proves (
27
).

For component 2, taking expectations in (
9
) gives

\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{t}^{T,2}]=\mu_{T}^{2}+\phi_{T}^{2}*\mathbb{E}[\lambda^{T,2}](t)+\phi_{T}^{12}*\mathbb{E}[\lambda^{T,1}](t).

Applying the resolvent
(\delta_{0}+\psi_{T}^{2})
and then using
(
27
) gives

\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{t}^{T,2}]\leq\frac{\mu_{T}^{2}}{1-a_{T}^{2}}+\frac{1}{1-a_{T}^{2}}\,b_{T}^{12}\|\psi^{12}\|_{1}\,\frac{\mu_{T}^{1}}{1-a_{T}^{1}},

which is (
28
). The order estimates follow by substituting

(1-a_{T}^{i})\sim\lambda_{i}T^{-\alpha_{i}}
,
\mu_{T}^{i}\sim m_{i}T^{\alpha_{i}-1}
,
and
b_{T}^{12}=O(T^{\alpha_{2}-2\alpha_{1}})
.
The locally uniform convergence of the deterministic profiles follows from
the
L^{2}
kernel convergences of Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
,
together with the above first-moment bounds.
∎

3.3.
Main proposition: cross-kernel and covariance

Proposition 3.5

(Cross-kernel structure and covariance formula)
.

Under Assumption
2.1
, the following hold.

(i)

Volterra decomposition.

For each
T\geq 1
, the process
V^{T,2}
admits the decomposition

(29)

V_{t}^{T,2}=D_{t}^{T}+\mathcal{S}_{t}^{T}+\mathcal{C}_{t}^{T},

where
D^{T}
is deterministic,

(30)

\mathcal{S}_{t}^{T}:=\frac{1-a_{T}^{2}}{m_{2}\,T^{\alpha_{2}-1}}\int_{0}^{Tt}\psi_{T}^{2}(Tt-u)\,dM_{u}^{T,2}

is the self-excited martingale component, and

(31)

\mathcal{C}_{t}^{T}:=\frac{1-a_{T}^{2}}{m_{2}\,T^{\alpha_{2}-1}}\int_{0}^{Tt}\Psi_{T}^{12}(Tt-u)\,dM_{u}^{T,1}

is the cross-excited martingale component, where

(32)

\widehat{\Psi_{T}^{12}}(z)=\widehat{\phi_{T}^{12}}(z)\cdot\frac{1}{1-a_{T}^{2}\widehat{\phi^{2}}(z)}\cdot\frac{1}{1-a_{T}^{1}\widehat{\phi^{1}}(z)}.

(ii)

Orthogonality.

\langle\mathcal{S}^{T},\mathcal{C}^{T}\rangle_{t}=0
for all
t\in[0,1]
.

(iii)

Cross-kernel identification and covariance formula.

There exists a deterministic kernel
L_{12}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
with

(33)

L_{12}=b_{\infty}^{12}\|\psi^{12}\|_{1}\cdot(K_{1}*K_{2}),

such that for all
t\in[0,1]
, the exact covariance of the limit process

(V^{1},V^{2})
of Theorem
7.8
is:

(34)

\mathrm{Cov}(V_{t}^{1},V_{t}^{2})=b_{\infty}^{12}\|\psi^{12}\|_{1}\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}(t-s)\,(K_{1}*K_{2})(t-s)\,b_{1}(s)\,ds,

where
b_{1}
is defined in (
11
).
In the stationary regime
b_{1}(s)\equiv\bar{V}^{1}
, formula (
34
)
simplifies to

(35)

\mathrm{Cov}(V_{t}^{1},V_{t}^{2})=b_{\infty}^{12}\|\psi^{12}\|_{1}\cdot\bar{V}^{1}\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}(u)\,(K_{1}*K_{2})(u)\,du.

Both formulas identify
L_{12}=b_{\infty}^{12}\|\psi^{12}\|_{1}\cdot(K_{1}*K_{2})

as the effective cross-kernel.

Remark 3.6

(Covariance scaling)
.

Formulas (
34
)–(
35
) concern the

limit process

(V^{1},V^{2})
only.
The raw pre-limit covariance
\mathrm{Cov}(V_{t}^{T,1},V_{t}^{T,2})
diverges
as
T\to\infty
at rate
T^{1-\alpha_{2}}
because the two components are
renormalized on different scales
T^{\alpha_{1}-1}
and
T^{\alpha_{2}-1}
.

(iv)

Regularity of
L_{12}
.

L_{12}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
with

(36)

L_{12}(t)\sim C_{12}\,t^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-1},\qquad t\downarrow 0,

where
C_{12}=b_{\infty}^{12}\|\psi^{12}\|_{1}/\bigl(\tilde{\delta}_{1}\tilde{\delta}_{2}\,\Gamma(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})\bigr)
.
In particular
L_{12}
is smoother than each
K_{i}
individually.

Proof.

We prove each part in order.

Part (i): Volterra decomposition.

Starting from the intensity equation (
9
), substitute

dN_{s}^{T,i}=dM_{s}^{T,i}+\lambda_{s}^{T,i}\,ds
:

\displaystyle\lambda_{t}^{T,2}

\displaystyle=\mu_{T}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\phi_{T}^{2}(t-s)\lambda_{s}^{T,2}\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\phi_{T}^{12}(t-s)\lambda_{s}^{T,1}\,ds

(37)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\phi_{T}^{2}(t-s)\,dM_{s}^{T,2}+\int_{0}^{t}\phi_{T}^{12}(t-s)\,dM_{s}^{T,1}.

This is a stochastic Volterra equation
f=g+\phi_{T}^{2}*f
,
whose unique
L^{2}
solution is given by the resolvent formula

\lambda^{T,2}=(\delta_{0}+\psi_{T}^{2})*g
.
We apply the resolvent operator to each of the four terms in
g

(the stochastic terms require the stochastic Fubini theorem
[
21
]
,
which applies since
\phi_{T}^{2},\phi_{T}^{12}\in L^{2}
and
M^{T,i}
are square-integrable):

•

Deterministic term
\mu_{T}^{2}
:

(\delta_{0}+\psi_{T}^{2})*\mu_{T}^{2}=\mu_{T}^{2}(1+\|\psi_{T}^{2}\|_{1})=\mu_{T}^{2}/(1-a_{T}^{2})
.
Together with the deterministic part of
\phi_{T}^{12}*\lambda^{T,1}
,
this yields
D_{t}^{T}
.

•

Self-stochastic term
\phi_{T}^{2}*dM^{T,2}
:

(\delta_{0}+\psi_{T}^{2})*(\phi_{T}^{2}*dM^{T,2})=(\phi_{T}^{2}+\psi_{T}^{2}*\phi_{T}^{2})*dM^{T,2}
.
By the resolvent identity,
\phi_{T}^{2}+\psi_{T}^{2}*\phi_{T}^{2}=\psi_{T}^{2}
exactly,
so the stochastic self-term equals
\int_{0}^{t}\psi_{T}^{2}(t-s)\,dM^{T,2}_{s}
, which
after the renormalization
T^{-(1-\alpha_{2})}/(1-a_{T}^{2})^{-1}
yields
\mathcal{S}_{t}^{T}
.

•

Cross-stochastic term
\phi_{T}^{12}*dM^{T,1}
, further convoluted by
(\delta_{0}+\psi_{T}^{2})
:
Using the Volterra representation of
\lambda^{T,1}

(which is scalar, so
\lambda^{T,1}=D^{T,1}+\int_{0}^{\cdot}\psi_{T}^{1}(\cdot-u)\,dM_{u}^{T,1}
),
the stochastic part of
\phi_{T}^{12}*\lambda^{T,1}
acting through
(\delta_{0}+\psi_{T}^{2})

has Laplace kernel

(1+\widehat{\psi_{T}^{2}})\cdot\widehat{\phi_{T}^{12}}\cdot(1+\widehat{\psi_{T}^{1}})=\frac{\widehat{\phi_{T}^{12}}}{(1-a_{T}^{2}\widehat{\phi^{2}})(1-a_{T}^{1}\widehat{\phi^{1}})}=\widehat{\Psi_{T}^{12}},

establishing (
32
) and yielding
\mathcal{C}_{t}^{T}
after renormalization.

Part (ii): Orthogonality.

Since
N^{T,1}
and
N^{T,2}
are simple point processes on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
they almost surely do not jump simultaneously:

\Delta N_{t}^{T,1}\cdot\Delta N_{t}^{T,2}=0
for all
t
.
Therefore
[N^{T,1},N^{T,2}]_{t}=\sum_{s\leq t}\Delta N_{s}^{T,1}\Delta N_{s}^{T,2}=0
,
hence
[M^{T,1},M^{T,2}]_{t}=0
.
For any square-integrable predictable integrands
f
,
g
:

\langle\mathcal{S}^{T},\mathcal{C}^{T}\rangle_{t}=\left\langle\int_{0}^{\cdot}f\,dM^{T,2},\int_{0}^{\cdot}g\,dM^{T,1}\right\rangle_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}f_{s}g_{s}\,d[M^{T,2},M^{T,1}]_{s}=0.

Parts (iii)–(iv): Covariance formula and regularity of
L_{12}
.

Step 1: Decompose via
\beta^{1}\perp\beta^{2}
.

Write
V^{1}_{t}=b_{1}(t)+X_{t}
and
V^{2}_{t}=b_{2}(t)+Y_{t}+Z_{t}
where

\displaystyle X_{t}

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}(t-s)\sqrt{V^{1}_{s}}\,d\beta^{1}_{s},\quad Y_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}K_{2}(t-s)\sqrt{V^{2}_{s}}\,d\beta^{2}_{s},

\displaystyle Z_{t}

\displaystyle:=b_{\infty}^{12}\|\psi^{12}\|_{1}\int_{0}^{t}(K_{1}*K_{2})(t-s)\sqrt{V^{1}_{s}}\,d\beta^{1}_{s}.

Since
\beta^{1}\perp\beta^{2}
, the processes
(X,Z)
are
\sigma(\beta^{1}_{s}:s\leq t)
-measurable
and
Y
is
\sigma(\beta^{2}_{s}:s\leq t)
-measurable.
Therefore:

(38)

\mathrm{Cov}(V^{1}_{t},V^{2}_{t})=\underbrace{\mathrm{Cov}(X_{t},Y_{t})}_{=\,0}+\mathrm{Cov}(X_{t},Z_{t}).

Step 2: Itô isometry.

Both
X_{t}
and
Z_{t}
are Itô integrals against
\beta^{1}
with square-integrable integrands
(since
K_{1},(K_{1}*K_{2})\in L^{2}
and
\mathbb{E}[\int_{0}^{t}V^{1}_{s}\,ds]<\infty
).
By the Itô isometry:

(39)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}(X_{t},Z_{t})

\displaystyle=b_{\infty}^{12}\|\psi^{12}\|_{1}\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}(t-s)\,(K_{1}*K_{2})(t-s)\,\mathbb{E}[V^{1}_{s}]\,ds.

Step 3: Exact formula and stationary special case.

Taking expectations in the
V^{1}
equation, the stochastic integral has zero mean, so

\mathbb{E}[V^{1}_{s}]=b_{1}(s)
exactly.
Substituting into (
39
) gives the exact
formula (
34
).

When
b_{1}(s)\equiv\bar{V}^{1}
(stationary initial condition), the integral

\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}(t-s)(K_{1}*K_{2})(t-s)\,ds=\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}(u)(K_{1}*K_{2})(u)\,du

(by the substitution
u=t-s
), yielding the stationary
formula (
35
) and identifying
L_{12}
.

For the non-stationary process starting at
V^{1}_{0}=0
, the dominated convergence theorem gives

\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}(t-s)(K_{1}*K_{2})(t-s)b_{1}(s)\,ds\to\bar{V}^{1}\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}(u)(K_{1}*K_{2})(u)\,du

as
t\to\infty
, so the two formulas agree in the long-time limit.

Remark 3.7

(Product integral versus triple convolution)
.

The Itô isometry in Step 2 produces the
product integral

\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}(u)(K_{1}*K_{2})(u)\,du
, which is the pointwise product of
the two kernel functions integrated over
[0,t]
.
This is not the same as the
triple convolution

(K_{1}*K_{1}*K_{2})(t)
,
which is the convolution of
K_{1}*K_{1}
with
K_{2}
evaluated at
t
.
Both objects have the same power-law exponent
2\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-1
,
but they differ by the multiplicative constant

\frac{(K_{1}*K_{1}*K_{2})(t)}{\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}(u)(K_{1}*K_{2})(u)\,du}\;\longrightarrow\;\frac{\Gamma(\alpha_{1})^{2}\,\Gamma(\alpha_{2})\,(2\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-1)}{\Gamma(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})\,\Gamma(2\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})}\;\neq 1

as
t\downarrow 0
.
The product integral is the correct output of the Itô isometry;
the triple convolution is not.

For the non-stationary process (starting at
V^{1}_{0}=0
), the exact formula (
34
)
applies with
b_{1}(s)=m_{1}s+m_{1}\int_{0}^{s}K_{1}(s-r)r\,dr
.
The dominated convergence theorem in the convolution integral gives convergence

\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}(t-s)(K_{1}*K_{2})(t-s)b_{1}(s)\,ds\to\bar{V}^{1}\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}(u)(K_{1}*K_{2})(u)\,du

as
t\to\infty
(using
b_{1}(s)\to\bar{V}^{1}
and
K_{1},(K_{1}*K_{2})\in L^{1}
).

Step 4 (Part iv):
L_{12}\in L^{2}
and asymptotics.

\widehat{L_{12}}(z)=b_{\infty}^{12}\|\psi^{12}\|_{1}/((1+\tilde{\delta}_{1}z^{\alpha_{1}})(1+\tilde{\delta}_{2}z^{\alpha_{2}}))

lies in the
L^{2}
-class of Lemma
3.2
.
As
z\to\infty
,
\widehat{L_{12}}(z)\sim b_{\infty}^{12}\|\psi^{12}\|_{1}/(\tilde{\delta}_{1}\tilde{\delta}_{2}\,z^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}})
.
By Karamata’s Tauberian theorem (see
[
18
]
, Chapter XIII,
or
[
19
]
): for any
\beta\in(0,\infty)
,

\widehat{f}(z)\sim c/z^{\beta}
as
z\to\infty
if and only if
f(t)\sim c\,t^{\beta-1}/\Gamma(\beta)

as
t\downarrow 0
(up to regular variation).
With
\beta=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}\in(1,2)
we obtain

L_{12}(t)\sim C_{12}\,t^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-1}
as
t\downarrow 0
,
with

C_{12}=b_{\infty}^{12}\|\psi^{12}\|_{1}/[\tilde{\delta}_{1}\tilde{\delta}_{2}\,\Gamma(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})]
,
consistent with the direct Beta-convolution computation (
43
)
of Corollary
3.8
.∎

Corollary 3.8

(Short-time decorrelation law)
.

Under Assumption
2.1
,
let
(V^{1},V^{2})
be the weak limit of
(V^{T,1},V^{T,2})
.
In the stationary regime
b_{1}(s)\equiv\bar{V}^{1}
, the functional correlation

\varrho(t):=\frac{\mathrm{Cov}(V_{t}^{1},V_{t}^{2})}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(V_{t}^{1})\,\mathrm{Var}(V_{t}^{2})}}

satisfies

(40)

\varrho(t)\sim C_{\varrho}\,t^{\alpha_{1}},\qquad t\downarrow 0,

where

(41)

C_{\varrho}=\frac{b_{\infty}^{12}\|\psi^{12}\|_{1}\sqrt{\bar{V}^{1}}}{\tilde{\delta}_{1}\sqrt{\bar{V}^{2}}}\cdot\frac{\Gamma(\alpha_{2})\,\sqrt{(2\alpha_{1}-1)(2\alpha_{2}-1)}}{\Gamma(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})\,(2\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-1)}.

In particular
\varrho(t)\to 0
as
t\downarrow 0
:
the two components are decorrelated at short time scales
(the lag
t\downarrow 0
corresponds to time scales shorter than
1/T
in the
pre-limit model, i.e. at intraday resolution),
with the exponent
\alpha_{1}
governed by the rougher component.

Remark 3.9
.

The stationary regime
b_{1}\equiv\bar{V}^{1}
is used here because it is the
regime in which both the numerator (formula (
35
))
and the denominator (
\mathrm{Var}(V_{t}^{i})\sim\bar{V}^{i}\int_{0}^{t}K_{i}(s)^{2}\,ds
)
take their simplest power-law form as
t\downarrow 0
.
The constant
C_{\varrho}
is therefore derived under this regime
and equals the leading coefficient in the short-time expansion of
\varrho
.
In the non-stationary case starting at
V^{1}_{0}=0
,
the formula (
34
) gives the same exponent
\alpha_{1}

but a different pre-constant depending on
b_{1}(s)
.

Proof.

We compute numerator and denominator separately as
t\downarrow 0
,
using the Mittag-Leffler short-time asymptotics

K_{i}(u)\sim u^{\alpha_{i}-1}/[\tilde{\delta}_{i}\Gamma(\alpha_{i})]

and the Beta-convolution identity

(42)

\int_{0}^{t}u^{\alpha_{1}-1}(t-u)^{\alpha_{2}-1}\,du=t^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-1}\,B(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})=t^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-1}\,\frac{\Gamma(\alpha_{1})\Gamma(\alpha_{2})}{\Gamma(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})}.

Step 1: Short-time asymptotics of
(K_{1}*K_{2})
.

By (
42
), as
u\downarrow 0
,

(43)

(K_{1}*K_{2})(u)\sim\frac{1}{\tilde{\delta}_{1}\tilde{\delta}_{2}\,\Gamma(\alpha_{1})\Gamma(\alpha_{2})}\int_{0}^{u}v^{\alpha_{1}-1}(u-v)^{\alpha_{2}-1}\,dv=\frac{u^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-1}}{\tilde{\delta}_{1}\tilde{\delta}_{2}\,\Gamma(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})},

which matches the Tauberian inversion of

\widehat{K_{1}*K_{2}}(z)=1/[(1+\tilde{\delta}_{1}z^{\alpha_{1}})(1+\tilde{\delta}_{2}z^{\alpha_{2}})]\sim 1/(\tilde{\delta}_{1}\tilde{\delta}_{2}\,z^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}})

as
z\to\infty
.

Step 2: Numerator.

By Proposition
3.5
(iii) with
b_{1}\equiv\bar{V}^{1}
,
and using
K_{1}(u)\sim u^{\alpha_{1}-1}/[\tilde{\delta}_{1}\Gamma(\alpha_{1})]

together with (
43
):

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}(u)(K_{1}*K_{2})(u)\,du

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{\tilde{\delta}_{1}\Gamma(\alpha_{1})}\cdot\frac{1}{\tilde{\delta}_{1}\tilde{\delta}_{2}\,\Gamma(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})}\int_{0}^{t}u^{2\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-2}\,du

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{2\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-1}}{\tilde{\delta}_{1}^{2}\,\tilde{\delta}_{2}\,\Gamma(\alpha_{1})\,\Gamma(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})\,(2\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-1)}.

Hence

\mathrm{Cov}(V_{t}^{1},V_{t}^{2})\sim\frac{b_{\infty}^{12}\|\psi^{12}\|_{1}\,\bar{V}^{1}}{\tilde{\delta}_{1}^{2}\,\tilde{\delta}_{2}\,\Gamma(\alpha_{1})\,\Gamma(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})\,(2\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-1)}\cdot t^{2\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-1}.

Step 3: Denominator.

For component
i
, the dominant short-time contribution to

\mathrm{Var}(V_{t}^{i})
comes from the self-term;
the cross-term contribution in
\mathrm{Var}(V_{t}^{2})
is of order

t^{2(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})-1}=o(t^{2\alpha_{2}-1})
since
\alpha_{1}>0
.
Hence, using
K_{i}(s)^{2}\sim s^{2\alpha_{i}-2}/[\tilde{\delta}_{i}^{2}\,\Gamma(\alpha_{i})^{2}]
:

\mathrm{Var}(V_{t}^{i})\sim\bar{V}^{i}\int_{0}^{t}K_{i}(s)^{2}\,ds\sim\frac{\bar{V}^{i}\,t^{2\alpha_{i}-1}}{\tilde{\delta}_{i}^{2}\,\Gamma(\alpha_{i})^{2}\,(2\alpha_{i}-1)}.

Therefore

\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(V_{t}^{1})\,\mathrm{Var}(V_{t}^{2})}\sim\frac{\sqrt{\bar{V}^{1}\bar{V}^{2}}}{\tilde{\delta}_{1}\,\tilde{\delta}_{2}\,\Gamma(\alpha_{1})\,\Gamma(\alpha_{2})\,\sqrt{(2\alpha_{1}-1)(2\alpha_{2}-1)}}\cdot t^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-1}.

Step 4: Ratio.

The power of
t
gives
(2\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-1)-(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-1)=\alpha_{1}
.
Dividing numerator by denominator and simplifying:

\displaystyle C_{\varrho}

\displaystyle=\frac{b_{\infty}^{12}\|\psi^{12}\|_{1}\,\bar{V}^{1}}{\tilde{\delta}_{1}^{2}\,\tilde{\delta}_{2}\,\Gamma(\alpha_{1})\,\Gamma(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})\,(2\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-1)}\cdot\frac{\tilde{\delta}_{1}\,\tilde{\delta}_{2}\,\Gamma(\alpha_{1})\,\Gamma(\alpha_{2})\,\sqrt{(2\alpha_{1}-1)(2\alpha_{2}-1)}}{\sqrt{\bar{V}^{1}\,\bar{V}^{2}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{b_{\infty}^{12}\|\psi^{12}\|_{1}\,\sqrt{\bar{V}^{1}/\bar{V}^{2}}}{\tilde{\delta}_{1}}\cdot\frac{\Gamma(\alpha_{2})\,\sqrt{(2\alpha_{1}-1)(2\alpha_{2}-1)}}{\Gamma(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})\,(2\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-1)}.

Since
\alpha_{1}>0
, we have
t^{\alpha_{1}}\to 0
as
t\downarrow 0
,
confirming
\varrho(t)\to 0
.
∎

4.
Analytic illustrations

The main results admit direct analytic illustration without simulation.

4.1.
Indicative calibration to equity market parameters

Gatheral, Jaisson, and Rosenbaum
[
8
]
estimate the Hurst
parameter of realized volatility for a broad range of equity indices
and find
H\approx 0.1
(
\alpha\approx 0.6
) robustly across markets.
In a bivariate setting, the two components of our model can represent
the intensities of buyer-initiated and seller-initiated trades on a single
asset, or the order flows of two correlated assets.
Bacry and Muzy
[
5
]
calibrate a bivariate Hawkes model to
high-frequency tick data and find asymmetric cross-excitation coefficients,
consistent with the triangular structure
\psi^{21}=0
of the present paper.

Based on these empirical observations, the parameter regime

\alpha_{1}=0.6
(
H_{1}=0.1
),
\alpha_{2}=0.8
(
H_{2}=0.3
),

\ell_{\infty}\in[0.3,0.6]

is representative of equity microstructure.
In this regime, Corollary
3.8
gives:

•

The functional correlation vanishes as

\varrho(t)\approx C_{\varrho}\,t^{0.6}
for small
t
,
with
C_{\varrho}\in[0.14,0.28]
for
\ell_{\infty}\in[0.3,0.6]

(Table
2
).

•

At a 5-minute horizon (
t\approx 5/390\approx 0.013
of a trading day),

\varrho(0.013)\approx C_{\varrho}\times 0.013^{0.6}\approx 0.05
–
0.10
,
consistent with the Epps effect
[
3
]
.

•

The rougher component (
\alpha_{1}=0.6
) governs the short-time
decorrelation rate, irrespective of
\alpha_{2}
.

A full statistical calibration procedure (MLE or GMM on the functional
correlation) is left for future work; the purpose here is to show that
the parameter regime of the present paper is empirically grounded.

4.2.
Cross-kernel structure and regularity hierarchy

The three kernels
K_{1}
,
K_{2}
,
K_{1}*K_{2}
are distinguished by their
short-time singularity exponents:
\alpha_{1}-1=-0.40
,
\alpha_{2}-1=-0.20
,
and
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-1=+0.40
respectively.
The cross-kernel
K_{1}*K_{2}
has a
positive
exponent, meaning it is
integrable at zero and strictly smoother than either self-kernel.
This is not a numerical observation but an exact consequence of
Proposition
3.5
(iv): the Laplace transform

\widehat{K_{1}*K_{2}}(z)\sim 1/(\tilde{\delta}_{1}\tilde{\delta}_{2}\,z^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}})

as
z\to\infty
, and Karamata’s theorem gives the singularity exponent

\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-1>0
.
This regularity hierarchy is the analytic foundation of the

C
-tightness of the cross-term (Proposition
5.5
).

5.
C
-tightness of the bivariate intensity processes

This section establishes the
C
-tightness of
(V^{T,1},V^{T,2})_{T\geq 1}

in
C([0,1];\mathbb{R}^{2})
.
We first recall the relevant criterion from Horst, Xu, and Zhang
[
15
]
,
then verify its conditions for each component.

5.1.
The tightness criterion

Proposition 5.1

(HXZ tightness criterion)
.

Let
(X^{T})_{T\geq 1}
be a sequence of continuous processes of the form

X_{t}^{T}=\int_{0}^{t}K_{T}(t-s)\,\sigma_{s}^{T}\,dW_{s}^{T},

where
W^{T}
is a continuous local martingale with
d\langle W^{T}\rangle_{t}=dt
,

K_{T}\in L^{2}([0,1])
, and
\sigma^{T}
is progressively measurable.
Suppose:

(H1)

K_{T}\to K
in
L^{2}([0,1])
for some
K\in L^{2}([0,1])

with
K(t)\sim c\,t^{\alpha-1}
as
t\downarrow 0
,
\alpha\in(1/2,1)
,

|K_{T}(t)|\leq Ct^{\alpha-1}
uniformly in
T
for
t\in(0,1]
,
and the uniform
L^{2}
-shift bound

(44)

\sup_{T\geq 1}\int_{0}^{\infty}|K_{T}(u+h)-K_{T}(u)|^{2}\,du\;\leq\;C_{\alpha}\,h^{2\alpha-1},\qquad h\in(0,1];

(H2)

there exists
p>1/(2\alpha-1)
with

\displaystyle\sup_{T\geq 1}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|\sigma_{t}^{T}|^{2p}\right]<\infty
.

Then
(X^{T})_{T\geq 1}
is
C
-tight in
C([0,1])
.

Remark 5.2
.

In our application
\sigma_{t}^{T}=\sqrt{\lambda_{Tt}^{T,i}}
;
the uniform
L^{2p}
bound for arbitrary
p<\infty
follows from the
subcritical Volterra moment estimates of Lemma
5.4
.
Condition (H1) is verified in Lemma
3.1
for the
self-kernels and Lemma
3.2
for the cross-kernel;
the
L^{2}
-shift bound (
44
) is established in
Lemma
5.3
below.

Proof.

We apply Kolmogorov–Chentsov at moment order
2p>2
.

Step 1: increment decomposition.

For
0\leq s\leq t\leq 1
write
X_{t}^{T}-X_{s}^{T}=A_{s,t}^{T}+B_{s,t}^{T}
with

A_{s,t}^{T}:=\int_{s}^{t}K_{T}(t-r)\sigma_{r}^{T}\,dW_{r}^{T},\quad B_{s,t}^{T}:=\int_{0}^{s}(K_{T}(t-r)-K_{T}(s-r))\sigma_{r}^{T}\,dW_{r}^{T}.

Step 2:
L^{2p}
bound on
A_{s,t}^{T}
.

By the BDG inequality and Hölder applied to the measure

K_{T}(t-r)^{2}\,dr
(with exponents
(p,p/(p-1))
):

\mathbb{E}\!\left[|A_{s,t}^{T}|^{2p}\right]\leq C_{p}\left(\int_{0}^{t-s}K_{T}(v)^{2}\,dv\right)^{p-1}\int_{s}^{t}K_{T}(t-r)^{2}\,\mathbb{E}[|\sigma_{r}^{T}|^{2p}]\,dr.

Using
|K_{T}(v)|\leq Cv^{\alpha-1}
and (H2) with constant
M
:

\mathbb{E}\!\left[|A_{s,t}^{T}|^{2p}\right]\leq C_{p}^{\prime}M\,(t-s)^{(2\alpha-1)p}.

Step 3:
L^{2p}
bound on
B_{s,t}^{T}
.

Set
h:=t-s
.
By BDG and the same Hölder argument applied with the
time-dependent integrand
(K_{T}(t-r)-K_{T}(s-r))^{2}
:

\mathbb{E}[|B_{s,t}^{T}|^{2p}]\leq C_{p}\left(\int_{0}^{s}(K_{T}(t-r)-K_{T}(s-r))^{2}\,dr\right)^{p-1}\!\!\cdot\!\int_{0}^{s}(K_{T}(t-r)-K_{T}(s-r))^{2}\mathbb{E}[|\sigma_{r}^{T}|^{2p}]\,dr.

The change of variable
u:=s-r
gives

\int_{0}^{s}(K_{T}(t-r)-K_{T}(s-r))^{2}\,dr=\int_{0}^{s}|K_{T}(u+h)-K_{T}(u)|^{2}\,du\leq C_{\alpha}h^{2\alpha-1}

uniformly in
T
by hypothesis (
44
).
Combined with (H2), this yields

\mathbb{E}[|B_{s,t}^{T}|^{2p}]\leq C_{p}^{\prime\prime}M\,(t-s)^{(2\alpha-1)p}
.

Step 4: Kolmogorov–Chentsov.

Combining Steps 2–3 via
(a+b)^{2p}\leq 2^{2p-1}(a^{2p}+b^{2p})
:

\mathbb{E}\!\left[|X_{t}^{T}-X_{s}^{T}|^{2p}\right]\leq C(t-s)^{(2\alpha-1)p}\quad\text{uniformly in }T.

Since
p>1/(2\alpha-1)
, the exponent
(2\alpha-1)p>1
.
Kolmogorov–Chentsov (Revuz–Yor, Theorem I.2.1)
with
\beta=2p
and
\gamma=(2\alpha-1)p-1>0
gives
C
-tightness.
∎

Lemma 5.3

(Uniform
L^{2}
-shift bound for the renormalized kernels)
.

For each
i\in\{1,2\}
, the family
(g_{T}^{i})_{T\geq 1}
defined
in (
16
) satisfies

\sup_{T\geq 1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigl(g_{T}^{i}(u+h)-g_{T}^{i}(u)\bigr)^{2}\,du\;\leq\;C_{\alpha_{i}}\,h^{2\alpha_{i}-1},\qquad h\in(0,1].

The analogous bound holds for
\tilde{h}_{T}
with exponent

\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}
in place of
\alpha_{i}
.

Proof.

By Plancherel on
\mathbb{R}
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|g_{T}^{i}(u+h)-g_{T}^{i}(u)|^{2}\,du=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|e^{ih\xi}-1|^{2}\,|\widehat{g_{T}^{i}}(i\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi.

By (
22
),
|\widehat{g_{T}^{i}}(i\xi)|\leq F_{i}(\xi):=C_{i}/(1+c_{i}^{\prime}|\xi|^{\alpha_{i}})
uniformly in
T
.
Using
|e^{ih\xi}-1|\leq\min(2,h|\xi|)
, split at
|\xi|=1/h
:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|e^{ih\xi}-1|^{2}F_{i}(\xi)^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|\xi|\leq 1/h}h^{2}\xi^{2}F_{i}(\xi)^{2}\,d\xi+\int_{|\xi|>1/h}4F_{i}(\xi)^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2}\int_{0}^{1/h}\xi^{2-2\alpha_{i}}\,d\xi+C\int_{1/h}^{\infty}\xi^{-2\alpha_{i}}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}\bigl(h^{2}\cdot h^{2\alpha_{i}-3}+h^{2\alpha_{i}-1}\bigr)=C^{\prime\prime}\,h^{2\alpha_{i}-1},

using
\alpha_{i}\in(1/2,1)
so that both integrals converge and give the
same exponent.
The same argument with (
25
) gives the bound for
\tilde{h}_{T}

with exponent
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}
.
∎

5.2.
Uniform supremum bound on the intensities

Lemma 5.4

(Uniform
L^{2}
bound on intensity suprema)
.

Under Assumption
2.1
:

(i)

\displaystyle\sup_{T\geq 1}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\left(V_{t}^{T,1}\right)^{2}\right]<\infty.

(ii)

\displaystyle\sup_{T\geq 1}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\left(V_{t}^{T,2}\right)^{2}\right]<\infty.

Proof.

Both parts follow the same scheme.
The key observation is that
\mathcal{S}^{T,1}
,
\mathcal{S}^{T}
,
\mathcal{C}^{T}

are stochastic convolutions,
not
martingales in
t
, because the kernel
inside the integral depends on the evaluation time
t
.
Doob’s maximal inequality therefore does not apply directly.
We instead bound the sup via a Garsia–Rodemich–Rumsey / stochastic Fubini
argument at moment order
2
.

Generic argument.

Let
U_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}K_{T}(t-s)\sqrt{q_{s}^{T}}\,dW_{s}^{T}
, where
W^{T}
is a continuous
martingale with
d\langle W^{T}\rangle_{s}=ds
and
q^{T}\geq 0
is progressively
measurable.
By Itô’s isometry, for each
t
,

(45)

\mathbb{E}[U_{t}^{2}]=\int_{0}^{t}K_{T}(t-s)^{2}\,\mathbb{E}[q_{s}^{T}]\,ds.

For the uniform sup bound we argue as follows.
Writing
U_{t}-U_{s}=A_{s,t}^{T}+B_{s,t}^{T}
as in
Proposition
5.1
, Step 2 and Step 3 give, at moment order
2p
,

\mathbb{E}[|U_{t}-U_{s}|^{2p}]\leq C(t-s)^{(2\alpha-1)p}

for any
p>1/(2\alpha-1)
, using (H2) for
\sigma_{s}^{T}=\sqrt{q_{s}^{T}}
.
Kolmogorov’s continuity theorem then yields

(46)

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|U_{t}|^{2p}\right]\leq C\cdot\left(1+\sup_{T}\mathbb{E}\!\Bigl[\sup_{s\leq 1}|q_{s}^{T}|^{p}\Bigr]\right),

where
C
depends on
p
,
\alpha
, and the constants in (H1)–(H2),
not on
T
.
(This is the quantitative form of Kolmogorov–Chentsov;
see Revuz–Yor, Theorem I.2.1 and the remark that the Hölder
constant in probability is
L^{2p}
-integrable.)

Part (i).

With
U^{1}_{t}:=\mathcal{S}_{t}^{T,1}=\int_{0}^{t}g_{T}^{1}(t-s)\,d\widetilde{M}_{s}^{T,1}
,
we have
d\langle\widetilde{M}^{T,1}\rangle_{s}=q_{s}^{T}\,ds
with

q_{s}^{T}:=\frac{1-a_{T}^{1}}{m_{1}\,T^{\alpha_{1}-1}}\lambda_{Ts}^{T,1}=V_{s}^{T,1}
.
Since
\mathbb{E}[V_{s}^{T,1}]\leq\bar{V}_{\sup}^{1}<\infty
uniformly
(Lemma
3.4
(i)), one gets

\mathbb{E}[\sup_{s}V_{s}^{T,1}]<\infty
by the supermartingale maximal inequality
applied to the Volterra representation;
iterating this bound to higher orders using the Volterra structure of
the Hawkes process (see, e.g.,
[
15
]
, Lemma 3.1) yields

\sup_{T}\mathbb{E}[\sup_{s}(V_{s}^{T,1})^{p}]<\infty
for every
p<\infty
.
Fix any
p>1/(2\alpha_{1}-1)
and apply (
46
)
with
\alpha=\alpha_{1}
,
K_{T}=g_{T}^{1}
: this gives

\sup_{T}\mathbb{E}[\sup_{t}|\mathcal{S}_{t}^{T,1}|^{2p}]<\infty

and hence (i) since
V^{T,1}=D^{T,1}+\mathcal{S}^{T,1}
with
D^{T,1}

bounded.

Part (ii).

The decomposition
V^{T,2}=D^{T,2}+\mathcal{S}^{T}+\mathcal{C}^{T}
gives

\sup_{t}|V_{t}^{T,2}|^{2}\leq 3(\sup_{t}|D_{t}^{T,2}|^{2}+\sup_{t}|\mathcal{S}_{t}^{T}|^{2}+\sup_{t}|\mathcal{C}_{t}^{T}|^{2})
.
The deterministic term is uniformly bounded by (A2)–(A4).
For
\mathcal{S}^{T}
, apply the generic argument with
\alpha=\alpha_{2}
,

K_{T}=g_{T}^{2}
,
q_{s}^{T}=V_{s}^{T,2}
, using (H1) of
Lemma
3.1
and the
L^{2}
-shift bound of
Lemma
5.3
, plus the
L^{2p}
moment bound on

V^{T,2}
analogous to Part (i) (which is proved by a bootstrap on the
Volterra representation of
\mathcal{S}^{T}
combined with the bound on

\mathcal{C}^{T}
below).

For
\mathcal{C}^{T}
, we note that

\mathcal{C}^{T}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\tilde{h}_{T}(t-s)\,d\widetilde{M}_{s}^{T,1}
,
where
\tilde{h}_{T}
satisfies (H1) with exponent
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}\in(1,2)

(by Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
5.3
).
Since
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}>1
,
\tilde{h}_{T}
is a bounded continuous function
on
(0,1]
, and the generic argument applies with any
p>1/(2\alpha_{1}-1)

to yield
\sup_{T}\mathbb{E}[\sup_{t}|\mathcal{C}_{t}^{T}|^{2p}]<\infty
,
using the same
V^{T,1}
moment bounds as in Part (i).

The claim (ii) then follows from combining the three bounds at moment
2
.
∎

5.3.
The main tightness result

Proposition 5.5

(
C
-tightness of the bivariate renormalized intensity)
.

Under Assumption
2.1
,
the sequence
(V^{T,1},V^{T,2})_{T\geq 1}
is
C
-tight in
C([0,1];\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove
C
-tightness of each component separately,
since joint
C
-tightness of
(X^{T},Y^{T})
in
C([0,1];\mathbb{R}^{2})

follows from
C
-tightness of
X^{T}
and
Y^{T}
individually
(using the product topology on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
).

Component 1.

We apply Proposition
5.1
with
K_{T}=g_{T}^{1}
,
K=K_{1}
,

\sigma_{t}^{T}=\sqrt{\lambda_{Tt}^{T,1}}
renormalized appropriately,
and
W_{t}^{T}=\widetilde{M}_{t}^{T,1}
(the renormalized compensated process).
Condition (H1) holds by Lemma
3.1
.
Condition (H2) holds by Lemma
5.4
(i).
Hence
V^{T,1}
is
C
-tight.

Component 2: self-term
\mathcal{S}^{T}
.

We apply Proposition
5.1
with
K_{T}=g_{T}^{2}
,
K=K_{2}
,

\sigma_{t}^{T}=\sqrt{\lambda_{Tt}^{T,2}}
.
Condition (H1) holds by Lemma
3.1
.
Condition (H2) holds by Lemma
5.4
(ii).
Hence
\mathcal{S}^{T}
is
C
-tight.

Component 2: cross-term
\mathcal{C}^{T}
.

As observed in the proof of Lemma
5.4
(ii), the cross-term
admits the representation

(47)

\mathcal{C}^{T}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\tilde{h}_{T}(t-s)\,d\widetilde{M}_{s}^{T,1},\qquad t\in[0,1],

where
\widetilde{M}^{T,1}
is the renormalized compensated martingale satisfying

d\langle\widetilde{M}^{T,1}\rangle_{s}=V_{s}^{T,1}\,ds
,
and
\tilde{h}_{T}
is the renormalized cross-kernel defined in (
17
).
This follows by expressing
\mathcal{C}^{T}
in terms of

d\widetilde{M}^{T,1}
via the relation

dM_{u}^{T,1}=(m_{1}T^{\alpha_{1}-1}/(1-a_{T}^{1}))\,d\widetilde{M}_{u/T}^{T,1}

and collecting factors using the definition (
17
) of
\tilde{h}_{T}
.

By the Itô isometry applied to (
47
),
for
0\leq s\leq t\leq 1
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|\mathcal{C}_{t}^{T}-\mathcal{C}_{s}^{T}|^{2}]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{s}\bigl[\tilde{h}_{T}(t-r)-\tilde{h}_{T}(s-r)\bigr]^{2}\mathbb{E}[V_{r}^{T,1}]\,dr+\int_{s}^{t}\tilde{h}_{T}(t-r)^{2}\,\mathbb{E}[V_{r}^{T,1}]\,dr

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{r\in[0,1]}\mathbb{E}[V_{r}^{T,1}]\cdot\Bigl(\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigl[\tilde{h}_{T}(r+h)-\tilde{h}_{T}(r)\bigr]^{2}\,dr+\int_{0}^{h}\tilde{h}_{T}(r)^{2}\,dr\Bigr),

where
h:=t-s
.
By Lemma
3.4
(i),
\sup_{T,r}\mathbb{E}[V_{r}^{T,1}]<\infty
.
The
L^{2}
-shift bound of Lemma
5.3
applied to

\tilde{h}_{T}
with effective exponent
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}
gives

\int_{0}^{\infty}|\tilde{h}_{T}(r+h)-\tilde{h}_{T}(r)|^{2}\,dr\leq C_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}\,h^{2(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})-1}.

For the second term, since
L_{12}(r)\sim C_{12}\,r^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-1}
as

\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}>1
, Lemma
3.2
gives

\sup_{T\geq 1}\int_{0}^{h}\tilde{h}_{T}(r)^{2}\,dr\leq C\,h^{2(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})-1}
.
Hence

(48)

\mathbb{E}[|\mathcal{C}_{t}^{T}-\mathcal{C}_{s}^{T}|^{2}]\leq C\,(t-s)^{2(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})-1},\qquad\text{uniformly in }T.

Since
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}>1
, the exponent
2(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})-1>1
;
Kolmogorov–Chentsov at moment
2
applied to the continuous process

\mathcal{C}^{T}
gives
C
-tightness of
(\mathcal{C}^{T})_{T\geq 1}
in
C([0,1])
.
The cross-term has strictly better Hölder regularity than the self-term:

2(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})-1>2\alpha_{2}-1
.

Conclusion.

D^{T}
is
C
-tight (it converges uniformly).

\mathcal{S}^{T}
is
C
-tight by Proposition
5.1

(verifying (H1) through Lemma
3.1
and
Lemma
5.3
, and (H2) through Lemma
5.4
(ii)).

\mathcal{C}^{T}
is
C
-tight by Kolmogorov–Chentsov via (
48
).
Sum-preservation of tightness (the map
(X,Y,Z)\mapsto X+Y+Z
is continuous
on
C([0,1])^{3}\to C([0,1])
) yields
C
-tightness of
V^{T,2}
.
The joint
C
-tightness of
(V^{T,1},V^{T,2})
in

follows from marginal

-tightness on each factor, since on a product of
Polish spaces, tightness of the marginals is equivalent to joint tightness.
∎

6.
Identification of limit points as solutions of the Volterra system

Having established
C
-tightness in Proposition
5.5
,
every subsequence of
(V^{T,1},V^{T,2})_{T\geq 1}
admits a further
subsequence converging in law in
C([0,1];\mathbb{R}^{2})
.
In this section we identify every such limit point as the unique
(in law) weak solution of the coupled stochastic Volterra system.

6.1.
Martingale limits via Rebolledo’s theorem

Lemma 6.1

(Convergence of the renormalized martingales)
.

Let
(V^{1},V^{2})
be any limit point of
(V^{T,1},V^{T,2})
in
C([0,1];\mathbb{R}^{2})
.
On a suitable extension of the underlying probability space, there exist
continuous
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
-martingales
W^{1}
,
W^{2}
and standard independent
Brownian motions
\beta^{1}
,
\beta^{2}
such that:

(i)

\langle W^{i}\rangle_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}V_{s}^{i}\,ds
for
i=1,2
;

(ii)

\langle W^{1},W^{2}\rangle_{t}=0
for all
t\in[0,1]
;

(iii)

W^{i}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\sqrt{V_{s}^{i}}\,d\beta_{s}^{i}
.

Proof.

Let
(V^{T_{n},1},V^{T_{n},2})\Rightarrow(V^{1},V^{2})
along a subsequence.

Step 1: Tightness of
\widetilde{M}^{T,i}
and convergence of
\langle\widetilde{M}^{T,i}\rangle
.

The compensated martingale
\widetilde{M}^{T,i}
is a continuous square-integrable
martingale with

\langle\widetilde{M}^{T,i}\rangle_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}V_{s}^{T,i}\,ds
.
By Lemma
5.4
,
\sup_{T}\mathbb{E}[\sup_{t}(V_{t}^{T,i})^{2}]<\infty
, so

\sup_{T}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\langle\widetilde{M}^{T,i}\rangle_{1}\right]<\infty
and also

\sup_{T}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sup_{t}(\widetilde{M}_{t}^{T,i})^{2}\right]<\infty
by
the ordinary Doob inequality on the martingale
\widetilde{M}^{T,i}
itself
(here Doob applies, as
\widetilde{M}^{T,i}

is
a martingale,
not a stochastic convolution).
By a direct Aldous tightness argument based on this
L^{2}
bound on the
quadratic variation (
[
12
]
, Theorem VI.4.13), the sequence

(\widetilde{M}^{T,i})_{T}
is
C
-tight in
C([0,1])
.

Extracting a subsequence so that
\widetilde{M}^{T,i}\Rightarrow W^{i}

and using the Skorokhod representation, we may work on a common probability
space where
V^{T,i}\to V^{i}
and
\widetilde{M}^{T,i}\to W^{i}
uniformly a.s.

Since
\sup_{T}\mathbb{E}[\sup_{s}(V_{s}^{T,i})^{2}]<\infty
, the family

\{\int_{0}^{t}V_{s}^{T,i}\,ds:T\geq 1\}
is uniformly integrable (Vitali),
and the a.s. limit
\int_{0}^{t}V_{s}^{T,i}\,ds\to\int_{0}^{t}V_{s}^{i}\,ds

(by continuity of
s\mapsto V_{s}^{T,i}
and uniform convergence) extends
to
L^{1}
convergence. In particular,

\langle\widetilde{M}^{T,i}\rangle_{t}\to\int_{0}^{t}V_{s}^{i}\,ds
in probability.

Step 2: Rebolledo’s theorem.

We have established the two hypotheses of Rebolledo’s martingale CLT
(
[
12
]
, Theorem VIII.3.11):
(\widetilde{M}^{T,i})_{T}

is
C
-tight, and
\langle\widetilde{M}^{T,i}\rangle_{t}\to\int_{0}^{t}V_{s}^{i}\,ds

in probability.
Hence
W^{i}
is a continuous local martingale with

\langle W^{i}\rangle_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}V_{s}^{i}\,ds
; it is a true
L^{2}
-martingale
since
\mathbb{E}[\langle W^{i}\rangle_{1}]<\infty
.

Step 3: Cross-covariation.

The point processes
N^{T,1}
,
N^{T,2}
are constructed by Poisson thinning
(Ogata
[
1
]
) from disjoint auxiliary Poisson random measures,
so they almost surely do not jump simultaneously.
Hence
[N^{T,1},N^{T,2}]_{t}=\sum_{s\leq t}\Delta N_{s}^{T,1}\Delta N_{s}^{T,2}=0

and
[\widetilde{M}^{T,1},\widetilde{M}^{T,2}]_{t}=0
for all
T
.

The map
(X,Y)\mapsto[X,Y]
is
continuous
from
D([0,1])^{2}
to
D([0,1])

at pairs whose limits are continuous (
[
12
]
,
Corollary VI.6.30 and surrounding discussion).
Since
(W^{1},W^{2})
has continuous paths, weak convergence of

(\widetilde{M}^{T,1},\widetilde{M}^{T,2})
to
(W^{1},W^{2})
gives

[W^{1},W^{2}]_{t}=0
a.s. for all
t\in[0,1]
.
For continuous local martingales,
[W^{i},W^{j}]=\langle W^{i},W^{j}\rangle
, so

\langle W^{1},W^{2}\rangle_{t}=0
.

Step 4: Construction of
\beta^{i}
via enlargement.

Enlarge the filtration by appending two independent standard Brownian
motions
B^{1},B^{2}
independent of
(V^{1},V^{2},W^{1},W^{2})
.
Define, for
i=1,2
,

\beta^{i}_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}(V_{s}^{i})^{-1/2}\mathbf{1}_{V_{s}^{i}>0}\,dW^{i}_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{1}_{V_{s}^{i}=0}\,dB^{i}_{s}.

The first integrand has
L^{2}
-integral
\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{1}_{V_{s}^{i}>0}\,ds\leq t

(after multiplication by the quadratic variation
V_{s}^{i}\,ds
, the

1/V^{i}
singularity cancels), and the two components are orthogonal by construction.
A direct computation yields
\langle\beta^{i}\rangle_{t}=t
, so
\beta^{i}
is
a standard Brownian motion by Lévy’s characterisation.
Similarly,
\langle\beta^{1},\beta^{2}\rangle_{t}=0
(by Step 3 for the first pieces
and independence of
B^{1},B^{2}
for the second pieces), so
\beta^{1}\perp\beta^{2}
.
Finally,

W_{t}^{i}=\int_{0}^{t}\sqrt{V_{s}^{i}}\,d\beta^{i}_{s}

because the Itô integral of
\sqrt{V^{i}}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{V^{i}=0}\cdot dB^{i}

vanishes (its integrand is zero) and the remaining term reconstructs
W^{i}
.
∎

6.2.
Passage to the limit in the Volterra representations

Lemma 6.2

(Convergence of stochastic convolutions)
.

Let
\alpha\in(1/2,\infty)\setminus\{1\}
and let
(K_{T})_{T\geq 1}
,

K\in L^{2}([0,1])
satisfy hypothesis
(H1)
of
Proposition
5.1
with this
\alpha

(when
\alpha>1
we only require
|K_{T}(t)|\leq C
bounded uniformly on
[0,1]

and the
L^{2}
-shift bound (
44
) remains valid with exponent
\alpha
).
Let
(M^{T})_{T}
be a sequence of continuous square-integrable martingales
with
M_{0}^{T}=0
, converging jointly in
C([0,1])
with some continuous martingale

M
in the sense that the pair
(M^{T},\langle M^{T}\rangle)\Rightarrow(M,\langle M\rangle)
,
and suppose
d\langle M^{T}\rangle_{t}=q_{t}^{T}\,dt
with

\sup_{T}\mathbb{E}[\sup_{t}|q_{t}^{T}|^{p}]<\infty
for some
p>\max(1,\,1/(2\alpha-1))
.
Then

\mathcal{I}_{\cdot}^{T}:=\int_{0}^{\cdot}K_{T}(\cdot-s)\,dM_{s}^{T}\longrightarrow\mathcal{I}_{\cdot}:=\int_{0}^{\cdot}K(\cdot-s)\,dM_{s}\quad\text{in law on }C([0,1]).

Proof.

By Skorokhod representation, work on a common probability space

(\hat{\Omega},\hat{\mathbb{P}})
on which
M^{T}\to M

\hat{\mathbb{P}}
-a.s. uniformly on
[0,1]
and

\langle M^{T}\rangle\to\langle M\rangle
uniformly a.s.
Decompose
\mathcal{I}_{t}^{T}-\mathcal{I}_{t}=R_{t}^{T}+S_{t}^{T}
with

R_{t}^{T}:=\int_{0}^{t}(K_{T}(t-s)-K(t-s))\,dM_{s}^{T},\qquad S_{t}^{T}:=\int_{0}^{t}K(t-s)\,d(M_{s}^{T}-M_{s}).

Step 1:
\sup_{t}|R_{t}^{T}|\to 0
in probability.

R^{T}
is a stochastic convolution with integrand

(K_{T}-K)(t-s)\sqrt{q_{s}^{T}}
against a martingale.
Proposition
5.1
Step 2 applied to the difference kernel

K_{T}-K
yields, for
0\leq s\leq t\leq 1
,

\mathbb{E}[|R_{t}^{T}-R_{s}^{T}|^{2p}]\leq C\|K_{T}-K\|_{L^{2}}^{2(p-1)}(t-s)^{(2\alpha-1)p}

by using (H2). Taking
s=0
also gives a pointwise bound at each
t
:

\mathbb{E}[|R_{t}^{T}|^{2}]\leq\|K_{T}-K\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\cdot\sup_{r}\mathbb{E}[q_{r}^{T}]\to 0
.
Hence
R_{t}^{T}\to 0
in
L^{2}
at each
t
, and the uniform Kolmogorov-Chentsov
tightness (giving a modulus of continuity bound with constant proportional to

\|K_{T}-K\|_{L^{2}}^{(p-1)/p}\to 0
) combines with pointwise convergence to yield

\sup_{t}|R_{t}^{T}|\to 0
in probability; see, e.g.,

[
12
]
, Theorem VI.3.21.

Step 2:
\sup_{t}|S_{t}^{T}|\to 0
in probability.

Define the truncated kernel
K^{\delta}(u):=K(u)\mathbf{1}_{u\geq\delta}

for
\delta\in(0,1)
, and write
S^{T}=S^{T,\delta}+E^{T,\delta}
with

S_{t}^{T,\delta}:=\int_{0}^{t}K^{\delta}(t-s)\,d(M_{s}^{T}-M_{s})
,

E_{t}^{T,\delta}:=\int_{0}^{t}(K-K^{\delta})(t-s)\,d(M_{s}^{T}-M_{s})
.

Control of
E^{T,\delta}
.

E_{t}^{T,\delta}
is a stochastic convolution with integrand

(K-K^{\delta})(t-s)=K(t-s)\mathbf{1}_{t-s<\delta}
.
By the same argument as Step 1, using
\|K-K^{\delta}\|_{L^{2}([0,1])}^{2}\leq C\delta^{2\alpha-1}/(2\alpha-1)
when
\alpha<1

(and
\leq C\delta
when
\alpha\geq 1
, since
K
is bounded there),
we get

\sup_{T}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sup_{t}|E_{t}^{T,\delta}|^{2}\right]\leq C\,\delta^{\min(2\alpha-1,\,1)}.

Control of
S^{T,\delta}
for fixed
\delta>0
.

Since
K^{\delta}\equiv 0
on
[0,\delta)
and

K^{\delta}|_{[\delta,1]}\in L^{\infty}\cap BV([\delta,1])
with total variation

\|K^{\delta}\|_{TV}<\infty
(for Mittag-Leffler-type kernels with power-law
short-time behavior), stochastic integration by parts in
s
for fixed
t
gives

S_{t}^{T,\delta}=K^{\delta}(\delta)\,(M_{t-\delta}^{T}-M_{t-\delta})+\int_{0}^{t-\delta}(M_{s}^{T}-M_{s})\,K^{\prime}_{\delta}(t-s)\,ds,

where
K^{\prime}_{\delta}
is the distributional derivative of
u\mapsto K^{\delta}(u)

on
(\delta,\infty)
, integrable against the
BV
measure.
(Here the right-hand side is a pathwise integral of the a.s. continuous
process
s\mapsto M_{s}^{T}-M_{s}
.)
Hence

\sup_{t}|S_{t}^{T,\delta}|\leq(\|K^{\delta}\|_{\infty}+\|K^{\delta}\|_{TV})\sup_{s}|M_{s}^{T}-M_{s}|\to 0

\hat{\mathbb{P}}
-a.s.

Closure.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. Choose
\delta>0
so that
C\delta^{\min(2\alpha-1,1)}\leq\varepsilon^{3}/8
;
then Markov gives
\hat{\mathbb{P}}(\sup_{t}|E_{t}^{T,\delta}|>\varepsilon/2)\leq\varepsilon/2

uniformly in
T
.
With
\delta
fixed,
\hat{\mathbb{P}}(\sup_{t}|S_{t}^{T,\delta}|>\varepsilon/2)\to 0

by a.s. convergence.
Hence
\sup_{t}|S_{t}^{T}|\to 0
in probability.

Step 3: Conclusion.

\sup_{t}|\mathcal{I}_{t}^{T}-\mathcal{I}_{t}|\leq\sup_{t}|R_{t}^{T}|+\sup_{t}|S_{t}^{T}|\to 0
in probability,
so
\mathcal{I}^{T}\to\mathcal{I}
in probability on
C([0,1])
, hence in law.
∎

6.3.
Main identification theorem

Theorem 6.3

(Identification of limit points)
.

Under Assumption
2.1
,
every limit point
(V^{1},V^{2})
of the sequence

(V^{T,1},V^{T,2})_{T\geq 1}
in
C([0,1];\mathbb{R}^{2})

satisfies the coupled stochastic Volterra system:

(49)

\displaystyle V_{t}^{1}

\displaystyle=b_{1}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}(t-s)\sqrt{V_{s}^{1}}\,d\beta_{s}^{1},

(50)

\displaystyle V_{t}^{2}

\displaystyle=b_{2}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}K_{2}(t-s)\sqrt{V_{s}^{2}}\,d\beta_{s}^{2}+b_{\infty}^{12}\|\psi^{12}\|_{1}\int_{0}^{t}(K_{1}*K_{2})(t-s)\sqrt{V_{s}^{1}}\,d\beta_{s}^{1},

where
\beta^{1}
,
\beta^{2}
are independent standard Brownian motions,

K_{i}
are the Mittag–Leffler kernels of Proposition
3.5
,

b_{1}
is defined by (
11
), and
b_{2}
is defined by (
12
).

Proof.

Let
(V^{1},V^{2})
be any limit point, with associated martingales

W^{1}
,
W^{2}
,
\beta^{1}
,
\beta^{2}
from Lemma

.
We pass to the limit in the Volterra representations of
V^{T,1}

and
V^{T,2}
separately.

Equation for
V^{1}
.

The Volterra representation of
V^{T,1}
reads:

V_{t}^{T,1}=D_{t}^{T,1}+\int_{0}^{t}g_{T}^{1}(t-s)\,d\widetilde{M}_{s}^{T,1}.

Taking the limit:

•

D_{t}^{T,1}\to b_{1}(t)
uniformly in
t
,
by Assumption (K2) and the explicit formula for the deterministic part.

•

\int_{0}^{t}g_{T}^{1}(t-s)\,d\widetilde{M}_{s}^{T,1}\to\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}(t-s)\,dW_{s}^{1}

in law in
C([0,1])
, by Lemma
6.2

with
K_{T}=g_{T}^{1}\to K_{1}
(Lemma
3.1
)
and
M^{T}=\widetilde{M}^{T,1}\Rightarrow W^{1}
(Lemma
6.1
).

Since

(Lemma

(iii)),
we obtain (
49
).

Equation for

: deterministic term.

The deterministic component of

has a self part and a triangular

4
), both are of
finite order after the normalization (

). More precisely,

Therefore

uniformly on compact time intervals, with

defined in (

Equation for
V^{2}
: self-term

By Lemma

applied with
K_{T}=g_{T}^{2}\to K_{2}

and

\mathcal{S}_{t}^{T}\longrightarrow\int_{0}^{t}K_{2}(t-s)\,dW_{s}^{2}=\int_{0}^{t}K_{2}(t-s)\sqrt{V_{s}^{2}}\,d\beta_{s}^{2}

in law in

.

Equation for
V^{2}
: cross-term
\mathcal{C}^{T}
.

This is the core new argument.
Recall from Proposition
3.5
(i) and Lemma

that

Combining the four terms:

.

∎

Remark 6.4
.

The system (
49
)–(
50
) is
triangular
:
equation (
49
) for
V^{1}
is autonomous
(it does not depend on
V^{2}
),
while equation (

) for

depends on

through the cross-kernel term

This triangular structure reflects the asymmetry of the microscopic model

The correlation between

Remark 6.5
.

The deterministic drift satisfies

b_{i}(t)=m_{i}t+m_{i}\int_{0}^{t}K_{i}(t-s)s\,ds
,
which is non-decreasing and grows like
b_{i}(t)\sim m_{i}t^{\alpha_{i}}/\Gamma(\alpha_{i}+1)

as

.
For

(vanishing baseline intensity in the limit),

and the system reduces to pure stochastic Volterra equations.

7.
Uniqueness for the limiting Volterra system

We now prove that the coupled system (

)–(
50
)
admits at most one solution in law among continuous
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
-valued processes.
Combined with Theorem

, this gives convergence
of the full sequence
(V^{T,1},V^{T,2})
(not merely of subsequences).

7.1.
The scalar uniqueness theorem of Abi Jaber, Larsson, and Pulido

We first recall the relevant result from
[

.

Theorem 7.1

(ALP 2019, Theorem 4.3)
.

Let

Then:

Equation (

.

.

Remark 7.2

We briefly record why.

Let
