3. Project Summary:
The American-Made Solar Forecasting Prize (FY22), Net Load Forecasting Prize (FY23), and
Data-Driven Distribution (3D) Solar Visibility Prize (FY24), funded by the DOE’s SETO and
administered by the American-Made Challenges team at the National Laboratory of the
Rockies (NLR), were part of a multimillion-dollar prize competition designed to energize U.S.
solar innovation through a series of contests that accelerate the entrepreneurial process from
years to months. Each of these prizes focused on modeling tools to manage the variability of
solar generation and support grid services and real-time control responses that are essential
for safe and reliable grid operations. The activities incentivized by these three prizes supported
the government-wide effort to increase American energy dominance by promoting innovation
and early deployment of energy technologies, resulting in wider adoption, which is critical for
secure, affordable, and reliable solar energy.
4. Background:
As of October 2024, solar accounted for about 6.9% of all U.S. electricity produced on an
annual basis. Integrating large amounts of variable energy resources, such as solar, is a
challenge for a power grid designed and built around central-generation thermal plants and
unidirectional power flows. SETO’s Systems Integration subprogram supports targeted
technology research, development, and demonstration that addresses the technical goals
identified in the Grid Modernization Multi-Year Program Plan. SETO’s previous efforts focused
on distribution power network analysis, targeting several technical areas, such as distribution
state estimation and optimal power flow, solar forecasting, increased cyber-physical resilience
of electric power, and interoperability of grid-forming inverters.

5. Project Objectives and Outcomes:
Year 1: Solar Forecasting Prize – FY22
The first year of project funding
aimed to increase the use of the
Solar Forecast Arbiter, an open
platform developed by the
University of Arizona and funded
by SETO, to allow for the
transparent, rigorous, and
consistent analysis and
evaluation of solar forecasts.
The prize was designed to
incentivize innovators to develop
probabilistic tools that predict
how much energy solar power
plants will generate days in
advance of the forecast, while
rewarding bold Figure 1 Solar Forecast Arbiter graphic showing what the platform does
commercialization plans and innovative
ideas to accelerate the adoption of probabilistic forecasts. For 28 consecutive days,
competitors submitted solar forecasts for 10 locations across the United States. This prize
offered a total of up to $375,000 in cash prizes, with five anticipated winners and five
anticipated runners-up. The prize had three goals:
 a. Increase stakeholder awareness of the state of the art in solar forecasting.
 b. Accelerate the commercialization and adoption of probabilistic forecast models.
 c. Promote the use of a common platform, such as the Solar Forecast Arbiter, to
 support transparent metrics and specifications of probabilistic solar forecasts.
Year 2: Net Load Forecasting Prize – FY23
The second year of project funding focused on incentivizing innovators to develop probabilistic
models that predict amounts of net load one day in advance while the actual utility power
measurement data is posted each day. The forecasts are then compared against actual net
load data (provided by four partner utilities) using the open-source Solar Forecast Arbiter
platform. Every day for four weeks (28 consecutive days), competitors submitted forecast
model results for four locations in the United States to the Solar Forecast Arbiter platform. This
prize offered a total of up to $600,000 in cash prizes, with three anticipated winners and three
anticipated runners-up. Through the Net Load Forecasting Prize, SETO, in partnership with the
Electric Power Research Institute (EPRI), aimed to:
 a. Increase stakeholder awareness of the state of the art in net load forecasting.
 b. Demonstrate the feasibility of fair and high-quality evaluations of probabilistic net
 load forecasts using a publicly available, open-source platform.
 c. Promote the use of probabilistic forecast models and an industry-common evaluation
 platform with transparent metrics and specifications for probabilistic net load
 forecasts.

 3

As cosponsor of the prize, EPRI provided historical net load data from regional utilities
(Georgia, Hawaii, Oregon, and Texas) for competitors to train their forecasting models. EPRI
also provided operational support for the forecast evaluation platform, Solar Forecast Arbiter,
used during the evaluation phase of the prize.
Year 3: Data-Driven Distribution (3D) Solar Visibility Prize – FY24
The third year of project funding created the 3D Solar Visibility Prize, where the focus shifted to
distribution system estimation tools. The prize called for applicants to develop models and
algorithms that could provide accurate and real-time information about distributed solar
generation in electric power distribution networks. This prize offered a total of up to $250,000
in cash prizes, with three anticipated winners and four anticipated runners-up. The goal for the
prize was to promote awareness and adoption of distribution system state estimation (DSSE)
algorithms and software tools to improve the visibility of distribution systems and their
operating conditions. Competitors in the 3D Solar Visibility Prize developed a DSSE tool with
measurements to test it, and then submitted their DSSE tool for two distribution system
networks through the Open Energy Data Initiative for solar systems (OEDI-SI) platform for 14
consecutive days. The results were compared against a set of benchmark metrics to evaluate
the tools and winners.

Solar Forecasting Prize Winners
• Leaptran - San Antonio, Texas
 This team used crowd-sourced weather data and algorithms, leveraging site-specific
 data fusion, to achieve intraday and days-ahead solar forecasting. This model offered a
 >50% improvement in days-ahead, intra-hour, and intraday solar forecasting accuracy
 by integrating asset-level data.
• Nimbus AI - Honolulu, Hawaii
 This team developed a fast and inexpensive system for geographically flexible,
 hyperlocal, day-ahead probabilistic solar forecasting. The team combined historical
 ground- and satellite-based instrument data with physics-based numerical weather
 prediction techniques to produce probabilistic forecasts.
• Northview Weather - Danville, Vermont
 This team developed a solar forecasting method that uses a mesoscale weather
 forecast model to produce a dynamic spread of probabilistic solar power forecast
 information. Probabilistic information is also tuned by blending statistical information and
 machine learning of historic sky cover observations.
• UM (University of Michigan) CLaSP - Ann Arbor, Michigan
 This team developed a hybrid solar forecasting method using ground horizontal
 irradiance based on a recursive neural network, trained with past observations and a
 weather-regime-dependent empirical bias correction scheme. The team based this
 scheme on the recursive neural network output and the multiday weather forecast made
 from numerical weather prediction.
• WenYuan Tang - Apex, North Carolina
 This team developed a simple (low-data-cost and low-computational-cost) yet effective
 solar forecasting method that includes learning from many base models, as well as
 physical and statistical models, to provide a comprehensive tool for both forecasting and
 grid optimization. The team provided a model that is easy to interpret, train, validate,
 and deploy, thus overcoming the barriers of experimentation and adoption by utilities
 and other end users.
Solar Forecasting Prize Runners-Up
• Matt Motoki - Aiea, Hawaii
 This team developed an advanced machine learning technique for probabilistic
 forecasting that uses custom deep neural networks to directly minimize the continuous
 ranked probability score loss with no post-processing calibration needed. This method
 allows for greater accuracy, and its lightweight architecture allows it to run faster than
 numerical weather prediction ensembles and other machine learning approaches.
• Syracuse University Team - Syracuse, New York

 This team developed a weather-adaptive probabilistic day-ahead solar forecast
 methodology that leverages innovations in machine learning and statistics. This model
 can adapt to different weather patterns for an accurate forecast under varying weather

Net Load Forecasting
The Net Load Forecasting Prize launched at the 2023 DISTRIBUTECH conference, an annual
transmission and distribution event that addresses technologies used to move electricity from
the power plant, through the transmission and distribution systems, to the meter, and finally
inside the home. The launch at DISTRIBUTECH marked a key milestone for the prize. The
NLR prize administration team, together with EPRI, successfully promoted the prize at the
conference through communication materials, outreach, and recruitment of potential
competitors.
As a direct result of the high-visibility launch and subsequent outreach during the recruitment
period, the prize received 94 submissions, 73 of which were deemed eligible for the prize, and
72 of those eligible submissions were active competitors during the four-week competition
period. This number of submissions and active competitors was deemed successful for the
prize administration team, as one of the project’s milestones was to have at least 75
competitors sign up for the competition.
To continue with the momentum of outreach, the prize administration leveraged a Power
Connector, an organization subcontracted with NLR who are a part of the American-Made
Network, to assist with outreach and recruitment efforts. The Power Connector, ADL Ventures,
supported the prize by conducting a strategic outreach campaign, where they identified
potential competitors through various channels as well as supporting competitors who had
questions about the prize or their submission materials.
Teams were able to participate in an optional test week prior to the four-week competition
where they could test their forecast submission process with the open-source Solar Forecast
Arbiter platform. During the four-week competition period, teams submitted their day-ahead
forecasts every day for locations in Hawaii, Oregon, Texas, and Georgia, with each location
representing one of the four utility companies and its data, which was also posted daily. Teams
were able to check their scores and rankings through a leaderboard, updated weekly on
HeroX, that showed the rankings for each of the four locations, with each team listed as an
anonymous gemstone pseudonym. DOE awarded six teams, three winners (first - $200,000;
second - $150,000; and third - $100,000), and three runners-up (each runner-up received
$50,000) based on the competitor’s performance during the four-week competition period.
Winners were announced at the RE+ conference approximately one month after the
competition period. The announcement at RE+ was another major milestone that allowed the
prize to be amplified to a large audience and highlighted its successes.
While the prize was ultimately successful, and met all of its milestones, there was an
unforeseen 2-month delay in the competition while attaining proper non-disclosure agreements
(NDAs) between the four utility companies and each of the competing teams. Despite the long
turnaround, all teams were able to access the utility's data for the competition. In future similar
situations, the prize administration team suggests providing an approved NDA to competitors

during the recruitment phase so there can be ample time for competitors to review prior to
signing.

3D Solar Visibility
The 3D Solar Visibility Prize received 37 submissions, 33 of which were deemed eligible for
the prize and moved forward to the competition period. There were 16 active competitors
participating in the two-week competition period. Although the number of submissions did not
meet the milestone of having at least 75 competitors sign up for the competition, some of this
can be attributed to not being able to launch at DISTRIBUTECH in February 2024 as intended.
At the time of the conference, DOE was not ready to publish and launch the prize. Additionally,
only nine weeks were available for recruitment of potential competitors, and in the future,
allowing for at least 12 weeks of promotion would provide ample time for outreach and
promotion.
Two Power Connectors, ADL Ventures and Entrepreneur Futures Network, were
subcontracted to help with outreach and recruitment efforts. Both Power Connectors supported
the prize by sending targeted outreach emails to large networks and identifying contacts that
were best suited for the prize. They each also amplified the prize outreach efforts, such as the
informational webinar, and hosted their own webinars where they connected with potential
competitors to talk through the technical aspects of the prize and answer their questions.
Leading up to the two-week competition period, competitors were provided historical data
through the OEDI-SI platform. For seven weeks, competitors could use the historical data and
pseudo-measurement analysis on the platform to begin their model training. On the last week
of historical data access, competitors began the optional distribution state estimation practice
week, in which competitors could test their submission processes through the OEDI-SI
platform before the competition took place. After the practice week, competitors began the
distribution state estimation competition period, where they submitted their DSSE tools. The
submitted tools were tested to see which one provided the most accurate real-time estimation
of the state of an electric power system. During the two-week competition period, competitors
submitted their DSSE solutions every day for two distribution system networks through the
OEDI-SI platform. Teams were able to check their scores and rankings through a leaderboard,
updated daily on HeroX, that showed the rankings for each competitor, with each team listed
as an anonymous gemstone pseudonym.
When the prize was launched, the intent was to award $175,000 to up to five competitors (two
winners, three runners-up); however, based on competitors’ high performance during the

 7

competition period, DOE modified the prize to award $250,000 to up to seven competitors
(three winners, four runners-up). The three winning teams each received a $50,000 cash prize,
and the four runners-up each received a $25,000 cash prize.
Throughout the historical data period, the prize administration team received many inquiries
regarding the OEDI-SI platform and the format for submitting data. In response, the prize
administration team offered four office hour webinars where competitors could ask questions of
the technical team at NLR. To provide ample time for competitors to practice their model
training and address questions about the platform and data, the prize administration team
extended the original time frame from four weeks to seven weeks. This pushed the practice
week and competition period by three weeks.
Although there were 16 active competitors throughout the competition period, a few had
difficulty formatting their data submissions, which resulted in negative scores. The formatting
difficulty was due to several competitors having existing data-input methods that differed from
what the NLR technical team supplied in the prize rules. The formatting was addressed
thoroughly in the technical webinar and subsequent office hours, as well as being written out in
the rules.
Some competitors wanted more specific information on the data to use real-world weather
patterns at work. Other competitors wanted to use existing simulation tools, which required
more information (in the form of time series) than what the NLR technical team provided. Much
of the data provided was encoded as JSON using Pydantic schemas, which some competitors
did not have experience in parsing reliably. Although less visible, the Docker, a platform for
building and running applications, setup and requirements slowed competitors down.
Simplifying the data could draw more talent to create better algorithms compared to using a
more comprehensive format.

7. Significant Accomplishments and Conclusions
Solar Forecasting Prize
The American-Made Solar Forecasting Prize advanced innovation in probabilistic solar
forecasting and increased awareness of its importance for grid operations. Over 28 days,
competitors submitted forecasts through the Solar Forecast Arbiter platform and presented
commercialization plans to expand adoption. These solutions not only outperformed baseline
models, but also highlighted practical, low-cost, and scalable methods that can be integrated
by utilities, system operators, and other end users. By incentivizing participation, raising

 8

awareness of state-of-the-art techniques, and promoting standardized evaluation through the
Solar Forecast Arbiter, the prize advanced the deployment of cutting-edge forecasting tools to
support greater integration of solar energy. Additionally, EPRI is still using the Solar Forecast
Arbiter platform and campaigns similar to the prize as a service to its members.
Net Load Forecasting Prize
The Net Load Forecasting Prize accelerated improvements in solar forecasting modeling,
supporting the secure and reliable integration of large amounts of variable amounts of energy
resources into the grid. Being able to accurately predict the net load of an electric grid allows
operators to better plan for optimized procurement of reserves and utilization of other
dispatchable energy sources. The prize challenged more than 70 teams to develop solar
forecasting models to create net load predictions for four locations across the United States.
The Net Load Forecasting Prize was one of the largest and most realistic forecast competitions
as of July 2023. Since the ending of the competition, EPRI continues to operate the Solar
Forecast Arbiter to support the way solar and net-load forecasts are evaluated. Users of the
platform are able to assess multiple vendor forecasts, make comparisons among their own
forecasts, and track changes in accuracy. For forecast providers, this standardization allows
the use of a single framework for all trial forecasts, removing a need to rebuild the system
based on the requests of each potential customer. The platform is an open-source tool that
enables evaluations of solar irradiance, solar power, and net load forecasts that are impartial,
repeatable, and auditable. The Solar Forecast Arbiter continues to provide clear, transparent
communication to the skill of a precisely defined forecast.
3D Solar Visibility Prize
As more renewable energy is brought into the power grid, the unidirectional power flow is
morphing into a two-way grid. However, utilities have limited visibility into what power is
created and when. In the 3D Solar Visibility Prize, competitors created DSSE tools to improve
the visibility of distribution systems and their operating conditions. This will equip utilities and
grid operators with a powerful tool for accurate planning and real-time dispatch of resources,
enabling them to make better operational decisions and optimize the use of distributed solar
energy, which in turn will help address SETO’s goal of accelerating the deployment of solar
energy technologies. The prize also raised the awareness of the OEDI-SI platform, a platform
that allows users to benchmark the performance of different distributed energy resource
management algorithms with open-source datasets, including DSSE algorithms.
Following the 3D Solar Visibility Prize, winners participated in an optional survey to provide
insights into their teams and companies. Among the seven winners, three had previously
competed in an American-Made Challenge, while two had never applied for DOE funding prior
to this prize. Six winners identified themselves as first-time entrepreneurs, with each company
employing fewer than 40 people and collectively raising under $3.3 million in funding after
participating in the prize. The competition provided an opportunity to access new funding
pathways, gain experience in the solar systems integration sector, and advance their work.

8. Path Forward:
Solar Forecasting Prize
The Solar Forecasting Prize increased awareness for stakeholders and competitors within the
solar forecasting industry with state-of-the-art solar forecasting capabilities through the use of
the Solar Forecast Arbiter. While the prize achieved its goals, additional research in solar
forecasting is suggested to continue awareness and strengthen adoption. Future research
should improve the accuracy and scalability of probabilistic forecasts and expand their
integration into grid operations and planning. Commercialization opportunities include low-cost,
high-performance models and broader use of the Solar Forecast Arbiter for standardized
evaluation. Key barriers are limited awareness, integration challenges with existing systems,
and the need for continued validation to build user confidence.
Net Load Forecasting Prize
The Net Load Forecasting Prize provided valuable insights into probabilistic models developed
to predict the net load of an electric grid. However, a lesson learned from this prize was
securing the NDAs from the four utilities and getting them to competitors to have them signed
in a timely manner. The NDAs were critical to the prize as they made the data available to be
shared from the four utilities, and the competition would not have been able to happen without
these NDAs in place. The NDAs had to go through multiple reviews by the utilities’ legal teams,
and then to competitors and potentially some of the competitors’ lawyers. This was eventually
settled, but it caused a two-month delay in the prize competition. For future prizes where
utilities are sharing their data with competitors and NDAs are warranted, the prize
administration team suggests building this into the timeline to provide competitors with realistic
timelines and information. Additionally, the NDAs should be ready to go prior to the intent to
compete deadline, if possible, so they can be sent to competitors as part of the next step of the
competition and have data released more quickly.
3D Solar Visibility Prize
The 3D Solar Visibility Prize was successful in encouraging competitors to develop DSSE tools
and testing their accuracy through the OEDI-SI platform. However, because this prize was
focused on awareness and adoption, NLR and DOE did not work with algorithms directly
beyond auditing due to potential licensing problems. For future prizes, it is recommended to
develop specific templates in the OEDI-SI platform that allow competitors to connect their
algorithms with the platform and evaluate the performance more easily in the platform. There is
also value in exploring how competitors could be engaged to contribute to the OEDI-SI
ecosystem with their algorithms for future research and development efforts. Additionally, any
future competitions could focus on a simpler data format while providing competitors with
immediate feedback on their solution format and enabling them to test a problem’s quality. This
would help with the competitors’ confusion about the data and lessen the number of questions
and office hours. Using a simpler format could have resulted in more competitors participating
during the competition period. The prize administration team suggests launching future prizes
at a conference with a targeted audience and allowing enough time to conduct outreach to
allow for a larger competitor pool. The NLR team plans to host the competition data on the
OEDI-SI website so external users can use the data to evaluate the performance of their
DSSE algorithms in the future.
