Locally finite sets of derivations
Michael Chitayat, Daniel Daigle and Andriy Regeta

Abstract
. Given an algebra B B over a field 𝐤 {\rm\bf k} , we study conditions under which a Lie subalgebra of Der 𝐤 ⁡ ( B ) \operatorname{{\rm Der}}_{\rm\bf k}(B) is locally finite as a set of derivations. As an application of our results, we show that if X X is a quasi-affine variety over an arbitrary field 𝐤 {\rm\bf k} , and if ℒ \mathscr{L} is a finitely generated solvable Lie subalgebra of Der 𝐤 ⁡ 𝒪 X ​ ( X ) \operatorname{{\rm Der}}_{\rm\bf k}\mathscr{O}_{X}(X) consisting of locally finite derivations, then ℒ \mathscr{L} is locally finite. If, moreover, 𝐤 {\rm\bf k} is algebraically closed and of characteristic zero, and X X is irreducible and affine, then ℒ \mathscr{L} is integrable.

1.
Introduction

Let
B
be an algebra over a field
{\rm\bf k}
and let
\operatorname{{\rm Der}}_{\rm\bf k}(B)
be the Lie algebra of
{\rm\bf k}
-derivations of
B
.
A subset
\Delta
of
\operatorname{{\rm Der}}_{\rm\bf k}(B)
is called
locally finite
if for each
b\in B

there exists a finite dimensional
{\rm\bf k}
-linear subspace
U
of
B
such that
b\in U
and
D(U)\subseteq U
for all
D\in\Delta
.
We say that
\Delta
is
weakly locally finite
if every finite subset of
\Delta
is locally finite.
It is straightforward to verify that if

\Delta
is locally finite (resp. weakly locally finite) then so is the Lie subalgebra
\langle\Delta\rangle_{\text{\rm Lie}}
of
\operatorname{{\rm Der}}_{\rm\bf k}(B)
generated by
\Delta
.
A derivation
D\in\operatorname{{\rm Der}}_{\rm\bf k}(B)
is
locally finite
if
\{D\}
is a locally finite subset of
\operatorname{{\rm Der}}_{\rm\bf k}(B)
in the above sense.
We denote the set of locally finite
{\rm\bf k}
-derivations of
B
by
\operatorname{{\rm LFD}}_{\rm\bf k}(B)
.

The concept of weak local finiteness is relevant to the study of automorphism groups of affine algebraic varieties,
but the terminology around it has not yet stabilized in the literature devoted to that subject.
In several sources the concept remains unnamed, with weakly locally finite Lie algebras described ad hoc as
“algebras that can be filtered by locally finite subalgebras.”

[
AZ26
]
introduced the term “locally integrable” for what we call “weakly locally finite”
(see Remark
3.4
for details),
but since we develop the theory at a level of generality where integrability does not have a meaning,
it could be misleading to use that term here.
To ensure clarity and avoid cumbersome phrasing, we use the neutral term “weakly locally finite.”

Clearly, if a Lie subalgebra
\mathscr{L}
of
\operatorname{{\rm Der}}_{\rm\bf k}(B)
is weakly locally finite then
\mathscr{L}\subseteq\operatorname{{\rm LFD}}_{\rm\bf k}(B)
.
This paper is motivated by the question of whether the converse holds,
and more generally by the problem of determining which hypotheses on a Lie subalgebra
\mathscr{L}\subseteq\operatorname{{\rm Der}}_{\rm\bf k}(B)

ensure that
\mathscr{L}
is weakly locally finite.

Variants of this question are investigated in
[
Zai26
]
in the case where
B
is the ring of regular functions on an affine variety.
In particular,
[
Zai26
]
shows that if
{\rm\bf k}
is an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero
and
\mathscr{L}
is a solvable Lie subalgebra of
\operatorname{{\rm Lie}}(\operatorname{{\rm Aut}}\mathbb{A}^{2})
generated by locally finite derivations, then
\mathscr{L}
is weakly locally finite.

We consider the above question from a very general standpoint.
In the present text, an
algebra
over a field
{\rm\bf k}
is a pair
(B,\cdot)
where
B
is a
{\rm\bf k}
-vector space and “
\cdot
” is
an arbitrary
{\rm\bf k}
-bilinear map
B\times B\to B
,
(x,y)\mapsto x\cdot y
(so
B
need not be commutative or associative).
We say that the
{\rm\bf k}
-algebra
B
is
derivation-finite
if there exists a finite subset
S
of
B
such that
the only
D\in\operatorname{{\rm Der}}_{\rm\bf k}(B)
that satisfies
D(x)=0
for all
x\in S
is the zero derivation.
Note that the algebras commonly encountered in algebraic geometry are derivation-finite (see
3.2
).

The following are the main results of this note.
In the first four statements,
{\rm\bf k}
is an arbitrary field and the only assumption on
B
is that it is a derivation-finite
{\rm\bf k}
-algebra.

Theorem
.

Let
B
be a derivation-finite algebra over a field
{\rm\bf k}
.
Let
S
be a subset of
\operatorname{{\rm LFD}}_{\rm\bf k}(B)
that is closed under the Lie bracket and define the sequence of sets
(S_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}

by declaring that
S_{0}=S
and that
S_{n+1}=\big\{\,[D,E]\,\mid\,D,E\in S_{n}\,\big\}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

(1)

S_{0}\supseteq S_{1}\supseteq S_{2}\supseteq\cdots
and each set
S_{n}
is closed under the Lie bracket.

(2)

If there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
S_{n}
is a weakly locally finite set, then
S
is a weakly locally finite set.

Given a Lie algebra
\mathscr{L}
, let
\mathscr{L}^{\prime}
be the Lie subalgebra of
\mathscr{L}
generated by
\big\{\,[x,y]\,\mid\,x,y\in\mathscr{L}\,\big\}
,
and define
\mathscr{L}^{(0)}=\mathscr{L}
and
\mathscr{L}^{(n+1)}=\big(\mathscr{L}^{(n)}\big)^{\prime}
for
n\geq 0
.
Recall that
\mathscr{L}
is
solvable
if there exists
d\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathscr{L}^{(d)}=0
.

Corollary 1
.

Let
B
be a derivation-finite algebra over a field
{\rm\bf k}

and let
\mathscr{L}
be a Lie subalgebra of
\operatorname{{\rm Der}}_{\rm\bf k}(B)
such that
\mathscr{L}\subseteq\operatorname{{\rm LFD}}_{\rm\bf k}(B)
.

(1)

If there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathscr{L}^{(n)}
is weakly locally finite, then
\mathscr{L}
is weakly locally finite.

(2)

If
\mathscr{L}
is solvable then it is weakly locally finite.

In the special case where
B
is the commutative polynomial ring in two variables over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero,
part (b) of Corollary

is also proved in
[
Zai26
]
.

Corollary 2
.

Let
B
be a derivation-finite algebra over a field
{\rm\bf k}
and let
\mathscr{L}
be a solvable Lie subalgebra of
\operatorname{{\rm Der}}_{\rm\bf k}(B)
.
If
\mathscr{L}
has a generating set included in
\operatorname{{\rm LFD}}_{\rm\bf k}(B)

then
\mathscr{L}
is weakly locally finite.

Corollary 3
.

Let
B
be a derivation-finite algebra over a field
{\rm\bf k}

and let
\mathscr{L}
be a Lie subalgebra of
\operatorname{{\rm Der}}_{\rm\bf k}(B)
.
If
\mathscr{L}

is weakly locally finite and
[\mathscr{M},\mathscr{M}]\subseteq\mathscr{L}
.

In the following statement,
{\rm\bf k}
is an arbitrary field and
X
is not necessarily irreducible:

Corollary 4
.

Let
X
be a quasi-affine variety over a field
{\rm\bf k}
.
If
\mathscr{L}
is a solvable Lie subalgebra of
\operatorname{{\rm Der}}_{\rm\bf k}\mathscr{O}_{X}(X)
consisting of locally finite derivations, then
\mathscr{L}
is weakly locally finite
and admits a countable increasing filtration by locally finite Lie subalgebras.

Remark

If
{\rm\bf k}
is an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero and

is an irreducible affine

-variety then the

\mathscr{L}

[

.

2.
Weakly locally finite sets of linear endomorphisms

If
V
is a vector space over a field
{\rm\bf k}
, we write
\mathcal{L}(V)
for the associative
{\rm\bf k}
-algebra whose elements are the linear maps
F:V\to V
and whose multiplication is the composition of maps.
Note that
\mathcal{L}(V)
is also a Lie algebra over
{\rm\bf k}
, with
[F,G]=F\circ G-G\circ F
for all
F,G\in\mathcal{L}(V)
.

2.1 Definition
.

Let
V
be a vector space over a field
{\rm\bf k}
and let
\Delta
be a subset of
\mathcal{L}(V)
.

(1)

The set
\Delta
is
locally finite
if for each
x\in V
there exists a finite dimensional subspace
U
of
V

such that
x\in U
and
F(U)\subseteq U
for all
F\in\Delta
.

(2)

The set
\Delta
is
weakly locally finite
if every finite subset of
\Delta
is locally finite.

Clearly, if
\Delta
is locally finite (resp. weakly locally finite) then so is every subset of
\Delta
.
We say that an element
F\in\mathcal{L}(V)
is
locally finite
if
\{F\}
is a locally finite subset of
\mathcal{L}(V)
in the above sense.

2.2 Remark
.

Let
V
be a vector space over a field
{\rm\bf k}
and
\Delta
a subset of
\mathcal{L}(V)
.
We write
\langle\Delta\rangle_{\text{\rm alg}}
for the
subalgebra of
\mathcal{L}(V)
generated by
\Delta
, i.e.,
the intersection of all
{\rm\bf k}
-subspaces
A
of
\mathcal{L}(V)
that are closed under composition of maps
and satisfy
\Delta\subseteq A
.
We write
\langle\Delta\rangle_{\text{\rm Lie}}
for the
Lie subalgebra of
\mathcal{L}(V)
generated by
\Delta
.
It is easy to see that if
\Delta
is locally finite (resp. weakly locally finite)
then so is each one of
\operatorname{{\rm Span}}_{\rm\bf k}(\Delta)\subseteq\langle\Delta\rangle_{\text{\rm Lie}}\subseteq\langle\Delta\rangle_{\text{\rm alg}}
.

If
X,Y
are subsets of a Lie algebra
\mathscr{L}
, we define
[X,Y]=\big\{\,[x,y]\,\mid\,x\in X\text{ and }y\in Y\,\big\}
, which is
also a subset of
\mathscr{L}
.
The following result and Lemma
3.6
are analogous to
[
Sku21
, Lemma 2]
.

2.3 Lemma
.

Let
V
be a vector space over a field
{\rm\bf k}
and let
\Delta
be a locally finite subset of
\mathcal{L}(V)
.
If
T
is a locally finite element of
\mathcal{L}(V)
such that
[T,\Delta]\subseteq\Delta
, then
\{T\}\cup\Delta
is locally finite.

Proof.

Let
x\in V
. Since
\Delta
is locally finite, there exists a finite dimensional subspace
U
of
V
such
that
x\in U
and
F(U)\subseteq U
for all
F\in\Delta
. Consider the subspace
W=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}T^{j}(U)
of
V
and note that
x\in W
.
Since
U
is finite dimensional and
T
is locally finite,
W
is finite dimensional.
Clearly,
T(W)\subseteq W
. To finish the proof, it suffices to show that
F(W)\subseteq W
for all
F\in\Delta
.

For each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, define
W_{j}=U+TU+T^{2}U+\cdots+T^{j}U
and let
\mathscr{P}(j)
be the assertion

FT^{j}U\subseteq W_{j}\qquad\text{for all $F\in\Delta$.}

Since
F(U)\subseteq U
for all
F\in\Delta
,
\mathscr{P}(0)
is true.
Suppose that
j\in\mathbb{N}
is such that
\mathscr{P}(j)
is true.
Given any
F\in\Delta
we have
FT^{j+1}=(FT)T^{j}=(TF-[T,F])T^{j}=TFT^{j}-F^{\prime}T^{j}
where
F^{\prime}=[T,F]\in\Delta
, so
\mathscr{P}(j)
gives

FT^{j+1}U\subseteq TFT^{j}U+F^{\prime}T^{j}U\subseteq TW_{j}+W_{j}\subseteq W_{j+1},

which shows that
\mathscr{P}(j+1)
is true. By induction, we obtain that
\mathscr{P}(j)
is true for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
.
It follows that if
F\in\Delta
then
F(W)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}FT^{j}U\subseteq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}W_{j}=W
, as desired.
∎

3.
Algebras and sets of derivations

In this section, an
algebra
over a field
{\rm\bf k}
is a pair
(B,\cdot)
where
B
is a
{\rm\bf k}
-vector space and “
\cdot
” is
an arbitrary
{\rm\bf k}
-bilinear map
B\times B\to B
,
(x,y)\mapsto x\cdot y
.

3.1 Definition
.

Let
B
be an algebra over a field
{\rm\bf k}
.

(1)

A
{\rm\bf k}
-derivation
of
B
is a
{\rm\bf k}
-linear map
D:B\to B
satisfying

D(x\cdot y)=D(x)\cdot y+x\cdot D(y)
for all
x,y\in B
.
The set of all
{\rm\bf k}
-derivations of
B
is denoted
\operatorname{{\rm Der}}_{\rm\bf k}(B)
and is a Lie algebra over
{\rm\bf k}
, with Lie bracket defined
by
[D,E]=D\circ E-E\circ D
for
D,E\in\operatorname{{\rm Der}}_{\rm\bf k}(B)
.

(2)

We say that a subset of
\operatorname{{\rm Der}}_{\rm\bf k}(B)

is
locally finite
(resp.
weakly locally finite
) if it is so when viewed as a set of
{\rm\bf k}
-linear endomorphisms of
B
.

(3)

A derivation
D\in\operatorname{{\rm Der}}_{\rm\bf k}(B)
is
locally finite
if
\{D\}
is a locally finite subset of
\operatorname{{\rm Der}}_{\rm\bf k}(B)
.
We use the notation
\operatorname{{\rm LFD}}_{\rm\bf k}(B)=\big\{\,D\in\operatorname{{\rm Der}}_{\rm\bf k}(B)\,\mid\,\text{$D$ is locally finite}\,\big\}

and note that the set
\operatorname{{\rm LFD}}_{\rm\bf k}(B)
is in general neither closed under addition nor under the Lie bracket.

Clearly, if
\Delta
is a weakly locally finite subset of
\operatorname{{\rm Der}}_{\rm\bf k}(B)
then
\Delta\subseteq\operatorname{{\rm LFD}}_{\rm\bf k}(B)
.

(4)

The
{\rm\bf k}
-algebra
B
is
derivation-finite
if there exists a finite subset
S
of
B
such that:

(*)
the only
D\in\operatorname{{\rm Der}}_{\rm\bf k}(B)
that satisfies
D(x)=0
for all
x\in S
is the zero derivation.

3.2
.

Examples of derivation-finite
{\rm\bf k}
-algebras.

(1)

If
{\rm\bf k}
is any field and
B
is any type of finitely generated
{\rm\bf k}
-algebra (for instance a finitely generated commutative
{\rm\bf k}
-algebra,
or a finitely generated Lie algebra over
{\rm\bf k}

B
is derivation-finite.

Indeed, any finite generating set of
B
satisfies condition
(*)

3.1
(d).

(2)

If
A
is a finitely generated commutative algebra over a field
{\rm\bf k}
, then every localization of
A
is a derivation-finite
{\rm\bf k}
-algebra.

To see this, let
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\}
be a generating set of
A
and
T
a multiplicative set of
A

S=\{a_{1}/1,\dots,a_{n}/1\}
of
B=T^{-1}A
. Suppose that
D\in\operatorname{{\rm Der}}_{\rm\bf k}(B)
is such that
D(s)=0
for all
s\in S
.
Since
\ker(D)
is a subalgebra of
B
that contains
S
, we have
D(\alpha/1)=0
for all
\alpha\in A
.
If
b\in B
then there exist
t\in T
and
\alpha\in A
such that
(t/1)b=\alpha/1
; since
D(t/1)=0=D(\alpha/1)
,
we have
(t/1)D(b)=0
, so
D(b)=0
because
t/1
is a unit of
B
.
This shows that
D=0
, so
B
is derivation-finite.

(3)

If
{\rm\bf k}
is a field of characteristic zero and
B
is a commutative integral domain containing
{\rm\bf k}

and of finite transcendence degree over
{\rm\bf k}
, then
B
is a derivation-finite
{\rm\bf k}
-algebra.

Indeed, there exists a finite subset
S
of

B
is algebraic over
{\rm\bf k}[S]

(*)
of Definition
3.1
(d).

(4)

If
{\rm\bf k}
is an arbitrary field and
B
is a commutative integral domain containing
{\rm\bf k}
and such that

is a finitely generated field extension (where

is a derivation-finite
{\rm\bf k}
-algebra.

Indeed, there exists a finite subset
S
of
B
such that
\operatorname{{\rm Frac}}(B)={\rm\bf k}(S)
; then
S
satisfies
(*)
.

(5)

If
X

(6)

If

3.3 Remarks
.

Let
B
be a derivation-finite algebra over a field
{\rm\bf k}
.

(1)

If
\mathscr{L}
is a locally finite Lie subalgebra of
\operatorname{{\rm Der}}_{\rm\bf k}(B)
then
\mathscr{L}
is a finite dimensional vector space.

To see this, choose a finite subset
S
of
B
that satisfies condition
(*)
of Definition
3.1
(d).
Since
\mathscr{L}
is locally finite, there exists a finite dimensional
{\rm\bf k}
-linear subspace
U
of
B
such that
S\subseteq U
and
D(U)\subseteq U
for
all
D\in\mathscr{L}
. The restriction map
\mathscr{L}\to\mathcal{L}(U)
,
D\mapsto D|_{U}:U\to U
, is an injective linear map,
and since
\mathcal{L}(U)
is finite-dimensional, so is
\mathscr{L}
.
(This generalizes
[
KZ24
, Lemma 1.6.2]
.)

(2)

If
\mathscr{L}
is a weakly locally finite Lie subalgebra of
\operatorname{{\rm Der}}_{\rm\bf k}(B)
then every finitely generated Lie subalgebra of
\mathscr{L}

is locally finite.

Indeed, if
\Delta
is a finite subset of
\mathscr{L}
then
\Delta
is locally finite,
so Remark
2.2
implies that
\langle\Delta\rangle_{\text{\rm Lie}}
is locally finite.

(3)

If
\dim_{\rm\bf k}(B)
is countable then
each weakly locally finite Lie subalgebra of
\operatorname{{\rm Der}}_{\rm\bf k}(B)
is the union of a countable increasing sequence
