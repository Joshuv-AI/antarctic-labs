A scalar c 0 c_{0} -approximation criterion and gap-coordinate preduals for Lipschitz-free spaces
Luigi D’Onofrio

Abstract
. We record a scalar c 0 c_{0} -approximation criterion for Banach spaces canonically embedded into ℓ ∞ \ell^{\infty} by a countable norming family. It identifies the dual of the associated c 0 c_{0} -subspace with the prescribed atomic predual precisely when bounded coordinatewise approximation by c 0 c_{0} -elements is available. The formulation is a self-contained scalar version of the atomic predual and two-stars framework of D’Onofrio–Greco–Perfekt–Sbordone–Schiattarella. We then apply the criterion to Lipschitz-free spaces. The weak-star density of Lipschitz functions with bounded support is due to Aliaga–Pernecká–Petitjean–Procházka; in proper spaces this yields a canonical quotient from the bidual of the norm-closed span of compactly supported Lipschitz functions onto Lip 0 ⁡ ( M ) \operatorname{Lip}_{0}(M) . The main concrete application concerns countable proper subsets K ⊂ [ 0 , ∞ ) K\subset[0,\infty) with the Euclidean metric. The connected components ( a j , b j ) (a_{j},b_{j}) of the complement of K K give gap coordinates Δ j ​ f = f ⁡ ( b j ) − f ⁡ ( a j ) b j − a j . \Delta_{j}f=\frac{f(b_{j})-f(a_{j})}{b_{j}-a_{j}}. We prove that, in the infinite case, these coordinates identify Lip 0 ⁡ ( K ) \operatorname{Lip}_{0}(K) with ℓ ∞ \ell^{\infty} , that the corresponding c 0 c_{0} -subspace coincides with Dalet’s predual S ⁡ ( K ) S(K) , and hence that S ​ ( K ) ∗ ≅ ℱ ⁡ ( K ) , S ​ ( K ) ∗ ⁣ ∗ ≅ Lip 0 ⁡ ( K ) S(K)^{*}\cong\mathcal{F}(K),\qquad S(K)^{**}\cong\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(K) canonically and isometrically. This gives a coordinate c 0 c_{0} realization, for a non-discrete class with accumulation points, of the preduality covered abstractly by Dalet’s theorem for countable proper metric spaces. No new proof of Dalet’s theorem in full generality is claimed.

1.
Introduction

Let
E
be a Banach space and let
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset E^{*}
be a
countable norming family. The coordinate map

V:E\longrightarrow\ell^{\infty},\qquad Vx=(f_{n}(x))_{n\in\mathbb{N}},

is an isometric embedding. It is natural to ask when the subspace

E_{c_{0}}:=\{x\in E:Vx\in c_{0}\}

still determines
E
by biduality. We give an elementary scalar answer. Let

V^{\sharp}:\ell^{1}\longrightarrow E^{*},\qquad V^{\sharp}\xi=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\xi_{n}f_{n},

where
V^{\sharp}
is the restriction to the canonical copy of
\ell^{1}
in

(\ell^{\infty})^{*}
of the Banach-space adjoint of
V
. Set

Y=V^{\sharp}(\ell^{1})
, endowed with the quotient norm inherited from

\ell^{1}/\ker V^{\sharp}
. Assuming that the canonical map
E\to Y^{*}
is an
isometric isomorphism, we prove that

E_{c_{0}}^{*}\cong Y

canonically and isometrically if and only if every element of
E
is the
coordinatewise limit, along the family
(f_{n})
, of a bounded sequence in

E_{c_{0}}
. This should be read as a scalar
c_{0}
-version of the more general
atomic predual and two-stars principle developed in
[
DGPSS20
]
for
Banach spaces with supremum-type norms induced by families of operators.

The second part concerns Lipschitz-free spaces. If
(M,d,0)
is a pointed
metric space, then
\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(M)
is canonically the dual of the Lipschitz-free
space
\mathcal{F}(M)
. We use the standard notation

m_{x,y}=\frac{\delta(x)-\delta(y)}{d(x,y)}\qquad(x\neq y)

for normalized molecules. A lemma of
Aliaga–Pernecká–Petitjean–Procházka states that Lipschitz functions
with bounded support are weak-star dense in
\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(M)

[
APPP20
, Lemma
2.2]
. In a proper space, bounded closed sets are compact, so this
implies the weak-star density of compactly supported Lipschitz functions.
We recall the result and record the resulting quotient map

G_{0}(M)^{**}\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(M),\qquad G_{0}(M)=\overline{\operatorname{Lip}_{0,c}(M)}^{\|\cdot\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}}.

The point where the scalar criterion gives a useful additional viewpoint is
coordinate selection. The naive family of all molecules is appropriate in
proper discrete spaces, but not in spaces with accumulation points: long
molecules approaching the same limiting configuration prevent the relevant
predual from being a
c_{0}
-coordinate space. We therefore introduce a simple
coordinate-admissibility formulation and then treat a non-discrete class
where the coordinates can be chosen explicitly.

Let
K\subset[0,\infty)
be infinite, countable, closed, and contain the base point

0
. Such
K
is a proper metric space; the finite case is trivial. Let
\mathcal{G}(K)
be the family of
bounded connected components
(a,b)
of the complement of
K
in its convex
hull. Since
K
is countable, it has Lebesgue measure zero, and the gaps in

\mathcal{G}(K)
carry all one-dimensional length. For each gap
I=(a,b)
we
consider the coordinate

\Delta_{I}f=\frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}.

We prove that

\|f\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}=\\
sup_{I\in\mathcal{G}(K)}|\Delta_{I}f|,

that
\mathcal{F}(K)
is the
\ell^{1}
-sum of the corresponding gap molecules, and
that the subspace defined by
(\Delta_{I}f)_{I\in\mathcal{G}(K)}\in c_{0}

coincides with Dalet’s predual

S(K)=\left\{f\in\operatorname{lip}_{0}(K):\lim_{r\to\infty}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\text{ or }y\notin B(0,r)\\
x\neq y\end{subarray}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|}=0\right\}.

Consequently

S(K)^{*}\cong\mathcal{F}(K),\qquad S(K)^{**}\cong\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(K)

isometrically. Dalet proved in full generality that
\mathcal{F}(M)
is a dual space
and has the metric approximation property whenever
M
is countable proper

[
Dal15b
]
; the present result does not extend that theorem. Rather, it
shows that for countable proper subsets of the real line, including spaces
with accumulation points, Dalet’s predual is exactly a natural gap-coordinate

c_{0}
-space.

All Banach spaces are real. Throughout the paper
B(x,r)
denotes the
closed ball.

2.
A scalar
c_{0}
-approximation criterion

Definition 2.1
.

Let
E
be a Banach space and let
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset E^{*}
satisfy

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|f_{n}\|\leq 1,\qquad\|x\|=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|f_{n}(x)|\quad(x\in E).

Define

V:E\longrightarrow\ell^{\infty},\qquad Vx=(f_{n}(x))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}.

Then
V
is an isometric embedding. Define also

V^{\sharp}:\ell^{1}\longrightarrow E^{*},\qquad V^{\sharp}\xi=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\xi_{n}f_{n}.

Equivalently,
V^{\sharp}
is the restriction of the adjoint

V^{*}:(\ell^{\infty})^{*}\to E^{*}
to the canonical subspace

\ell^{1}\subset(\ell^{\infty})^{*}
.

Let

Y:=V^{\sharp}(\ell^{1})\subset E^{*},

e equip
Y
with the quotient norm inherited from
\ell^{1}/\ker V^{\sharp}
.
Finally, set

E_{c_{0}}:=\{x\in E:Vx\in c_{0}\}.

The space
E_{c_{0}}
is closed in
E
. Indeed,

V(E_{c_{0}})=V(E)\cap c_{0}
, and
V(E)
is closed in
\ell^{\infty}
because

V
is an isometry.

The canonical evaluation map

J:E\longrightarrow Y^{*},\qquad Jx(y)=y(x),

is contractive. In the criterion below it is assumed to be onto and
isometric.

Theorem 2.2

(Scalar
c_{0}
-criterion)
.

Assume, in the setting of Definition
2.1
, that

J:E\longrightarrow Y^{*}

is an isometric isomorphism. Then the following assertions are equivalent.

(i)

The canonical restriction map

\Lambda:Y\longrightarrow E_{c_{0}}^{*},\qquad\Lambda(y)=y|_{E_{c_{0}}},

is an isometric isomorphism.

(ii)

For every
x\in E
there exists a sequence
(x_{k})\subset E_{c_{0}}

such that

\sup_{k}\|x_{k}\|<\infty\qquad\text{and}\qquad f_{n}(x_{k})\longrightarrow f_{n}(x)\quad(n\in\mathbb{N}).

When these conditions hold, the sequence in
(ii)
may be chosen
with
\sup_{k}\|x_{k}\|\leq\|x\|
. Moreover, on bounded sequences in
E
,
coordinatewise convergence on the family
(f_{n})
is equivalent to
convergence in
\sigma(E,Y)
.

Proof.

Set

W:=V(E_{c_{0}})=V(E)\cap c_{0}\subset c_{0}.

The restriction
V:E_{c_{0}}\to W
is an isometric isomorphism. Hence

E_{c_{0}}^{*}\cong W^{*}.

Since
W
is a closed subspace of
c_{0}
, the classical duality of subspaces
of
c_{0}
gives

W^{*}\cong\ell^{1}/W^{\perp},

where

W^{\perp}=\left\{\xi\in\ell^{1}:\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\xi_{n}w_{n}=0\text{ for every }w=(w_{n})\in W\right\}.

On the other hand, by definition of the quotient norm on
Y
,

Y\cong\ell^{1}/\ker V^{\sharp}.

We first observe that

\ker V^{\sharp}\subset W^{\perp}.

Indeed, if
\xi\in\ker V^{\sharp}
and
w=Vx\in W
with
x\in E_{c_{0}}
, then

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\xi_{n}w_{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\xi_{n}f_{n}(x)=V^{\sharp}\xi(x)=0.

Therefore the canonical quotient map

\ell^{1}/\ker V^{\sharp}\to\ell^{1}/W^{\perp}
is contractive; it is precisely

\Lambda:Y\to E_{c_{0}}^{*}
under the identifications above.

Assume now (ii). Let
\xi\in W^{\perp}
and let
x\in E
. Choose

(x_{k})\subset E_{c_{0}}
as in (ii), say
\sup_{k}\|x_{k}\|\leq M
. For
every
k
,

0=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\xi_{n}f_{n}(x_{k}),

because
Vx_{k}\in W
and
\xi
annihilates
W
. Since

|\xi_{n}f_{n}(x_{k})|\leq M|\xi_{n}|,\qquad\xi=(\xi_{n})\in\ell^{1},

the dominated convergence theorem for series yields

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\xi_{n}f_{n}(x)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\xi_{n}f_{n}(x_{k})=0.

Thus
V^{\sharp}\xi=0
, and therefore
W^{\perp}\subset\ker V^{\sharp}
.
Consequently

W^{\perp}=\ker V^{\sharp}.

It follows that

E_{c_{0}}^{*}\cong W^{*}\cong\ell^{1}/W^{\perp}=\ell^{1}/\ker V^{\sharp}\cong Y

canonically and isometrically. This proves (i).

Conversely, assume (i). Then

\Lambda:Y\to E_{c_{0}}^{*}
is an isometric isomorphism, and hence its adjoint

\Lambda^{*}:E_{c_{0}}^{**}\longrightarrow Y^{*}

is an isometric isomorphism. Let
x\in E
and set

\widetilde{x}=(\Lambda^{*})^{-1}(Jx)\in E_{c_{0}}^{**}.

Then
\|\widetilde{x}\|=\|x\|
. By Goldstine’s theorem, there exists a net

(u_{\alpha})\subset E_{c_{0}}
such that

\sup_{\alpha}\|u_{\alpha}\|\leq\|x\|,\qquad\kappa_{E_{c_{0}}}(u_{\alpha})\xrightarrow{w^{*}}\widetilde{x}\quad\text{in }E_{c_{0}}^{**}.

For
u\in E_{c_{0}}
and
y\in Y
one has

\langle\Lambda^{*}\kappa_{E_{c_{0}}}(u),y\rangle=\langle\kappa_{E_{c_{0}}}(u),\Lambda y\rangle=(\Lambda y)(u)=y(u)=\langle Ju,y\rangle.

Hence
\Lambda^{*}\kappa_{E_{c_{0}}}(u)=Ju
. Applying
\Lambda^{*}
to the
convergent net gives

Ju_{\alpha}\xrightarrow{\sigma(Y^{*},Y)}Jx.

Since
Y
is a quotient of
\ell^{1}
, it is separable. Let
(y_{j})_{j\geq 1}

be dense in the unit ball of
Y
. We choose a sequence from the net as
follows. For each
k
choose an index
\alpha_{k}
such that

|(Ju_{\alpha_{k}}-Jx)(y_{j})|<\frac{1}{k},\qquad 1\leq j\leq k.

This is possible because the net converges to
Jx
in
\sigma(Y^{*},Y)
.
Set
x_{k}=u_{\alpha_{k}}
. Then
\sup_{k}\|x_{k}\|\leq\|x\|
. If
y\in Y
and

\varepsilon>0
, choose
j
such that
\|y-y_{j}\|
is sufficiently small;
using the uniform boundedness of
(Jx_{k})
and
Jx
, one obtains

(Jx_{k})(y)\longrightarrow(Jx)(y).

Thus
x_{k}\to x
in
\sigma(E,Y)
. Since each
f_{n}
belongs to
Y
, this
implies

f_{n}(x_{k})\longrightarrow f_{n}(x)\qquad(n\in\mathbb{N}),

and proves (ii).

It remains to justify the final assertion. The linear span of
(f_{n})
is
dense in
Y
, because finitely supported sequences are dense in
\ell^{1}
and

Y
is the quotient image of
\ell^{1}
. If
(z_{k})
is bounded in
E
and

f_{n}(z_{k})\to f_{n}(z)
for every
n
, then convergence holds first for finite
linear combinations of the
f_{n}
and then for every
y\in Y
by density and
boundedness. Thus
z_{k}\to z
in
\sigma(E,Y)
. The converse is immediate.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

Under the equivalent conditions of Theorem
2.2
, one has a
canonical isometric identification

E_{c_{0}}^{**}\cong E.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.2
,
E_{c_{0}}^{*}\cong Y
isometrically. Taking
duals gives
E_{c_{0}}^{**}\cong Y^{*}
. Since
J:E\to Y^{*}
is an isometric
isomorphism, the result follows.
∎

3.
Compact support and a canonical quotient

Let
(M,d,0)
be a pointed metric space. We denote by
\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(M)
the
Banach space of all real-valued Lipschitz functions
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
such that

f(0)=0
, equipped with the norm

\|f\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}=\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{d(x,y)}.

The Lipschitz-free space over
M
is

\mathcal{F}(M)=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{\delta(x):x\in M\}\subset\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(M)^{*},

where
\langle f,\delta(x)\rangle=f(x)
. It is the canonical predual of

\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(M)
:

\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(M)=\mathcal{F}(M)^{*}

isometrically. For
x\neq y
we write

m_{x,y}=\frac{\delta(x)-\delta(y)}{d(x,y)}\in\mathcal{F}(M)

for the elementary normalized molecule.

We use the notation

\operatorname{Lip}_{0,bs}(M)=\{f\in\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(M):\operatorname{supp}(f)\text{ is bounded}\}

and

\operatorname{Lip}_{0,c}(M)=\{f\in\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(M):\operatorname{supp}(f)\text{ is compact}\}.

Here
\operatorname{supp}(f)
denotes the closed support.

Theorem 3.1

(Aliaga–Pernecká–Petitjean–Procházka)
.

For every pointed metric space
M
, the space
\operatorname{Lip}_{0,bs}(M)
is weak-star
dense in
\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(M)
, that is,

\overline{\operatorname{Lip}_{0,bs}(M)}^{\sigma(\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(M),\mathcal{F}(M))}=\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(M).

Proof.

This is
[
APPP20
, Lemma 2.2]
.
∎

For proper metric spaces, bounded closed sets are compact. Hence
Theorem
3.1
implies weak-star density of compactly supported
Lipschitz functions. We also recall a norm-controlled cut-off proof, since
it is useful below.

Lemma 3.2

(Norm-preserving compactly supported cut-off)
.

Let
(M,d,0)
be a pointed proper metric space and let
f\in\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(M)
.
Put
L=\|f\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}
. For every
R>0
there exists

f_{R}\in\operatorname{Lip}_{0,c}(M)
such that

(1)

f_{R}=f
on
B(0,R)
;

(2)

f_{R}=0
on
M\setminus B(0,2R)
;

(3)

\|f_{R}\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}\leq\|f\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}
.

Proof.

Let

A_{R}=B(0,R)\cup(M\setminus B(0,2R)).

Define
g_{R}:A_{R}\to\mathbb{R}
by

g_{R}(x)=\begin{cases}f(x),&x\in B(0,R),\\
0,&x\in M\setminus B(0,2R).\end{cases}

The two pieces are disjoint. The map
g_{R}
is
L
-Lipschitz on
A_{R}
. The
only non-trivial case is when
x\in B(0,R)
and
y\in M\setminus B(0,2R)
.
Then

|g_{R}(x)-g_{R}(y)|=|f(x)|\leq Ld(x,0)\leq LR,

while

d(x,y)\geq d(y,0)-d(x,0)\geq 2R-R=R.

Thus
|g_{R}(x)-g_{R}(y)|\leq Ld(x,y)
.

By the McShane extension theorem
[
McS34
]
,
g_{R}
admits an

L
-Lipschitz extension
f_{R}:M\to\mathbb{R}
. Since
0\in B(0,R)
and
f(0)=0
, one
has
f_{R}(0)=0
. Moreover
f_{R}=f
on
B(0,R)
and
f_{R}=0
on

M\setminus B(0,2R)
. Hence
\operatorname{supp}(f_{R})\subset B(0,2R)
, which is compact
because
M
is proper. Therefore
f_{R}\in\operatorname{Lip}_{0,c}(M)
and

\|f_{R}\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}\leq L
.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

If
(M,d,0)
is proper, then
\operatorname{Lip}_{0,c}(M)
is weak-star dense in

\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(M)
. Moreover, for every
f\in\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(M)
the approximating net may
be chosen in
\|f\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}B_{\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(M)}
.

Proof.

Let
f_{R}
be the functions given by Lemma
3.2
. If

\mu=\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\delta(x_{j})
is finitely supported and
R
is large
enough that all
x_{j}
belong to
B(0,R)
, then

\langle f_{R},\mu\rangle=\langle f,\mu\rangle
. Since finitely supported
elements are dense in
\mathcal{F}(M)
and
(f_{R})
is norm bounded, it follows that

f_{R}\to f
in
\sigma(\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(M),\mathcal{F}(M))
.
∎

Let

G_{0}(M):=\overline{\operatorname{Lip}_{0,c}(M)}^{\|\cdot\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}}\subset\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(M).

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
(M,d,0)
be proper. The restriction map

R:\mathcal{F}(M)\longrightarrow G_{0}(M)^{*},\qquad R\mu=\mu|_{G_{0}(M)},

is an isometric embedding. Consequently, its adjoint

R^{*}:G_{0}(M)^{**}\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}(M)^{*}=\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(M)

is a contractive weak-star continuous quotient map. Moreover,
R^{*}
extends
the canonical inclusion
G_{0}(M)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(M)
.

Proof.

For
\mu\in\mathcal{F}(M)
,

\|R\mu\|=\sup_{g\in B_{G_{0}(M)}}|\langle g,\mu\rangle|.

By Corollary
3.3
, the unit ball of
G_{0}(M)
is
weak-star dense in the unit ball of
\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(M)
. Since

f\mapsto\langle f,\mu\rangle
is weak-star continuous on
\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(M)
,

\|R\mu\|=\sup_{f\in B_{\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(M)}}|\langle f,\mu\rangle|=\|\mu\|_{\mathcal{F}(M)}.

Thus
R
is an isometric embedding. The adjoint of an isometric embedding is
a quotient map of norm one. Finally, if
g\in G_{0}(M)
and

\kappa_{G_{0}(M)}:G_{0}(M)\to G_{0}(M)^{**}
denotes the canonical embedding,
then, for every
\mu\in\mathcal{F}(M)
,

\langle R^{*}\kappa_{G_{0}(M)}(g),\mu\rangle=\langle\kappa_{G_{0}(M)}(g),R\mu\rangle=\langle g,\mu\rangle.

Hence
R^{*}\kappa_{G_{0}(M)}(g)=g
in
\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(M)
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

The preceding quotient statement is a consequence of the weak-star density
of compact support. It does not imply, and is not meant to imply, a general
identification
\operatorname{lip}_{0}(M)^{**}\cong\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(M)
. Compactly supported Lipschitz
functions need not be little Lipschitz functions; for instance, on

[0,1]
the function
x\mapsto x(1-x)
is compactly supported but its
incremental quotients along
(1/k,0)
tend to
1
. The relationship between compactly supported Lipschitz functions
and the little Lipschitz space on compact metric spaces is
further analysed in
[
1
]
, where atomic decompositions
of the predual and biduality results for the pair

(\mathrm{lip}(K,\rho),\mathrm{Lip}(K,\rho))
are obtained
in the framework of o-O structures.

4.
Coordinate families in Lipschitz-free spaces

We isolate the coordinate condition needed to apply
Theorem
2.2
to Lipschitz-free spaces.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(M,d,0)
be a pointed metric space. A sequence

\mu=(\mu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset B_{\mathcal{F}(M)}
is called a
metric quotient
coordinate family
if the summation operator

T_{\mu}:\ell^{1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}(M),\qquad T_{\mu}((a_{n}))=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}\mu_{n},

is a metric quotient map onto
\mathcal{F}(M)
.

For such a family define

E_{c_{0}}(\mu)=\left\{f\in\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(M):(\langle f,\mu_{n}\rangle)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in c_{0}\right\}.

We say that
\mu
is
c_{0}
-admissible
if, for every

f\in\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(M)
, there exists a sequence
(f_{k})\subset E_{c_{0}}(\mu)
such
that

\sup_{k}\|f_{k}\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}\leq\|f\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}

and

\langle f_{k},\mu_{n}\rangle\longrightarrow\langle f,\mu_{n}\rangle\qquad(n\in\mathbb{N}).

If
\mu
is a metric quotient coordinate family, then its coordinates are
isometrically norming for
\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(M)
. Indeed,

\|f\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}=\|f\|_{\mathcal{F}(M)^{*}}=\|T_{\mu}^{*}f\|_{\ell^{\infty}}=\sup_{n}|\langle f,\mu_{n}\rangle|.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
(M,d,0)
be a pointed metric space and let

\mu=(\mu_{n})\subset B_{\mathcal{F}(M)}
be a
c_{0}
-admissible metric quotient
coordinate family. Then the canonical restriction map

\mathcal{F}(M)\longrightarrow E_{c_{0}}(\mu)^{*},\qquad\eta\longmapsto\eta|_{E_{c_{0}}(\mu)},

is an isometric isomorphism. Consequently,

E_{c_{0}}(\mu)^{**}\cong\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(M)

canonically and isometrically.

Proof.

Set
E=\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(M)
and define

f_{n}(g)=\langle g,\mu_{n}\rangle,\qquad g\in E.

The preceding observation shows that
(f_{n})
is a countable norming family.
Moreover, the space
Y
associated with
(f_{n})
in
Definition
2.1
is canonically
\mathcal{F}(M)
, because

T_{\mu}
is a metric quotient map onto
\mathcal{F}(M)
. Under this identification

J:E\to Y^{*}
is precisely the canonical isometric identification

\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(M)=\mathcal{F}(M)^{*}
.

The
c_{0}
-subspace associated with the coordinates
(f_{n})
is exactly

E_{c_{0}}(\mu)
, and
c_{0}
-admissibility is precisely condition (ii)
of Theorem
2.2
. The conclusion follows from
Theorem
2.2
and Corollary
2.3
.
∎

Corollary 4.3

(A compact-support sufficient condition)
.

Let
(M,d,0)
be proper and let
\mu=(\mu_{n})\subset B_{\mathcal{F}(M)}
be a metric
quotient coordinate family. If

\operatorname{Lip}_{0,c}(M)\subset E_{c_{0}}(\mu),

then
\mu
is
c_{0}
-admissible. Hence

E_{c_{0}}(\mu)^{*}\cong\mathcal{F}(M),\qquad E_{c_{0}}(\mu)^{**}\cong\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(M)

canonically and isometrically.

Proof.

Let
f\in\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(M)
. By Lemma
3.2
and
Corollary
3.3
, there is a net, and in particular a
coordinatewise approximating family,
(f_{R})\subset\operatorname{Lip}_{0,c}(M)
such that

\|f_{R}\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}\leq\|f\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}
and

f_{R}\to f
in
\sigma(\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(M),\mathcal{F}(M))
. Since each
\mu_{n}
belongs to

\mathcal{F}(M)
,

\langle f_{R},\mu_{n}\rangle\to\langle f,\mu_{n}\rangle\qquad(n\in\mathbb{N}).

The assumption gives
f_{R}\in E_{c_{0}}(\mu)
. Choosing a sequence from the
net by the same separability argument as in Theorem
2.2

proves
c_{0}
-admissibility, and Proposition
4.2
applies.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

Corollary
4.3
explains why the family of all
molecules works in proper discrete spaces: compact sets are finite there.
It also explains why the same family is too large in the presence of
accumulation points. A Lipschitz function may have non-zero macroscopic
slope along infinitely many molecules converging to the same limiting
molecule, so the corresponding coordinate sequence need not lie in
c_{0}
.
The next section uses a smaller, adapted family of coordinates.

5.
Gap coordinates for countable proper subsets of the real line

We now give the main concrete application. It goes beyond the discrete case
while remaining explicit enough to be proved directly from the scalar
criterion.

Let
K\subset[0,\infty)
be an infinite countable closed subset containing
0
. With the Euclidean metric and base point
0
, the space

K
is proper. Let

R_{K}:=\sup K\in(0,\infty]

and put

I_{K}=\begin{cases}[0,R_{K}],&R_{K}<\infty,\\
[0,\infty),&R_{K}=\infty.\end{cases}

Let
\mathcal{G}(K)
be the family of bounded connected components of

I_{K}\setminus K
. Each
I\in\mathcal{G}(K)
is an open interval

I=(a_{I},b_{I})
with
a_{I},b_{I}\in K
and length

|I|=b_{I}-a_{I}>0.

Since
K
is countable, it has Lebesgue measure zero; hence the intervals in

\mathcal{G}(K)
cover
I_{K}
up to a null set. We fix once and for all an
enumeration

\mathcal{G}(K)=\{I_{j}:j\in\mathbb{N}\}.

For
f\in\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(K)
define the gap slopes

\Delta_{I}f=\frac{f(b_{I})-f(a_{I})}{b_{I}-a_{I}}\qquad(I\in\mathcal{G}(K)).

Lemma 5.1

(Gap decomposition of the free space)
.

For
I=(a_{I},b_{I})\in\mathcal{G}(K)
put

\mu_{I}:=m_{b_{I},a_{I}}=\frac{\delta(b_{I})-\delta(a_{I})}{b_{I}-a_{I}}\in\mathcal{F}(K).

Then the map

U:\ell^{1}(\mathcal{G}(K))\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}(K),\qquad U((c_{I}))=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{G}(K)}c_{I}\mu_{I},

is an isometric isomorphism onto
\mathcal{F}(K)
. Consequently,

\|f\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}=\sup_{I\in\mathcal{G}(K)}|\Delta_{I}f|\qquad(f\in\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(K)).

Proof.

We use the standard identification of
\mathcal{F}([0,R])
with
L^{1}[0,R]
, and of

\mathcal{F}([0,\infty))
with
L^{1}[0,\infty)
, under which

\delta(t)\longmapsto\mathbf{1}_{[0,t]}\qquad(t\geq 0);

see, for example,
[
Wea18
]
. The canonical linearization embeds

\mathcal{F}(K)
isometrically as the closed linear span of

\{\mathbf{1}_{[0,t]}:t\in K\}
in the corresponding
L^{1}
space.

More precisely, the isometric embedding
F([0,\infty))\hookrightarrow L^{1}[0,\infty)

restricts to an isometric embedding of
F(K)
as follows.
The inclusion
\iota\colon K\hookrightarrow[0,\infty)
induces a
norm-one restriction map
\iota^{*}\colon\operatorname{Lip}_{0}([0,\infty))\to\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(K)

and a canonical isometric embedding

\iota_{*}\colon F(K)\hookrightarrow F([0,\infty))

satisfying
\iota_{*}(\delta_{K}(t))=\delta(t)
for
t\in K

(see
[
Wea18
, Proposition 2.2.2]
).
Under the identification
F([0,\infty))\cong L^{1}[0,\infty)
,
the image of
\iota_{*}
is precisely the closed linear span of

\{1_{[0,t]}:t\in K\}
in
L^{1}[0,\infty)
, and the embedding is isometric
because
\iota_{*}
is an isometric embedding and the identification

F([0,\infty))\cong L^{1}[0,\infty)
is an isometric isomorphism.

Under this identification,

\mu_{I}\longmapsto\frac{\mathbf{1}_{I}}{|I|}.

The intervals
I\in\mathcal{G}(K)
are pairwise disjoint. Therefore, for

(c_{I})\in\ell^{1}(\mathcal{G}(K))
,

\left\|\sum_{I}c_{I}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{I}}{|I|}\right\|_{L^{1}}=\sum_{I}|c_{I}|.

Thus
U
is an isometry into
\mathcal{F}(K)
.

It remains to see that the range is all of
\mathcal{F}(K)
. Let
t\in K
. Since

K
has measure zero,

\mathbf{1}_{[0,t]}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in\mathcal{G}(K)\\
I\subset(0,t)\end{subarray}}\mathbf{1}_{I}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in\mathcal{G}(K)\\
I\subset(0,t)\end{subarray}}|I|\frac{\mathbf{1}_{I}}{|I|}

in
L^{1}
, and the coefficients have sum
t<\infty
. Hence

\delta(t)
belongs to the closed span of the
\mu_{I}
. Since the elements

\delta(t)
span a dense subspace of
\mathcal{F}(K)
,
U
is onto.

The norm identity for
\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(K)=\mathcal{F}(K)^{*}
follows by duality, because the
unit ball of
\mathcal{F}(K)
is the unit ball of the
\ell^{1}
-sum generated by the
basis
(\mu_{I})
.
∎

Define the gap
c_{0}
-space

E_{\rm gap}(K):=\left\{f\in\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(K):(\Delta_{I_{j}}f)_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\in c_{0}\right\}.

By Lemma
5.1
, the map

\Gamma:\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(K)\longrightarrow\ell^{\infty}(\mathcal{G}(K)),\qquad\Gamma f=(\Delta_{I}f)_{I\in\mathcal{G}(K)},

is an isometric isomorphism. In particular
E_{\rm gap}(K)
is isometric to

c_{0}(\mathcal{G}(K))
.

Proposition 5.2

(Gap coordinates are
c_{0}
-admissible)
.

The family
(\mu_{I_{j}})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a
c_{0}
-admissible metric quotient
coordinate family for
K
. Consequently,

E_{\rm gap}(K)^{*}\cong\mathcal{F}(K),\qquad E_{\rm gap}(K)^{**}\cong\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(K)

canonically and isometrically.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.1
, the summation map associated with

(\mu_{I_{j}})
is an isometric isomorphism from
\ell^{1}
onto
\mathcal{F}(K)
, hence
in particular a metric quotient map.

Let
f\in\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(K)
and write

\alpha_{j}=\Delta_{I_{j}}f.

For
N\in\mathbb{N}
define a bounded scalar sequence

\alpha^{(N)}_{j}=\begin{cases}\alpha_{j},&j\leq N,\\
0,&j>N.\end{cases}

The sequence
(\alpha^{(N)}_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
belongs to
\ell^{\infty}(G(K))
,
since
\sup_{j}|\alpha^{(N)}_{j}|\leq\sup_{j}|\alpha_{j}|=\|f\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}<\infty
.
Since
\Gamma\colon\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(K)\to\ell^{\infty}(G(K))
is an isometric isomorphism
(Lemma 5.1), there exists a unique
f_{N}\in\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(K)
such that

\Gamma f_{N}=(\alpha^{(N)}_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
, i.e.
\Delta_{I_{j}}f_{N}=\alpha^{(N)}_{j}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
and
\|f_{N}\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}=\|(\alpha^{(N)}_{j})\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\leq\|f\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}
.
Then
f_{N}\in E_{\rm gap}(K)
,
\|f_{N}\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}\leq\|f\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}
, and

\Delta_{I_{j}}f_{N}\longrightarrow\Delta_{I_{j}}f\qquad(j\in\mathbb{N}).

Thus the family
(\mu_{I_{j}})
is
c_{0}
-admissible. The conclusion follows
from Proposition
4.2
.
∎

We now compare the gap space with Dalet’s predual. For a proper pointed
metric space
M
, Dalet considers the space

S(M)=\left\{f\in\operatorname{lip}_{0}(M):\lim_{r\to\infty}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\text{ or }y\notin B(0,r)\\
x\neq y\end{subarray}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{d(x,y)}=0\right\}.

When
M
is bounded, the second condition is void. Dalet proved that, for
countable proper
M
,
\mathcal{F}(M)
is isometric to
S(M)^{*}
and has the metric
approximation property
[
Dal15b
]
. The following result identifies

S(K)
with the gap
c_{0}
-space for countable proper subsets of the real
line.

Theorem 5.3

(Gap
c_{0}
coordinates and Dalet’s predual)
.

Let
K\subset[0,\infty)
be infinite, countable, closed, and contain
0
. Then

E_{\rm gap}(K)=S(K)

isometrically. Consequently,

S(K)^{*}\cong\mathcal{F}(K),\qquad S(K)^{**}\cong\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(K)

canonically and isometrically.

Proof.

Let
f\in\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(K)
and set
\alpha_{I}=\Delta_{I}f
. We shall use the
following elementary identity. If
x<y
are points of
K
, then

f(y)-f(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in\mathcal{G}(K)\\
I\subset(x,y)\end{subarray}}\alpha_{I}|I|,\qquad y-x=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in\mathcal{G}(K)\\
I\subset(x,y)\end{subarray}}|I|.

Both equalities follow from the
L^{1}
representation in Lemma 5.1.
For the first, one writes
f(y)-f(x)=\langle f,\delta_{K}(y)-\delta_{K}(x)\rangle

and expands
\delta_{K}(y)-\delta_{K}(x)=\sum_{I\subset(x,y)}|I|\,\mu_{I}

in
F(K)\cong\ell^{1}(G(K))
(Lemma 5.1).
For the second, note that
K
has Lebesgue measure zero
(it is countable), so
[x,y]
is covered up to a null set
by the disjoint gaps
I\in G(K)
with
I\subset(x,y)
,
giving
y-x=\sum_{I\subset(x,y)}|I|
..

Assume first that
f\in E_{\rm gap}(K)
, so
(\alpha_{I})
belongs to
c_{0}
.
We prove that
f\in S(K)
. Let
\varepsilon>0
and choose a finite set

\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{G}(K)
such that

|\alpha_{I}|<\varepsilon\qquad(I\notin\mathcal{F}).

To prove the little Lipschitz condition, let

\delta<\min\{|I|:I\in\mathcal{F}\}

if
\mathcal{F}\neq\varnothing
, and take any
\delta>0
otherwise. If

x<y
in
K
and
y-x<\delta
, then no interval of
\mathcal{F}
is contained
in
(x,y)
. The identity above gives

\frac{|f(y)-f(x)|}{y-x}\leq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in\mathcal{G}(K)\\
I\subset(x,y)\end{subarray}}|\alpha_{I}|\leq\varepsilon.

Thus
f\in\operatorname{lip}_{0}(K)
.

It remains to prove the vanishing condition at infinity. If
K
is bounded,
there is nothing to prove. Suppose
K
is unbounded. Choose the finite set

\mathcal{F}
so that
|\alpha_{I}|<\varepsilon/2
for
I\notin\mathcal{F}
.
Let

A=1+\max\{b_{I}:I\in\mathcal{F}\}

with
A=1
if
\mathcal{F}=\varnothing
, and put

C=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{F}}|\alpha_{I}||I|.

Choose
r>A
so large that
C/(r-A)<\varepsilon/2
. Let
x<y
in
K
with

y>r
. If
x\geq A
, then
(x,y)
contains no interval of
\mathcal{F}
, and
the quotient is at most
\varepsilon/2
. If
x<A
, then the contribution of
intervals outside
\mathcal{F}
is at most
(\varepsilon/2)(y-x)
, while the
contribution of intervals in
\mathcal{F}
is at most
C
. Hence

\frac{|f(y)-f(x)|}{y-x}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\frac{C}{y-x}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\frac{C}{r-A}<\varepsilon.

Therefore
f\in S(K)
.

Conversely, assume
f\in S(K)
. We prove that
(\alpha_{I})\in c_{0}
. Let

\varepsilon>0
. If
K
is unbounded, choose
r>0
such that

\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|}<\varepsilon

whenever
x\neq y
and
x
or
y
lies outside
B(0,r)
. If
K
is bounded,
choose
r>R_{K}
. Since
f\in\operatorname{lip}_{0}(K)
, choose
\delta>0
such that

0<|x-y|<\delta\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|}<\varepsilon.

If
I=(a_{I},b_{I})
satisfies
b_{I}>r
, then
|\alpha_{I}|<\varepsilon
by the
vanishing condition at infinity. If
b_{I}\leq r
and
|I|<\delta
, then

|\alpha_{I}|<\varepsilon
by the little Lipschitz condition. Hence the only
gaps with
|\alpha_{I}|\geq\varepsilon
are contained in
[0,r]
and have
length at least
\delta
. There are only finitely many such disjoint
intervals. Thus
(\alpha_{I})\in c_{0}
.

We have proved
E_{\rm gap}(K)=S(K)
. The duality and biduality statements
now follow from Proposition
5.2
.
∎

Example 5.4
.

Let

K=\{0\}\cup\left\{\frac{1}{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}\subset[0,1].

The gaps are
(1/(n+1),1/n)
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Theorem
5.3
says
that the Dalet predual is the space of all
f\in\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(K)
such that

\frac{f(1/n)-f(1/(n+1))}{1/n-1/(n+1)}\longrightarrow 0.

Thus the correct
c_{0}
coordinates near the accumulation point are adjacent
gap slopes, not the long molecules
m_{1/n,0}
.

Remark 5.5
.

Theorem
5.3
recovers Dalet’s predual only for countable
proper subsets of the real line. It does not recover Dalet’s theorem for
arbitrary countable proper metric spaces. In general, selecting a

c_{0}
-coordinate family must reflect the accumulation structure of the
space. The gap coordinates provide such a selection in one dimension; for
more general countable proper spaces, this becomes a separate coordinate
selection problem and is not pursued here.

6.
The proper discrete case

We finish by recording the discrete molecular case in the language of the
preceding sections. It is included mainly as a comparison with Dalet’s
predual.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
(M,d,0)
be an infinite pointed proper discrete metric space. Let

\{(p_{n},q_{n}):n\in\mathbb{N}\}
be an enumeration of all ordered pairs of distinct
points of
M
, and set

\mu_{n}=m_{p_{n},q_{n}}=\frac{\delta(p_{n})-\delta(q_{n})}{d(p_{n},q_{n})}.

Define

E_{\rm mol}(M)=\left\{f\in\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(M):(\langle f,\mu_{n}\rangle)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in c_{0}\right\}.

Then

E_{\rm mol}(M)^{*}\cong\mathcal{F}(M),\qquad E_{\rm mol}(M)^{**}\cong\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(M)

canonically and isometrically. Moreover
E_{\rm mol}(M)
coincides with
Dalet’s predual
S(M)
.

Proof.

Since
M
is proper and discrete, closed balls are finite and
M
is
countable. The family of all molecules is a metric quotient coordinate
family: the Arens–Eells description of the free norm says that

\ell^{1}\ni(a_{n})\longmapsto\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}m_{p_{n},q_{n}}\in\mathcal{F}(M)

is a metric quotient map onto
\mathcal{F}(M)
; see, for instance,
[
GK03
,
Wea18
]
.

Every compact subset of
M
is finite. If
h\in\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(M)
has finite
support and
\varepsilon>0
, then only finitely many pairs
(x,y)
satisfy

\frac{|h(x)-h(y)|}{d(x,y)}\geq\varepsilon.

Indeed, at least one of
x,y
must belong to
\operatorname{supp}(h)
, and the other must
lie in a ball of radius
2\|h\|_{\infty}/\varepsilon
around that finite
support. These balls are finite. Hence

\operatorname{Lip}_{0,c}(M)\subset E_{\rm mol}(M)
. Corollary
4.3

therefore gives the two duality assertions.

It remains to identify the space with
S(M)
. The condition

f\in E_{\rm mol}(M)
says that for every
\varepsilon>0
there are only
finitely many ordered pairs
(x,y)
with

\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{d(x,y)}\geq\varepsilon.

This immediately implies the little Lipschitz condition and the vanishing
condition at infinity in Dalet’s definition. Conversely, if
f\in S(M)
,
then large quotients can occur only inside a fixed closed ball and at
scales bounded below. Such a ball is finite, because
M
is proper and
discrete; hence there are only finitely many corresponding pairs. Thus

E_{\rm mol}(M)=S(M)
.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

Dalet’s theorem applies to all countable proper metric spaces, and therefore
already implies that
\mathcal{F}(M)
is a dual space in the proper discrete case.
Proposition
6.1
should not be read as a new duality
theorem in that setting. Its role is to identify Dalet’s predual with the
molecular
c_{0}
-coordinate space and to show how this identification follows
from the scalar criterion.
