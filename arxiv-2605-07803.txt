Global Dynamics and Synchronization of Hodgkin-Huxley-Wilson Neural Networks
Yuncheng You

Abstract
. Hodgkin-Huxley equations as a monumental breakthrough in biological and physiological theory of the 20th century had been distilled into many simplified models to study, typically FitzHugh-Nagumo equations and Hindmarsh-Rose equations, but the model itself not being fully investigated in terms of global and asymptotic dynamics due to its strong nonlinearity and higher dimensionality. In this paper a new model called Hodgkin-Huxley-Wilson neural networks is proposed and investigated. This model captured the essential features of the nonlinearity and the conductances of two dominant ionic current channels of sodium and potassium coupled with the membrane equation in the original Hodgkin-Huxley model. Through uniform and sharp a priori estimates by hard analysis on the solutions of the model equations and the interneuron differencing equations, It is rigorously proved that global solution dynamics are robustly dissipative with a sharp ultimate bound and that complete synchronization of the Hodgkin-Huxley-Wilson neural networks at an exponential convergence rate occurs if the interneuron coupling strength satisfies an explicitly computable threshold condition. The main results are further extended to Caputo fractional memristive Hodgkin-Huxley-Wilson neural networks.

Introduction

All biological neurons especially human neuron networks are extremely complex with marvelous behavior and functions. To study the neuronal dynamics and brain-like spiking neural networks, one can build mathematical models characterizing the key variables of neurons, which are the voltage potential of neuron cell membrane and the salient ions currents. The primary mathematical model of biological neurons is the classical Hodgkin-Huxley equations initiated in
[
1
]
and four more papers
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
based on plausibly equivalent electric circuits and large amount of data from experiments by the British physiologists and Nobel laureates (1963) Alan Lloyd Hodgkin and Andrew Fielding Huxley in 1952 based on the study of the giant axon of squid. Hodgkin-Huxley equations laid down a theoretical foundation for neuroscience
[
6
,
7
]
as well as vast researches of brain-like spiking neural networks.

The original Hodgkin-Huxley neuron model consists of four coupled nonlinear ordinary differential equations describing how the membrane potential
V
of a neuron cell changes and governed by ion channel gating of three variables
x=(m,n,h)
:

(1.1)

\begin{split}C_{m}\frac{dV}{dt}&=I-g_{N_{a}}m^{3}h(V-E_{Na})-g_{K}n^{4}(V-E_{k})-g_{L}(V-E_{L}),\\
\frac{dm}{dt}&=\alpha_{m}(V)(1-m)-\beta_{m}(V)m,\\
\frac{dn}{dt}&=\alpha_{n}(V)(1-n)-\beta_{n}(V)n,\\
\frac{dh}{dt}&=\alpha_{h}(V)(1-h)-\beta_{h}(V)h,\end{split}

where the gating current variables of sodium ions
N_{a}^{+}
denoted by
m(V)
, potassium ions
K^{+}
denoted by
n(V)
, and the other (leakage) ions denoted by
h(V)
can be called sodium, potassium, and leakage activation, respectively. Total external current is denoted by
I
. The positive constant
C_{m}
is the membrane capacitance, without loss of generality which is usually taken as
1\mu F/cm^{2}
.

For the space clamped axons in the original model of Hodgkin-Huxley equations
[
6
]
, the ionic channel conductance parameters are
g_{N_{a}}=120\,ms/cm^{2},g_{K}=36\,ms/cm^{2},g_{L}=0.3\,ms/cm^{2}
. Here
ms/cm^{2}
meaning milliSiemens per
cm^{2}
is a physics unit of electrical conductivity. The Nernst (rest equilibrium) potentials of the three voltage-gated channels are

(1.2)

E_{N_{a}}=50\,mV,\quad E_{K}=-70\,mV,\quad E_{L}=-10\,mV.

The voltage-dependent rates
(\alpha(V),\beta(V))
reflect the speed of depolarization and repolarization gating. They were originally set by experiments and curve-fitting as

(1.3)

\begin{split}\alpha_{m}(V)&=\frac{0.1(25-V)}{\exp{(\frac{25-V}{10})-1}},\qquad\beta_{m}(V)=4\exp{\left(-\frac{V}{18}\right)},\\[4.0pt]
\alpha_{n}(V)&=\frac{0.01\,(10-V)}{\exp{(1-V/10)}-1},\quad\beta_{n}(V)=0.125\,\exp{\left(-\frac{V}{80}\right)},\\[3.0pt]
\alpha_{h}(V)&=0.07\exp{\left(-\frac{V}{20}\right)},\qquad\beta_{h}(V)=\frac{1}{\exp{(\frac{30-V}{10})+1}}.\end{split}

While the original Hodgkin-Huxley neuron model was built up using the empirical curve fitting and the voltage-clamp experiments data for the rate constants in
\alpha(V)
and
\beta(V)
functions of each ion gating channel
[
7
]
, the modern description based on statistical mechanics presents the rates of gate transition from closed to open and vise versa in terms of probability so that the ion equations can take the following form, say, for the sodium channel (similarly for the potassium and leakage channels),

(1.4)

\frac{dm}{dt}=\alpha_{m}(V)(1-m)-\beta_{m}(V)m=(m_{\infty}(V)-m)/\tau_{m}(V)

where

(1.5)

m_{\infty}(V)=\frac{\alpha_{m}(V)}{\alpha_{m}(V)+\beta_{m}(V)}=\frac{1}{1+\exp{(-\lambda_{s}(V-V_{f}))}},\;\;\tau_{m}(V)=\frac{1}{\alpha_{m}(V)+\beta_{m}(V)}.

Here the sigmoidal function
m_{\infty}(V)
is a standard Boltzmann distribution function with two parameters depending on the ion channel:
V_{f}
is the half-activation voltage to open the ion gate in half and the slope factor
\lambda_{s}
which determines the sensitivity of this ion gating to voltage change.

Neuronal signals are electrical pulses called spikes or the action potential transmitting through the membrane of axon in neuron cells. Neuron bursting in alternating phases of rapid firing spikes and then quiescence constitutes a mechanism to pace-setting and modulate for brain or nervous system functionalities and communication. Neurons coordinate actions through electrical or chemical synapses at the dendrites or called gap junctions in neuroscience. Since the axon of neuron cell is a long branch for propagating bioelectrical signals and neurons are immersed in aqueous chemical solutions with diffusive charged ions, it is meaningful to consider distributed PDE models with the spacial variable involved in neuron dynamics. It remains widely open after more than 70 years to make a substantial breakthrough and establish a rigorous analytic theory on global dynamics and asymptotic synchronization for the four-dimensional highly nonlinear Hodgkin-Huxley equations and for Hodgkin-Huxley neural networks.

In this paper we shall formulate and investigate a new model of biologically inspired neural networks, which we call Hodgkin-Huxley-Wilson (briefly HHW) neural networks. This new model of neuron dynamical equations is a simplification of the Hodgkin-Huxley equations but different from the FitzHugh-Nagumo equations (FHN) and Hindmarsh-Rose equations (HRE), which are conductance-independent neuron models without characterizing neurons physiological behavior

We shall analyze global dynamics and rigorously prove the complete exponential synchronization of the Hodgkin-Huxley-Wilson neural networks if the network coupling strength satisfies an explicitly computable threshold condition. We will also generalize the main results to fractional memristive Hodgkin-Huxley-Wilson neural networks presented by Caputo-fractional differential equations.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 will be the formulation of Hodgkin-Huxley-Wilson neural networks, the new model we proposed, in a mathematical framework together with the quantitative definition of complete synchronization and some preliminaries. In Section 3 we show that global dynamics of the solutions of the model equations is dissipative in terms of the existence of an absorbing set and the global attractor. In Section 4 we shall prove the main result on the main topic of complete synchronization of the Hodgkin-Huxley-Wilson neural networks based on
a priori

In Section 5 we shall the extend the global dynamics and synchronization results to fractional memristive Hodgkin-Huxley-Wilson neural networks. Section 6 will be conclusions to summarize the contributions and future outlooks.

2.
Formulation of Hodgkin-Huxley-Wilson Neural Networks

Consider a mathematical model of biological neural networks composed of
n
coupled neurons, denoted by
\mathcal{NW}=\{\mathcal{N}_{i}:i=1,2,\cdots,n\}
, where
n\geq 2
is a positive integer. Each neuron
\mathcal{N}_{i},1\leq i\leq n
, is presented by the following Hodgkin-Huxley-Wilson equations:

(2.1)

\begin{split}\frac{dV_{i}}{dt}&=-m_{\infty}(V_{i})(V_{i}-E_{N_{a}})-g_{K}R_{i}(V_{i})(V_{i}-E_{K})+J+\sum_{j=1}^{n}P(V_{j}-V_{i}),\\
\frac{dR_{i}}{dt}&=\frac{1}{\tau_{K}}\left(-R_{i}+R_{\infty}(V_{i})\right),\quad\text{for}\;\;t>0,\;\;1\leq i\leq n.\end{split}

Here the exponentially nonlinear conductances in the original Hodgkin-Huxley model (which were postulated though by curve fitting in electrical circuit experiments emulating excitable neuron cells rather than biophysical reasoning) are well approximated by quadratic polynomials:

(2.2)

m_{\infty}(s)=a_{0}+a_{1}s+a_{2}s^{2},\quad R_{\infty}(s)=\frac{H}{1+e^{-\lambda(s-E_{K})}},\quad s\in\mathbb{R}.

The variable
V_{i}(t)
is the electrical potential on the membrane of the
i
th neuron
\mathcal{N}_{i}
, for
1\leq i\leq m
. The variable
R_{i}(t)
is the recovery
K^{+}
ionic current in the
i
th neuron. The initial states of the ODE system (
2.1
) in the state space
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
will be denoted by

(2.3)

V^{0}=\{V_{i}^{0}=V_{i}(0):1\leq i\leq n\}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,\;R^{0}=\{R_{i}^{0}=R_{i}(0):1\leq i\leq n\}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

We make the following generic assumption on the parameters in the model (
2.1
) of Hodgkin-Huxley-Wilson neural networks:

(2.4)

E_{N_{a}},\,g_{K},\,P,\,a_{0},\,a_{2},\,H,\,\tau_{K}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}=(0,\infty),\quad E_{K}<0,\;\;\text{and}\quad J,\,a_{1},\,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.

In the original paper by Hugh R. Wilson
[
11
]
, the above parameters were set based on the scaled units of
mV/100
:

\displaystyle a_{0}=17.8,\;\;a_{1}=47.6,\quad a_{2}=33.8,\quad g_{K}=26\,ms/cm^{2},

\displaystyle E_{N_{a}}=0.5\,(=50\,mV),\quad E_{K}=-0.95\,(-95\,mV),\quad H=1\quad\tau_{K}=4.2\,ms.

The main equation of the membrane potential
V_{i}(t)
in this model (
2.1
) depends on the two most important ionic currents of
N_{a}^{+}
and
K^{+}
along with the stimulating current
J
. For simplicity we set the membrane capacitance
C_{m}=1
as mentioned for (
1.1
). The coefficient
P>0
is called the network coupling strength. The
N_{a}^{+}
ionic channel is called activation and its conductance is set by
m_{\infty}(V_{i})
sufficiently fast
[
51
]
. The second equation in (
2.1
) describes the potassium
K^{+}
gating channel also called the recovery channel which depends on the equilibrium state
R_{\infty}(V_{i})
and the time constant
\tau_{K}
. Note that
N_{a}^{+}
conductance is not involved in recovery (depolarization) ion channel due to the observations that sodium ion currents do not inactivate in human and mammalian neocortical neurons
[
52
,
53
]
. It is important to notice that potassium channel time constants
\tau_{K}
and rest current
R_{\infty}(V_{i})
in neuron models can vary widely depending on several different types of
K^{+}
channels
[
7
,
11
,
47
,
54
]
.
R_{\infty}(V_{i})
may take different nonlinear forms and
\tau_{K}
may be voltage-dependent. For Hodgkin-Huxley
K^{+}
channel called the
n
-gate in (
1.1
) the time constant
\tau_{K}
is in the order of
1ms
to
6ms
.

This two-dimensional model (
2.1
) of Hodgkin-Huxley-Wilson neural networks can certainly include a general leakage channel as in (
1.1
) and/or more ionic (such as
C_{a}^{2+}
and
Cl^{-}
) channel equations to build a multi-dimensional system. A remark is that the analytical proof methodology in this work can be adapted and applied to the extended models as well.

A quantitative definition of complete synchronization for biological neural networks is stated below.

Definition 2.1
.

A biological neural network presented by a mathematical model of differential equations in a state space
Z
such as the HHW neural networks
\mathcal{NW}
is said to be completely synchronizable if there exists a sufficient threshold condition on the network coupling strength parameter
\mathcal{P}
such that if this threshold condition is satisfied then the network synchronizing degree

(2.5)

\deg_{s}\,(\mathcal{NW})=\sup_{(V^{0},R^{0})\in Z}\left\{\max_{1\leq i<j\leq m}\left\{\limsup_{t\to\infty}\,(|V_{i}(t)-V_{j}(t)|+|R_{i}(t)-R_{j}(t)|)\right\}\right\}=0,

If the limsup convergence in (
2.5
) admits a uniform exponential convergence rate, then it is called complete exponential synchronization.

As preliminaries for global dynamics of differential equations
[
55
,
56
]
in the rest sections, a general dynamical system only for time
t\geq 0
can be called semiflow. For a semiflow denoted by
\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
on a Banach space
\mathscr{X}
, a bounded subset
B^{*}\subset\mathscr{X}
is called absorbing set if for any given bounded set
B
in the space
\mathscr{X}
there is a finite time
T_{B}\geq 0
such that
S(t)B\subset B^{*}
for all
t>T_{B}
. A semiflow is called dissipative if there exists an absorbing set in the state space
\mathscr{X}
, which also implies the existence of a global attractor
\mathscr{A}
if the state space
\mathscr{X}
is finite dimensional. Moreover, the global attarctor
\mathscr{A}
is the
\omega
-limit set of any absorbing set.

Young’s inequality is instrumental and will be used in differential inequality management. It states that for any positive numbers
x
and
y
, if
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
and
p>1,q>1
, then

(2.6)

x\,y\leq\frac{1}{p}\varepsilon x^{p}+\frac{1}{q}C(\varepsilon,p)\,y^{q}\leq\varepsilon x^{p}+C(\varepsilon,p)\,y^{q},

where
C(\varepsilon,p)=\varepsilon^{-q/p}
and the constant
\varepsilon>0
can be arbitrarily given.

3.
Dissipative Dynamics and Global Attractor

We start with the existence and uniqueness of global solutions in time for the initial value problem (
2.1
)-(
2.3
) of Hodgkin-Huxley-Wilson neural network
\mathcal{NW}
. Through the approach of
a priori
estimates we can tackle the strong nonlinearity in the model equations and prove the dissipative global dynamics of this neural network system.

Theorem 3.1
.

For any given initial state
(V^{0},R^{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
, there exists a unique global solution in time,

V(t;V^{0})=\{V_{i}(t;V_{i}^{0}):1\leq i\leq n\},\;\;R(t;R^{0})=\{R_{i}(t;R_{i}^{0}):1\leq i\leq n\},\;\,t\in[0,\infty),

for the initial value problem (
2.1
)-(
2.3
) of Hodgkin-Huxley-Wilson neural network
\mathcal{NW}
.

Proof.

Since the nonlinear functions on the right-hand sides of the two differential equations in (
2.1
) are locally Lipschitz continuous, there exists a unique local solution
(V(t),R(t))
in time to the initial value problem (
2.1
)-(
2.3
). We want to show that that the maximal existence interval of every local solution is
[0,\infty)
.

Multiply the
V_{i}
-equation in (
2.1
) by
V_{i}(t)
for
t\in[0,T_{max})
, which is the maximal existence interval of that local solution and
T_{max}
may depend on the initial state. Then sum them altogether for all
1\leq i\leq n
. By (
2.2
) we get

(3.1)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}^{2}(t)\leq\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}^{2}(t)+\frac{1}{2}P\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(V_{i}-V_{i})^{2}\\
=&\,\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[-m_{\infty}(V_{i})(V_{i}-E_{N_{a}})V_{i}-g_{K}R_{i}(V_{i}-E_{K})V_{i}+JV_{i}(t)\right]\\
=&\,\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[-(a_{0}+a_{1}V_{i}+a_{2}V_{i}^{2})(V_{i}-E_{N_{a}})V_{i}-g_{K}R_{i}(V_{i}-E_{K})V_{i}+JV_{i}\right]\\
=&\,-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[a_{0}V_{i}^{2}+a_{1}V_{i}^{3}+a_{2}V_{i}^{4}+g_{K}R_{i}V_{i}^{2}\right]\\
&\,+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[E_{N_{a}}(a_{0}V_{i}+a_{1}V_{i}^{2}+a_{2}V_{i}^{3})+g_{K}R_{i}E_{K}V_{i}+JV_{i}\right],\;\;t\in[0,T_{max}),\end{split}

in which the double summation term on the right-hand side of the first inequality comes from

-P\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left[(V_{j}-V_{i})V_{i}+(V_{i}-V_{j})V_{j}\right]=P\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(V_{i}-V_{j})^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\,P\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(V_{i}-V_{i})^{2}.

Then multiply the
R_{i}
-equation in (
2.1
) with
R_{i}(t)
. Since
0<R_{\infty}(V_{i})<H
according to (
2.2
), we have

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}R_{i}^{2}(t)=\frac{1}{\tau_{K}}(-R_{i}^{2}+R_{\infty}(V_{i})R_{i})\leq\frac{1}{\tau_{K}}(-R_{i}^{2}+H|R_{i}|)\leq\frac{1}{2\tau_{K}}(-R_{i}^{2}(t)+H^{2})

so that

(3.2)

\frac{d}{dt}R_{i}^{2}(t)\leq\frac{1}{\tau_{K}}(-R_{i}^{2}(t)+H^{2}),\quad t\in[0,T_{max}).

By the Gronwall differential inequality, (
3.2
) implies that

(3.3)

R_{i}^{2}(t)\leq R_{i}^{2}(0)\,e^{-t/\tau_{K}}+H^{2}\leq R_{i}^{2}(0)+H^{2},\quad t\in[0,T_{max}).

In order to treat the differential inequality (
3.1
) term by term, we can appropriately use the Young’s inequality (
2.6
) to obtain

(3.4)

\begin{split}&(E_{N_{a}}a_{0}+g_{K}R_{i}E_{K}+J_{i})V_{i}\leq\frac{1}{2}a_{0}V_{i}^{2}+\frac{1}{2a_{0}}(E_{N_{a}}a_{0}+g_{K}R_{i}E_{K}+J)^{2},\\[2.0pt]
&(E_{N_{a}}a_{1}+g_{K}R_{i})V_{i}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}a_{2}V_{i}^{4}+\frac{1}{2a_{2}}(E_{N_{a}}a_{1}+g_{K}R_{i})^{2},\\[2.0pt]
&(a_{1}+E_{N_{a}}a_{2})V_{i}^{3}\leq\frac{1}{2}a_{2}V_{i}^{4}+\frac{(a_{1}+E_{N_{a}}a_{2})^{4}}{(a_{2}/2)^{3}},\quad 1\leq i\leq n.\end{split}

Substitute (
3.4
) in the inequality (
3.1
). We have

(3.5)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}^{2}(t)\leq\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}^{2}(t)+\frac{1}{2}P\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(V_{i}-V_{i})^{2}\\
=&\,-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[a_{0}V_{i}^{2}+a_{1}V_{i}^{3}+a_{2}V_{i}^{4}+g_{K}R_{i}V_{i}^{2}\right]\\
&\,+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[E_{N_{a}}(a_{0}V_{i}+a_{1}V_{i}^{2}+a_{2}V_{i}^{3})+g_{K}R_{i}E_{K}V_{i}+JV_{i}\right]\\
\leq&\,-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{0}V_{i}(t)^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2a_{0}}(E_{N_{a}}a_{0}+g_{K}R_{i}(t)E_{K}+J)^{2}\\
&\,+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\frac{1}{2a_{2}}(E_{N_{a}}a_{1}+g_{K}R_{i}(t))^{2}+\frac{(a_{1}+E_{N_{a}}a_{2})^{4}}{(a_{2}/2)^{3}}\right],\quad t\in[0,T_{max}),\end{split}

after plus and minus cancellations of the
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{2}V_{i}^{4}
terms. Then by the bounding estimates (
3.3
) and the non-negativity of the cross-coupling terms on the right-hand side of the first inequality in (
3.5
), it follows that

(3.6)

\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}^{2}(t)\leq-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{0}V_{i}(t)^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{a_{0}}\left(|E_{N_{a}}|a_{0}+g_{K}|R_{i}(t)||E_{K}|+|J|\right)^{2}\\
&+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\frac{1}{a_{2}}(|E_{N_{a}}a_{1}|+g_{K}|R_{i}(t)|)^{2}+\frac{2(a_{1}+E_{N_{a}}a_{2})^{4}}{(a_{2}/2)^{3}}\right]\\
\leq&\,-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{0}V_{i}^{2}(t)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{a_{0}}\left(|E_{N_{a}}|a_{0}+g_{K}\sqrt{R_{i}^{2}(0)+H^{2}}\,|E_{K}|+|J|\right)^{2}\\
&\,+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\frac{1}{a_{2}}\left(|E_{N_{a}}a_{1}|+g_{K}\sqrt{R_{i}^{2}(0)+H^{2}}\right)^{2}+\frac{2(a_{1}+E_{N_{a}}a_{2})^{4}}{(a_{2}/2)^{3}}\right],\;\;t\in[0,T_{max}).\end{split}

Denote the positive constant on the right-hand side of (
3.6
) by

(3.7)

\begin{split}M(R^{0})&\,=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{a_{0}}\left(|E_{N_{a}}|a_{0}+g_{K}\sqrt{R_{i}^{2}(0)+H^{2}}\,|E_{K}|+|J|\right)^{2}\\
&\,+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\frac{1}{a_{2}}\left(|E_{N_{a}}a_{1}|+g_{K}\sqrt{R_{i}^{2}(0)+H^{2}}\right)^{2}+\frac{2(a_{1}+E_{N_{a}}a_{2})^{4}}{(a_{2}/2)^{3}}\right],\end{split}

which depends on the initial data
R^{0}
shown in (
2.3
). Then the differential inequality (
3.6
) simply written as

\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}^{2}(t)\leq-a_{0}\sum_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}(t)^{2}+M(R^{0}),\quad t\in[0,T_{max}),

shows that any local solution of the initial value problem (
2.1
)-(
2.3
) admits a finite bound which depends on the initial data
(V^{0},R^{0})
but is uniform in time
0\leq t<T_{max}
, namely,

(3.8)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(V_{i}^{2}(t)+R_{i}^{2}(t)\right)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[(V_{i}^{0})^{2}e^{-a_{0}t}+(R_{i}^{0})^{2}e^{-t/\tau_{K}}\right]+\frac{1}{a_{0}}M(R^{0})+nH^{2}.

It means that all the solutions of the initial value problem (
2.1
)-(
2.3
) will not blow up at any finite time. Therefore, for any initial state
(V^{0},R^{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
, there exists a unique global solution in time, the maximal existence interval is
[0,T_{max})=[0,\infty)
. The proof is completed.
∎

Based on the global existence of solutions shown in Theorem
3.1
, the solution semiflow
\{S(t):(V^{0},R^{0})\longmapsto(V(t;V^{0}),R(t;R^{0})\}_{t\geq 0}
on the state space
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
is called the Hodgkin-Huxley-Wilson (HHW) neural network semiflow.

Theorem 3.2
.

There exists a bounded absorbing set for the Hodgkin-Huxley-Wilson neural network semiflow
\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
in the state space
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
, which is the bounded ball

(3.9)

B^{*}=\{(v,r)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}:\|(v,r)\|^{2}\leq G\}.

Here the uniform constant

(3.10)

\begin{split}G&\,=1+nH^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{a_{0}^{2}}\left(|E_{N_{a}}|a_{0}+g_{K}\sqrt{1+H^{2}}\,|E_{K}|+|J|\right)^{2}\\
&\,+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\frac{1}{a_{0}a_{2}}\left(|E_{N_{a}}a_{1}|+g_{K}\sqrt{1+H^{2}}\right)^{2}+\frac{2(a_{1}+E_{N_{a}}a_{2})^{4}}{a_{0}(a_{2}/2)^{3}}\right]\end{split}

is independent of any initial state
(V^{0},R^{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
.
\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is a dissipative dynamical system and there exists a global attractor
\mathscr{A}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2n}
for this semiflow.

Proof.

From (
3.7
) and the estimate (
3.8
) of global solutions for this Hodgkin-Huxley-Wilson neural network
\mathcal{NW}
and more precisely with
R_{i}^{2}(0)
replaced by
R_{i}^{2}(0)e^{-t/\tau_{K}}
therein due to (
3.3
), since
e^{-a_{0}t}\to 0
and
e^{-t/\tau_{K}}
as
t\to\infty
, we can assert that

(3.11)

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\left\|\left(V(t),R(t)\right)\right\|^{2}=\limsup_{t\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(V_{i}^{2}(t)+R_{i}^{2}(t)\right)<G

for all the solutions of (
2.1
) with any initial state
(V^{0},R^{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
. Moreover, (
3.8
) also shows that for any given bounded set
B=\{(v,r)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}:\|(v,r)\|^{2}\leq L\}
in the state space, there exists a finite time

(3.12)

T_{B}\geq\max\left\{\frac{1}{a_{0}},\,\tau_{K}\right\}\log^{+}(L/G)\geq 0

such that all the solution trajectories started at the initial time
t=0
from inside that set
B
will permanently enter the bounded ball
B^{*}
shown in (
3.9
) when
t>T_{B}
. Therefore, the bounded ball
B^{*}
is an absorbing set for the semiflow
\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
so that this Hodgkin-Huxley-Wilson neural network semiflow is a dissipative dynamical system..

Finally, since the state space
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
is a finite-dimensional Hilbert space, the closed bounded absorbing set
B^{*}
is a compact set. According to
[
55
, Theorem 1.1]
, there exists a global attractor

\mathscr{A}=\Omega(B^{*})=\bigcap_{\tau\geq 0}\;\overline{\bigcup_{t\geq\tau}S(t)B^{*}}

which is the
\Omega
-limit set of an absorbing set for this semiflow.
∎

4.
Complete Exponential Synchronization of HHW Neural Networks

In this section we shall prove the main result on complete synchronization of the Hodgkin-Huxley-Wilson neural networks described by the proposed model (
2.1
).
For this HHW neural network
\mathcal{NW}
with the assumptions (
2.4
), we define the interneuron gap function between any two neurons
\mathcal{N}_{i}
and
\mathcal{N}_{j}
to be

\displaystyle U_{ij}(t)=V_{i}(t)-V_{j}(t),\quad\Pi_{ij}=R_{i}(t)-R_{j}(t),\quad\text{for}\;\;1\leq i,j\leq n.

The differencing equations for
U_{ij}(t)
and
\Pi_{ij}(t)
are

(4.1)

\begin{split}\frac{dU_{ij}}{dt}=&\,-(m_{\infty}(V_{i})-m_{\infty}(V_{j}))(V_{i}-E_{N_{a}})-m_{\infty}(V_{j})U_{ij}\\
&\,-g_{K}(R_{i}(V_{i})-R_{j}(V_{j}))(V_{i}-E_{K})-g_{K}R_{j}(V_{j})U_{ij}-nPU_{ij},\\
\frac{d\Pi_{ij}}{dt}=&\,\frac{1}{\tau_{K}}\left(-\Pi_{ij}+R_{\infty}(V_{i})-R_{\infty}(V_{j})\right),\quad\text{for}\;\;t>0,\;\;1\leq i,j\leq n,\end{split}

where the coupling term
\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}P[(V_{\ell}-V_{i})-(V_{\ell}-V_{j})]=-P\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}(V_{i}-V_{j})=-nPU_{ij}
.

Now we present and prove the main result of this paper.

Theorem 4.1
.

The Hodgkin-Huxley-Wilson neural network
\mathcal{NW}
described by the model (
2.1
)-(
2.2
) with the assumptions (
2.4
) is exponentially synchronizable. If the threshold condition

(4.2)

P>P^{*}=\max\left\{0,\;\frac{1}{n}\left[Q-\left(a_{0}+\frac{1}{2\tau_{K}}\lambda^{2}H^{2}\right)\right]\right\}

is satisfied by the network coupling strength, where the positive constant
Q
shown in (
4.10
) is independent of initial states, then the Hodgkin-Huxley-Wilson neural network
\mathcal{NW}
is completely synchronized at a uniform exponential convergence rate

(4.3)

\mu(P)=\min\left\{\frac{1}{2\tau_{K}},\left[a_{0}+\frac{1}{2\tau_{K}}\lambda^{2}H^{2}+nP-Q\right]\right\}>0.

Proof.

We denote
U_{ij}(t)
by
U(t)
and
\Pi_{ij}(t)
by
\Pi(t)
for any given indices
(i,j),1\leq i\neq j\leq n
, for convenience. The proof will go through three steps.

Step 1. Multiply the
\Pi_{ij}
-equation in (
4.1
) by
\Pi_{ij}(t),1\leq i\neq j\leq n
. We get

(4.4)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\Pi^{2}(t)=\frac{1}{\tau_{K}}\left[-\Pi^{2}(t)+\Pi(t)(R_{\infty}(V_{i})-R_{\infty}(V_{j}))\right]\\
=&\,\frac{1}{\tau_{K}}\left[-\Pi^{2}(t)+\Pi(t)(V_{i}(t)-V_{j}(t))\frac{\lambda He^{-\lambda(\xi-E_{K})}}{(1+e^{-\lambda(\xi-E_{K})})^{2}}\right]\\
\leq&\,\frac{1}{\tau_{K}}\left[-\Pi^{2}(t)+\lambda H\,\Pi(t)U(t)\right],\quad t>0,\end{split}

where the mean value theorem is used and
\xi
is between
V_{i}(t)
and
V_{j}(t)
. Then multiply the
U_{ij}
-equation in (
4.1
) by
U_{ij}(t)
to get

(4.5)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}&\,\frac{d}{dt}\,U^{2}=-(m_{\infty}(V_{i})-m_{\infty}(V_{j}))(V_{i}-E_{N_{a}})U-m_{\infty}(V_{j})U^{2}\\[3.0pt]
&\,-g_{K}(R_{i}(V_{i})-R_{j}(V_{j}))(V_{i}-E_{K})U-g_{K}R_{j}(V_{j})U^{2}-nPU^{2}\\[6.0pt]
=&\,-(a_{1}U+a_{2}U(V_{i}+V_{j}))(V_{i}-E_{N_{a}})U-(a_{0}+a_{1}V_{j}+a_{2}V_{j}^{2})U^{2}\\
&\,-g_{K}\frac{\lambda He^{-\lambda(\eta-E_{K})}}{(1+e^{-\lambda(\eta-E_{K})})^{2}}(V_{i}-E_{K})U^{2}-g_{K}R_{j}(V_{j})U^{2}-nPU^{2}\\[2.0pt]
\leq&\,-(a_{0}+a_{2}(V_{i}^{2}+V_{j}^{2}))U^{2}+\left[a_{1}V_{j}+a_{2}E_{N_{a}}V_{i}+(E_{N_{a}}-V_{i})(a_{1}+a_{2}V_{j})\right]U^{2}\\[6.0pt]
&\,+g_{K}\lambda H|E_{K}-V_{i}|U^{2}-g_{K}R_{j}(V_{j})U^{2}-nPU^{2},\quad t>0,\end{split}

in which the signs of constants
g_{K},E_{N_{a}},E_{K}
are used as well as the mean value theorem for
R_{\infty}(V_{i})-R_{\infty}(V_{j})
with
\eta
between
V_{i}(t)
and
V_{j}(t)
.

Step 2. We now take the approach of grouping estimates to discover the key differencing inequality of the gap functions among the neurons of the entire neural network
\mathcal{NW}
. Add up the inequalities (
4.4
) and (
4.5
). We obtain

(4.6)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}&\,\frac{d}{dt}(U^{2}+\Pi^{2})\leq\frac{1}{\tau_{K}}\left(-\Pi^{2}+\lambda H\,\Pi\,U\right)\\
&\,-(a_{0}+a_{2}(V_{i}^{2}+V_{j}^{2}))U^{2}+\left[a_{1}V_{j}+a_{2}E_{N_{a}}V_{i}+(E_{N_{a}}-V_{i})(a_{1}+a_{2}V_{j})\right]U^{2}\\[5.0pt]
&\,+g_{K}\lambda H|E_{K}-V_{i}|U^{2}-g_{K}R_{j}(V_{j})U^{2}-nPU^{2}\\
=&\,\frac{1}{\tau_{K}}\left(-\Pi^{2}+\lambda H\,\Pi\,U\right)\\
&\,-(a_{0}+a_{2}(V_{i}^{2}+V_{i}V_{j}+V_{j}^{2}))U^{2}+\left[a_{1}V_{j}+a_{2}E_{N_{a}}(V_{i}+V_{j})+a_{1}(E_{N_{a}}-V_{i})\right]U^{2}\\[5.0pt]
&\,+g_{K}\lambda H|E_{K}-V_{i}|U^{2}-g_{K}R_{j}(V_{j})U^{2}-nPU^{2}\\
\leq&\,\frac{1}{\tau_{K}}\left(-\Pi^{2}+\lambda H\,\Pi\,U\right)\\
&\,-(a_{0}+a_{2}(V_{i}^{2}+V_{i}V_{j}+V_{j}^{2}))U^{2}+\left[a_{1}(E_{N_{a}}-V_{i}+V_{j})+a_{2}E_{N_{a}}(V_{i}+V_{j})\right]U^{2}\\[6.0pt]
&\,+g_{K}\lambda H|E_{K}-V_{i}|U^{2}-g_{K}R_{j}(V_{j})U^{2}-nPU^{2}\\
=&\,-\left[\frac{1}{\tau_{k}}\Pi^{2}+\left(a_{0}+a_{2}(V_{i}^{2}+V_{i}V_{j}+V_{j}^{2}\right)+nP)U^{2}\right]+\frac{1}{\tau_{K}}\lambda H\Pi\,U\\
&\,+\left[a_{1}(E_{N_{a}}-V_{i}+V_{j})+a_{2}E_{N_{a}}(V_{i}+V_{j})+g_{K}\lambda H(|E_{K}|+|V_{i}|)-g_{K}R_{j}(V_{j})\right]U^{2}.\end{split}

To handle the above differential inequality (
4.6
), by Cauchy inequality and (
3.3
) we can deduce that

(4.7)

\begin{split}&V_{i}^{2}+V_{i}V_{j}+V_{j}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\,(V_{i}^{2}+V_{j}^{2}),\\[3.0pt]
&\frac{1}{\tau_{K}}\lambda H\,\Pi(t)U(t)\leq\frac{1}{2\tau_{K}}\Pi^{2}(t)+\frac{1}{2\tau_{K}}\lambda^{2}H^{2}U^{2}(t),\\[3.0pt]
&a_{1}(-V_{i}+V_{j})\leq\frac{6a_{1}^{2}}{a_{2}}+\frac{1}{6}\,a_{2}\,(V_{i}^{2}+V_{j}^{2}),\\[3.0pt]
&a_{2}E_{N_{a}}(V_{i}+V_{j})\leq\frac{6a_{2}}{E_{N_{a}}^{2}}+\frac{1}{6}a_{2}(V_{i}^{2}+V_{j}^{2}),\\[3.0pt]
&\,g_{K}\lambda H|V_{i}|\leq\frac{6}{a_{2}}(g_{K}\lambda H)^{2}+\frac{1}{6}\,a_{2}\,V_{i}^{2},\\[3.0pt]
-&\,g_{K}R_{j}(V_{j})\leq g_{K}\sqrt{R_{i}^{2}(0)e^{-t/\tau_{K}}+H^{2}}\leq g_{K}(|R_{i}(0)|e^{-t/(2\tau_{K})}+H).\end{split}

Substitute (
4.7
) in the last step of the inequality (
4.6
). Then we reach the following time-varying Gronwall-type differential inequality

(4.8)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}(U^{2}(t)+\Pi^{2}(t))\\[2.0pt]
\leq&\,-\left[\frac{1}{\tau_{k}}\Pi^{2}+\left(a_{0}+a_{2}(V_{i}^{2}+V_{i}V_{j}+V_{j}^{2}\right)-\left(a_{1}E_{N_{a}}+g_{K}\lambda H|E_{K}|\right)+nP)U^{2}\right]\\
&\,+\frac{1}{\tau_{K}}\lambda H\Pi\,U+\left[a_{1}(-V_{i}+V_{j})+a_{2}E_{N_{a}}(V_{i}+V_{j})+g_{K}\lambda H|V_{i}|-g_{K}R_{j}(V_{j})\right]U^{2}\\
\leq&\,-\left[\frac{1}{\tau_{k}}\Pi^{2}+\left(a_{0}+\frac{1}{2}a_{2}(V_{i}^{2}+V_{j}^{2})\right)U^{2}-\left(a_{1}E_{N_{a}}+g_{K}\lambda H|E_{K}|\right)+nP)U^{2}\right]\\
&\,+\frac{1}{2\tau_{K}}\Pi^{2}+\frac{1}{2\tau_{K}}\lambda^{2}H^{2}U^{2}+\left[\frac{1}{6}\,a_{2}\,(V_{i}^{2}+V_{j}^{2})+\frac{1}{6}a_{2}(V_{i}^{2}+V_{j}^{2})+\frac{1}{6}\,a_{2}\,V_{i}^{2}\right]U^{2}\\
&\,+g_{K}(|R_{i}(0)|e^{-t/(2\tau_{K})}+H)U^{2}+6\left[\frac{a_{1}^{2}}{a_{2}}+\frac{a_{2}}{E_{N_{a}}^{2}}+\frac{1}{a_{2}}(g_{K}\lambda H)^{2}\right]U^{2}\\
\leq&\,-\left[\frac{1}{2\tau_{k}}\Pi^{2}+\left(a_{0}+\frac{1}{2\tau_{K}}\lambda^{2}H^{2}+nP\right)U^{2}\right]+g_{K}\left(|R_{i}(0)|e^{-t/(2\tau_{K})}+H\right)U^{2}\\
&\,+\left(a_{1}E_{N_{a}}+g_{K}\lambda H|E_{K}|\right)U^{2}+6\left[\frac{a_{1}^{2}}{a_{2}}+\frac{a_{2}}{E_{N_{a}}^{2}}+\frac{1}{a_{2}}(g_{K}\lambda H)^{2}\right]U^{2},\quad t>0,\end{split}

where we see the cancellation of the terms

-\frac{1}{2}a_{2}(V_{i}^{2}+V_{j}^{2})+\left[\frac{1}{6}\,a_{2}\,(V_{i}^{2}+V_{j}^{2})+\frac{1}{6}a_{2}(V_{i}^{2}+V_{j}^{2})+\frac{1}{6}\,a_{2}\,V_{i}^{2}\right]\leq 0.

Obviously there exists a finite time
T_{0}>0
depending on the initial state
(V^{0},R^{0})
of a solution such that
|R_{i}(0)|e^{-t/(2\tau_{K})}\leq 1
for any
t>T_{0}
. Thus we have

(4.9)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}(U^{2}(t)+\Pi^{2}(t))\\
\leq&\,-\left[\frac{1}{2\tau_{k}}\Pi^{2}(t)+\left(a_{0}+\frac{1}{2\tau_{K}}\lambda^{2}H^{2}+nP\right)U^{2}(t)\right]+g_{K}(1+H)U^{2}(t)\\
&\,+\left(a_{1}E_{N_{a}}+g_{K}\lambda H|E_{K}|\right)U^{2}(t)+6\left[\frac{a_{1}^{2}}{a_{2}}+\frac{a_{2}}{E_{N_{a}}^{2}}+\frac{1}{a_{2}}(g_{K}\lambda H)^{2}\right]U^{2}(t)\\
\leq&\,-\left[\frac{1}{2\tau_{k}}\Pi^{2}(t)+\left(a_{0}+\frac{1}{2\tau_{K}}\lambda^{2}H^{2}+nP\right)U^{2}(t)\right]\\
&\,+\left[g_{K}(1+H)+|a_{1}E_{N_{a}}|+g_{K}\lambda H|E_{K}|+\frac{6a_{1}^{2}}{a_{2}}+\frac{6a_{2}}{E_{N_{a}}^{2}}+\frac{6}{a_{2}}(g_{K}\lambda H)^{2}\right]U^{2}(t),\;\,t>T_{0}.\end{split}

Denote the sum of positive constants in the rightmost term of (
4.9
) by

(4.10)

Q=g_{K}(1+H)+|a_{1}E_{N_{a}}|+g_{K}\lambda H|E_{K}|+\frac{6a_{1}^{2}}{a_{2}}+\frac{6a_{2}}{E_{N_{a}}^{2}}+\frac{6}{a_{2}}(g_{K}\lambda H)^{2}.

Now we have shown that the gap functions
U(t)=U_{ij}(t)=V_{i}(t)-V_{j}(t)
and
\Pi(t)=\Pi_{ij}(t)=R_{i}(t)-R_{j}(t)
for all neuron pairs
(\mathcal{N}_{i},\mathcal{N}_{j}),1\leq i\neq j\leq n
, in the Hodgkin-Huxley-Wilson neural network
\mathcal{NW}
satisfy the Gronwall inequality

(4.11)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}(U^{2}(t)+\Pi^{2}(t))\leq-\left[\frac{1}{2\tau_{k}}\Pi^{2}(t)+\left(a_{0}+\frac{1}{2\tau_{K}}\lambda^{2}H^{2}+nP-Q\right)U^{2}(t)\right],\;\,t>T_{0}.

Step 3. Finally we can solve this linear differential inequality with respect to all the gap functions
(U^{2}(t),\Pi^{2}(t))
and establish an explicitly computable threshold condition for the network coupling strength
P
to achieve complete synchronization.

If the threshold condition (
4.2
) in this theorem is satisfied, then

a_{0}++\frac{1}{2\tau_{K}}\lambda^{2}H^{2}+nP>Q.

From the inequality (
4.9
) we can confirm

(4.12)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}(U_{ij}^{2}+\Pi_{ij}^{2})\leq-\min\left\{\frac{1}{2\tau_{k}},\left[a_{0}+\frac{1}{2\tau_{K}}\lambda^{2}H^{2}+nP-Q\right]\right\}(U_{ij}^{2}+\Pi_{ij}^{2}),\;\;t>T_{0}.

Therefore, for any solution
(V(t),R(t))
of the model equations (
2.1
)-(
2.2
), all the gap functions
U_{ij}(t),\Pi_{ij}(t)
as
t\to\infty
converge to zero at a uniform exponential rate
\mu(P)
shown in (
4.3
). Namely, for any
1\leq i<j\leq n
,

(4.13)

\begin{split}\|U_{ij}(t)&\,\|^{2}+\|\Pi_{ij}(t)\|^{2}=\|V_{i}(t)-V_{j}(t)\|^{2}+\|R_{i}(t)-R_{j}(t)\|^{2}\\[2.0pt]
\leq&\,e^{-\mu(P)t}\,(\|V_{i}^{0}-V_{j}^{0}\|^{2}+\|R_{i}^{0}-R_{j}^{0}\|^{2})\to 0,\;\;\text{as}\;\;t\to\infty.\end{split}

According to Definition (
2.1
), the Hodgkin-Huxley-Wilson neural network
\mathcal{NW}
is complete exponentially synchronized if the threshold condition is satisfied. The proof is completed.
∎

5.
Fractional Memristive Hodgkin-Huxley-Wilson Neural Networks

In this section we shall extend the main results to another new model called fractional memristive Hodgkin-Huxley-Wilson neural networks composed of
n
coupled neurons, denoted by
\mathcal{MW}
. Each neuron
\mathcal{N}_{i},1\leq i\leq n
, is presented by the Caputo fractional differential equations:

(5.1)

\begin{split}D_{c}^{\alpha}V_{i}(t)&\,=-m_{\infty}(V_{i})(V_{i}-E_{N_{a}})-g_{K}R_{i}(V_{i})(V_{i}-E_{K})\\[3.0pt]
&\,+k\,\psi(\rho)V_{i}+J+\sum_{j=1}^{n}P(V_{j}-V_{i}),\;\;1\leq i\leq n,\\
D_{c}^{\alpha}R_{i}(t)&\,=\frac{1}{\tau_{K}}\left(-R_{i}+R_{\infty}(V_{i})\right),\;\;1\leq i\leq n,\\
D_{c}^{\alpha}\rho(t)&\,=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\gamma_{i}V_{i}-b\rho,\quad\text{for}\;\;t>0,\;\;\alpha\in(0,1).\end{split}

Here the Caputo fractional time-derivative of order
\alpha\in(0,1)
denoted by
D_{c}^{\alpha}
of a scalar function
y(t)
is defined by

D_{c}^{\alpha}y(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}y^{\prime}(s)\,ds,\quad t>0,

and
\Gamma(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{z-1}e^{-t}\,dt,\,z>0,
is the Gamma function, for
y(\cdot)\in C[0,\infty)
and
y^{\prime}(\cdot)\in L^{1}_{loc}[0,\infty)
. The initial states of the system (
5.1
) will be denoted by

(5.2)

u_{i}^{0}=u_{i}(0),\quad R_{i}^{0}=R_{i}(0),\quad\rho^{0}=\rho(0),\quad 1\leq i\leq n.

Beside the assumption (
2.4
) still in place, we assume that the memristive coupling constant
k>0
and the memristor window function
\psi(s)=s(1-\beta s),\,s\in\mathbb{R}
. The constants
\beta>0,\gamma_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
and
b>0
. The basic theory and some applications of fractional calculus and fractional differential eqations can be found in books
[
57
,
58
]
and references therein.

In order to pursue the goal of asymptotic dynamics for solutions of this new fractional memristive model, we recall the following lemmas presented and briefly proved in
[
59
]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

For
\alpha\in(0,1)
and any given
T>0
, if
f\in AC[0,T]
, then it holds that

(5.3)

\frac{1}{2}\,D_{c}^{\alpha}f^{2}(t)\leq f(t)D_{c}^{\alpha}f(t),\quad\text{for}\;\;t\in(0,T).

Lemma 5.2
.

(Fractional Gronwall Inequality)

If a nonnegative function
x(t)
is absolutely continuous for
t\geq 0
and satisfies the inequality

(5.4)

D_{c}^{\alpha}x(t)\leq p-q\,x(t),\quad\text{for}\;\;t>0,

where the fractional order is
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
p
and
q
are positive constants, then it holds that

(5.5)

\begin{split}x(t)&\,\leq x(0)\,E_{\alpha}(-q\,t^{\alpha})+\,\frac{p}{q}\,\Gamma(\alpha)\left(1-E_{\alpha}(-q\,t^{\alpha})\right)\\
&\,<x(0)\left(\frac{\Gamma(1+\alpha)}{\Gamma(1+\alpha)+qt^{\alpha}}\right)+\,\frac{p}{q}\,\Gamma(\alpha),\quad\text{for}\;\;t>0.\end{split}

The Mittag-Leffler function
E_{\alpha}(z)
and the two-parameter Mittag-Leffler function
E_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}(z)

[
10
]
defined by

E_{\alpha}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{\Gamma(n\alpha+1)},\quad E_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{\Gamma(n\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})},\quad z\in\mathbb{C},

played key roles in fractional differential equations (FDE) and analysis.
E_{\alpha}(z)
is an entire functio, in particular
E_{1}(z)=e^{z}
. The function
E_{\alpha}(-x)
with real variable
x>0
is especially more involved in treatment of FDE.

Lemma 5.3
.

For an initial value problem of autonomous Caputo fractional differential equation
(
s
)

D_{c}^{\alpha}x(t)=f(x),\quad x(0)=x_{0}\in\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},

if the scalar or vector function
f(x)
is locally Lipschitz on its bounded or unbounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a solution of this initial value problem satisfies

(5.6)

\|x(t)\|\leq\|x_{0}\|\,C(t)+L,\qquad t\in I_{max}=[0,T_{max}),

where
C(t)
is a positive non-increasing continuous function,
C(t)\to 0
as
t\to\infty
, and
L>0
is a constant, then the maximal existence interval of this solution
I_{max}=[0,\infty)
so that
x(t)
is a global solution in time.

The next two theorems will show that the fractional memristive Hodgkin-Huxley-Wilson neural network
\mathcal{MW}
presented by the model (
5.1
) generates the solution semiflow which is globally dissipative and the neural network
\mathcal{MW}
is complete synchronizable at a uniform but not exponential rate.

Theorem 5.4
.

For the fractional memristive Hodgkin-Huxley-Wilson neural network
\mathcal{MW}
presented by the model (
5.1
) with the assumption (
2.4
) and
k>0,\beta>0,a_{0}>k/\beta
, there exists a bounded absorbing set in the state space
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
, which is the ball

(5.7)

B_{\alpha}^{*}=\{(v,r)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}:\|(v,r)\|^{2}\leq G_{\alpha}\}.

where
G_{\alpha}
given in (
5.13
) is a positive constant independent of any initial state. The solution semiflow generated by (
5.1
) is globally dissipative and there exists a global attractor
\mathscr{A}_{\alpha}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2n}
.

Proof.

The memductance-potential synapse function in the first equation of this neural network model (
5.1
) has the property

(5.8)

k\psi(\rho)V_{i}^{2}=k\rho(1-\beta\rho)V_{i}^{2}=k(\rho-\beta\rho^{2})V_{i}^{2}\leq k\left(\frac{1}{2\beta}-\frac{\beta}{2}\rho^{2}\right)V_{i}^{2}\leq\frac{k}{2\beta}V_{i}^{2}

since
k>0
and
\beta>0
, which is plugged in the estimate inequality similar to (
3.1
) due to Lemma
5.1
. Then we get

(5.9)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}D_{c}^{\alpha}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}^{2}(t)\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}D_{c}^{\alpha}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}^{2}(t)\right)+\frac{1}{2}P\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(V_{i}-V_{i})^{2}\\
=&\,-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\left((a_{0}-\frac{k}{2\beta}\right)V_{i}^{2}+a_{1}V_{i}^{3}+a_{2}V_{i}^{4}+g_{K}R_{i}V_{i}^{2}\right]\\
&\,+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[E_{N_{a}}(a_{0}V_{i}+a_{1}V_{i}^{2}+a_{2}V_{i}^{3})+g_{K}R_{i}E_{K}V_{i}+JV_{i}\right],\;t\in[0,T_{max}).\end{split}

Follow the steps in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, consequently it holds that

(5.10)

D_{c}^{\alpha}\sum_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}^{2}(t)\leq-\left(a_{0}-\frac{k}{\beta}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}(t)^{2}+M^{*}(R^{0}),\quad t\in[0,T_{max}),

where

(5.11)

\begin{split}M^{*}(R^{0})&\,=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(a_{0}-\frac{k}{\beta})}\left[|E_{N_{a}}|\left(a_{0}-\frac{k}{\beta}\right)+g_{K}\sqrt{R_{i}^{2}(0)+H^{2}}\,|E_{K}|+|J|\right]^{2}\\
&\,+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\frac{1}{a_{2}}\left(|E_{N_{a}}a_{1}|+g_{K}\sqrt{R_{i}^{2}(0)+H^{2}}\right)^{2}+\frac{2(a_{1}+E_{N_{a}}a_{2})^{4}}{(a_{2}/2)^{3}}\right].\end{split}

By the fractional Gronwall inequality (
5.5
) stated in Lemma
5.2
, (
5.10
) implies that

(5.12)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}^{2}(t)<\sum_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}^{2}(0)\left(\frac{\Gamma(1+\alpha)}{\Gamma(1+\alpha)+(a_{0}-k/\beta)t^{\alpha}}\right)+\,\frac{M^{*}(R^{0})}{(a_{0}-k/\beta)}\,\Gamma(\alpha).

Note that the fractional order
\alpha\in(0,1)
so that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\Gamma(1+\alpha)}{\Gamma(1+\alpha)+(a_{0}-k/\beta)t^{\alpha}}=0.

Therefore all the solutions of (
5.1
) exists for
t\in[0,\infty)
. Moreover, similar to (
3.10
) and (
3.11
), here it follows that

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\left\|\left(V(t),R(t)\right)\right\|^{2}=\limsup_{t\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(V_{i}^{2}(t)+R_{i}^{2}(t)\right)<G_{\alpha}

and

(5.13)

\begin{split}G_{\alpha}=&\,1+nH^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)}{(a_{0}-\frac{k}{\beta})^{2}}\left[|E_{N_{a}}|\left(a_{0}-\frac{k}{\beta}\right)+g_{K}\sqrt{1+H^{2}}\,|E_{K}|+|J|\right]^{2}\\
&\,+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)}{(a_{0}-\frac{k}{\beta})}\left[\frac{1}{a_{2}}\left(|E_{N_{a}}a_{1}|+g_{K}\sqrt{1+H^{2}}\right)^{2}+\frac{2(a_{1}+E_{N_{a}}a_{2})^{4}}{(a_{2}/2)^{3}}\right].\end{split}

It shows that
B^{*}_{\alpha}
in (
5.7
) is an absorbing set for the solution semiflow of this fractional memristive Hodgkin-Huxley-Wilson neural network
\mathcal{MW}
and its asymptotic dynamics is globally dissipative. Consequently there exists a global attractor for this solution semiflow.
∎

As a corollary, it can be shown that the memductance function
\rho(t)
as an auxiliary state variable of this neural network also admits a predicable ultimate upper bound. From the
\rho
-equation in (
5.1
) and the ultimate upper bound
G_{\alpha}
of
\sum_{i=1}^{n}V^{2}_{i}(t)
in Theorem
5.4
, we have

(5.14)

\frac{1}{2}D_{c}^{\alpha}\rho^{2}(t)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\,\gamma_{i}V_{i}(t)\rho(t)-b\,\rho^{2}(t)\leq\frac{1}{2b}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\,\gamma_{i}V_{i}(t)\right]^{2}-\frac{b}{2}\,\rho^{2}(t).

By Theorem
5.4
, there is a finite time
T_{\alpha}(V^{0},R^{0})\geq 0
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}V^{2}_{i}(t)\leq G_{\alpha}
for all
t>T_{\alpha}
. It follows from (
5.14
) that

D_{c}^{\alpha}\rho^{2}(t)\leq\frac{G_{\alpha}}{b}\max\{\gamma_{i}^{2}:1\leq i\leq n\}-b\,\rho^{2}(t),\quad T>T_{\alpha}.

Then we can use Lemma
5.2
to obtain

\rho^{2}(t)\leq\rho^{2}(T_{\alpha})\left(\frac{\Gamma(1+\alpha)}{\Gamma(1+\alpha)+b\,t^{\alpha}}\right)+\frac{G_{\alpha}}{b^{2}}\max\{\gamma_{i}^{2}:1\leq i\leq n\}\,\Gamma(\alpha),\quad t>T_{\alpha}.

Consequently, we get the ultimate upper bound of the memductance function:

(5.15)

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\rho^{2}(t)<1+\frac{G_{\alpha}}{b^{2}}\max\{\gamma_{i}^{2}:1\leq i\leq n\}\,\Gamma(\alpha).

Finally we prove the complete synchronization of the fractional memristive Hodgkin-Huxley-Wilson neural network
\mathcal{MW}
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Under the assumption (
2.4
) and
k>0,\beta>0,a_{0}>k/\beta
, the fractional memristive Hodgkin-Huxley-Wilson neural network
\mathcal{MW}
described by the model (
5.1
) is complete synchronizable. If the threshold condition

(5.16)

P>P_{*}=\max\left\{0,\;\frac{1}{n}\left[Q+\frac{k}{2\beta}-\left(a_{0}+\frac{1}{2\tau_{K}}\lambda^{2}H^{2}\right)\right]\right\}

is satisfied by the network coupling strength, where the positive constant
Q
in (
4.10
) is independent of initial states, then the neural network
\mathcal{MW}
is complete synchronized at a uniform convergence rate

(5.17)

\mu_{\alpha}(P,t)=\frac{\Gamma(1+\alpha)}{\Gamma(1+\alpha)+2\delta(P)\,t^{\alpha}},

where

(5.18)

\delta(P)=\min\left\{\frac{1}{2\tau_{K}},\left[a_{0}+\frac{1}{2\tau_{K}}\lambda^{2}H^{2}+nP-Q-\frac{k}{2\beta}\right]\right\}>0.

Proof.

For any given indices
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
, denote by
W_{ij}(t)=V_{i}(t)-V_{j}(t)
and
\Pi_{ij}(t)=R_{i}(t)-R_{j}(t)
in regard to this fractional memristive Hodgkin-Huxley-Wilson neural network
\mathcal{MW}
.

By the memristive synapse property (
5.8
), similar to (
4.5
) we have the estimation about the gap functions of the membrane potential

(5.19)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}D_{c}^{\alpha}\,W_{ij}^{2}\leq&\,-\left(a_{0}-\frac{k}{2\beta}+a_{2}(V_{i}^{2}+V_{j}^{2})\right)W_{ij}^{2}\\
&\,+\left[a_{1}V_{j}+a_{2}E_{N_{a}}V_{i}+(E_{N_{a}}-V_{i})(a_{1}+a_{2}V_{j})\right]W_{ij}^{2}\\[4.0pt]
&\,+g_{K}\lambda H(E_{K}-V_{i})W_{ij}^{2}-g_{K}R_{j}(V_{j})W_{ij}^{2}-nPW_{ij}^{2},\quad t>0,\end{split}

and similar to (
4.6
) the combined estimation with the gap functions of the recovery potassium channel:

(5.20)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}D_{c}^{\alpha}(W_{ij}^{2}+\Pi_{ij}^{2})\leq-\left[\frac{1}{\tau_{k}}\Pi^{2}+\left(a_{0}-\frac{k}{2\beta}+a_{2}(V_{i}^{2}+V_{i}V_{j}+V_{j}^{2})+nP\right)W_{ij}^{2}\right]\\
&\,+\frac{1}{\tau_{K}}\lambda H\Pi_{ij}\,W_{ij}\\[3.0pt]
&\,+\left[a_{1}(E_{N_{a}}-V_{i}+V_{j})+a_{2}E_{N_{a}}(V_{i}+V_{j})+g_{K}\lambda H(E_{K}-V_{i})-g_{K}R_{j}(V_{j})\right]W_{ij}^{2}.\end{split}

Through the same treatment (
4.7
) term by term in (
5.20
), we obtain a time-varying Gronwall-type differential inequality

(5.21)

\begin{split}&\,\frac{1}{2}D_{c}^{\alpha}(W_{ij}^{2}(t)+\Pi_{ij}^{2}(t))\\
\leq&\,-\left[\frac{1}{2\tau_{k}}\Pi^{2}+\left(a_{0}-\frac{k}{2\beta}+\frac{1}{2\tau_{K}}\lambda^{2}H^{2}+nP\right)W_{ij}^{2}\right]+g_{K}\left(|R_{i}(0)|e^{-t/(2\tau_{K})}+H\right)W_{ij}^{2}\\
&\,+\left(a_{1}E_{N_{a}}+g_{K}\lambda HE_{K}\right)W_{ij}^{2}+\left[\frac{6a_{1}^{2}}{a_{2}}+\frac{6a_{2}}{E_{N_{a}}^{2}}+\frac{6}{a_{2}}(g_{K}\lambda H)^{2}\right]W_{ij}^{2}\\
\leq&\,-\left[\frac{1}{2\tau_{k}}\Pi^{2}(t)+\left(a_{0}-\frac{k}{2\beta}+\frac{1}{2\tau_{K}}\lambda^{2}H^{2}+nP\right)W_{ij}^{2}(t)\right]\\
&\,+\left[g_{K}(1+H)+|a_{1}E_{N_{a}}|+g_{K}\lambda H|E_{K}|+\frac{6a_{1}^{2}}{a_{2}}+\frac{6a_{2}}{E_{N_{a}}^{2}}+\frac{6}{a_{2}}(g_{K}\lambda H)^{2}\right]W_{ij}^{2}(t)\\
\leq&\,-\left[\frac{1}{2\tau_{k}}\Pi^{2}(t)+\left(a_{0}-\frac{k}{2\beta}+\frac{1}{2\tau_{K}}\lambda^{2}H^{2}+nP-Q\right)W_{ij}^{2}(t)\right],\;\;t>T_{0},\end{split}

because according to Theorem
5.4
there exists a finite time
T_{0}>0
depending on the initial state
(V^{0},R^{0})
of a solution such that
|R_{i}(0)|e^{-t/(2\tau_{K})}\leq 1
for any
t>T_{0}
. Here the positive constant
Q
is still the same as in (
4.10
),

Q=g_{K}(1+H)+|a_{1}E_{N_{a}}|+g_{K}\lambda H|E_{K}|+\frac{6a_{1}^{2}}{a_{2}}+\frac{6a_{2}}{E_{N_{a}}^{2}}+\frac{6}{a_{2}}(g_{K}\lambda H)^{2}.

From the inequality (
5.21
), we see

(5.22)

\frac{1}{2}D_{c}^{\alpha}(W_{ij}^{2}+\Pi_{ij}^{2})\leq-\min\left\{\frac{1}{2\tau_{k}},\left[a_{0}-\frac{k}{2\beta}+\frac{1}{2\tau_{K}}\lambda^{2}H^{2}+nP-Q\right]\right\}(W_{ij}^{2}+\Pi_{ij}^{2})

for
t>T_{0}
. If the threshold condition (
4.2
) in this theorem is satisfied so that

a_{0}+\frac{1}{2\tau_{K}}\lambda^{2}H^{2}+nP>Q+\frac{k}{2\beta},

then (
5.22
) is simply

(5.23)

\frac{1}{2}D_{c}^{\alpha}(W_{ij}^{2}+\Pi_{ij}^{2})\leq-\delta(P)(W_{ij}^{2}+\Pi_{ij}^{2}),\quad t>T_{0},

and
\delta(P)
given in (
5.18
) is a positive constants.

Finally we can apply the fractional Gronwall inequality (
5.4
)-(
5.5
) to solve this linear differential inequality with respect to
(W_{ij}^{2}(t),\Pi_{ij}^{2}(t))
and reach the conclusion that for any initial state
(V^{0},R^{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
, all the gap functions
W_{ij}(t)=V_{i}(t)-V_{j}(t)
and
\Pi_{ij}(t)=R_{i}(t)-R_{j}(t)
as
t\to\infty
converge to zero at a uniform but not exponential decaying rate
\mu_{\alpha}(P,t)
shown in (
5.17
). Namely, for any initial state
(V^{0},R^{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
and any
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
,

(5.24)

\begin{split}\|W_{ij}(t)&\,\|^{2}+\|\Pi_{ij}(t)\|^{2}=\|V_{i}(t)-V_{j}(t)\|^{2}+\|R_{i}(t)-R_{j}(t)\|^{2}\\[2.0pt]
\leq&\,\frac{\Gamma(1+\alpha)}{\Gamma(1+\alpha)+2\delta(P)t^{\alpha}}\left(\|V_{i}^{0}-V_{j}^{0}\|^{2}+\|R_{i}^{0}-R_{j}^{0}\|^{2}\right)\\[3.0pt]
=&\,\mu_{\alpha}(P,t)\left(\|V_{i}^{0}-V_{j}^{0}\|^{2}+\|R_{i}^{0}-R_{j}^{0}\|^{2}\right)\to 0,\;\;\text{as}\;\;t\to\infty.\end{split}

According to Definition (
2.1
), the fractional memristive Hodgkin-Huxley-Wilson neural network
\mathcal{MW}
is complete synchronized at a non-exponential convergence rate. The proof is completed.
∎

As a remark, the memristor window function
\psi(s)=s(1-\beta s)
in this model (
5.1
) of fractional memristive Hodgkin-Huxley-Wilson neural networks is for the typical Titanium dioxide memristors including the CMOS memristors (complimentary metal-oxide-semiconductor circuits). The proof in this section can certainly be adaptable to deal with other types of memristor window functions such as the piecewise linear functions, hyperbolic-tangent functions, or Strukov-Williams memristors, etc.

6.
Conclusions

Hodgkin-Huxley equations established in 1952 is the primary mathematical model of biological neurons and this model laid down a theoretical foundation for neuroscience as well as the modern researches on biologically inspired neural networks. However, the global and asymptotic dynamics of this higher dimensional and strongly nonlinear model along with the affiliated spiking neural networks had not been deeply investigated in comparison with several simplified neuron and neural network models like FitzHigh-Nagumo equations and Hindmarsh-Rose equations.

In this paper we introduced and investigated a new model of biological neural networks, which is called Hodgkin-Huxley-Wilson neural networks. This model captured the essential features of the original Hodgkin-Huxley equations in terms of the cubic-power nonlinearity as an approximation of the exponential nonlinearity in the membrane potential equation and the conductance coupling of the two key ionic gating channels of sodium and potassium. We should emphasize that the proof methodology and techniques presented in this work can be adapted or generalized to explore future models of neuron networks with even higher order polynomial nonlinearity and with multiple ionic or other dynamic parameter (such as temperature and hyperpolarizing current) channels.

Asymptotic synchronization dynamics is an important and booming topic about cognitive functionality and signals processing for biological neural networks as well as artificial neural networks in machine learning. Synchronization is more diversified and complicated than the classical stability properties converging to equilibrium states. It also encompasses chaotic synchronization and various approximate synchronization.

The main results in this paper are Theorem
3.2
, in which we analytically proved the existence of absorbing set and global attractor with a sharp ultimate bound, and Theorem
4.1
, in which we established a sufficient threshold condition for achieving complete exponential synchronization if satisfied by the cross-network coupling strength. The contribution of this work lies not only in the explicit expression of the complete synchronization threshold and the exponential convergence rate in terms of the original parameters, but also lies in the proof approach of uniform and sharp
a priori
estimates of the solutions for the model equations and for the interneuron differencing equations by the leverage of hard analysis.

Furthermore we have extended the main results to Caputo fractional memristive Hodgkin-Huxley-Wilson neural networks in Theorem
5.4
and Theorem
5.5
through the fractional Gronwall inequality. The derived complete synchronization threshold condition in this case features a uniform but non-exponential convergence rate.

It is conceivable and promising that one can pursue the exploration of the complete synchronization for partly diffusive Hodgkin-Huxley-Wilson neural networks governed by distributed partial differential equations or by fractional partial differential equations.
