A Finite-Iteration Theory for Asynchronous Categorical Distributional Temporal-Difference Learning
Ege C. Kaya, Abolfazl Hashemi

Abstract
Recent non-asymptotic analyses have substantially advanced the theory of distributional policy evaluation, but they largely concern synchronous full-state updates under a generative model, model-based estimators, accelerated variants, or different approximation architectures. Standard categorical temporal-difference learning is typically used in a different regime. It asynchronously performs a single-state update at each iteration and, in online settings, is driven by a Markovian trajectory. This leaves an important gap between existing finite-iteration theory and the categorical recursions most closely aligned with practical distributional temporal-difference implementations. We bridge this gap for two categorical policy-evaluation methods: scalar categorical temporal-difference learning in the Cramér geometry and multivariate signed-categorical temporal-difference learning in the maximum mean discrepancy geometry. After suitable isometric embeddings, both algorithms take the form of asynchronous single-state stochastic-approximation recursions that contract in a statewise supremum norm. This permits finite-iteration guarantees in discounted problems under both i.i.d. and Markovian state sampling, and in undiscounted fixed-horizon problems under i.i.d. episodic sampling.

Practical interpretation of the sampling regimes.
Each sampling model considered in this work has a practical RL interpretation. The discounted i.i.d. regime points to a replay-based or generative-model benchmark, where updates are formed from approximately independent samples drawn from a buffer or simulator, also akin to the synchronous analyses often used to study TD-style methods theoretically. The discounted Markovian regime is the standard online setting for TD learning, in which samples are generated sequentially along a single behavior trajectory. In the finite-horizon undiscounted case, the episodic regime is the standard reset-based formulation of finite-horizon RL, where interaction proceeds for a fixed horizon

Discounted categorical policy evaluation

We consider a discounted Markov decision process
[
45
]

(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},P,R,\gamma)
with finite state space
\mathcal{S}
, finite action space
\mathcal{A}
, reward function
R(s,a)
, transition kernel
P(\cdot\mid s,a)
, and discount factor
\gamma\in(0,1)
. We assume that
R:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\to[0,1]
in the scalar case, and
R:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\to[0,1]^{q},q\geq 2
in the multivariate case. A policy
\pi
is fixed throughout. The induced state trajectory is either i.i.d. with law
\rho
or a Markov chain with stationary distribution
\mu_{\mathcal{S}}
. In the Markovian case we assume irreducibility and aperiodicity. The finiteness of
\mathcal{S}
implies that the Markov chain resulting from the MDP with fixed policy
\pi
mixes geometrically
[
27
]
, i.e., that there exist constants
C_{\mathrm{mix}}\geq 1
and
\sigma_{\mathrm{mix}}\in(0,1)
such that

\sup_{x\in\mathcal{S}}\left\lVert\Pr(S_{k}\in\cdot\mid S_{0}=x)-\mu_{\mathcal{S}}(\cdot)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq C_{\mathrm{mix}}\sigma_{\mathrm{mix}}^{k}\qquad\text{for all }k\geq 0.

(1)

For
\delta>0
, we define the associated mixing time

t_{\delta}:=\min\Bigl\{k\geq 0:\sup_{x\in\mathcal{S}}\left\lVert\Pr(S_{k}\in\cdot\mid S_{0}=x)-\mu_{\mathcal{S}}(\cdot)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq\delta\Bigr\}.

(2)

For both CTD and MTD, we work with a block-supremum contraction metric
\ell_{\infty}
, which admits a statewise isometric embedding
I
to a product space with contraction norm
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{2,\infty}
, and a statewise projection
\Pi^{\Theta}
onto the space of
\Theta

\mathcal{F}^{\mathcal{S}}_{\Theta}
. We can then compose the distributional Bellman operator
T^{\pi}
to obtain the embedded, projected operator

\mathcal{O}:=I\circ\Pi^{\Theta}T^{\pi}\circ I^{-1}.

(3)

This operator then admits a one-step sampled Bellman target
\widehat{T}(U_{k};s,(R_{k},S_{k+1}))
computed from a current estimate
U_{k}
and a random sample
(R_{k},S_{k+1})
, satisfying

\mathbb{E}\left[\widehat{T}(U_{k};S_{k},(R_{k},S_{k+1}))\mid U_{k},S_{k}=s\right]=(\mathcal{O}U_{k})(s),

(4)

and a recursion that takes the form

U_{k+1}=U_{k}+\alpha_{k}P_{S_{k}}(\widehat{T}(U_{k};S_{k},(R_{k},S_{k+1}))-U_{k}(S_{k})),

(5)

where for each
s\in\mathcal{S}

denotes the coordinate projector onto block
s
.

The point of the discounted analysis is that the exact one-state recursion already has the ingredients needed for finite-iteration SA bounds. Concretely, the proof uses the contraction of the averaged operator in
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{2,\infty}
, a Moreau-envelope smoothing of
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{2,\infty}
based on
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{2,p}

,
26
,
3
,
31
,
2
]
, an affine conditional second-moment bound or a centered pathwise perturbation bound depending on the method, the samplewise
1
-Lipschitzness of the one-step target map in
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{2,\infty}
, and the geometric mixing in the Markovian case. More precisely, with
p^{\star}:=\max\{2,\lceil\log\lvert\mathcal{S}\rvert\rceil\}

3.1
Discounted CTD

For each state
s\in\mathcal{S}
, fix an ordered support
\Theta(s)=\{\theta_{1}(s)<\cdots<\theta_{d}(s)\}\subset\mathbb{R}
.
\mathcal{F}^{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{C},\Theta}
is the class of state-indexed categorical laws supported on these statewise grids. The contraction metric
\ell_{\mathrm{C},\infty}
is the supremum Cramér metric, the embedding
I_{\mathrm{C}}
is the standard cumulative-mass isometry
I_{\mathrm{C},s}

[
39
,
5
]
applied to all states, and the statewise projection
\Pi_{\mathrm{C}}^{\Theta}
is the usual linear-interpolation categorical projection
\Pi_{\mathrm{C}}^{\Theta(s)}
applied to all states.

Given a sampled transition
(S_{k},A_{k},R_{k},S_{k+1})
, the sampled Bellman target is

\widehat{T}_{\mathrm{C}}(U_{k};S_{k},(R_{k},S_{k+1})):=I_{\mathrm{C},S_{k}}\Bigl(\Pi_{\mathrm{C}}^{\Theta(S_{k})}\bigl((f_{R_{k},\gamma})_{\#}I_{\mathrm{C},S_{k+1}}^{-1}(U_{k}(S_{k+1}))\bigr)\Bigr),

(6)

where
f_{r,\gamma}(z)=r+\gamma z
and
\#
is the measure-pushforward operator.

B
verifies the contraction, Lipschitz continuity and perturbation ingredients and records the explicit constants.

Theorem 1

(Discounted asynchronous CTD)
.

Let
\eta^{\star}\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{C},\Theta}^{\mathcal{S}}
denote the unique fixed point of
\Pi_{\mathrm{C}}^{\Theta}T^{\pi}
, and let
\eta_{k}:=I_{\mathrm{C}}^{-1}(U_{k})

5
) with

(i) i.i.d. sampling.
Suppose

\rho

and explicit thresholds

for which the following results hold on three step size regimes.

Constant step size.
If

, then the iterates converge geometrically to an
O(\alpha)
neighborhood:

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\ell_{\mathrm{C},\infty}(\eta_{k},\eta^{\star})^{2}\bigr]\leq C^{\mathrm{iid}}_{\mathrm{C}}\ell_{\mathrm{C},\infty}(\eta_{0},\eta^{\star})^{2}(1-c^{\mathrm{iid}}_{\mathrm{C}}\alpha)^{k}+C^{\mathrm{iid}}_{\mathrm{C}}\alpha.

(7)

(9)

(ii) Markovian trajectory sampling.
Suppose
(S_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is irreducible and aperiodic with stationary distribution
\mu_{\mathcal{S}}
satisfying
\min_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\mu_{\mathcal{S}}(s)>0
and the mixing condition (
1
). Let
t_{k}:=t_{\alpha_{k}}

and
h^{\mathrm{mk}}_{\mathrm{C}}(\alpha,z)

Constant step size.
If
\alpha_{k}\equiv\alpha

Polynomially-diminishing step size.
If
\alpha_{k}=\alpha/(k+h)^{z}

(12)

Proof sketch.

The Cramér embedding makes the projected Bellman map a
\sqrt{\gamma}
-contraction. Under i.i.d. sampling, the averaged asynchronous map contracts according to the minimum state mass, while under Markovian sampling, the same drift is recovered after the mixing-time comparison. The sampled target is samplewise Lipschitz and its centered perturbation is uniformly bounded by the support radius. A sufficiently small Moreau envelope smoothing parameter makes the drift constants positive, and the upper bounds on
\alpha
or lower bounds on
h
ensure the quadratic remainder is dominated by the negative drift. Appendix
B
gives the exact constants and thresholds.

5
Representation error

Both the discounted and undiscounted fixed-horizon theorems control the distance to the fixed point of a projected Bellman operator. A common deterministic decomposition turns these projected-fixed-point guarantees into total-error guarantees relative to the exact return-distribution fixed point. Let
\ell
denote either
\ell_{\mathrm{C},\infty}
or
\ell_{\mathrm{M},\infty}
in the discounted setting, or either
\ell_{H,\mathrm{C},\infty}
or
\ell_{H,\mathrm{M},\infty}
in the finite-horizon setting. Let
T
denote the corresponding Bellman operator with contraction modulus
\beta
and let
\Pi
denote the corresponding projection. Furthermore, let
\eta^{\pi}
be the fixed point of
T
,
\eta^{\star}
be the fixed point of
\Pi T
and
\varepsilon^{\mathrm{repr}}:=\ell(\Pi\eta^{\pi},\eta^{\pi})
. Then

\ell(\eta^{\star},\eta^{\pi})\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{\mathrm{repr}}}{1-\beta},\qquad\mathbb{E}\bigl[\ell(\eta_{k},\eta^{\pi})^{2}\bigr]\leq 2\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[\ell(\eta_{k},\eta^{\star})^{2}\bigr]+2\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{\mathrm{repr}}}{1-\beta}\right)^{2}.

(44)

The decomposition in (
44
) separates two conceptually different sources of error. The first is the algorithmic term, controlled by the finite-iteration bounds of the preceding theorems. The second is the deterministic projection term
\varepsilon^{\mathrm{repr}}/(1-\beta)
, which depends only on how well the chosen categorical support can approximate the exact return-distribution fixed point. Thus, once the support family is fixed, the SA analysis and the discretization bias can be seen independently. The full statement and proof are deferred to Appendix
F
.

6
Discussion of the results

Theorems
1
and
3
show that the standard categorical recursions are structurally simpler than they may initially appear. After suitable statewise isometric embeddings, both CTD and MTD become asynchronous SA schemes that update one state block at a time and contract in a block-supremum norm. This common structure is what makes a unified finite-iteration analysis possible. The real point of departure is the perturbation geometry: CTD lives in a bounded-noise regime because bounded categorical supports and the Cramér geometry give uniform samplewise control, whereas MTD produces affine perturbations in
\lVert U\rVert_{2,\infty}
because the signed-categorical MMD projection is affine rather than uniformly bounded.

In the undiscounted fixed-horizon half, the learned object is a horizon-indexed stack, and one sampled transition updates the whole stack block at the visited state because every horizon shares the same first reward and differs only in the bootstrapped tail. There is also no discount-driven contraction property to exploit. Instead, contraction is recovered by weighting horizon layers, and the theory is stated at episode boundaries because the averaged drift changes with the within-episode phase. Across both discounted and undiscounted fixed-horizon settings, the step size results are consistent: constant steps give a controllable
O(\alpha)
neighborhood (with logarithmic mixing terms entering in the Markovian case), linearly-diminishing step sizes give the sharpest asymptotic decay once the threshold condition is met, and polynomially-diminishing step sizes trade rate for milder admissibility conditions. Further discussion is deferred to Appendix
G
.

7
Conclusion

We established finite-iteration guarantees for asynchronous categorical distributional temporal-difference learning in the scalar Cramér and multivariate signed-categorical MMD settings. The scope of the present theory is tabular finite-state policy-evaluation analysis, it does not treat control or policy improvement, and its guarantees are expectation bounds rather than high-probability bounds. Within that scope, the discounted results cover i.i.d. sampled states and Markovian trajectories, while the undiscounted results treat the fixed-horizon episodic regime under the exact online recursions.

(h,s)
. After this flattening, the weighted fixed-horizon norm becomes an ordinary block-supremum norm on a finite product of Euclidean blocks, so the present appendix applies verbatim with the number of blocks equal to the cardinality of that flattened index set.

A.2
Abstract i.i.d. finite-iteration framework

Assume first that
(S_{k})_{k\geq 0}
are i.i.d. with law
\rho
on
\mathcal{S}
and let

\rho_{\min}:=\min_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\rho(s)>0.

(58)

Let
\mathcal{O}:V\to V
be a contraction in
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{2,\infty}
with modulus
\beta\in(0,1)
and fixed point
U^{\star}
:

\lVert\mathcal{O}U-\mathcal{O}U^{\prime}\rVert_{2,\infty}\leq\beta\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{2,\infty}\quad\text{ for all }U,U^{\prime}\in V.

(59)

Let
\widehat{T}(U;s,\xi)
be a sampled target satisfying

\mathbb{E}\left[\widehat{T}(U_{k};S_{k},\xi_{k})\mid U_{k},S_{k}=s\right]=(\mathcal{O}U_{k})(s).

(60)

Consider the asynchronous recursion

U_{k+1}=U_{k}+\alpha_{k}P_{S_{k}}\left(\widehat{T}(U_{k};S_{k},\xi_{k})-U_{k}(S_{k})\right).

(61)

For the centered recursion, write

W_{k}:=U_{k}-U^{\star}.

(62)

Assume there exists a finite constant
A^{\mathrm{iid}}\geq 0
such that

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lVert\widehat{T}(U_{k};S_{k},\xi_{k})-(\mathcal{O}U_{k})(S_{k})\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\mid U_{k},S_{k}\right]\leq A^{\mathrm{iid}}\bigl(1+\lVert W_{k}\rVert_{2,\infty}^{2}\bigr)\qquad\text{a.s.}

(63)

Proposition 15

(Abstract i.i.d. finite-iteration bound)
.

Fix any
\vartheta>0
and define

\beta_{\rho}:=1-\rho_{\min}(1-\beta),\qquad a_{1}:=\frac{1+\vartheta\lvert\mathcal{S}\rvert^{2/p^{\star}}}{1+\vartheta},\qquad a_{2}:=1-\beta_{\rho}\sqrt{\frac{1+\vartheta\lvert\mathcal{S}\rvert^{2/p^{\star}}}{1+\vartheta}},

(64)

and

a_{3}:=\frac{4(p^{\star}-1)\lvert\mathcal{S}\rvert^{2/p^{\star}}(A^{\mathrm{iid}}+2)(1+\vartheta)}{\vartheta},\qquad a_{4}:=\frac{2(p^{\star}-1)\lvert\mathcal{S}\rvert^{2/p^{\star}}A^{\mathrm{iid}}(1+\vartheta)}{\vartheta}.

(65)

Since
a_{2}\to 1-\beta_{\rho}>0
as
\vartheta\downarrow 0
, the condition
a_{2}>0
holds for all sufficiently small
\vartheta
. If
a_{2}>0
,
(\alpha_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is nonincreasing, and
\alpha_{0}\leq a_{2}/a_{3}
, then for all
k\geq 0
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\lVert W_{k}\rVert_{2,\infty}^{2}\right]\leq a_{1}\lVert W_{0}\rVert_{2,\infty}^{2}\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(1-a_{2}\alpha_{j})+a_{4}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\alpha_{i}^{2}\prod_{j=i+1}^{k-1}(1-a_{2}\alpha_{j}).

(66)

In particular:

1.

if
\alpha_{k}\equiv\alpha
and
\alpha\leq a_{2}/a_{3}
, then for all
k\geq 0
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\lVert W_{k}\rVert_{2,\infty}^{2}\right]\leq a_{1}\lVert W_{0}\rVert_{2,\infty}^{2}(1-a_{2}\alpha)^{k}+\frac{a_{4}}{a_{2}}\alpha,

(67)

2.

if
\alpha_{k}=\alpha/(k+h)
,
\alpha>1/a_{2}
, and

h\geq\max\left\{1,\frac{\alpha a_{3}}{a_{2}}\right\},

(68)

then for all
k\geq 0
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\lVert W_{k}\rVert_{2,\infty}^{2}\right]\leq a_{1}\lVert W_{0}\rVert_{2,\infty}^{2}\left(\frac{h}{k+h}\right)^{a_{2}\alpha}+\frac{4e\,\alpha^{2}a_{4}}{a_{2}\alpha-1}\cdot\frac{1}{k+h},

(69)

3.

if
\alpha_{k}=\alpha/(k+h)^{z}
with
z\in(0,1)
and

h\geq\max\left\{1,\left(\frac{\alpha a_{3}}{a_{2}}\right)^{1/z},\left(\frac{2z}{a_{2}\alpha}\right)^{1/(1-z)}\right\},

(70)

then for all
k\geq 0
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\lVert W_{k}\rVert_{2,\infty}^{2}\right]\leq a_{1}\lVert W_{0}\rVert_{2,\infty}^{2}\exp\left(-\frac{a_{2}\alpha}{1-z}\bigl((k+h)^{1-z}-h^{1-z}\bigr)\right)+\frac{2\alpha a_{4}}{a_{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{(k+h)^{z}}.

(71)

Proof.

Define the averaged asynchronous operator

H_{\rho}(U):=U+\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\rho(s)P_{s}\bigl((\mathcal{O}U)(s)-U(s)\bigr).

(72)

For each state block
s
,

\bigl(H_{\rho}(U)-H_{\rho}(V)\bigr)(s)=(1-\rho(s))(U(s)-V(s))+\rho(s)\bigl((\mathcal{O}U)(s)-(\mathcal{O}V)(s)\bigr).

(73)

Since
\mathcal{O}
is a
\beta
-contraction in
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{2,\infty}
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\bigl(H_{\rho}(U)-H_{\rho}(V)\bigr)(s)\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\bigl((1-\rho(s))+\rho(s)\beta\bigr)\lVert U-V\rVert_{2,\infty}

(74)

\displaystyle=\bigl(1-\rho(s)(1-\beta)\bigr)\lVert U-V\rVert_{2,\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{\rho}\lVert U-V\rVert_{2,\infty}.

Taking the maximum over
s
on both sides gives

\lVert H_{\rho}(U)-H_{\rho}(V)\rVert_{2,\infty}\leq\max_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\bigl(1-\rho(s)(1-\beta)\bigr)\lVert U-V\rVert_{2,\infty}=\beta_{\rho}\lVert U-V\rVert_{2,\infty},

(75)

since the coefficient
1-\rho(s)(1-\beta)
is decreasing in
\rho(s)
. Now apply Corollary 2.1 of
12
to the centered recursion for
W_{k}
, with contraction norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{2,\infty}
, smoothing norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{2,p^{\star}}
, Lyapunov function
M_{\vartheta,p^{\star}}
from Proposition
13
, and noise constant
A^{\mathrm{iid}}
. The approximation factors in Proposition
13
and the smoothness constant in Proposition
12
produce exactly the displayed constants. The constant-step, linearly-diminishing, and polynomially-diminishing step size bounds follow by specializing the displayed recursion.
∎

A.3
Abstract Markovian finite-iteration framework

Assume now that
(S_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is an irreducible, aperiodic Markov chain on
\mathcal{S}
with stationary distribution
\mu_{\mathcal{S}}
. Let

\mu_{\min}:=\min_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\mu_{\mathcal{S}}(s)>0,\qquad\bar{\beta}_{\mu}:=1-\mu_{\min}(1-\beta).

(76)

Assume further that the chain satisfies the geometric mixing condition from Section
3
and let
t_{\delta}
denote the corresponding mixing time.

Define the full-sample map

\widehat{H}(U;s,\xi):=U+P_{s}\bigl(\widehat{T}(U;s,\xi)-U(s)\bigr)

(77)

and the expected full-sample map

F(U,s):=U+P_{s}\bigl((\mathcal{O}U)(s)-U(s)\bigr).

(78)

Assume there exist finite constants
A_{1}\geq 0
,
A_{2}\geq 0
, and
B_{2}\geq 0
such that

\lVert\widehat{H}(U;s,\xi)-\widehat{H}(U^{\prime};s,\xi)\rVert_{2,\infty}\leq A_{1}\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{2,\infty}

(79)

for all
U,U^{\prime}\in V
, and

\Delta_{k}:=\widehat{H}(U_{k};S_{k},\xi_{k})-F(U_{k},S_{k})

(80)

satisfies

\mathbb{E}\left[\Delta_{k}\,\mid\,\mathcal{G}_{k}\right]=0,\qquad\lVert\Delta_{k}\rVert_{2,\infty}\leq A_{2}\lVert U_{k}\rVert_{2,\infty}+B_{2}\qquad\text{a.s.}

(81)

where

\mathcal{G}_{k}:=\sigma(U_{0},S_{0},\xi_{0},\dots,U_{k},S_{k}).

(82)

Proposition 16

(Abstract Markovian finite-iteration bound)
.

Fix any
\vartheta>0
and define

\phi_{1}:=\frac{1+\vartheta\lvert\mathcal{S}\rvert^{2/p^{\star}}}{1+\vartheta},\qquad\phi_{2}:=1-\bar{\beta}_{\mu}\sqrt{\frac{1+\vartheta\lvert\mathcal{S}\rvert^{2/p^{\star}}}{1+\vartheta}},\qquad\phi_{3}:=\frac{114(p^{\star}-1)\bigl(1+\vartheta\lvert\mathcal{S}\rvert^{2/p^{\star}}\bigr)}{\vartheta}.

(83)

Since
\phi_{2}\to 1-\bar{\beta}_{\mu}>0
as
\vartheta\downarrow 0
, the condition
\phi_{2}>0
for all sufficiently small
\vartheta
. Set

A:=A_{1}+A_{2}+1,\qquad c_{1}:=\left(2\lVert U_{0}-U^{\star}\rVert_{2,\infty}+\frac{B_{2}}{A}\right)^{2},\qquad c_{2}:=B_{2}^{2}.

(84)

Let
t_{k}:=t_{\alpha_{k}}
and
K:=\min\{k\geq 0:k\geq t_{k}\}
. If
\phi_{2}>0
,
(\alpha_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is nonincreasing, and

\sum_{i=k-t_{k}}^{k-1}\alpha_{i}\leq\min\left\{\frac{\phi_{2}}{\phi_{3}A^{2}},\frac{1}{4A}\right\}\qquad\text{for all }k\geq K,

(85)

then:

1.

if
\alpha_{k}\equiv\alpha
, then for all
k\geq t_{\alpha}
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\lVert W_{k}\rVert_{2,\infty}^{2}\right]\leq\phi_{1}c_{1}(1-\phi_{2}\alpha)^{k-t_{\alpha}}+\frac{\phi_{3}}{\phi_{2}}c_{2}\,\alpha t_{\alpha};

(86)

2.

if
\alpha_{k}=\alpha/(k+h)
,
\alpha>1/\phi_{2}
, and
h\geq 1
, then for all
k\geq K
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\lVert W_{k}\rVert_{2,\infty}^{2}\right]\leq\phi_{1}c_{1}\left(\frac{K+h}{k+h}\right)^{\phi_{2}\alpha}+\frac{8e\,\alpha^{2}\phi_{3}c_{2}}{\phi_{2}\alpha-1}\cdot\frac{t_{k}}{k+h};

(87)

3.

if
\alpha_{k}=\alpha/(k+h)^{z}
with
z\in(0,1)
, then for all
k\geq K
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\lVert W_{k}\rVert_{2,\infty}^{2}\right]\leq\phi_{1}c_{1}\exp\left(-\frac{\phi_{2}\alpha}{1-z}\bigl((k+h)^{1-z}-(K+h)^{1-z}\bigr)\right)+\frac{4\phi_{3}c_{2}\alpha}{\phi_{2}}\cdot\frac{t_{k}}{(k+h)^{z}}.

(88)

Proof.

This is the Markovian finite-iteration theorem of
11
, translated into the present block-supremum notation and applied to the centered recursion
W_{k}:=U_{k}-U^{\star}
. The contraction factor is
\bar{\beta}_{\mu}
. The smoothness and approximation factors come from Propositions
12
,
13
, and
11
. The constants
A_{1}
,
A_{2}
, and
B_{2}
enter exactly through the samplewise Lipschitz and pathwise affine perturbation hypotheses.
∎

Appendix B
CTD details and proof of Theorem
1

We verify the hypotheses of Appendix
A
for the discounted scalar categorical temporal-difference recursion and thereby prove Theorem
1
. The proof has three parts. First, we record the statewise Cramér isometry and the induced block-supremum contraction of the projected Bellman operator. Second, we verify the samplewise Lipschitz property of the sampled target map. Third, we verify the centered perturbation bounds required by the i.i.d. and Markovian finite-iteration arguments.

B.1
Statewise Cramér isometry and contraction

For each state
s\in\mathcal{S}
, let

\Theta(s)=\{\theta_{1}(s)<\cdots<\theta_{d}(s)\}\subset\mathbb{R}

(89)

and let
\mathcal{F}^{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{C},\Theta}
denote the class of state-indexed categorical laws supported on these statewise grids. For

\eta(s)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}p_{i}(s)\delta_{\theta_{i}(s)},

(90)

define the cumulative masses and the grid separations

c^{\eta}_{j}(s):=\sum_{i=1}^{j}p_{i}(s),\qquad\Delta_{j}(s):=\theta_{j+1}(s)-\theta_{j}(s),\qquad j=1,\dots,d-1.

(91)

The statewise Cramér embedding is

I_{s}(\eta(s)):=\bigl(\sqrt{\Delta_{1}(s)}\,c^{\eta}_{1}(s),\dots,\sqrt{\Delta_{d-1}(s)}\,c^{\eta}_{d-1}(s),0\bigr)^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(92)

Proposition 17

(Statewise Cramér isometry)
.

For every state
s\in\mathcal{S}
and all
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{C},\Theta(s)}
,

\ell_{\mathrm{C}}(\mu,\nu)^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\Delta_{j}(s)\bigl(c^{\mu}_{j}(s)-c^{\nu}_{j}(s)\bigr)^{2}=\lVert I_{s}(\mu)-I_{s}(\nu)\rVert_{2}^{2}.

(93)

Consequently, with the product embedding

I_{\mathrm{C}}(\eta):=(I_{s}(\eta(s)))_{s\in\mathcal{S}},

(94)

we have

\lVert I_{\mathrm{C}}(\eta)-I_{\mathrm{C}}(\eta^{\prime})\rVert_{2,\infty}=\ell_{\mathrm{C},\infty}(\eta,\eta^{\prime}).

(95)

Proof.

For laws supported on the ordered grid
\Theta(s)
, the cumulative distribution functions are piecewise
constant on the intervals

[\theta_{j}(s),\theta_{j+1}(s)),\qquad j=1,\dots,d-1,

(96)

with values
c^{\eta}_{j}(s)
and
c^{\nu}_{j}(s)
respectively. Therefore,

\ell_{\mathrm{C}}(\mu,\nu)^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigl(F_{\mu}(z)-F_{\nu}(z)\bigr)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}z=\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\Delta_{j}(s)\bigl(c^{\mu}_{j}(s)-c^{\nu}_{j}(s)\bigr)^{2},

(97)

which is exactly
\lVert I_{s}(\mu)-I_{s}(\nu)\rVert_{2}^{2}
. Taking the maximum over
s\in\mathcal{S}
gives the product-space identity.
∎

Recall from Section
3.1
that

\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{C}}:=I_{\mathrm{C}}\circ\Pi_{\mathrm{C}}^{\Theta}T^{\pi}\circ I_{\mathrm{C}}^{-1}.

(98)

Proposition 18

(Embedded CTD contraction)
.

For all
U,U^{\prime}\in I_{\mathrm{C}}(\mathcal{F}^{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{C},\Theta})
,

\lVert\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{C}}U-\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{C}}U^{\prime}\rVert_{2,\infty}\leq\sqrt{\gamma}\,\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{2,\infty}.

(99)

Proof.

Let
\eta:=I_{\mathrm{C}}^{-1}(U)
and
\eta^{\prime}:=I_{\mathrm{C}}^{-1}(U^{\prime})
. By Proposition
17
and the contraction of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{C}}
in the supremum Cramér metric
[
5
]
,

\lVert\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{C}}U-\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{C}}U^{\prime}\rVert_{2,\infty}=\ell_{\mathrm{C},\infty}(\Pi_{\mathrm{C}}^{\Theta}T^{\pi}\eta,\Pi_{\mathrm{C}}^{\Theta}T^{\pi}\eta^{\prime})\leq\sqrt{\gamma}\,\ell_{\mathrm{C},\infty}(\eta,\eta^{\prime})=\sqrt{\gamma}\,\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{2,\infty}.

(100)

∎

B.2
Unbiased sampled target and samplewise Lipschitz continuity

Recall the sampled CTD target from Section
3.1
:

\widehat{T}_{\mathrm{C}}(U_{k};S_{k},(R_{k},S_{k+1})):=I_{S_{k}}\Bigl(\Pi_{\mathrm{C}}^{\Theta(S_{k})}\bigl((f_{R_{k},\gamma})_{\#}I_{S_{k+1}}^{-1}(U_{k}(S_{k+1}))\bigr)\Bigr),

(101)

where
f_{r,\gamma}(z)=r+\gamma z
.

Proposition 19

(Conditional unbiasedness of the sampled CTD target)
.

For every
U\in I_{\mathrm{C}}(\mathcal{F}^{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{C},\Theta})
and every state
s\in\mathcal{S}
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\widehat{T}_{\mathrm{C}}(U;s,(R_{k},S_{k+1}))\mid U_{k}=U,S_{k}=s\right]=(\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{C}}U)(s).

(102)

Proof.

Condition on
U_{k}=U
and
S_{k}=s
. By definition,

\widehat{T}_{\mathrm{C}}(U;s,(R_{k},S_{k+1}))=I_{s}\Bigl(\Pi_{\mathrm{C}}^{\Theta(s)}\bigl((f_{R_{k},\gamma})_{\#}I_{S_{k+1}}^{-1}(U(S_{k+1}))\bigr)\Bigr).

(103)

Taking conditional expectation with respect to the one-step transition and reward law under
\pi
gives exactly the Bellman expectation at state
s
. Applying the deterministic statewise projection and then the embedding
I_{s}
yields

\mathbb{E}\left[\widehat{T}_{\mathrm{C}}(U;s,(R_{k},S_{k+1}))\mid U_{k}=U,S_{k}=s\right]=I_{s}\bigl((\Pi_{\mathrm{C}}^{\Theta}T^{\pi}I_{\mathrm{C}}^{-1}U)(s)\bigr)=(\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{C}}U)(s).

(104)

∎

Proposition 20

(CTD samplewise Lipschitz continuity)
.

For every
s\in\mathcal{S}
, every
(r,s^{\prime})\in[0,1]\times\mathcal{S}
, and every
U,U^{\prime}\in I_{\mathrm{C}}(\mathcal{F}^{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{C},\Theta})
,

\left\lVert\widehat{T}_{\mathrm{C}}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))-\widehat{T}_{\mathrm{C}}(U^{\prime};s,(r,s^{\prime}))\right\rVert_{2}\leq\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{2,\infty}.

(105)

Consequently, if

\widehat{H}_{\mathrm{C}}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime})):=U+P_{s}\bigl(\widehat{T}_{\mathrm{C}}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))-U(s)\bigr),

(106)

then

\lVert\widehat{H}_{\mathrm{C}}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))-\widehat{H}_{\mathrm{C}}(U^{\prime};s,(r,s^{\prime}))\rVert_{2,\infty}\leq\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{2,\infty}.

(107)

Proof.

Fix
s\in\mathcal{S}
and
(r,s^{\prime})\in[0,1]\times\mathcal{S}
. Let

\mu:=I_{s^{\prime}}^{-1}(U(s^{\prime})),\qquad\nu:=I_{s^{\prime}}^{-1}(U^{\prime}(s^{\prime})).

(108)

Then

\widehat{T}_{\mathrm{C}}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))=I_{s}\bigl(\Pi_{\mathrm{C}}^{\Theta(s)}((f_{r,\gamma})_{\#}\mu)\bigr)

(109)

and similarly for
U^{\prime}
.
Using the statewise Cramér isometry, the nonexpansiveness of the categorical projection in the Cramér metric, and the deterministic pushforward contraction by
\sqrt{\gamma}
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\widehat{T}_{\mathrm{C}}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))-\widehat{T}_{\mathrm{C}}(U^{\prime};s,(r,s^{\prime}))\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle=\ell_{\mathrm{C},s}\bigl(\Pi_{\mathrm{C}}^{\Theta(s)}((f_{r,\gamma})_{\#}\mu),\Pi_{\mathrm{C}}^{\Theta(s)}((f_{r,\gamma})_{\#}\nu)\bigr)

(110)

\displaystyle\leq\ell_{\mathrm{C},s}((f_{r,\gamma})_{\#}\mu,(f_{r,\gamma})_{\#}\nu)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\gamma}\,\ell_{\mathrm{C},s^{\prime}}(\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\gamma}\,\lVert U(s^{\prime})-U^{\prime}(s^{\prime})\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{2,\infty}.

For the full-sample map, every block other than
s
is unchanged, while the
s
th block is replaced by the sampled target. Therefore,

\displaystyle\lVert\widehat{H}_{\mathrm{C}}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))-\widehat{H}_{\mathrm{C}}(U^{\prime};s,(r,s^{\prime}))\rVert_{2,\infty}=\max\Bigl\{\max_{x\neq s}\lVert U(x)-U^{\prime}(x)\rVert_{2},\lVert\widehat{T}_{\mathrm{C}}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))-\widehat{T}_{\mathrm{C}}(U^{\prime};s,(r,s^{\prime}))\rVert_{2}\Bigr\},

(111)

which is bounded by
\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{2,\infty}
by the previous result.
∎

B.3
Uniform perturbation bound

Proposition 21

(Uniform bound on embedded categorical blocks)
.

For every state
s\in\mathcal{S}
and every embedded categorical law
U(s)\in I_{s}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{C},\Theta(s)})
,

\lVert U(s)\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\theta_{d}(s)-\theta_{1}(s).

(112)

Proof.

Write

U(s)=I_{s}(\eta(s))=\bigl(\sqrt{\Delta_{1}(s)}\,c^{\eta}_{1}(s),\dots,\sqrt{\Delta_{d-1}(s)}\,c^{\eta}_{d-1}(s),0\bigr)^{\top}.

(113)

Because
\eta(s)
is a probability law, every cumulative mass
c^{\eta}_{j}(s)
lies in
[0,1]
. Hence

\lVert U(s)\rVert_{2}^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\Delta_{j}(s)\bigl(c^{\eta}_{j}(s)\bigr)^{2}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\Delta_{j}(s)=\theta_{d}(s)-\theta_{1}(s).

(114)

∎

Proposition 22

(CTD centered perturbation bound)
.

Let the CTD expected full-sample map be

F_{\mathrm{C}}(U,s):=U+P_{s}\bigl((\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{C}}U)(s)-U(s)\bigr)

(115)

and

\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{C}}:=\widehat{H}_{\mathrm{C}}(U_{k};S_{k},(R_{k},S_{k+1}))-F_{\mathrm{C}}(U_{k},S_{k}).

(116)

Then

\mathbb{E}\left[\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{C}}\,\mid\,\mathcal{G}_{k}\right]=0

(117)

and

\lVert\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{C}}\rVert_{2,\infty}\leq 2B_{\mathrm{C}}\qquad\text{a.s. for all }k,

(118)

where

B_{\mathrm{C}}:=\max_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\sqrt{\theta_{d}(s)-\theta_{1}(s)}.

(119)

Consequently,

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lVert\widehat{T}_{\mathrm{C}}(U_{k};S_{k},(R_{k},S_{k+1}))-(\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{C}}U_{k})(S_{k})\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\,\mid\,U_{k},\ S_{k}\right]\leq 4B_{\mathrm{C}}^{2}.

(120)

Proof.

The conditional mean identity follows from Proposition
19
. For the pathwise bound, only the
S_{k}
th block is nonzero, so

\lVert\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{C}}\rVert_{2,\infty}=\left\lVert\widehat{T}_{\mathrm{C}}(U_{k};S_{k},(R_{k},S_{k+1}))-(\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{C}}U_{k})(S_{k})\right\rVert_{2}.

(121)

Both terms on the right are embedded categorical laws at state
S_{k}
. By Proposition
21
, each has Euclidean norm at most
B_{\mathrm{C}}
. Therefore, the triangle inequality yields

\lVert\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{C}}\rVert_{2,\infty}\leq 2B_{\mathrm{C}}.

(122)

Squaring gives the conditional second-moment bound.
∎

B.4
Completion of the proof of Theorem
1

Proof of Theorem
1
.

We now verify the hypotheses of Appendix
A
for the discounted CTD recursion.

First, Proposition
18
gives the block-supremum contraction

\lVert\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{C}}U-\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{C}}U^{\prime}\rVert_{2,\infty}\leq\beta_{\mathrm{C}}\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{2,\infty},\qquad\beta_{\mathrm{C}}:=\sqrt{\gamma}.

(123)

Second, Proposition
20
gives the samplewise Lipschitz property in the Markovian setting with

A_{\mathrm{C},1}=1.

(124)

Moreover, the proof of Proposition
20
gives the stronger target-level estimate

\lVert\widehat{T}_{\mathrm{C}}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))-\widehat{T}_{\mathrm{C}}(U^{\prime};s,(r,s^{\prime}))\rVert_{2}\leq\beta_{\mathrm{C}}\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{2,\infty}\leq\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{2,\infty}.

(125)

Third, Proposition
22
gives the centered pathwise perturbation bound

\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{C}}\mid\mathcal{G}_{k}]=0,\qquad\lVert\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{C}}\rVert_{2,\infty}\leq 2B_{\mathrm{C}}\quad\text{a.s.},

(126)

so Proposition
16
applies with

A_{\mathrm{C},1}=1,\qquad A_{\mathrm{C},2}=0,\qquad B_{\mathrm{C},2}=2B_{\mathrm{C}},\qquad A_{\mathrm{C}}:=A_{\mathrm{C},1}+A_{\mathrm{C},2}+1=2.

(127)

In particular, the i.i.d. conditional second-moment condition in Proposition
15
holds with

A_{\mathrm{C}}^{\mathrm{iid}}:=4B_{\mathrm{C}}^{2}.

(128)

Applying Proposition
15
with

\beta=\beta_{\mathrm{C}},\qquad A^{\mathrm{iid}}=A_{\mathrm{C}}^{\mathrm{iid}},\qquad\vartheta=\vartheta_{\mathrm{C},\rho},\qquad p^{\star}=\max\{2,\lceil\log\lvert\mathcal{S}\rvert\rceil\},

(129)

yields Theorem
1
(i) with

a_{\mathrm{C},1}^{\mathrm{iid}}:=\frac{1+\vartheta_{\mathrm{C},\rho}\lvert\mathcal{S}\rvert^{2/p^{\star}}}{1+\vartheta_{\mathrm{C},\rho}},\qquad a_{\mathrm{C},2}^{\mathrm{iid}}:=1-\bar{\beta}_{\mathrm{C},\rho}\sqrt{\frac{1+\vartheta_{\mathrm{C},\rho}\lvert\mathcal{S}\rvert^{2/p^{\star}}}{1+\vartheta_{\mathrm{C},\rho}}},

(130)

where

\bar{\beta}_{\mathrm{C},\rho}:=1-\rho_{\min}(1-\beta_{\mathrm{C}}),

s

(148)

Proposition 23

(Statewise MMD isometry)
.

For every state
s\in\mathcal{S}
and all signed-categorical laws
\mu,\nu
supported on
\Theta(s)

\mathrm{MMD}_{\kappa}(\mu,\nu)^{2}=\lVert I_{s}(\mu)-I_{s}(\nu)\rVert_{2}^{2}.

(150)

Consequently, with

I_{\mathrm{M}}(\eta):=(I_{s}(\eta(s)))_{s\in\mathcal{S}},

(151)

one has

\lVert I_{\mathrm{M}}(\eta)-I_{\mathrm{M}}(\eta^{\prime})\rVert_{2,\infty}=\ell_{\mathrm{M},\infty}(\eta,\eta^{\prime}).

(152)

Proof.

Write

\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{d}p_{i}\delta_{\theta_{i}(s)},\qquad\nu=\sum_{i=1}^{d}q_{i}\delta_{\theta_{i}(s)}.

(153)

Then

\mu-\nu=\sum_{i=1}^{d}(p_{i}-q_{i})\delta_{\theta_{i}(s)}.

(154)

By the definition of MMD,

\mathrm{MMD}_{\kappa}(\mu,\nu)^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}(p_{i}-q_{i})(p_{j}-q_{j})\kappa(\theta_{i}(s),\theta_{j}(s))=(p-q)^{\top}K_{s}(p-q).

(155)

Since

is positive semidefinite,

(p-q)^{\top}K_{s}(p-q)=\lVert K_{s}^{1/2}(p-q)\rVert_{2}^{2}=\lVert I_{s}(\mu)-I_{s}(\nu)\rVert_{2}^{2}.

(156)

Taking the maximum over
s\in\mathcal{S}
gives the product isometry.

Proposition 25

(Embedded MTD projection as an affine Euclidean projector)
.

For each state
s\in\mathcal{S}
, let

\mathcal{A}_{s}:=K_{s}^{1/2}\mathbb{R}_{1}^{d}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(161)

For every mass-
1
finite signed measure
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}^{q}
, define

b_{s}(\nu):=\left(\int\kappa(\theta_{i}(s),y)\,d\nu(y)\right)_{i=1}^{d}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

(162)

and

\zeta_{s}(\nu):=K_{s}^{\dagger/2}b_{s}(\nu),

(163)

where
K_{s}^{\dagger}
is the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse of
K_{s}
. Then
b_{s}(\nu)\in\operatorname{range}(K_{s})
and

I_{\mathrm{M},s}\bigl(\Pi_{\mathrm{M}}^{\Theta(s)}\nu\bigr)=\operatorname{proj}_{\mathcal{A}_{s}}\bigl(\zeta_{s}(\nu)\bigr),

(164)

where
\operatorname{proj}_{\mathcal{A}_{s}}
is the Euclidean orthogonal projection onto
\mathcal{A}_{s}
. Consequently, the map

J_{s}(\nu):=I_{\mathrm{M},s}\bigl(\Pi_{\mathrm{M}}^{\Theta(s)}\nu\bigr)

(165)

is affine on the affine space of mass-
1
finite signed measures.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}
be the RKHS of
\kappa
, let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{q}\to\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}
denote the feature map, and define the linear operator

\Phi_{s}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathcal{H}_{\kappa},\qquad\Phi_{s}p:=\sum_{i=1}^{d}p_{i}\varphi(\theta_{i}(s)).

(166)

Then

K_{s}=\Phi_{s}^{*}\Phi_{s}.

(167)

For a mass-
1
finite signed measure
\nu
, define its kernel mean embedding by

m_{\nu}:=\int\varphi(y)\,d\nu(y)\in\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}.

(168)

Let
P_{s}
denote the orthogonal projection in
\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}
onto
\operatorname{range}(\Phi_{s})
. Since
\Phi_{s}^{*}(I-P_{s})=0
, we have

b_{s}(\nu)=\Phi_{s}^{*}m_{\nu}=\Phi_{s}^{*}P_{s}m_{\nu}\in\operatorname{range}(\Phi_{s}^{*}\Phi_{s})=\operatorname{range}(K_{s}).

(169)

Now fix
p\in\mathbb{R}_{1}^{d}
and write

\mu_{p}:=\sum_{i=1}^{d}p_{i}\delta_{\theta_{i}(s)}.

(170)

By the reproducing-kernel representation of MMD,

\mathrm{MMD}_{\kappa}(\mu_{p},\nu)^{2}=\lVert\Phi_{s}p-m_{\nu}\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}}^{2}=\lVert\Phi_{s}p-P_{s}m_{\nu}\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}}^{2}+\lVert(I-P_{s})m_{\nu}\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}}^{2}.

(171)

We next identify
P_{s}m_{\nu}
in coefficient form. Since
K_{s}=\Phi_{s}^{*}\Phi_{s}
, the standard Moore-Penrose identity gives

\Phi_{s}K_{s}^{\dagger}\Phi_{s}^{*}=P_{s}.

(172)

Therefore,

\Phi_{s}\bigl(K_{s}^{\dagger}b_{s}(\nu)\bigr)=\Phi_{s}K_{s}^{\dagger}\Phi_{s}^{*}m_{\nu}=P_{s}m_{\nu}.

(173)

Substituting this into the previous display yields

\lVert\Phi_{s}p-P_{s}m_{\nu}\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}}=\lVert\Phi_{s}(p-K_{s}^{\dagger}b_{s}(\nu))\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}}.

(174)

Since
\lVert\Phi_{s}x\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}}^{2}=x^{\top}K_{s}x=\lVert K_{s}^{1/2}x\rVert_{2}^{2}
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we obtain

\lVert\Phi_{s}p-P_{s}m_{\nu}\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}}=\lVert K_{s}^{1/2}(p-K_{s}^{\dagger}b_{s}(\nu))\rVert_{2}=\lVert K_{s}^{1/2}p-K_{s}^{1/2}K_{s}^{\dagger}b_{s}(\nu)\rVert_{2}.

(175)

Because
K_{s}
is symmetric positive semidefinite, spectral calculus gives

K_{s}^{1/2}K_{s}^{\dagger}=K_{s}^{\dagger/2}.

(176)

Hence

\lVert\Phi_{s}p-P_{s}m_{\nu}\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}}=\lVert K_{s}^{1/2}p-K_{s}^{\dagger/2}b_{s}(\nu)\rVert_{2}=\lVert K_{s}^{1/2}p-\zeta_{s}(\nu)\rVert_{2}.

(177)

Therefore

\mathrm{MMD}_{\kappa}(\mu_{p},\nu)^{2}=\lVert K_{s}^{1/2}p-\zeta_{s}(\nu)\rVert_{2}^{2}+c_{s}(\nu),

(178)

where

c_{s}(\nu):=\lVert(I-P_{s})m_{\nu}\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}}^{2}

(179)

does not depend on
p
.

Minimizing over
p\in\mathbb{R}_{1}^{d}
is therefore equivalent to Euclidean projection of
\zeta_{s}(\nu)
onto

\mathcal{A}_{s}=K_{s}^{1/2}\mathbb{R}_{1}^{d}.

(180)

Since
I_{\mathrm{M},s}\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{d}p_{i}\delta_{\theta_{i}(s)}\bigr)=K_{s}^{1/2}p
, it follows that

I_{\mathrm{M},s}\bigl(\Pi_{\mathrm{M}}^{\Theta(s)}\nu\bigr)=\operatorname{proj}_{\mathcal{A}_{s}}\bigl(\zeta_{s}(\nu)\bigr).

(181)

Finally,
\nu\mapsto b_{s}(\nu)
is linear, hence so is
\nu\mapsto\zeta_{s}(\nu)=K_{s}^{\dagger/2}b_{s}(\nu)
. Since orthogonal projection onto an affine subspace is an affine map, the composition

J_{s}(\nu)=\operatorname{proj}_{\mathcal{A}_{s}}\bigl(\zeta_{s}(\nu)\bigr)

(182)

is affine on the affine space of mass-
1
finite signed measures.

Appendix D
Undiscounted fixed-horizon CTD

This appendix records the undiscounted fixed-horizon CTD ingredients culminating in the proof of Theorem
6
.

D.1
Flattened weighted horizon-state space

To apply the framework of Appendix
A
, we rewrite the weighted fixed-horizon recursion on a flattened product space indexed by horizon-state pairs. Let

\mathcal{S}_{H}:=\{(h,s):h\in\{1,\dots,H\},\ s\in\mathcal{S}\},\qquad\lvert\mathcal{S}_{H}\rvert=H\lvert\mathcal{S}\rvert.

(233)

Let

V_{H,\mathrm{C}}:=\prod_{(h,s)\in\mathcal{S}_{H}}\mathbb{R}^{d}

(234)

denote the corresponding flattened Euclidean product space. For a horizon-stacked iterate
U=(U^{h}(s))_{1\leq h\leq H,\ s\in\mathcal{S}}
, define the weighted flattening

\bar{U}(h,s):=\lambda^{h}U^{h}(s),\qquad(h,s)\in\mathcal{S}_{H}.

(235)

Then

\lVert U\rVert_{H,2,\infty}=\max_{(h,s)\in\mathcal{S}_{H}}\lVert\bar{U}(h,s)\rVert_{2},\qquad\lVert U\rVert_{H,2,p}=\left(\sum_{(h,s)\in\mathcal{S}_{H}}\lVert\bar{U}(h,s)\rVert_{2}^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

(236)

For a single horizon stack
U=(U^{1},\dots,U^{H})
, also define

\lVert U\rVert_{H,2}:=\max_{1\leq h\leq H}\lambda^{h}\lVert u^{h}\rVert_{2}.

(237)

Then

\lVert U\rVert_{H,2,\infty}=\max_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\lVert U(s)\rVert_{H,2}.

(238)

Thus the fixed-horizon weighted norm is an ordinary block-supremum norm on a finite product of Euclidean blocks, and Appendix
A
applies with
\lvert\mathcal{S}_{H}\rvert
in place of
\lvert\mathcal{S}\rvert
. In particular, throughout this appendix we set

p_{H}^{\star}:=\max\{2,\lceil\log\lvert\mathcal{S}_{H}\rvert\rceil\}.

(239)

D.2
Fixed-horizon CTD residual map

For each state
s\in\mathcal{S}
, let

U(s):=\bigl(U^{1}(s),\dots,U^{H}(s)\bigr)

(240)

denote the horizon stack at state
s
, and let
P_{s}^{H}
denote the coordinate projector onto that stack. Define

F_{H,\mathrm{C}}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime})):=P_{s}^{H}\bigl(\widehat{T}_{H,\mathrm{C}}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))-U(s)\bigr),

(241)

where
\widehat{T}_{H,\mathrm{C}}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))
is the full horizon-stacked CTD target from Section
4
. Then the online fixed-horizon CTD recursion is

U_{k+1}=U_{k}+\alpha_{k}F_{H,\mathrm{C}}(U_{k};S_{k},(R_{k},S_{k+1})).

(242)

Define also the CTD embedded averaged operator by

\mathcal{O}_{H,\mathrm{C}}:=I_{H,\mathrm{C}}\circ\Pi_{H,\mathrm{C}}^{\Theta}T_{H}^{\pi}\circ I_{H,\mathrm{C}}^{-1}.

(243)

Proposition 29

(Fixed-horizon CTD weighted contraction)
.

For all
U,U^{\prime}\in I_{H,\mathrm{C}}(\mathcal{F}_{H,\mathrm{C},\Theta}^{\mathcal{S}})
,

\lVert\mathcal{O}_{H,\mathrm{C}}U-\mathcal{O}_{H,\mathrm{C}}U^{\prime}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq\lambda\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}.

(244)

Proof.

Let
\eta:=I_{H,\mathrm{C}}^{-1}(U)
and
\eta^{\prime}:=I_{H,\mathrm{C}}^{-1}(U^{\prime})
. Fix
(h,s)\in\mathcal{S}_{H}
with
h\geq 1
. By the statewise CTD isometry and nonexpansiveness of the categorical projection in the Cramér metric,

\displaystyle\lambda^{h}\lVert(\mathcal{O}_{H,\mathrm{C}}U)^{h}(s)-(\mathcal{O}_{H,\mathrm{C}}U^{\prime})^{h}(s)\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{h}\ell_{\mathrm{C}}\left((\Pi_{H,\mathrm{C}}^{\Theta}T_{H}^{\pi}\eta)^{h}(s),(\Pi_{H,\mathrm{C}}^{\Theta}T_{H}^{\pi}\eta^{\prime})^{h}(s)\right)

(245)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{h}\ell_{\mathrm{C}}\left((T_{H}^{\pi}\eta)^{h}(s),(T_{H}^{\pi}\eta^{\prime})^{h}(s)\right).

Now

(T_{H}^{\pi}\eta)^{h}(s)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi(a\mid s)\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}P(s^{\prime}\mid s,a)\,(f_{R(s,a),1})_{\#}\eta^{h-1}(s^{\prime}),

(246)

and the same formula holds for
\eta^{\prime}
. Since translation preserves the Cramér metric and the Cramér metric is convex under mixtures,

\displaystyle\ell_{\mathrm{C}}\left((T_{H}^{\pi}\eta)^{h}(s),(T_{H}^{\pi}\eta^{\prime})^{h}(s)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi(a\mid s)\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}P(s^{\prime}\mid s,a)\,\ell_{\mathrm{C}}\left(\eta^{h-1}(s^{\prime}),{\eta^{\prime}}^{h-1}(s^{\prime})\right)

(247)

\displaystyle\leq\max_{x\in\mathcal{S}}\ell_{\mathrm{C}}\left(\eta^{h-1}(x),{\eta^{\prime}}^{h-1}(x)\right).

Therefore

\displaystyle\lambda^{h}\lVert(\mathcal{O}_{H,\mathrm{C}}U)^{h}(s)-(\mathcal{O}_{H,\mathrm{C}}U^{\prime})^{h}(s)\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\max_{x\in\mathcal{S}}\lambda^{h-1}\ell_{\mathrm{C}}\left(\eta^{h-1}(x),{\eta^{\prime}}^{h-1}(x)\right)

(248)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\ell_{H,\mathrm{C},\infty}(\eta,\eta^{\prime})=\lambda\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}.

Taking the maximum over
(h,s)\in\mathcal{S}_{H}
proves the claim.
∎

D.3
One-step bounds

Proposition 30

(Fixed-horizon CTD samplewise Lipschitz continuity)
.

For every
s\in\mathcal{S}
,
(r,s^{\prime})\in[0,1]\times\mathcal{S}
and
U,U^{\prime}\in I_{H,\mathrm{C}}(\mathcal{F}_{H,\mathrm{C},\Theta}^{\mathcal{S}})
,

\lVert F_{H,\mathrm{C}}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))-F_{H,\mathrm{C}}(U^{\prime};s,(r,s^{\prime}))\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq 2\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}.

(249)

Proof.

For each horizon
h
, the local target
\widehat{T}_{H,\mathrm{C}}^{h}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))
depends only on the
(h-1)
st horizon block at
s^{\prime}
. By the discounted CTD samplewise Lipschitz bound, that local target is
1
-Lipschitz in the Euclidean block norm. Multiplying by
\lambda^{h}
and using the fact that
\lambda\in(0,1)
gives

\lambda^{h}\lVert\widehat{T}_{H,\mathrm{C}}^{h}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))-\widehat{T}_{H,\mathrm{C}}^{h}(U^{\prime};s,(r,s^{\prime}))\rVert_{2}\leq\lambda^{h-1}\lVert U^{h-1}(s^{\prime})-U^{\prime\,h-1}(s^{\prime})\rVert_{2}\leq\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}.

(250)

Taking the maximum over
h
yields

\lVert\widehat{T}_{H,\mathrm{C}}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))-\widehat{T}_{H,\mathrm{C}}(U^{\prime};s,(r,s^{\prime}))\rVert_{H,2}\leq\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}.

(251)

Since
F_{H,\mathrm{C}}
is the projected residual

F_{H,\mathrm{C}}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))=P_{s}^{H}\bigl(\widehat{T}_{H,\mathrm{C}}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))-U(s)\bigr),

(252)

the difference
F_{H,\mathrm{C}}(U)-F_{H,\mathrm{C}}(U^{\prime})
splits into a projected target difference and a projected current-stack difference. Each is bounded by
\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}
, yielding the factor
2
through triangle inequality.
∎

Proposition 31

(Fixed-horizon CTD pathwise boundedness)
.

For every
s\in\mathcal{S}
,
(r,s^{\prime})\in[0,1]\times\mathcal{S}
and
U\in I_{H,\mathrm{C}}(\mathcal{F}_{H,\mathrm{C},\Theta}^{\mathcal{S}})
,

\lVert F_{H,\mathrm{C}}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq 2B_{H,\mathrm{C}},

(253)

where

B_{H,\mathrm{C}}:=\max_{1\leq h\leq H,\ s\in\mathcal{S}}\lambda^{h}\sqrt{\theta_{h,d}(s)-\theta_{h,1}(s)}.

(254)

Proof.

Fix
h
and
s
. Both the local target
\widehat{T}_{H,\mathrm{C}}^{h}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))
and the current iterate
U^{h}(s)
are embedded categorical laws supported on
\Theta_{h}(s)
. By the statewise CTD support radius bound, each has Euclidean norm at most
\sqrt{\theta_{h,d}(s)-\theta_{h,1}(s)}
. After weighting by
\lambda^{h}
, both are bounded by
B_{H,\mathrm{C}}
. The triangle inequality then yields the claim.
∎

D.4
Phasewise averaged maps

Recall the definition of phase distributions from Section
4
:

\rho_{t}(s):=\Pr(S_{t}=s),\qquad 0\leq t\leq H-1,\ s\in\mathcal{S}.

(255)

For each phase
t\in\{0,\dots,H-1\}
, define

\Gamma_{t,\mathrm{C}}(U):=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\rho_{t}(s)\,P_{s}^{H}\bigl((\mathcal{O}_{H,\mathrm{C}}U)(s)-U(s)\bigr),\qquad G_{t,\mathrm{C}}(U):=U+\Gamma_{t,\mathrm{C}}(U).

(256)

Proposition 32

(Phasewise averaged contraction)
.

For each phase
t\in\{0,\dots,H-1\}
,

\lVert G_{t,\mathrm{C}}(U)-G_{t,\mathrm{C}}(U^{\prime})\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq\bar{\beta}_{t}^{\mathrm{fh}}\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{H,2,\infty},

(257)

where

\bar{\beta}_{t}^{\mathrm{fh}}:=1-\rho_{t,\min}(1-\lambda),\qquad\rho_{t,\min}:=\min_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\rho_{t}(s).

(258)

Hence, with

\bar{\beta}_{H,\mathrm{C}}^{\mathrm{fh}}:=1-\rho_{\min}(1-\lambda),\qquad\rho_{\min}:=\min_{0\leq t\leq H-1,\ s\in\mathcal{S}}\rho_{t}(s),

(259)

we have

\lVert G_{t,\mathrm{C}}(U)-G_{t,\mathrm{C}}(U^{\prime})\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq\bar{\beta}_{H,\mathrm{C}}^{\mathrm{fh}}\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\qquad\text{for all }t.

(260)

Proof.

At phase
t
, the update acts as the identity on all blocks
x
such that
x\neq s
and as the
\lambda
-contractive projected Bellman map on the block
s
. More precisely, for each state block
s
,

\bigl(G_{t,\mathrm{C}}(U)-G_{t,\mathrm{C}}(U^{\prime})\bigr)(s)=(1-\rho_{t}(s))(U(s)-U^{\prime}(s))\,+\,\rho_{t}(s)\bigl((\mathcal{O}_{H,\mathrm{C}}U)(s)-(\mathcal{O}_{H,\mathrm{C}}U^{\prime})(s)\bigr).

(261)

By Proposition
29
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\bigl(G_{t,\mathrm{C}}(U)-G_{t,\mathrm{C}}(U^{\prime})\bigr)(s)\right\rVert_{H,2}

\displaystyle\leq\bigl((1-\rho_{t}(s))+\rho_{t}(s)\lambda\bigr)\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}

(262)

\displaystyle=\bigl(1-\rho_{t}(s)(1-\lambda)\bigr)\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}.

Taking the maximum over
s
on both sides gives

\displaystyle\lVert G_{t,\mathrm{C}}(U)-G_{t,\mathrm{C}}(U^{\prime})\rVert_{H,2,\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\bigl(1-\rho_{t}(s)(1-\lambda)\bigr)\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}

(263)

\displaystyle=\bigl(1-\rho_{t,\min}(1-\lambda)\bigr)\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{H,2,\infty},

since the coefficient
1-\rho_{t}(s)(1-\lambda)
is decreasing in
\rho_{t}(s)
. This proves the first displayed bound with coefficient
\bar{\beta}_{t}^{\mathrm{fh}}
. For the uniform estimate, note that
\rho_{t,\min}\geq\rho_{\min}
for every phase
t
, hence

\bar{\beta}_{t}^{\mathrm{fh}}=1-\rho_{t,\min}(1-\lambda)\leq 1-\rho_{\min}(1-\lambda)=\bar{\beta}_{H,\mathrm{C}}^{\mathrm{fh}}.

(264)

Substituting this larger phase-independent coefficient into the preceding bound yields the claimed uniform contraction factor.
∎

D.5
Auxiliary phasewise bounds

For episode
m
, write

U_{m,t}:=U_{mH+t},\qquad t=0,\dots,H.

(265)

Also define the phasewise filtration

\mathcal{F}_{m,t}:=\sigma\bigl(U_{m,0},(S_{u}^{m},R_{u}^{m},S_{u+1}^{m})_{0\leq u<t}\bigr),\qquad 0\leq t\leq H,

(266)

where the superscript
m
indicates transitions within episode
m
. Then
U_{m,t}
is
\mathcal{F}_{m,t}
-measurable for every
t
.

Lemma 33

(Within-episode deviation)
.

For all
m
and all
t\in\{0,\dots,H\}
,

\lVert U_{m,t}-U_{m,0}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq 2B_{H,\mathrm{C}}\sum_{u=0}^{t-1}\alpha_{mH+u}.

(267)

Proof.

From the recursion,

U_{m,t}-U_{m,0}=\sum_{u=0}^{t-1}\alpha_{mH+u}F_{H,\mathrm{C}}\bigl(U_{m,u};S_{u}^{m},(R_{u}^{m},S_{u+1}^{m})\bigr).

(268)

Taking
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{H,2,\infty}
and using Proposition
31
term by term gives

\lVert U_{m,t}-U_{m,0}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq\sum_{u=0}^{t-1}\alpha_{mH+u}\,\lVert F_{H,\mathrm{C}}(U_{m,u};S_{u}^{m},(R_{u}^{m},S_{u+1}^{m}))\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq 2B_{H,\mathrm{C}}\sum_{u=0}^{t-1}\alpha_{mH+u}.

(269)

∎

Lemma 34

(Frozen residual decomposition)
.

For each phase
t\in\{0,\dots,H-1\}
, define

\zeta_{m,t}^{\mathrm{C}}:=F_{H,\mathrm{C}}(U_{m,0};S_{t}^{m},(R_{t}^{m},S_{t+1}^{m}))-\Gamma_{t,\mathrm{C}}(U_{m,0})

(270)

and

\xi_{m,t}^{\mathrm{C}}:=\Bigl(F_{H,\mathrm{C}}(U_{m,t};S_{t}^{m},(R_{t}^{m},S_{t+1}^{m}))-F_{H,\mathrm{C}}(U_{m,0};S_{t}^{m},(R_{t}^{m},S_{t+1}^{m}))\Bigr)-\Bigl(\Gamma_{t,\mathrm{C}}(U_{m,t})-\Gamma_{t,\mathrm{C}}(U_{m,0})\Bigr).

(271)

Then

\begin{gathered}F_{H,\mathrm{C}}(U_{m,t};S_{t}^{m},(R_{t}^{m},S_{t+1}^{m}))-\Gamma_{t,\mathrm{C}}(U_{m,t})=\zeta_{m,t}^{\mathrm{C}}+\xi_{m,t}^{\mathrm{C}},\\
\mathbb{E}\left[\zeta_{m,t}^{\mathrm{C}}\,\mid\,U_{m,0}\right]=0,\qquad\lVert\zeta_{m,t}^{\mathrm{C}}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq 4B_{H,\mathrm{C}},\end{gathered}

(272)

and

\lVert\xi_{m,t}^{\mathrm{C}}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq 4\lVert U_{m,t}-U_{m,0}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq 8B_{H,\mathrm{C}}\sum_{u=0}^{t-1}\alpha_{mH+u}\leq 8B_{H,\mathrm{C}}\sum_{u=0}^{H-1}\alpha_{mH+u}.

(273)

Proof.

Since episode
m
is independent of
U_{m,0}
and its phase-
t
sample has marginal law
\rho_{t}
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\zeta_{m,t}^{\mathrm{C}}\,\mid\,U_{m,0}\right]=0.

(274)

\lVert\zeta_{m,t}^{\mathrm{C}}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq\lVert F_{H,\mathrm{C}}(U_{m,0};S_{t}^{m},(R_{t}^{m},S_{t+1}^{m}))\rVert_{H,2,\infty}+\lVert\Gamma_{t,\mathrm{C}}(U_{m,0})\rVert_{H,2,\infty}.

(275)

By Proposition
31
,

\lVert F_{H,\mathrm{C}}(U_{m,0};S_{t}^{m},(R_{t}^{m},S_{t+1}^{m}))\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq 2B_{H,\mathrm{C}},

(276)

and, since
\Gamma_{t,\mathrm{C}}(U_{m,0})
is a convex combination of such residuals,

\lVert\Gamma_{t,\mathrm{C}}(U_{m,0})\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq 2B_{H,\mathrm{C}}.

(277)

This gives
\lVert\zeta_{m,t}^{\mathrm{C}}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq 4B_{H,\mathrm{C}}
.

Proposition
30
also gives

\left\lVert F_{H,\mathrm{C}}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))-F_{H,\mathrm{C}}(U^{\prime};s,(r,s^{\prime}))\right\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq 2\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}

(278)

for every admissible
(s,r,s^{\prime})
. Averaging the same estimate over the phase-

\left\lVert\Gamma_{t,\mathrm{C}}(U)-\Gamma_{t,\mathrm{C}}(U^{\prime})\right\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq 2\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}.

(279)

Therefore

\lVert\xi_{m,t}^{\mathrm{C}}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq 4\lVert U_{m,t}-U_{m,0}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}.

(280)

Applying Lemma

yields the remaining bound.

D.6
Episode-level finite-iteration drift

Since
\Gamma_{t,\mathrm{C}}
is defined by averaging with respect to the phase-

marginal
\rho_{t}
, the drift argument is formulated for the episode-boundary sequence
(U_{mH})_{m\geq 0}
conditioned under

Let

W_{m,0}:=U_{m,0}-U^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{C}},\qquad U^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{C}}:=I_{H,\mathrm{C}}(\eta_{H,\mathrm{C}}^{\star}),

(281)

where
\eta_{H,\mathrm{C}}^{\star}
denotes the unique fixed point of
\Pi_{H,\mathrm{C}}^{\Theta}T_{H}^{\pi}
, and define

\bar{\alpha}_{m}:=\sum_{u=0}^{H-1}\alpha_{mH+u},\qquad\bar{\alpha}_{m}^{(2)}:=\sum_{u=0}^{H-1}\alpha_{mH+u}^{2}.

(282)

Also write

\lvert\mathcal{S}_{H}\rvert:=H\lvert\mathcal{S}\rvert,\quad\bar{\beta}_{H,\mathrm{C}}^{\mathrm{fh}}:=\max_{0\leq t\leq H-1}\bar{\beta}_{t}^{\mathrm{fh}}=1-\rho_{\min}(1-\lambda),\quad\kappa_{H,\mathrm{C}}:=1-\bar{\beta}_{H,\mathrm{C}}^{\mathrm{fh}}=\rho_{\min}(1-\lambda)>0.

(283)

Fix any
\vartheta_{H,\mathrm{C}}>0
and define the smoothed potential

M_{H,\mathrm{C}}(W):=\inf_{Z\in V_{H,\mathrm{C}}}\left\{\frac{1}{2}\lVert Z\rVert^{2}_{H,2,\infty}+\frac{1}{2\vartheta_{H,\mathrm{C}}}\lVert W-Z\rVert^{2}_{H,2,p^{\star}_{H}}\right\}.

(284)

By Proposition
13
on the flattened weighted space,

(1+\vartheta_{H,\mathrm{C}})M_{H,\mathrm{C}}(W)\leq\frac{1}{2}\lVert W\rVert^{2}_{H,2,\infty}\leq\bigl(1+\vartheta_{H,\mathrm{C}}\lvert\mathcal{S}_{H}\rvert^{2/p^{\star}_{H}}\bigr)M_{H,\mathrm{C}}(W).

(285)

Define

r^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}:=\frac{1+\vartheta_{H,\mathrm{C}}\lvert\mathcal{S}_{H}\rvert^{2/p^{\star}_{H}}}{1+\vartheta_{H,\mathrm{C}}},\qquad L^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}:=\frac{p^{\star}_{H}-1}{\vartheta_{H,\mathrm{C}}},\qquad\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}:=1-\bar{\beta}^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}\sqrt{r^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}},

(286)

Since
r^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}\to 1
as
\vartheta_{H,\mathrm{C}}\downarrow 0
and
\bar{\beta}^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}<1
, we have
\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}>0
for all sufficiently small
\vartheta_{H,\mathrm{C}}
. Define the explicit CTD constants

d^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}:=8L^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}\lvert\mathcal{S}_{H}\rvert^{2/p^{\star}_{H}}(1+\vartheta_{H,\mathrm{C}}),\qquad\bar{\alpha}_{H,\mathrm{C}}:=\min\left\{\frac{1}{H},\frac{\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}}{d^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}}\right\},

(287)

and

c^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C},1}:=\frac{\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}}{4},\qquad c^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C},2}:=HL^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}\lvert\mathcal{S}_{H}\rvert^{2/p^{\star}_{H}}B^{2}_{H,\mathrm{C}}\left(\frac{128}{\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}}+72\right).

(288)

Proposition 35

(Episodewise CTD potential drift)
.

Assume that
\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}>0
, that
(\alpha_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is nonincreasing, and that

\alpha_{0}\leq\bar{\alpha}_{H,\mathrm{C}}.

(289)

Then, for every episode
m
,

\mathbb{E}\left[M_{H,\mathrm{C}}(W_{m+1,0})\mid U_{m,0}\right]\leq\bigl(1-c^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C},1}\bar{\alpha}_{m}\bigr)M_{H,\mathrm{C}}(W_{m,0})+c^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C},2}\bar{\alpha}^{(2)}_{m}.

(290)

Proof.

For each phase
t\in\{0,\dots,H-1\}
and each
\alpha\in[0,1]
, define

A_{t,\alpha,\mathrm{C}}(U):=U+\alpha\Gamma_{t,\mathrm{C}}(U)=(1-\alpha)U+\alpha G_{t,\mathrm{C}}(U).

(291)

By Proposition
32
,

\left\lVert G_{t,\mathrm{C}}(U)-G_{t,\mathrm{C}}(U^{\prime})\right\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq\bar{\beta}^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}.

(292)

Hence, by convexity of the norm,

\lVert A_{t,\alpha,\mathrm{C}}(U)-A_{t,\alpha,\mathrm{C}}(U^{\prime})\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq\bigl((1-\alpha)+\alpha\bar{\beta}^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}\bigr)\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}=\bigl(1-\kappa_{H,\mathrm{C}}\alpha\bigr)\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}.

(293)

Define the deterministic phasewise averaged trajectory over episode
m
by

\bar{U}_{m,0}:=U_{m,0},\qquad\bar{U}_{m,t+1}:=A_{t,\alpha_{mH+t},\mathrm{C}}(\bar{U}_{m,t}),\qquad 0\leq t\leq H-1.

(294)

Since
G_{t,\mathrm{C}}(U^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{C}})=U^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{C}}
, the contraction estimate above, the Moreau-envelope comparison, and the smoothness of the envelope give the deterministic relaxed-map estimate

M_{H,\mathrm{C}}\bigl(A_{t,\alpha,\mathrm{C}}(U)-U^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{C}}\bigr)\leq(1-2\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}\alpha+d^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}\alpha^{2})M_{H,\mathrm{C}}(U-U^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{C}}).

(295)

Since
\alpha_{mH+t}\leq\alpha_{0}\leq\bar{\alpha}_{H,\mathrm{C}}\leq\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}/d^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}
, this implies

M_{H,\mathrm{C}}\bigl(A_{t,\alpha_{mH+t},\mathrm{C}}(U)-U^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{C}}\bigr)\leq\bigl(1-\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}\alpha_{mH+t}\bigr)M_{H,\mathrm{C}}\bigl(U-U^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{C}}\bigr).

(296)

Therefore

M_{H,\mathrm{C}}(\bar{U}_{m,H}-U^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{C}})\leq\prod_{u=0}^{H-1}(1-\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}\alpha_{mH+u})M_{H,\mathrm{C}}(W_{m,0})\leq\left(1-\frac{\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}}{2}\bar{\alpha}_{m}\right)M_{H,\mathrm{C}}(W_{m,0}).

(297)

Next, define the approximation error

D_{m,t}:=U_{m,t}-\bar{U}_{m,t},\qquad D_{m,0}=0.

(298)

Because each horizonwise CTD target is obtained by linear interpolation on a fixed support, the map
U\mapsto\widehat{T}_{H,\mathrm{C}}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))
is affine for every admissible
(s,r,s^{\prime})
. Hence,
F_{H,\mathrm{C}}
,
\Gamma_{t,\mathrm{C}}
and
A_{t,\alpha,\mathrm{C}}
are all affine maps. Let
L_{m,t,\mathrm{C}}
denote the linear part of
A_{t,\alpha_{mH+t},\mathrm{C}}
. Then

A_{t,\alpha_{mH+t},\mathrm{C}}(U)-A_{t,\alpha_{mH+t},\mathrm{C}}(U^{\prime})=L_{m,t,\mathrm{C}}(U-U^{\prime}),

(299)

and the contraction bound implies that, for every admissible
Z
,

\lVert L_{m,t,\mathrm{C}}(Z)\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq(1-\kappa_{H,\mathrm{C}}\alpha_{mH+t})\lVert Z\rVert_{H,2,\infty}.

(300)

Using the recursion for
U_{m,t+1}
, the definition of
\bar{U}_{m,t+1}
, and the decomposition from Lemma
34
, we obtain

\begin{gathered}D_{m,t+1}=U_{m,t+1}-\bar{U}_{m,t+1}\\
=U_{m,t}+\alpha_{mH+t}F_{H,\mathrm{C}}\bigl(U_{m,t};S^{m}_{t},(R^{m}_{t},S^{m}_{t+1})\bigr)-\bar{U}_{m,t}-\alpha_{mH+t}\Gamma_{t,\mathrm{C}}(\bar{U}_{m,t})\\
=U_{m,t}-\bar{U}_{m,t}+\alpha_{mH+t}\bigl(\Gamma_{t,\mathrm{C}}(U_{m,t})-\Gamma_{t,\mathrm{C}}(\bar{U}_{m,t})\bigr)\\
+\alpha_{mH+t}\Bigl(F_{H,\mathrm{C}}\bigl(U_{m,t};S^{m}_{t},(R^{m}_{t},S^{m}_{t+1})\bigr)-\Gamma_{t,\mathrm{C}}(U_{m,t})\Bigr)\\
=A_{t,\alpha_{mH+t},\mathrm{C}}(U_{m,t})-A_{t,\alpha_{mH+t},\mathrm{C}}(\bar{U}_{m,t})+\alpha_{mH+t}\Bigl(F_{H,\mathrm{C}}\bigl(U_{m,t};S^{m}_{t},(R^{m}_{t},S^{m}_{t+1})\bigr)-\Gamma_{t,\mathrm{C}}(U_{m,t})\Bigr)\\
=L_{m,t,\mathrm{C}}(D_{m,t})+\alpha_{mH+t}\zeta^{\mathrm{C}}_{m,t}+\alpha_{mH+t}\xi^{\mathrm{C}}_{m,t}.\end{gathered}

(301)

Therefore

\mathbb{E}[D_{m,t+1}\mid U_{m,0}]=L_{m,t,\mathrm{C}}\bigl(\mathbb{E}[D_{m,t}\mid U_{m,0}]\bigr)+\alpha_{mH+t}\mathbb{E}[\xi^{\mathrm{C}}_{m,t}\mid U_{m,0}],

(302)

since
\mathbb{E}[\zeta^{\mathrm{C}}_{m,t}\mid U_{m,0}]=0
. Taking
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{H,2,\infty}
gives

\begin{gathered}\nu_{m,t+1}:=\left\lVert\mathbb{E}[D_{m,t+1}\mid U_{m,0}]\right\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\\
\leq(1-\kappa_{H,\mathrm{C}}\alpha_{mH+t})\left\lVert\mathbb{E}[D_{m,t}\mid U_{m,0}]\right\rVert_{H,2,\infty}+8B_{H,\mathrm{C}}\alpha_{mH+t}\bar{\alpha}_{m}.\end{gathered}

(303)

Since
\nu_{m,0}=0
, induction over
t
yields

\left\lVert\mathbb{E}[D_{m,H}\mid U_{m,0}]\right\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq 8B_{H,\mathrm{C}}\bar{\alpha}^{2}_{m}.

(304)

Also, iterating the recursion for
D_{m,t+1}
, using

\left\lVert L_{m,t,\mathrm{C}}(Z)\right\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq\left\lVert Z\right\rVert_{H,2,\infty},

(305)

the identity
D_{m,0}=0
, and the bounds from Lemma
34
give the pathwise upper bound

\left\lVert D_{m,H}\right\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}\alpha_{mH+t}\bigl(\left\lVert\zeta^{\mathrm{C}}_{m,t}\right\rVert_{H,2,\infty}+\left\lVert\xi^{\mathrm{C}}_{m,t}\right\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\bigr)\leq 4B_{H,\mathrm{C}}\bar{\alpha}_{m}+8B_{H,\mathrm{C}}\bar{\alpha}^{2}_{m}.

(306)

Since
\alpha_{0}\leq\bar{\alpha}_{H,\mathrm{C}}\leq 1/H
, we have
\bar{\alpha}_{m}\leq 1
, and hence

\left\lVert D_{m,H}\right\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq 12B_{H,\mathrm{C}}\bar{\alpha}_{m}.

(307)

pathwise. Therefore

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\left\lVert D_{m,H}\right\rVert^{2}_{H,2,\infty}\mid U_{m,0}\bigr]\leq 144B^{2}_{H,\mathrm{C}}\bar{\alpha}^{2}_{m}.

(308)

Let

\bar{W}_{m,H}:=\bar{U}_{m,H}-U^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{C}},\qquad\delta_{m}:=\frac{\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}\bar{\alpha}_{m}}{4L^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}}.

(309)

Because
M_{H,\mathrm{C}}
is nonnegative, convex,
L^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}
-smooth with respect to
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{H,2,p^{\star}_{H}}
, and minimized at zero, its gradient satisfies

\left\lVert\nabla M_{H,\mathrm{C}}(\bar{W}_{m,H})\right\rVert^{2}_{*}\leq 2L^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}M_{H,\mathrm{C}}(\bar{W}_{m,H}),

(310)

where
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{*}
denotes the dual norm of
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{H,2,p^{\star}_{H}}
. Therefore

\begin{gathered}\mathbb{E}\bigl[M_{H,\mathrm{C}}(W_{m+1},0)\mid U_{m,0}\bigr]\leq M_{H,\mathrm{C}}(\bar{W}_{m,H})+\left\langle\nabla M_{H,\mathrm{C}}(\bar{W}_{m,H}),\mathbb{E}[D_{m,H}\mid U_{m,0}]\right\rangle\\
+\frac{L^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}}{2}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\left\lVert D_{m,H}\right\rVert^{2}_{H,2,p^{\star}_{H}}\mid U_{m,0}\bigr].\end{gathered}

(311)

By Young’s inequality,

\begin{gathered}\left\langle\nabla M_{H,\mathrm{C}}(\bar{W}_{m,H}),\mathbb{E}[D_{m,H}\mid U_{m,0}]\right\rangle\leq\frac{\delta_{m}}{2}\left\lVert\nabla M_{H,\mathrm{C}}(\bar{W}_{m,H})\right\rVert^{2}_{*}+\frac{1}{2\delta_{m}}\left\lVert\mathbb{E}[D_{m,H}\mid U_{m,0}]\right\rVert^{2}_{H,2,p^{\star}_{H}}\\
\leq\frac{\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}\bar{\alpha}_{m}}{4}M_{H,\mathrm{C}}(\bar{W}_{m,H})+\frac{2L^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}}{\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}\bar{\alpha}_{m}}\left\lVert\mathbb{E}[D_{m,H}\mid U_{m,0}]\right\rVert^{2}_{H,2,p^{\star}_{H}}.\end{gathered}

(312)

Hence

\begin{gathered}\mathbb{E}[M_{H,\mathrm{C}}(W_{m+1,0})\mid U_{m,0}]\leq\left(1+\frac{\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}\bar{\alpha}_{m}}{4}\right)M_{H,\mathrm{C}}(\bar{W}_{m,H})\\
+\frac{2L^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}}{\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}\bar{\alpha}_{m}}\left\lVert\mathbb{E}[D_{m,H}\mid U_{m,0}]\right\rVert^{2}_{H,2,p^{\star}_{H}}+\frac{L^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}}{2}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\left\lVert D_{m,H}\right\rVert^{2}_{H,2,p^{\star}_{H}}\mid U_{m,0}\bigr].\end{gathered}

(313)

Since
\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}\bar{\alpha}_{m}\leq 1
, we have

\left(1+\frac{\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}\bar{\alpha}_{m}}{4}\right)\left(1-\frac{\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}}{2}\bar{\alpha}_{m}\right)\leq 1-\frac{\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}\bar{\alpha}_{m}}{4}.

(314)

Also,

\left\lVert\mathbb{E}[D_{m,H}\mid U_{m,0}]\right\rVert^{2}_{H,2,p^{\star}_{H}}\leq\lvert\mathcal{S}_{H}\rvert^{2/p^{\star}_{H}}\left\lVert\mathbb{E}[D_{m,H}\mid U_{m,0}]\right\rVert^{2}_{H,2,\infty}\leq 64\lvert\mathcal{S}_{H}\rvert^{2/p^{\star}_{H}}B^{2}_{H,\mathrm{C}}\bar{\alpha}^{4}_{m},

(315)

and

\mathbb{E}[\left\lVert D_{m,H}\right\rVert^{2}_{H,2,p^{\star}_{H}}\mid U_{m,0}]\leq\lvert\mathcal{S}_{H}\rvert^{2/p^{\star}_{H}}\mathbb{E}[\left\lVert D_{m,H}\right\rVert^{2}_{H,2,\infty}\mid U_{m,0}]\leq 144\lvert\mathcal{S}_{H}\rvert^{2/p^{\star}_{H}}B^{2}_{H,\mathrm{C}}\bar{\alpha}^{2}_{m}.

(316)

Using
\bar{\alpha}_{m}\leq 1
,
\bar{\alpha}^{2}_{m}\leq H\bar{\alpha}^{(2)}_{m}
, and
\bar{\alpha}^{3}_{m}\leq H\bar{\alpha}_{m}^{(2)}

\mathbb{E}[M_{H,\mathrm{C}}(W_{m+1,0})\mid U_{m,0}]\leq\left(1-\frac{\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}}{4}\bar{\alpha}_{m}\right)M_{H,\mathrm{C}}(W_{m,0})+HL^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}\lvert\mathcal{S}_{H}\rvert^{2/p^{\star}_{H}}B^{2}_{H,\mathrm{C}}\left(\frac{128}{\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}}+72\right)\bar{\alpha}^{(2)}_{m}.

(317)

Proposition 36

Define

a^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C},1}:=r^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}},\quad a^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C},2}:=\frac{\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}}{4},\quad a^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C},3}:=\frac{\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C}}}{4\bar{\alpha}_{H,\mathrm{C}}},\quad a^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C},4}:=2(1+\vartheta_{H,\mathrm{C}}\lvert\mathcal{S}_{H}\rvert^{2/p^{\star}_{H}})c^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C},2}.

(318)

If
a^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C},2}>0
,

\alpha_{0}\leq\frac{a^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C},2}}{a^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C},3}},

\begin{gathered}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\ell_{H,\mathrm{C},\infty}(\eta_{mH},\eta^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{C}})^{2}\bigr]\leq a^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C},1}\ell_{H,\mathrm{C},\infty}(\eta_{0},\eta^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{C}})^{2}\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}(1-a^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{C},2}\bar{\alpha}_{j})\\

Iterating Proposition
35

By the Moreau envelope comparison,

\mathbb{E}[\left\lVert W_{m,0}\right\rVert^{2}_{H,2,\infty}]\leq 2(1+\vartheta_{H,\mathrm{C}}\lvert\mathcal{S}_{H}\rvert^{2/p^{\star}_{H}})\mathbb{E}\bigl[M_{H,\mathrm{C}}(W_{m,0})\bigr]

(322)

M_{H,\mathrm{C}}(W_{0,0})\leq\frac{1}{2(1+\vartheta_{H,\mathrm{C}})}\left\lVert W_{0,0}\right\rVert^{2}_{H,2,\infty}.

(323)

\left\lVert W_{m,0}\right\rVert_{H,2,\infty}=\ell_{H,\mathrm{C},\infty}(\eta_{mH},\eta^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{C}})

(324)

Appendix E

This appendix records the fixed-horizon MTD ingredients and the proof of Theorem

.

E.1
Flattened weighted state–horizon space

We use the same weighted flattening as in Appendix
D
. Let

\mathcal{S}_{H}:=\{(h,s):h\in\{1,\dots,H\},\ s\in\mathcal{S}\},\qquad\lvert\mathcal{S}_{H}\rvert=H\lvert\mathcal{S}\rvert,\qquad p_{H}^{\star}:=\max\{2,\lceil\log\lvert\mathcal{S}_{H}\rvert\rceil\}.

(339)

Let

V_{H,\mathrm{M}}:=\prod_{(h,s)\in\mathcal{S}_{H}}\mathbb{R}^{d}

(340)

denote the corresponding flattened Euclidean product space. For a horizon-stacked iterate
U=(U^{h}(s))
, define

\bar{U}(h,s):=\lambda^{h}U^{h}(s).

(341)

Then

\lVert U\rVert_{H,2,\infty}=\max_{(h,s)\in\mathcal{S}_{H}}\lVert\bar{U}(h,s)\rVert_{2},\qquad\lVert U\rVert_{H,2,p}=\left(\sum_{(h,s)\in\mathcal{S}_{H}}\lVert\bar{U}(h,s)\rVert_{2}^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

(342)

For a single horizon stack
U=(U^{1},\dots,U^{H})
, also set

\lVert U\rVert_{H,2}:=\max_{1\leq h\leq H}\lambda^{h}\lVert U^{h}\rVert_{2}.

(343)

Then

\lVert U\rVert_{H,2,\infty}=\max_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\lVert U(s)\rVert_{H,2}.

(344)

Thus Appendix
A
applies directly to the flattened weighted process.

E.2
Fixed-horizon MTD residual map

For each state
s\in\mathcal{S}
, let

U(s):=\bigl(U^{1}(s),\dots,U^{H}(s)\bigr),

(345)

and let
P_{s}^{H}
denote the coordinate projector onto that stack. Define

F_{H,\mathrm{M}}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime})):=P_{s}^{H}\bigl(\widehat{T}_{H,\mathrm{M}}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))-U(s)\bigr),

(346)

where
\widehat{T}_{H,\mathrm{M}}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))
is the full horizon-stacked MTD target from Section
4
. Then the online fixed-horizon MTD recursion is

U_{k+1}=U_{k}+\alpha_{k}F_{H,\mathrm{M}}(U_{k};S_{k},(R_{k},S_{k+1})).

(347)

Define also the MTD embedded averaged operator by

\mathcal{O}_{H,\mathrm{M}}:=I_{H,\mathrm{M}}\circ\Pi_{H,\mathrm{M}}^{\Theta}T_{H}^{\pi}\circ I_{H,\mathrm{M}}^{-1}.

(348)

Proposition 38

(Fixed-horizon MTD weighted contraction)
.

For all
U,U^{\prime}\in I_{H,\mathrm{M}}(\mathcal{F}_{H,\mathrm{M},\Theta}^{\mathcal{S}})
,

\lVert\mathcal{O}_{H,\mathrm{M}}U-\mathcal{O}_{H,\mathrm{M}}U^{\prime}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq\lambda\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}.

(349)

Proof.

Let
\eta:=I_{H,\mathrm{M}}^{-1}(U)
and
\eta^{\prime}:=I_{H,\mathrm{M}}^{-1}(U^{\prime})
. Fix
(h,s)\in\mathcal{S}_{H}
with
h\geq 1
. By the statewise MMD isometry and nonexpansiveness of the signed-categorical projection,

\displaystyle\lambda^{h}\lVert(\mathcal{O}_{H,\mathrm{M}}U)^{h}(s)-(\mathcal{O}_{H,\mathrm{M}}U^{\prime})^{h}(s)\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{h}\ell_{\mathrm{M}}\left((\Pi_{H,\mathrm{M}}^{\Theta}T_{H}^{\pi}\eta)^{h}(s),(\Pi_{H,\mathrm{M}}^{\Theta}T_{H}^{\pi}\eta^{\prime})^{h}(s)\right)

(350)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{h}\ell_{\mathrm{M}}\left((T_{H}^{\pi}\eta)^{h}(s),(T_{H}^{\pi}\eta^{\prime})^{h}(s)\right).

Now

(T_{H}^{\pi}\eta)^{h}(s)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi(a\mid s)\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}P(s^{\prime}\mid s,a)\,(f_{R(s,a),1})_{\#}\eta^{h-1}(s^{\prime}),

(351)

and likewise for
\eta^{\prime}
. Since
(f_{r,1})_{\#}
is an isometry in the MMD metric associated with a shift-invariant kernel and MMD is convex under mixtures,

\displaystyle\ell_{\mathrm{M}}\left((T_{H}^{\pi}\eta)^{h}(s),(T_{H}^{\pi}\eta^{\prime})^{h}(s)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi(a\mid s)\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}P(s^{\prime}\mid s,a)\,\ell_{\mathrm{M}}\left(\eta^{h-1}(s^{\prime}),{\eta^{\prime}}^{h-1}(s^{\prime})\right)

(352)

\displaystyle\leq\max_{x\in\mathcal{S}}\ell_{\mathrm{M}}\left(\eta^{h-1}(x),{\eta^{\prime}}^{h-1}(x)\right).

Therefore

\displaystyle\lambda^{h}\lVert(\mathcal{O}_{H,\mathrm{M}}U)^{h}(s)-(\mathcal{O}_{H,\mathrm{M}}U^{\prime})^{h}(s)\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\max_{x\in\mathcal{S}}\lambda^{h-1}\ell_{\mathrm{M}}\left(\eta^{h-1}(x),{\eta^{\prime}}^{h-1}(x)\right)

(353)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\ell_{H,\mathrm{M},\infty}(\eta,\eta^{\prime})=\lambda\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}.

Taking the maximum over
(h,s)\in\mathcal{S}_{H}
proves the claim.
∎

E.3
One-step bounds

Proposition 39

(Fixed-horizon MTD samplewise Lipschitzness)
.

For every
s\in\mathcal{S}
,
(r,s^{\prime})\in[0,1]^{q}\times\mathcal{S}
and
U,U^{\prime}\in I_{H,\mathrm{M}}(\mathcal{F}_{H,\mathrm{M},\Theta}^{\mathcal{S}})
,

\lVert F_{H,\mathrm{M}}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))-F_{H,\mathrm{M}}(U^{\prime};s,(r,s^{\prime}))\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq 2\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}.

(354)

Proof.

For each horizon
h
, the local target
\widehat{T}_{H,\mathrm{M}}^{h}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))
depends only on the
(h-1)
st horizon block at
s^{\prime}
. By Proposition
27
,

\left\lVert\widehat{T}_{H,\mathrm{M}}^{h}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))-\widehat{T}_{H,\mathrm{M}}^{h}(U^{\prime};s,(r,s^{\prime}))\right\rVert_{2}\leq\lVert U^{h-1}(s^{\prime})-{U^{\prime}}^{h-1}(s^{\prime})\rVert_{2}.

(355)

Multiplying by
\lambda^{h}
yields

\lambda^{h}\left\lVert\widehat{T}_{H,\mathrm{M}}^{h}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))-\widehat{T}_{H,\mathrm{M}}^{h}(U^{\prime};s,(r,s^{\prime}))\right\rVert_{2}\leq\lambda^{h-1}\lVert U^{h-1}(s^{\prime})-{U^{\prime}}^{h-1}(s^{\prime})\rVert_{2}\leq\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}.

(356)

Taking the maximum over
h
gives

\lVert\widehat{T}_{H,\mathrm{M}}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))-\widehat{T}_{H,\mathrm{M}}(U^{\prime};s,(r,s^{\prime}))\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}.

(357)

Since

F_{H,\mathrm{M}}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))=P_{s}^{H}\bigl(\widehat{T}_{H,\mathrm{M}}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))-U(s)\bigr),

(358)

the residual difference splits into a projected target difference and a projected current-stack difference. Bounding both by
\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}
gives the stated factor
2
.
∎

Proposition 40

(Fixed-horizon MTD affine perturbation bound)
.

Let

U^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{M}}:=I_{H,\mathrm{M}}(\eta_{H,\mathrm{M}}^{\star}).

(359)

Here
\eta_{H,\mathrm{M}}^{\star}
denotes the unique fixed point of
\Pi_{H,\mathrm{M}}^{\Theta}T_{H}^{\pi}
. Then there exist finite constants
B^{\mathrm{tar}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\geq 0
and
B^{\mathrm{res}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\geq 0
such that, for every admissible sample
(s,(r,s^{\prime}))
and every admissible iterate
U
,

\left\lVert\widehat{T}_{H,\mathrm{M}}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))-(\mathcal{O}_{H,\mathrm{M}}U)(s)\right\rVert_{H,2}\leq 2\lambda\lVert U\rVert_{H,2,\infty}+B^{\mathrm{tar}}_{H,\mathrm{M}},

(360)

and

\lVert F_{H,\mathrm{M}}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq 2\lVert U\rVert_{H,2,\infty}+B^{\mathrm{res}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}.

(361)

Consequently,

\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\left\lVert\widehat{T}_{H,\mathrm{M}}(U_{k};S_{k},(R_{k},S_{k+1}))-(\mathcal{O}_{H,\mathrm{M}}U_{k})(S_{k})\right\rVert_{H,2}^{2}\mid U_{k},S_{k}\Bigr]\leq 2\bigl(B^{\mathrm{tar}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\bigr)^{2}+8\lambda^{2}\lVert U_{k}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}^{2}.

(362)

Proof.

For fixed
s,s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}
and
r\in[0,1]^{q}
, Proposition
27
gives, for each horizon
h
,

\left\lVert\widehat{T}_{H,\mathrm{M}}^{h}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))-\widehat{T}_{H,\mathrm{M}}^{h}(U^{\prime};s,(r,s^{\prime}))\right\rVert_{2}\leq\lVert U^{h-1}(s^{\prime})-{U^{\prime}}^{h-1}(s^{\prime})\rVert_{2}.

(363)

After multiplying by
\lambda^{h}
and taking the maximum over
h
, the horizon shift contributes exactly one factor
\lambda
. Therefore

\left\lVert\widehat{T}_{H,\mathrm{M}}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))-\widehat{T}_{H,\mathrm{M}}(U^{\prime};s,(r,s^{\prime}))\right\rVert_{H,2}\leq\lambda\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}.

(364)

Likewise, Proposition
38
yields

\lVert(\mathcal{O}_{H,\mathrm{M}}U)(s)-(\mathcal{O}_{H,\mathrm{M}}U^{\prime})(s)\rVert_{H,2}\leq\lambda\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}.

(365)

Now define

B^{\star,\mathrm{tar}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}:=\max_{s,s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\sup_{r\in[0,1]^{q}}\left\lVert\widehat{T}_{H,\mathrm{M}}(U^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{M}};s,(r,s^{\prime}))-(\mathcal{O}_{H,\mathrm{M}}U^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{M}})(s)\right\rVert_{H,2}.

(366)

As in the discounted case, this constant is finite because
[0,1]^{q}
is compact, there are only finitely many state pairs, and the embedded projected target depends continuously on
r
.

Then

\begin{gathered}\left\lVert\widehat{T}_{H,\mathrm{M}}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))-(\mathcal{O}_{H,\mathrm{M}}U)(s)\right\rVert_{H,2}\leq\left\lVert\widehat{T}_{H,\mathrm{M}}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))-\widehat{T}_{H,\mathrm{M}}(U^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{M}};s,(r,s^{\prime}))\right\rVert_{H,2}\\
+\left\lVert\widehat{T}_{H,\mathrm{M}}(U^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{M}};s,(r,s^{\prime}))-(\mathcal{O}_{H,\mathrm{M}}U^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{M}})(s)\right\rVert_{H,2}+\left\lVert(\mathcal{O}_{H,\mathrm{M}}U^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{M}})(s)-(\mathcal{O}_{H,\mathrm{M}}U)(s)\right\rVert_{H,2}\\
\leq 2\lambda\lVert U-U^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}+B^{\star,\mathrm{tar}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\\
\leq 2\lambda\lVert U\rVert_{H,2,\infty}+\Bigl(2\lambda\lVert U^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}+B^{\star,\mathrm{tar}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\Bigr).\end{gathered}

(367)

Hence the target-level affine bound holds with

B^{\mathrm{tar}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}:=2\lambda\lVert U^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}+B^{\star,\mathrm{tar}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}.

(368)

For the residual map, Proposition
39
gives

\left\lVert F_{H,\mathrm{M}}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))-F_{H,\mathrm{M}}(U^{\prime};s,(r,s^{\prime}))\right\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq 2\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}.

(369)

Define

B^{\star,\mathrm{res}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}:=\max_{s,s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\sup_{r\in[0,1]^{q}}\lVert F_{H,\mathrm{M}}(U^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{M}};s,(r,s^{\prime}))\rVert_{H,2,\infty},

(370)

which is finite by the same continuity and compactness argument. Then

\lVert F_{H,\mathrm{M}}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq 2\lVert U-U^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}+B^{\star,\mathrm{res}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\leq 2\lVert U\rVert_{H,2,\infty}+\Bigl(2\lVert U^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}+B^{\star,\mathrm{res}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\Bigr).

(371)

Thus the residual-level affine bound holds with

B^{\mathrm{res}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}:=2\lVert U^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}+B^{\star,\mathrm{res}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}.

(372)

Finally, the conditional second-moment estimate follows from the target-level affine bound by squaring and using

(a+b)^{2}\leq 2a^{2}+2b^{2}

(373)

with

a:=2\lambda\lVert U_{k}\rVert_{H,2,\infty},\qquad b:=B^{\mathrm{tar}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}.

(374)

∎

Proposition 41

(Fixed-horizon MTD affine conditional second moment)
.

There exist finite constants
C_{H,\mathrm{M},1},C_{H,\mathrm{M},2}\geq 0
such that

\mathbb{E}\left[\lVert\widehat{T}_{H,\mathrm{M}}(U_{k};S_{k},(R_{k},S_{k+1}))-(\mathcal{O}_{H,\mathrm{M}}U_{k})(S_{k})\rVert_{H,2}^{2}\mid U_{k},S_{k}\right]\leq C_{H,\mathrm{M},1}+C_{H,\mathrm{M},2}\lVert U_{k}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}^{2}.

(375)

Proof.

Square the affine pathwise bound and use
(a+b)^{2}\leq 2a^{2}+2b^{2}
.
∎

E.4
Phasewise averaged maps

For each phase
t\in\{0,\dots,H-1\}
, define

\Gamma_{t,\mathrm{M}}(U):=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\rho_{t}(s)\,P_{s}^{H}\bigl((\mathcal{O}_{H,\mathrm{M}}U)(s)-U(s)\bigr),\qquad G_{t,\mathrm{M}}(U):=U+\Gamma_{t,\mathrm{M}}(U).

(376)

Proposition 42

(Phasewise averaged contraction)
.

For each phase
t\in\{0,\dots,H-1\}
,

\lVert G_{t,\mathrm{M}}(U)-G_{t,\mathrm{M}}(U^{\prime})\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq\bar{\beta}_{H,\mathrm{M}}^{\mathrm{fh}}\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{H,2,\infty},\qquad\bar{\beta}_{H,\mathrm{M}}^{\mathrm{fh}}:=1-\rho_{\min}(1-\lambda).

(377)

Proof.

For each state block
s
,

\bigl(G_{t,\mathrm{M}}(U)-G_{t,\mathrm{M}}(U^{\prime})\bigr)(s)=(1-\rho_{t}(s))(U(s)-U^{\prime}(s))\,+\,\rho_{t}(s)\bigl((\mathcal{O}_{H,\mathrm{M}}U)(s)-(\mathcal{O}_{H,\mathrm{M}}U^{\prime})(s)\bigr).

(378)

By Proposition
38
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\bigl(G_{t,\mathrm{M}}(U)-G_{t,\mathrm{M}}(U^{\prime})\bigr)(s)\right\rVert_{H,2}

\displaystyle\leq\bigl((1-\rho_{t}(s))+\rho_{t}(s)\lambda\bigr)\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}

(379)

\displaystyle=\bigl(1-\rho_{t}(s)(1-\lambda)\bigr)\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}.

Taking the maximum over
s
on both sides gives

\displaystyle\lVert G_{t,\mathrm{M}}(U)-G_{t,\mathrm{M}}(U^{\prime})\rVert_{H,2,\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\bigl(1-\rho_{t}(s)(1-\lambda)\bigr)\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}

(380)

\displaystyle\leq\bigl(1-\rho_{\min}(1-\lambda)\bigr)\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{H,2,\infty},

since the coefficient
1-\rho_{t}(s)(1-\lambda)
is decreasing in
\rho_{t}(s)
and
\rho_{t}(s)\geq\rho_{\min}
for every phase-state block. Equivalently,

\bar{\beta}_{t}^{\mathrm{fh}}:=1-\rho_{t,\min}(1-\lambda)\leq 1-\rho_{\min}(1-\lambda)=\bar{\beta}_{H,\mathrm{M}}^{\mathrm{fh}},

(381)

so the phasewise bound is dominated by the claimed uniform contraction factor.
∎

E.5
Auxiliary phasewise bounds

For episode
m
, write

U_{m,t}:=U_{mH+t},\qquad t=0,\dots,H.

(382)

Lemma 43

(Within-episode deviation)
.

For every episode
m
and every
t\in\{0,\dots,H\}
,

\lVert U_{m,t}-U_{m,0}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq\sum_{u=0}^{t-1}\alpha_{mH+u}\Bigl(2\lVert U_{m,u}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}+B^{\mathrm{res}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\Bigr).

(383)

Proof.

From the recursion,

U_{m,t}-U_{m,0}=\sum_{u=0}^{t-1}\alpha_{mH+u}F_{H,\mathrm{M}}\bigl(U_{m,u};S_{u}^{m},(R_{u}^{m},S_{u+1}^{m})\bigr).

(384)

Take norms and apply the residual-level affine bound from Proposition
40
term by term:

\lVert U_{m,t}-U_{m,0}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq\sum_{u=0}^{t-1}\alpha_{mH+u}\Bigl(2\lVert U_{m,u}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}+B^{\mathrm{res}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\Bigr).

(385)

∎

Lemma 44

(Frozen residual decomposition)
.

For each phase
t\in\{0,\dots,H-1\}
, define

\zeta_{m,t}^{\mathrm{M}}:=F_{H,\mathrm{M}}(U_{m,0};S_{t}^{m},(R_{t}^{m},S_{t+1}^{m}))-\Gamma_{t,\mathrm{M}}(U_{m,0})

(386)

and

\xi_{m,t}^{\mathrm{M}}:=\Bigl(F_{H,\mathrm{M}}(U_{m,t};S_{t}^{m},(R_{t}^{m},S_{t+1}^{m}))-F_{H,\mathrm{M}}(U_{m,0};S_{t}^{m},(R_{t}^{m},S_{t+1}^{m}))\Bigr)-\Bigl(\Gamma_{t,\mathrm{M}}(U_{m,t})-\Gamma_{t,\mathrm{M}}(U_{m,0})\Bigr).

(387)

Then

F_{H,\mathrm{M}}(U_{m,t};S_{t}^{m},(R_{t}^{m},S_{t+1}^{m}))-\Gamma_{t,\mathrm{M}}(U_{m,t})=\zeta_{m,t}^{\mathrm{M}}+\xi_{m,t}^{\mathrm{M}},

(388)

\mathbb{E}\left[\zeta_{m,t}^{\mathrm{M}}\,\mid\,U_{m,0}\right]=0,\qquad\lVert\zeta_{m,t}^{\mathrm{M}}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq 4\lVert U_{m,0}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}+2B^{\mathrm{res}}_{H,\mathrm{M}},

(389)

and

\lVert\xi_{m,t}^{\mathrm{M}}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq 4\lVert U_{m,t}-U_{m,0}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}.

(390)

Proof.

Since episode
m
is independent of
U_{m,0}
and its phase-
t
sample is distributed according to the phase-
t
law used in
\Gamma_{t,\mathrm{M}}
, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[\zeta_{m,t}^{\mathrm{M}}\,\mid\,U_{m,0}\right]=0.

(391)

By Proposition
40
,

\lVert F_{H,\mathrm{M}}(U_{m,0};S_{t}^{m},(R_{t}^{m},S_{t+1}^{m}))\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq 2\lVert U_{m,0}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}+B^{\mathrm{res}}_{H,\mathrm{M}},

(392)

and, since
\Gamma_{t,\mathrm{M}}(U_{m,0})
is the average of the same residual map under the phase-
t
law,

\lVert\Gamma_{t,\mathrm{M}}(U_{m,0})\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq 2\lVert U_{m,0}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}+B^{\mathrm{res}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}.

(393)

Hence

\lVert\zeta_{m,t}^{\mathrm{M}}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq 4\lVert U_{m,0}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}+2B^{\mathrm{res}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}.

(394)

Proposition
39
gives

\left\lVert F_{H,\mathrm{M}}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))-F_{H,\mathrm{M}}(V;s,(r,s^{\prime}))\right\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq 2\lVert U-V\rVert_{H,2,\infty},

(395)

and averaging the same estimate yields

\lVert\Gamma_{t,\mathrm{M}}(U)-\Gamma_{t,\mathrm{M}}(V)\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq 2\lVert U-V\rVert_{H,2,\infty}.

(396)

Therefore

\lVert\xi_{m,t}^{\mathrm{M}}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq 4\lVert U_{m,t}-U_{m,0}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}.

(397)

∎

E.6
Episode-level finite-iteration drift

Since
\Gamma_{t,\mathrm{M}}
is defined by averaging with respect to the phase-
t
marginal
\rho_{t}
, the drift argument is formulated for the episode-boundary sequence
(U_{mH})_{m\geq 0}
under the conditioning on
U_{m,0}
.

Let

W_{m,0}:=U_{m,0}-U^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{M}},\qquad U^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{M}}:=I_{H,\mathrm{M}}(\eta_{H,\mathrm{M}}^{\star}),

(398)

and define

\bar{\alpha}_{m}:=\sum_{u=0}^{H-1}\alpha_{mH+u},\qquad\bar{\alpha}_{m}^{(2)}:=\sum_{u=0}^{H-1}\alpha_{mH+u}^{2}.

(399)

Also write

\lvert\mathcal{S}_{H}\rvert:=H\lvert\mathcal{S}\rvert,\qquad\bar{\beta}_{H,\mathrm{M}}^{\mathrm{fh}}:=1-\rho_{\min}(1-\lambda),\qquad\kappa_{H,\mathrm{M}}:=1-\bar{\beta}_{H,\mathrm{M}}^{\mathrm{fh}}=\rho_{\min}(1-\lambda)>0.

(400)

Fix any
\vartheta_{H,\mathrm{M}}>0
and define the smoothed potential

M_{H,\mathrm{M}}(W):=\inf_{Z\in V_{H,\mathrm{M}}}\left\{\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert Z\right\rVert^{2}_{H,2,\infty}+\frac{1}{2\vartheta_{H,\mathrm{M}}}\left\lVert W-Z\right\rVert^{2}_{H,2,p^{\star}_{H}}\right\}.

(401)

By Proposition
13
on the flattened weighted space,

(1+\vartheta_{H,\mathrm{M}})M_{H,\mathrm{M}}(W)\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert W\right\rVert^{2}_{H,2,\infty}\leq(1+\vartheta_{H,\mathrm{M}}\lvert\mathcal{S}_{H}\rvert^{2/p^{\star}_{H}})M_{H,\mathrm{M}}(W).

(402)

Define

r^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}:=\frac{1+\vartheta_{H,\mathrm{M}}\lvert\mathcal{S}_{H}\rvert^{2/p^{\star}_{H}}}{1+\vartheta_{H,\mathrm{M}}},\qquad L^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}:=\frac{p^{\star}_{H}-1}{\vartheta_{H,\mathrm{M}}},\qquad\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}:=1-\bar{\beta}^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\sqrt{r^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}}.

(403)

Since
r^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\to 1
as
\vartheta_{H,\mathrm{M}}\downarrow 0
and
\bar{\beta}^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}<1
, we have
\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}>0
for all sufficiently small
\vartheta_{H,\mathrm{M}}
. Define the preliminary threshold and the explicit MTD constants

\begin{gathered}d^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}:=8L^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\lvert\mathcal{S}_{H}\rvert^{2/p^{\star}_{H}}(1+\vartheta_{H,\mathrm{M}}),\qquad\widehat{\alpha}_{H,\mathrm{M}}:=\min\left\{\frac{1}{H},\frac{1}{2H\kappa_{H,\mathrm{M}}},\frac{\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}}{d^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}}\right\},\\
C_{H,\mathrm{M},\mathrm{g}}:=\exp\left(\frac{1}{\kappa_{H,\mathrm{M}}}\right)\max\left\{1,\frac{B^{\mathrm{res}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}}{2\kappa_{H,\mathrm{M}}}\right\},\qquad C_{H,\mathrm{M},\mathrm{w}}:=2C_{H,\mathrm{M},\mathrm{g}}+B^{\mathrm{res}}_{H,\mathrm{M}},\\
C_{H,\mathrm{M},\mathrm{p}}:=HL^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\lvert\mathcal{S}_{H}\rvert^{2/p^{\star}_{H}}C^{2}_{H,\mathrm{M},\mathrm{w}}\left(\frac{32}{\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}}+36\right),\end{gathered}

(404)

and

\bar{\alpha}_{H,\mathrm{M}}:=\min\left\{\widehat{\alpha}_{H,\mathrm{M}},\frac{\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}}{64C_{H,\mathrm{M},\mathrm{p}}(1+\vartheta_{H,\mathrm{M}}\lvert\mathcal{S}_{H}\rvert^{2/p^{\star}_{H}})}\right\}.

(405)

Finally, define

\begin{gathered}c_{H,\mathrm{M},1}^{\mathrm{fh}}:=\frac{\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}}{4},\qquad c_{H,\mathrm{M},2}^{\mathrm{fh}}:=2C_{H,\mathrm{M},\mathrm{p}}\bigl(1+2\lVert U^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}^{2}\bigr),\\
c_{H,\mathrm{M},3}^{\mathrm{fh}}:=8C_{H,\mathrm{M},\mathrm{p}}(1+\vartheta_{H,\mathrm{M}}\lvert\mathcal{S}_{H}\rvert^{2/p^{\star}_{H}}).\end{gathered}

(406)

Lemma 45

(Pathwise growth control inside one episode)
.

Assume that
(\alpha_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is nonincreasing and that
\alpha_{0}\leq\bar{\alpha}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\leq 1
. Then, for every episode
m
,

\max_{0\leq t\leq H}\lVert U_{m,t}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq C_{H,\mathrm{M},\mathrm{g}}\bigl(1+\lVert U_{m,0}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\bigr)

(407)

pathwise.

Proof.

By Proposition
40
,

\lVert F_{H,\mathrm{M}}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq 2\lVert U\rVert_{H,2,\infty}+B^{\mathrm{res}}_{H,\mathrm{M}},

(408)

where
B^{\mathrm{res}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}
is the residual-level affine constant from Proposition
40
. Hence the recursion gives

\lVert U_{m,t+1}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq\bigl(1+2\alpha_{mH+t}\bigr)\lVert U_{m,t}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}+\alpha_{mH+t}B^{\mathrm{res}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}.

(409)

Iterating over at most
H
steps and using

\sum_{u=0}^{H-1}\alpha_{mH+u}\leq H\alpha_{0}\leq H\bar{\alpha}_{H,\mathrm{M}}

(410)

yields

\max_{0\leq t\leq H}\lVert U_{m,t}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq e^{2H\bar{\alpha}_{H,\mathrm{M}}}\bigl(\lVert U_{m,0}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}+H\bar{\alpha}_{H,\mathrm{M}}B^{\mathrm{res}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\bigr).

(411)

Since
\bar{\alpha}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\leq\widehat{\alpha}_{H,\mathrm{M}}
, we have

e^{2H\bar{\alpha}_{H,\mathrm{M}}}\leq e^{1/\kappa_{H,\mathrm{M}}},\qquad H\bar{\alpha}_{H,\mathrm{M}}B^{\mathrm{res}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\leq\frac{B^{\mathrm{res}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}}{2\kappa_{H,\mathrm{M}}}.

(412)

Therefore

\max_{0\leq t\leq H}\lVert U_{m,t}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq C_{H,\mathrm{M},\mathrm{g}}\bigl(1+\lVert U_{m,0}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\bigr).

(413)

∎

Proposition 46

(Episodewise MTD potential drift)
.

Assume that
\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}>0
, that
(\alpha_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is nonincreasing and that

\alpha_{0}\leq\bar{\alpha}_{H,\mathrm{M}}

(414)

Then, for every episode
m
,

\mathbb{E}\left[M_{H,\mathrm{M}}(W_{m+1,0})\mid U_{m,0}\right]\leq\bigl(1-c_{H,\mathrm{M},1}^{\mathrm{fh}}\bar{\alpha}_{m}\bigr)M_{H,\mathrm{M}}(W_{m,0})+c_{H,\mathrm{M},2}^{\mathrm{fh}}\bar{\alpha}_{m}^{(2)}+c_{H,\mathrm{M},3}^{\mathrm{fh}}\bar{\alpha}_{m}^{(2)}M_{H,\mathrm{M}}(W_{m,0}).

(415)

Consequently,

\mathbb{E}\left[M_{H,\mathrm{M}}(W_{m+1,0})\mid U_{m,0}\right]\leq\left(1-\frac{\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}}{8}\bar{\alpha}_{m}\right)M_{H,\mathrm{M}}(W_{m,0})+c_{H,\mathrm{M},2}^{\mathrm{fh}}\bar{\alpha}_{m}^{(2)}.

(416)

Proof.

For each phase
t
and each
\alpha\in[0,1]
, define

A_{t,\alpha,\mathrm{M}}(U):=U+\alpha\Gamma_{t,\mathrm{M}}(U)=(1-\alpha)U+\alpha G_{t,\mathrm{M}}(U).

(417)

By Proposition
42
,

\lVert A_{t,\alpha,\mathrm{M}}(U)-A_{t,\alpha,\mathrm{M}}(U^{\prime})\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq\bigl(1-\kappa_{H,\mathrm{M}}\alpha\bigr)\lVert U-U^{\prime}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}.

(418)

Define the deterministic phasewise averaged trajectory over episode
m
by

\bar{U}_{m,0}:=U_{m,0},\qquad\bar{U}_{m,t+1}:=A_{t,\alpha_{mH+t},\mathrm{M}}(\bar{U}_{m,t}),\qquad 0\leq t\leq H-1.

(419)

Since
G_{t,\mathrm{M}}(U^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{M}})=U^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{M}}
, the contraction estimate above, the Moreau-envelope comparison, and the smoothness of the envelope give the deterministic relaxed-map estimate

M_{H,\mathrm{M}}\bigl(A_{t,\alpha,\mathrm{M}}(U)-U^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\bigr)\leq(1-2\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\alpha+d^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\alpha^{2})M_{H,\mathrm{M}}(U-U^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{M}}).

(420)

Since
\alpha_{mH+t}\leq\alpha_{0}\leq\bar{\alpha}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\leq\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}/d^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}
, this implies

M_{H,\mathrm{M}}\bigl(A_{t,\alpha_{mH+t},\mathrm{M}}(U)-U^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\bigr)\leq(1-\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\alpha_{mH+t})M_{H,\mathrm{M}}(U-U^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{M}}).

(421)

Therefore

M_{H,\mathrm{M}}(\bar{U}_{m,H}-U^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{M}})\leq\prod_{u=0}^{H-1}(1-\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\alpha_{mH+u})M_{H,\mathrm{M}}(W_{m,0})\leq\left(1-\frac{\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}}{2}\bar{\alpha}_{m}\right)M_{H,\mathrm{M}}(W_{m,0}).

(422)

Now define

D_{m,t}:=U_{m,t}-\bar{U}_{m,t},\qquad D_{m,0}=0.

(423)

By Proposition
25
, each statewise signed-categorical projection is affine. Applying this horizon-by-horizon shows that
U\mapsto\widehat{T}_{H,\mathrm{M}}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))
and
U\mapsto(\mathcal{O}_{H,\mathrm{M}}U)(s)
are affine for every admissible
(s,r,s^{\prime})
. Hence
F_{H,\mathrm{M}}
,
\Gamma_{t,\mathrm{M}}
, and
A_{t,\alpha,\mathrm{M}}
are affine. Let
L_{m,t,\mathrm{M}}
denote the linear part of
A_{t,\alpha_{mH+t},\mathrm{M}}
. Then

A_{t,\alpha_{mH+t},\mathrm{M}}(U)-A_{t,\alpha_{mH+t},\mathrm{M}}(U^{\prime})=L_{m,t,\mathrm{M}}(U-U^{\prime}),

(424)

and for every admissible
Z

\lVert L_{m,t,\mathrm{M}}Z\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq\bigl(1-\kappa_{H,\mathrm{M}}\alpha_{mH+t}\bigr)\lVert Z\rVert_{H,2,\infty}.

(425)

Using the recursion for
U_{m,t+1}
, the definition of
\bar{U}_{m,t+1}
, and the decomposition from Lemma
44
, we obtain

\begin{gathered}D_{m,t+1}=U_{m,t+1}-\bar{U}_{m,t+1}\\
=U_{m,t}+\alpha_{mH+t}F_{H,\mathrm{M}}\bigl(U_{m,t};S_{t}^{m},(R_{t}^{m},S_{t+1}^{m})\bigr)-\bar{U}_{m,t}-\alpha_{mH+t}\Gamma_{t,\mathrm{M}}(\bar{U}_{m,t})\\
=U_{m,t}-\bar{U}_{m,t}+\alpha_{mH+t}\Bigl(\Gamma_{t,\mathrm{M}}(U_{m,t})-\Gamma_{t,\mathrm{M}}(\bar{U}_{m,t})\Bigr)\\
+\alpha_{mH+t}\Bigl(F_{H,\mathrm{M}}\bigl(U_{m,t};S_{t}^{m},(R_{t}^{m},S_{t+1}^{m})\bigr)-\Gamma_{t,\mathrm{M}}(U_{m,t})\Bigr)\\
=A_{t,\alpha_{mH+t},\mathrm{M}}(U_{m,t})-A_{t,\alpha_{mH+t},\mathrm{M}}(\bar{U}_{m,t})+\alpha_{mH+t}\Bigl(F_{H,\mathrm{M}}\bigl(U_{m,t};S_{t}^{m},(R_{t}^{m},S_{t+1}^{m})\bigr)-\Gamma_{t,\mathrm{M}}(U_{m,t})\Bigr)\\
=L_{m,t,\mathrm{M}}D_{m,t}+\alpha_{mH+t}\zeta_{m,t}^{\mathrm{M}}+\alpha_{mH+t}\xi_{m,t}^{\mathrm{M}}.\end{gathered}

(426)

By Lemma
45
and Lemma
43
,

\lVert U_{m,t}-U_{m,0}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq C_{H,\mathrm{M},\mathrm{w}}\bar{\alpha}_{m}\bigl(1+\lVert U_{m,0}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\bigr),

(427)

and, since
C_{H,\mathrm{M},\mathrm{g}}\geq 1
,

4\lVert U_{m,0}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}+2B^{\mathrm{res}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\leq 2C_{H,\mathrm{M},\mathrm{w}}\bigl(1+\lVert U_{m,0}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\bigr).

(428)

Hence Lemma
44
gives

\lVert\zeta_{m,t}^{\mathrm{M}}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq 2C_{H,\mathrm{M},\mathrm{w}}\bigl(1+\lVert U_{m,0}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\bigr),

(429)

and

\lVert\xi_{m,t}^{\mathrm{M}}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq 4C_{H,\mathrm{M},\mathrm{w}}\bar{\alpha}_{m}\bigl(1+\lVert U_{m,0}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\bigr).

(430)

Therefore

\mathbb{E}\left[D_{m,t+1}\,\mid\,U_{m,0}\right]=L_{m,t,\mathrm{M}}\mathbb{E}\left[D_{m,t}\,\mid\,U_{m,0}\right]+\alpha_{mH+t}\mathbb{E}\left[\xi_{m,t}^{\mathrm{M}}\,\mid\,U_{m,0}\right],

(431)

because
\mathbb{E}\left[\zeta_{m,t}^{\mathrm{M}}\,\mid\,U_{m,0}\right]=0
. Hence

\begin{gathered}\left\lVert\mathbb{E}\left[D_{m,t+1}\,\mid\,U_{m,0}\right]\right\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq\bigl(1-\kappa_{H,\mathrm{M}}\alpha_{mH+t}\bigr)\left\lVert\mathbb{E}\left[D_{m,t}\,\mid\,U_{m,0}\right]\right\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\\
+4C_{H,\mathrm{M},\mathrm{w}}\alpha_{mH+t}\bar{\alpha}_{m}\bigl(1+\lVert U_{m,0}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\bigr).\end{gathered}

(432)

Since
D_{m,0}=0
, induction gives

\left\lVert\mathbb{E}\left[D_{m,H}\,\mid\,U_{m,0}\right]\right\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq 4C_{H,\mathrm{M},\mathrm{w}}\bar{\alpha}_{m}^{2}\bigl(1+\lVert U_{m,0}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\bigr).

(433)

Iterating the recursion for
D_{m,t+1}
, using

\lVert L_{m,t,\mathrm{M}}(Z)\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq\lVert Z\rVert_{H,2,\infty},

(434)

the identity
D_{m,0}=0
, and the bounds from Lemma
44
give the pathwise estimate

\lVert D_{m,H}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}\alpha_{mH+t}\Bigl(\lVert\zeta_{m,t}^{\mathrm{M}}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}+\lVert\xi_{m,t}^{\mathrm{M}}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\Bigr)

(435)

and therefore

\lVert D_{m,H}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq\Bigl(2C_{H,\mathrm{M},\mathrm{w}}\bar{\alpha}_{m}+4C_{H,\mathrm{M},\mathrm{w}}\bar{\alpha}_{m}^{2}\Bigr)\bigl(1+\lVert U_{m,0}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\bigr).

(436)

Since
\alpha_{0}\leq\bar{\alpha}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\leq\widehat{\alpha}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\leq 1/H
, we have
\bar{\alpha}_{m}\leq 1
, so

\lVert D_{m,H}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq 6C_{H,\mathrm{M},\mathrm{w}}\bar{\alpha}_{m}\bigl(1+\lVert U_{m,0}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\bigr)

(437)

pathwise. Hence

\mathbb{E}\left[\lVert D_{m,H}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}^{2}\,\mid\,U_{m,0}\right]\leq 36C_{H,\mathrm{M},\mathrm{w}}^{2}\bar{\alpha}_{m}^{2}\bigl(1+\lVert U_{m,0}\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\bigr)^{2}.

(438)

Define

\bar{W}_{m,H}:=\bar{U}_{m,H}-U^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{M}},\qquad\delta_{m}:=\frac{\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\bar{\alpha}_{m}}{4L^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}}.

(439)

Because
M_{H,\mathrm{M}}
is nonnegative, convex,
L^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}
-smooth with respect to
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{H,2,p^{\star}_{H}}
, and minimized at zero, its gradient satisfies

\left\lVert\nabla M_{H,\mathrm{M}}(\bar{W}_{m,H})\right\rVert^{2}_{*}\leq 2L^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}M_{H,\mathrm{M}}(\bar{W}_{m,H}),

(440)

where
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{*}
denotes the dual norm of
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{H,2,p^{\star}_{H}}
. Therefore

\begin{gathered}\mathbb{E}\bigl[M_{H,\mathrm{M}}(W_{m+1},0)\mid U_{m,0}\bigr]\leq M_{H,\mathrm{M}}(\bar{W}_{m,H})+\left\langle\nabla M_{H,\mathrm{M}}(\bar{W}_{m,H}),\mathbb{E}[D_{m,H}\mid U_{m,0}]\right\rangle\\
+\frac{L^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}}{2}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\left\lVert D_{m,H}\right\rVert^{2}_{H,2,p^{\star}_{H}}\mid U_{m,0}\bigr].\end{gathered}

(441)

By Young’s inequality,

\begin{gathered}\left\langle\nabla M_{H,\mathrm{M}}(\bar{W}_{m,H}),\mathbb{E}[D_{m,H}\mid U_{m,0}]\right\rangle\leq\frac{\delta_{m}}{2}\left\lVert\nabla M_{H,\mathrm{M}}(\bar{W}_{m,H})\right\rVert^{2}_{*}+\frac{1}{2\delta_{m}}\left\lVert\mathbb{E}[D_{m,H}\mid U_{m,0}]\right\rVert^{2}_{H,2,p^{\star}_{H}}\\
\leq\frac{\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\bar{\alpha}_{m}}{4}M_{H,\mathrm{M}}(\bar{W}_{m,H})+\frac{2L^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}}{\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\bar{\alpha}_{m}}\left\lVert\mathbb{E}[D_{m,H}\mid U_{m,0}]\right\rVert^{2}_{H,2,p^{\star}_{H}}.\end{gathered}

(442)

Hence

\begin{gathered}\mathbb{E}[M_{H,\mathrm{M}}(W_{m+1,0})\mid U_{m,0}]\leq\left(1+\frac{\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\bar{\alpha}_{m}}{4}\right)M_{H,\mathrm{M}}(\bar{W}_{m,H})\\
+\frac{2L^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}}{\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\bar{\alpha}_{m}}\left\lVert\mathbb{E}[D_{m,H}\mid U_{m,0}]\right\rVert^{2}_{H,2,p^{\star}_{H}}+\frac{L^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}}{2}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\left\lVert D_{m,H}\right\rVert^{2}_{H,2,p^{\star}_{H}}\mid U_{m,0}\bigr].\end{gathered}

(443)

Since
\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\bar{\alpha}_{m}\leq 1
, we have

\left(1+\frac{\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\bar{\alpha}_{m}}{4}\right)\left(1-\frac{\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}}{2}\bar{\alpha}_{m}\right)\leq 1-\frac{\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\bar{\alpha}_{m}}{4}.

(444)

Also,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathbb{E}[D_{m,H}\mid U_{m,0}]\right\rVert^{2}_{H,2,p^{\star}_{H}}

\displaystyle\leq\lvert\mathcal{S}_{H}\rvert^{2/p^{\star}_{H}}\left\lVert\mathbb{E}[D_{m,H}\mid U_{m,0}]\right\rVert^{2}_{H,2,\infty}

(445)

\displaystyle\leq 16\lvert\mathcal{S}_{H}\rvert^{2/p^{\star}_{H}}C^{2}_{H,\mathrm{M},\mathrm{w}}\bar{\alpha}^{4}_{m}(1+\left\lVert U_{m,0}\right\rVert_{H,2,\infty})^{2},

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\left\lVert D_{m,H}\right\rVert^{2}_{H,2,p^{\star}_{H}}\mid U_{m,0}]

\displaystyle\leq\lvert\mathcal{S}_{H}\rvert^{2/p^{\star}_{H}}\mathbb{E}[\left\lVert D_{m,H}\right\rVert^{2}_{H,2,\infty}\mid U_{m,0}]

(446)

\displaystyle\leq 36\lvert\mathcal{S}_{H}\rvert^{2/p^{\star}_{H}}C^{2}_{H,\mathrm{M},\mathrm{w}}\bar{\alpha}^{2}_{m}(1+\left\lVert U_{m,0}\right\rVert_{H,2,\infty})^{2}.

Using
\bar{\alpha}_{m}\leq 1
,
\bar{\alpha}^{2}_{m}\leq H\bar{\alpha}^{(2)}_{m}
, and
\bar{\alpha}^{3}_{m}\leq H\bar{\alpha}_{m}^{(2)}
, we obtain

\mathbb{E}[M_{H,\mathrm{M}}(W_{m+1,0})\mid U_{m,0}]\leq\left(1-\frac{\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}}{4}\bar{\alpha}_{m}\right)M_{H,\mathrm{M}}(W_{m,0})+C_{H,\mathrm{M},\mathrm{p}}\bar{\alpha}^{(2)}_{m}(1+\left\lVert U_{m,0}\right\rVert_{H,2,\infty})^{2}.

(447)

Since
(1+x)^{2}\leq 2(1+x^{2})
and

\displaystyle\left\lVert U_{m,0}\right\rVert^{2}_{H,2,\infty}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\lVert W_{m,0}\right\rVert^{2}_{H,2,\infty}+2\left\lVert U^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\right\rVert^{2}_{H,2,\infty}

(448)

\displaystyle\leq 4(1+\vartheta_{H,\mathrm{M}}\lvert\mathcal{S}_{H}\rvert^{2/p^{\star}_{H}})M_{H,\mathrm{M}}(W_{m,0})+2\left\lVert U^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\right\rVert^{2}_{H,2,\infty},

this yields

\mathbb{E}\left[M_{H,\mathrm{M}}(W_{m+1,0})\mid U_{m,0}\right]\leq\bigl(1-c_{H,\mathrm{M},1}^{\mathrm{fh}}\bar{\alpha}_{m}\bigr)M_{H,\mathrm{M}}(W_{m,0})+c_{H,\mathrm{M},2}^{\mathrm{fh}}\bar{\alpha}_{m}^{(2)}+c_{H,\mathrm{M},3}^{\mathrm{fh}}\bar{\alpha}_{m}^{(2)}M_{H,\mathrm{M}}(W_{m,0}),

(449)

which is the first stated inequality. Finally, since
(\alpha_{k})
is nonincreasing and
\alpha_{0}\leq\bar{\alpha}_{H,\mathrm{M}}
,

\bar{\alpha}_{m}^{(2)}\leq\alpha_{0}\bar{\alpha}_{m}\leq\bar{\alpha}_{H,\mathrm{M}}\bar{\alpha}_{m}\leq\frac{\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}}{64C_{H,\mathrm{M},\mathrm{p}}(1+\vartheta_{H,\mathrm{M}}\lvert\mathcal{S}_{H}\rvert^{2/p^{\star}_{H}})}\bar{\alpha}_{m}.

(450)

Therefore

c_{H,\mathrm{M},3}^{\mathrm{fh}}\bar{\alpha}_{m}^{(2)}M_{H,\mathrm{M}}(W_{m,0})\leq\frac{\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}}{8}\bar{\alpha}_{m}M_{H,\mathrm{M}}(W_{m,0}),

(451)

which gives the second inequality.
∎

Proposition 47

(Fixed-horizon MTD finite-iteration bound)
.

Define

\begin{gathered}a_{H,\mathrm{M},1}^{\mathrm{fh}}:=r^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}},\qquad a_{H,\mathrm{M},2}^{\mathrm{fh}}:=\frac{\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}}{8},\qquad a_{H,\mathrm{M},3}^{\mathrm{fh}}:=\frac{\omega^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M}}}{8\bar{\alpha}_{H,\mathrm{M}}},\\
a_{H,\mathrm{M},4}^{\mathrm{fh}}:=2(1+\vartheta_{H,\mathrm{M}}\lvert\mathcal{S}_{H}\rvert^{2/p^{\star}_{H}})c_{H,\mathrm{M},2}^{\mathrm{fh}}.\end{gathered}

(452)

If
a^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M},2}>0
,
(\alpha_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is nonincreasing and

\alpha_{0}\leq\frac{a_{H,\mathrm{M},2}^{\mathrm{fh}}}{a_{H,\mathrm{M},3}^{\mathrm{fh}}},

(453)

then, for all episodes
m\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\ell_{H,\mathrm{M},\infty}\bigl(\eta_{mH},\eta_{H,\mathrm{M}}^{\star}\bigr)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq a_{H,\mathrm{M},1}^{\mathrm{fh}}\ell_{H,\mathrm{M},\infty}\bigl(\eta_{0},\eta_{H,\mathrm{M}}^{\star}\bigr)^{2}\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}\bigl(1-a_{H,\mathrm{M},2}^{\mathrm{fh}}\bar{\alpha}_{j}\bigr)

(454)

\displaystyle+a_{H,\mathrm{M},4}^{\mathrm{fh}}\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\bar{\alpha}_{i}^{(2)}\prod_{j=i+1}^{m-1}\bigl(1-a_{H,\mathrm{M},2}^{\mathrm{fh}}\bar{\alpha}_{j}\bigr).

Proof.

Iterating the second bound in Proposition
46
gives

\mathbb{E}\left[M_{H,\mathrm{M}}(W_{m,0})\right]\leq M_{H,\mathrm{M}}(W_{0,0})\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}\bigl(1-a_{H,\mathrm{M},2}^{\mathrm{fh}}\bar{\alpha}_{j}\bigr)+c^{\mathrm{fh}}_{H,\mathrm{M},2}\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\bar{\alpha}^{(2)}_{i}\prod_{j=i+1}^{m-1}\bigl(1-a_{H,\mathrm{M},2}^{\mathrm{fh}}\bar{\alpha}_{j}\bigr).

(455)

By the Moreau envelope comparison,

\mathbb{E}[\left\lVert W_{m,0}\right\rVert^{2}_{H,2,\infty}]\leq 2(1+\vartheta_{H,\mathrm{M}}\lvert\mathcal{S}_{H}\rvert^{2/p^{\star}_{H}})\mathbb{E}\bigl[M_{H,\mathrm{M}}(W_{m,0})\bigr]

(456)

and

M_{H,\mathrm{M}}(W_{0,0})\leq\frac{1}{2(1+\vartheta_{H,\mathrm{M}})}\left\lVert W_{0,0}\right\rVert^{2}_{H,2,\infty}.

(457)

The embedding isometric identity

\left\lVert W_{m,0}\right\rVert_{H,2,\infty}=\ell_{H,\mathrm{M},\infty}(\eta_{mH},\eta^{\star}_{H,\mathrm{M}})

(458)

provides the claim.
∎

Corollary 48

(Boundary-iterate step size consequences)
.

Under the hypotheses of Proposition
47
:

(a)
if
\alpha_{k}\equiv\alpha
and

\alpha\leq\frac{a_{H,\mathrm{M},2}^{\mathrm{fh}}}{a_{H,\mathrm{M},3}^{\mathrm{fh}}},

(459)

then for all episodes
m\geq 0
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\ell_{H,\mathrm{M},\infty}(\eta_{mH},\eta_{H,\mathrm{M}}^{\star})^{2}\right]\leq a_{H,\mathrm{M},1}^{\mathrm{fh}}\ell_{H,\mathrm{M},\infty}(\eta_{0},\eta_{H,\mathrm{M}}^{\star})^{2}\bigl(1-a_{H,\mathrm{M},2}^{\mathrm{fh}}H\alpha\bigr)^{m}+\frac{a_{H,\mathrm{M},4}^{\mathrm{fh}}}{a_{H,\mathrm{M},2}^{\mathrm{fh}}}\alpha.

(460)

For the two diminishing-step cases below, where
g
is the step-size offset, write
\tau_{m}:=mH+g+H-1
, so
\tau_{0}=g+H-1
.

(b)
if
\alpha_{k}=\alpha/(k+g)
,
\alpha>1/a_{H,\mathrm{M},2}^{\mathrm{fh}}
, and

then the boundary iterates satisfy

\mathbb{E}\left[\ell_{H,\mathrm{M},\infty}(\eta_{mH},\eta_{H,\mathrm{M}}^{\star})^{2}\right]\leq a_{H,\mathrm{M},1}^{\mathrm{fh}}\ell_{H,\mathrm{M},\infty}(\eta_{0},\eta_{H,\mathrm{M}}^{\star})^{2}\left(\frac{\tau_{0}}{\tau_{m}}\right)^{a_{H,\mathrm{M},2}^{\mathrm{fh}}\alpha}+\frac{a_{H,\mathrm{M},4}^{\mathrm{fh}}H^{2}\alpha^{2}}{a_{H,\mathrm{M},2}^{\mathrm{fh}}\alpha-1}\cdot\frac{1}{\tau_{m}}.

(462)

(c)
if
\alpha_{k}=\alpha/(k+g)^{z}
with
z\in(0,1)
and

g\geq\max\left\{1,\left(\frac{\alpha a_{H,\mathrm{M},3}^{\mathrm{fh}}}{a_{H,\mathrm{M},2}^{\mathrm{fh}}}\right)^{1/z},\left(\frac{2z}{a_{H,\mathrm{M},2}^{\mathrm{fh}}\alpha}\right)^{1/(1-z)}\right\},

(463)

then the boundary iterates satisfy

\begin{gathered}\mathbb{E}\left[\ell_{H,\mathrm{M},\infty}(\eta_{mH},\eta_{H,\mathrm{M}}^{\star})^{2}\right]\leq a_{H,\mathrm{M},1}^{\mathrm{fh}}\ell_{H,\mathrm{M},\infty}(\eta_{0},\eta_{H,\mathrm{M}}^{\star})^{2}\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{a_{H,\mathrm{M},2}^{\mathrm{fh}}\alpha}{1-z}\bigl(\tau_{m}^{1-z}-\tau_{0}^{1-z}\bigr)\right)\\
+\frac{2a_{H,\mathrm{M},4}^{\mathrm{fh}}H^{2}\alpha}{a_{H,\mathrm{M},2}^{\mathrm{fh}}}\cdot\frac{1}{\tau_{m}^{z}}.\end{gathered}

(464)

Proof.

For part (a), substitution of
\bar{\alpha}_{m}=H\alpha
and
\bar{\alpha}_{m}^{(2)}=H\alpha^{2}
into Proposition
47
yields part (a).

For part (b), set
q=\tau_{0}/H
and
\lambda=a_{H,\mathrm{M},2}^{\mathrm{fh}}\alpha
. The bounds

\frac{H\alpha}{\tau_{m}}\leq\bar{\alpha}_{m}\leq\frac{H\alpha}{mH+g},\qquad\bar{\alpha}_{m}^{(2)}\leq\frac{H\alpha^{2}}{(mH+g)^{2}}.

(465)

imply

\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}(1-a_{H,\mathrm{M},2}^{\mathrm{fh}}\bar{\alpha}_{j})\leq\left(\frac{q}{m+q}\right)^{\lambda}.

(466)

The same elementary product-sum estimate gives

\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\bar{\alpha}_{i}^{(2)}\prod_{j=i+1}^{m-1}(1-a_{H,\mathrm{M},2}^{\mathrm{fh}}\bar{\alpha}_{j})\leq\frac{H\alpha^{2}}{\lambda-1}\cdot\frac{1}{m+q},

(467)

and the displayed bound follows from Proposition
47
after substituting
q=\tau_{0}/H
and
\tau_{m}=H(m+q)
.

For part (c), keep the same
q
and set
A=a_{H,\mathrm{M},2}^{\mathrm{fh}}\alpha H^{1-z}
. The bounds

\frac{H\alpha}{\tau_{m}^{z}}\leq\bar{\alpha}_{m}\leq\frac{H\alpha}{(mH+g)^{z}},\qquad\bar{\alpha}_{m}^{(2)}\leq\frac{H\alpha^{2}}{(mH+g)^{2z}}.

(468)

imply

\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}(1-a_{H,\mathrm{M},2}^{\mathrm{fh}}\bar{\alpha}_{j})\leq\exp\left(-\frac{A}{1-z}\bigl((m+q)^{1-z}-q^{1-z}\bigr)\right).

(469)

The lower bound on
g
implies
q\geq(2z/A)^{1/(1-z)}
. The elementary polynomial product-sum estimate gives

\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\bar{\alpha}_{i}^{(2)}\prod_{j=i+1}^{m-1}(1-a_{H,\mathrm{M},2}^{\mathrm{fh}}\bar{\alpha}_{j})\leq\frac{2H\alpha^{2}}{A}\cdot\frac{1}{(m+q)^{z}}.

(470)

Substituting the definitions of
A
and
q
into Proposition
47
, using
\tau_{m}=H(m+q)
, and using
H^{2z}\leq H^{2}
, gives the displayed bound.

Proof of Corollary

.

By Corollary

(b),

\mathbb{E}\left[\ell_{H,\mathrm{M},\infty}(\eta_{mH},\eta_{H,\mathrm{M}}^{\star})^{2}\right]=O\left(\frac{1}{m+1}\right).

(471)

By Jensen’s inequality,

\mathbb{E}\left[\ell_{H,\mathrm{M},\infty}(\eta_{mH},\eta_{H,\mathrm{M}}^{\star})\right]=O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{m+1}}\right).

(472)

Thus
m=O(\varepsilon^{-2})
episodes suffice. Since
k=mH
and
H
is fixed, this is equivalently
k=O(\varepsilon^{-2})
transitions.

Appendix F
Representation error for projected categorical policy evaluation

In all projected categorical settings considered in this paper, the algorithm converges to the fixed point of a projected Bellman operator rather than directly to the exact return-distribution fixed point. This appendix records the corresponding deterministic representation error and combines it with the finite-iteration bounds of the main text.

Let
(\mathcal{Y},\ell)
be a complete metric space. Let
T:\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{Y}
be a contraction with modulus
\beta\in(0,1)
, and let
\Pi:\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{Y}
be nonexpansive in
\ell
. Let
\eta^{\pi}
be the fixed point of
T
and
\eta^{\star}
be the fixed point of
\Pi T
. Let

\varepsilon^{\mathrm{repr}}:=\ell(\Pi\eta^{\pi},\eta^{\pi}).

(473)

Proposition 49
.

We have

\ell(\eta^{\star},\eta^{\pi})\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{\mathrm{repr}}}{1-\beta}.

(474)

Proof.

Using
\eta^{\star}=\Pi T\eta^{\star}
and
\eta^{\pi}=T\eta^{\pi}
,

\displaystyle\ell(\eta^{\star},\eta^{\pi})

\displaystyle=\ell(\Pi T\eta^{\star},\eta^{\pi})

(475)

\displaystyle\leq\ell(\Pi T\eta^{\star},\Pi T\eta^{\pi})+\ell(\Pi T\eta^{\pi},\eta^{\pi})

\displaystyle\leq\ell(T\eta^{\star},T\eta^{\pi})+\varepsilon^{\mathrm{repr}}

\displaystyle\leq\beta\,\ell(\eta^{\star},\eta^{\pi})+\varepsilon^{\mathrm{repr}}.

Rearranging gives the claim.
∎

Corollary 50
.

For every random iterate
\eta_{k}
,

\ell(\eta_{k},\eta^{\pi})^{2}\leq 2\ell(\eta_{k},\eta^{\star})^{2}+2\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{\mathrm{repr}}}{1-\beta}\right)^{2}.

(476)

Consequently,

\mathbb{E}[\ell(\eta_{k},\eta^{\pi})^{2}]\leq 2\mathbb{E}[\ell(\eta_{k},\eta^{\star})^{2}]+2\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{\mathrm{repr}}}{1-\beta}\right)^{2}.

(477)

Proof.

By the triangle inequality and
(a+b)^{2}\leq 2a^{2}+2b^{2}
,

\ell(\eta_{k},\eta^{\pi})^{2}\leq 2\ell(\eta_{k},\eta^{\star})^{2}+2\ell(\eta^{\star},\eta^{\pi})^{2}.

(478)

Now apply Proposition
49
.
∎

Instantiation in the present paper.

In the discounted setting,
\ell
is either
\ell_{\mathrm{C},\infty}
or
\ell_{\mathrm{M},\infty}
, and

Appendix G
Further discussion of the results

Bounded and affine perturbation regimes.

After the statewise isometric embeddings, both CTD and MTD fit the same asynchronous contractive stochastic-approximation template. The main difference is the geometry of the sampled perturbation. In the scalar categorical case, bounded supports together with the Cramér geometry yield uniform samplewise bounds in both the discounted and fixed-horizon undiscounted settings. In the discounted setting,

\lVert\widehat{T}_{\mathrm{C}}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))-(\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{C}}U)(s)\rVert_{2}\leq 2B_{\mathrm{C}}.

(479)

In the fixed-horizon setting,

\lVert F_{H,\mathrm{C}}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq 2B_{H,\mathrm{C}}.

(480)

Thus CTD falls into a bounded-noise regime throughout the paper. In particular, the i.i.d. conditional second moment is uniformly bounded in the discounted analysis, and the fixed-horizon episodewise argument inherits only support-radius constants.

By contrast, in the multivariate signed-categorical case one obtains only an affine perturbation bounds. In the discounted setting,

\lVert\widehat{T}_{\mathrm{M}}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))-(\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{M}}U)(s)\rVert_{2}\leq 2\beta_{\mathrm{M}}\lVert U\rVert_{2,\infty}+B_{\mathrm{M}},

(481)

together with a local second-moment estimate

\mathbb{E}\left[\lVert\widehat{T}_{\mathrm{M}}(U_{k};S_{k},(R_{k},S_{k+1}))-(\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{M}}U_{k})(S_{k})\rVert_{2}^{2}\,\mid\,U_{k},S_{k}\right]\leq C_{1}+C_{2}\lVert U_{k}\rVert_{2,\infty}^{2}.

(482)

In the fixed-horizon setting, the analogous residual bound is

\lVert F_{H,\mathrm{M}}(U;s,(r,s^{\prime}))\rVert_{H,2,\infty}\leq 2\lVert U\rVert_{H,2,\infty}+B^{\mathrm{res}}_{H,\mathrm{M}},

(483)

again with an affine conditional second-moment estimate. This is why the MTD theorem constants contain additional growth, bias, and absorption quantities, whereas the CTD bounds depend only on support-radius constants. This is also the reason for the MTD theorem constants containing additional quantities such as
C_{1},C_{2},B_{\mathrm{M}},\Upsilon_{\mathrm{M}}
, whereas the CTD bounds depend only on the support-radius constant
B_{\mathrm{C}}
.

Smoothing constants and the number of asynchronous blocks.

The finite-iteration analysis is driven by a block-supremum contraction norm and a smoothed block-
\ell_{p}
potential. The only explicit dimension loss in this smoothing step comes from the comparison between
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{2,\infty}
and
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{2,p}
, and therefore depends only on the number of asynchronously updated blocks. In the discounted appendices, those blocks are indexed only by the state variable and the relevant count is
\lvert\mathcal{S}\rvert
. In the fixed-horizon appendices, the stacked process is first flattened over horizon and state, so the relevant count becomes
\lvert\mathcal{S}_{H}\rvert=H\lvert\mathcal{S}\rvert
. The inner representation dimension
d
, and in the multivariate case the reward dimension
q
, still affect problem-dependent constants, but they do not enter through the Moreau-envelope smoothing comparison itself.

Discounted and fixed-horizon contraction mechanisms.

The fixed-horizon undiscounted theorems should not be read as the discounted results with
\gamma=1
. In the discounted setting, the contraction comes directly from the Bellman discount factor and each update modifies only one sampled state block. In the fixed-horizon setting there is no discount-driven contraction. Instead, the contraction is recovered by weighting the horizon layers and exploiting the fact that horizon
h
bootstraps from horizon
h-1
. The corresponding online recursion is also structurally different: one sampled transition updates the entire horizon stack at the sampled state. Thus the fixed-horizon setting is different both in its contraction mechanism and in its stochastic approximation structure.

Interpretation of the step size regimes.

Across all theorem statements, the product-sum bounds imply the same qualitative picture. Constant step sizes yield geometric convergence to a controllable
O(\alpha)
neighborhood of the projected fixed point. Linearly-diminishing step sizes yield the strongest asymptotic decay, namely
O(1/k)
in the squared error and hence
O(\varepsilon^{-2})
sample complexity for mean error at most
\varepsilon
. Polynomially-diminishing step sizes trade a weaker asymptotic decay for milder admissibility requirements. In the discounted Markovian case, these conclusions hold up to the expected logarithmic mixing-time overhead inherited from the Markovian finite-iteration theorem. This is the standard price of replacing i.i.d. sampling by a trajectory-generated sample path.

Algorithmic and representation error.

The finite-iteration theorems control distance to the fixed point of a projected Bellman operator. The representation-error decomposition then separates total error into an algorithmic term and a deterministic approximation term. This separation is conceptually useful: the stochastic-approximation analysis explains how quickly the recursion approaches the projected fixed point for a fixed support family, whereas the deterministic term isolates the bias introduced by the categorical approximation itself.
