Controllability of quasilinear parabolic equations under multiplicative mobile controls
Lingyang Liu

Abstract
This paper addresses the controllability of a class of quasi-linear parabolic equations governed by multiplicative controls with mobile support. To prove the existence of such a control forcing the solution to rest at time T > 0 T>0 , we first establish the decay property of solutions for the uncontrolled system. Unlike the case of the linear heat equation, the nonlinearity in the principal part of the operator introduces significant challenges. These difficulties necessitate a novel approach, ultimately leading us to solve the controllability problem within the framework of classical solutions. Through a carefully constructed smooth transition, we demonstrate that there exists a multiplicative control driving the state exactly to rest at time t = T t=T .



Keywords: Nonlinear parabolic equation, Controllability, Multiplicative control, Nonnegative additive control

1
Introduction and main result

It is known that linear, semilinear and quasilinear parabolic systems–in the absence of constraints–are controllable in any positive time (see, e.g.,
[
1
,
8
]
). However,
controllability under nonnegativity constraints in parabolic systems does not hold when the
time horizon is too short. The first relevant work appears to be
[
9
]
. There, the authors studied the existence of a positive minimal controllability time for the heat equation under unilateral state or control constraints. In a later study, the authors of
[
11
]
extended the analysis of
[
9
]
to semilinear equations with
C^{1}
nonlinearities. In
[
10
]
, the same question for quasilinear parabolic equations was investigated. It is also worth mentioning that the nonnegative controllability of a class of Newtonian filtration equations with interior degeneracy was discussed in
[
7
]
. It was shown that the target set is approximately controllable when the control acts on the entire spatial domain, while controllability fails if the control is restricted to a strict open subset. These works focus on the constrained controllability of parabolic equations governed by additive controls acting on a proper subset of the boundary or the domain.

Regarding multiplicative controllability in parabolic systems, the authors of
[
2
]
proved the approximate controllability of the one-dimensional heat equation governed by either multiplicative or nonnegative additive controls with mobile local support. Interestingly, they showed that for any
T>0
, the heat equation lacks controllability under static support. Nevertheless, global approximate controllability was achieved with mobile support. In
[
5
]
, the authors generalized the results of
[
2
]
to the case of heat equations with boundary degeneracy.

Let
T>0
and denote
Q_{T}:=(0,1)\times(0,T)
. In this paper, we consider the following Dirichlet problems:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}y_{t}-(a(y)y_{x})_{x}=u(x,t)y(x,t)\chi_{\omega(t)}(x)&\text{in}\ Q_{T},\\[5.69054pt]
y(0,t)=y(1,t)=0&\text{in}\ (0,T),\\[5.69054pt]
y(x,0)=y_{0}(x)&\text{in}\ (0,1),\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
y_{0}\in L^{2}_{+}(0,1)=\{\phi\in L^{2}(0,1):\phi(x)\geq 0\ \text{a.e.}\ \text{in}\ (0,1)\}
,
\omega(t)
is a non-empty open subset of
(0,1)
for every
t\in(0,T)
,
\chi_{\omega(t)}

denotes the characteristic function of
\omega(t)
,
u
is a multiplicative control that, at each time
t
, is supported in
{\omega(t)}
, and
a
is assumed to be a real-valued function satisfying

a\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}),\quad\inf\limits_{r\in\mathbb{R}}a(r)>0\quad\text{and}\quad\sup\limits_{r\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{|a^{\prime}(r)|,|a^{\prime\prime}(r)|\right\}<\infty.

(1.2)

We address the null controllability of quasilinear parabolic equations under multiplicative control. Our objective is to find an
L^{\infty}
-control steering the state of the parabolic system to zero exactly at any finite time
T>0
. It is observed that the parabolic system (
1.1
) is not controllable in
L^{2}_{+}(0,1)
with a static support. Hence, we allow the control support to move in order to achieve the null controllability of (
1.1
). Specifically, the authors of
[
2
]
applied the method of separation of variables to design controls that achieve the desired steering, even allowing for the control to be zero at the end of the process. However, the method of constructing an explicit control function seems inapplicable to quasilinear parabolic problems, due to the presence of time-space dependent coefficients in the principal part.

In the sequel, we assume that

\omega(t)=(r(t),r(t)+l),

(1.3)

where the length
l\in(0,1)
is fixed and
r:[0,T]\rightarrow[0,1-l]
is a function that defines the position of the support
at time
t
. By definition, the support
\omega(t)
is static if
r
is constant and mobile if
r
is time-varying.
Furthermore, we denote by
\tilde{y}_{y_{0}}
the solution of the uncontrolled system
and by
PC[0,T]
the set of piecewise constant functions
r:[0,T]\to[0,1-l]
with at most a finite number of discontinuities.

First, we will show the lack of controllability for (
1.1
) with a static subdomain.

Theorem 1.1

Let us assume that
y_{0}\in L^{2}_{+}(0,1)
,
y_{0}\not\equiv 0
, and
\omega
is
a fixed proper subinterval of
(0,1)
. Denote by
y_{y_{0},u,\omega}
the solution of system (
1.1
). Then, for any
T>0
, the set

F_{y_{0},\omega}(T):=\left\{y_{y_{0},u,\omega}(x,T):x\in(0,1),u\in L^{\infty}(Q_{T}),\omega(t)\equiv\omega,\forall t\in(0,T)\right\}

is not dense in
L^{2}_{+}(0,1)
.

The main result is stated as follows.

Theorem 1.2

System (
1.1
) is locally approximately null controllable in
L^{2}_{+}(0,1)
. That is, for any
T>0
,
\epsilon>0
,
\sigma\in(0,1)
,
y_{d}\in L^{2}_{+}(0,1)
, there exists a positive constant
\gamma
, such that for any given initial datum
y_{0}\in L^{2}_{+}(0,1)\cap C^{2+\frac{\sigma}{2}}([0,1])
, satisfying
|y_{0}|_{C^{2+\frac{\sigma}{2}}([0,1])}\leq\gamma
and the first order compatibility condition, there exists a bilinear control
u\in C^{\frac{\sigma}{2},\frac{\sigma}{4}}(\overline{Q_{T}})
such that the corresponding solution of (
1.1
) satisfies

\|u(T)-y_{d}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}<\epsilon.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we prove the lack of controllability for the system with a static control support. Section
3
is devoted to proving the constrained approximate controllability of the linearized system via an additive, nonnegative, locally distributed control. In Section
4
, we analyze the multiplicative approximate controllability of the linearized system. Finally, Section
5
contains the proof of our main result, namely, Theorem
1.2
.

2
Noncontrollability of system (
1.1
) in
L^{2}_{+}(0,1)

In this section, we show that system (
1.1
) is not approximately controllable in
L^{2}_{+}(0,1)
at any time
T>0
when the control acts on a static subinterval of
(0,1)
.
To prove this result, we first consider quasilinear parabolic equations of the form:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}y_{t}-(a(y)y_{x})_{x}=u(x,t)y(x,t)+v(x,t)&\text{in}\ Q_{T},\\[5.69054pt]
y(x,0)=y_{0}(x)&\text{in}\ (0,1),\end{array}\right.

(2.1)

where
a
satisfies the condition (
1.2
).

With respect to (
2.1
), we present the following lemma:

Lemma 2.1
(Maximum Principle)

Let us assume that
y_{0}\in L^{2}(0,1)
,
v\in L^{2}(Q_{T})
and
u\in L^{\infty}(Q_{T})
.
Let
y\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(0,1))\cap C([0,T];L^{2}(0,1))
be a solution of equation (
2.1
). Then the following properties hold:

(i)

If
y_{0}\geq 0
in
(0,1)
,
v\geq 0
in
Q_{T}
,
y(0,t)\geq 0
and
y(1,t)\geq 0
in
(0,T)
, then
y(x,t)\geq 0
a.e. in
Q_{T}
;

(ii)

if
v\equiv 0
,
u\leq 0
in
Q_{T}
,
y(0,\cdot)
,
y(1,\cdot)\in L^{\infty}(0,T)
and
y_{0}\in L^{\infty}(0,1)
, then
\sup\limits_{{Q_{T}}}|y(x,t)|\leq\sup\limits_{{\Gamma_{T}}}|y(x,t)|
, where
\Gamma_{T}=([0,1]\times\{0\})\cup(\{0,1\}\times(0,T])
;

(iii)

If
y\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(0,1))
with
y_{0}\geq 0
in
(0,1)
(
y_{0}\not\equiv 0
),
v\geq 0
in
Q_{T}
and
u=u(x)\leq 0
in
(0,1)
, then
y(\cdot,t)>0
in
(0,1)
for all
t\in(0,T]
.

By the fixed point technique and the maximum principle for linear parabolic equations (see
[
4
, Ch. III, §7, pp. 181-191]
and
[
12
, pp. 21-23]
), one can prove the results of Lemma
2.1
. Regarding the classical setting, see also
[
4
, Ch. I, §2, pp. 11-25]
and
[
3
, pp. 121-122]
.

Moreover, the following comparison principle is valid:

Lemma 2.2

Let
y_{1}
and
y_{2}
be two solutions of equation (
2.1
) such that

y_{1}(0,t)\leq y_{2}(0,t)
and
y_{1}(1,t)\leq y_{2}(1,t)
in
(0,T)
.
Then
y_{1}\leq y_{2}
in
Q_{T}
.

Now, we are in a position to give a proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.
We will show that the set
F_{y_{0},\omega}(T)
(see (
1.2
)) is not dense in
L^{2}_{+}(0,1)
. To prove this, we argue by contradiction. Let
y_{d}\in L^{2}_{+}(0,1)
. Suppose that there exists a sequence

\{u_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset L^{\infty}(Q_{T})
such that:

y_{y_{0},u_{k},\omega}(\cdot,T)\rightarrow y_{d}\quad\text{in}\ L^{2}_{+}(0,1)\ \text{as}\ k\rightarrow+\infty.

Put
y_{d}=0
. Then for any
\widetilde{\omega}=(a,b)\subset(0,1)\setminus\omega
, we have

y_{y_{0},u_{k},\omega}(\cdot,T)\mid_{\widetilde{\omega}}\rightarrow 0\quad\text{in}\ L^{2}_{+}(\widetilde{\omega})\ \text{as}\ k\rightarrow+\infty.

It follows from Lemma
2.1
(i) that
y_{y_{0},u_{k},\omega}\geq 0
in
Q_{T}
. Let
y_{y_{0},u_{k},\omega}\mid_{\widetilde{\omega}}
be the solution to the following parabolic problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}y_{t}-(a(y)y_{x})_{x}=0,&\text{in}\ \widetilde{\omega}\times(0,T),\\[5.69054pt]
y(a,t)=h_{0}(t),\ y(b,t)=h_{1}(t),&\text{in}\ (0,T),\\[5.69054pt]
y(x,0)=y_{0}(x),&\text{in}\ \widetilde{\omega},\end{array}\right.

where
h_{0}(t)
and
h_{1}(t)
are nonnegative boundary values.

Denote by
\hat{y}
the solution to the homogeneous parabolic problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\hat{y}_{t}-(a(\hat{y})\hat{y}_{x})_{x}=0,&\text{in}\ \widetilde{\omega}\times(0,T),\\[5.69054pt]
\hat{y}(a,t)=0,\ \hat{y}(b,t)=0,&\text{in}\ (0,T),\\[5.69054pt]
\hat{y}(x,0)=y_{0}(x),&\text{in}\ \widetilde{\omega}.\end{array}\right.

If
y_{0}\geq 0
and
y_{0}\not\equiv 0
in
\widetilde{\omega}
, then Lemma
2.1
(iii) implies that
\hat{y}(\cdot,T)>0
in
\widetilde{\omega}
.

Applying Lemma
2.2
in
\widetilde{\omega}\times(0,T)
, we see that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,

y_{y_{0},u_{k},\omega}(\cdot,T)\mid_{\widetilde{\omega}}\geq\hat{y}(\cdot,T)>0\quad\text{in}\ \widetilde{\omega}.

Thus, we arrive at the contradiction

0=\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\|y_{y_{0},u_{k},\omega}(\cdot,T)\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{\omega})}\geq\|\hat{y}(\cdot,T)\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{\omega})}>0,

which proves the original claim.

3
Approximately controllability of linear parabolic equations by additive control

This section addresses the additive nonnegative controllability problem with mobile support:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}y_{t}-(by_{x})_{x}=v(x,t)\chi_{\omega(t)}(x),&\text{in }Q_{T},\\[5.69054pt]
y(0,t)=y(1,t)=0,&\text{in }(0,T),\\[5.69054pt]
y(x,0)=y_{0}(x),&\text{in }(0,1),\end{array}\right.

(3.1)

where
b\in C^{1,1}(\overline{Q_{T}})
satisfies for some constant
\rho>0
,

b(x,t)\geq\rho\quad\forall(x,t)\in\overline{Q_{T}},

(3.2)

and
\chi_{\omega(t)}
denotes the characteristic function with moving support given by (
1.3
).

We establish the following approximate controllability result for (
3.1
):

Theorem 3.1

Let us assume that
{y}_{0}\in L^{2}_{+}(0,1)
,
y_{0}\not\equiv 0
.
Then, for any
T>0
, the set

F^{+}_{1,0,mb}(T):=\left\{y_{y_{0},v,r}(\cdot,T):v\in L^{2}_{+}(Q_{T})\ \text{and}\ r\in PC[0,T]\right\}

is dense in
L^{2}_{+}(0,1)
.

To prove Theorem
3.1
, we first establish the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1

For
y_{0}\in H_{0}^{1}(0,1)\cap L^{2}_{+}(0,1)
, denote by
\tilde{y}
the unique solution of (
3.1
) with
v=0
. Then the following estimate holds:

\|\tilde{y}(\cdot,T)-y_{0}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\leq\sqrt{|b|_{C(\overline{Q_{T}})}T}\|y_{0,x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}.

(3.3)

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.
Define
Y(x,t)=\tilde{y}(x,t)-y_{0}(x)
. Then
Y
satisfies the initial-boundary value problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}Y_{t}-(bY_{x})_{x}=(by_{0,x})_{x},&\text{in }Q_{T},\\[5.69054pt]
Y(0,t)=Y(1,t)=0,&\text{in }(0,T),\\[5.69054pt]
Y(x,0)=0,&\text{in }(0,1).\end{array}\right.

(3.4)

Multiplying both sides of the first equation of (
3.4
) by
Y
and integrating over
(0,1)
, we obtain

\frac{d}{2dt}\int^{1}_{0}Y^{2}dx+\int^{1}_{0}bY^{2}_{x}dx=-\int^{1}_{0}by_{0,x}Y_{x}dx.

Combining (
3.2
) with the Cauchy inequality, we find

\frac{d}{dt}\int^{1}_{0}Y^{2}dx\leq\int^{1}_{0}by^{2}_{0,x}dx.

Since
Y(x,0)=0
, it follows that

\|Y(\cdot,T)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\leq\sqrt{|b|_{C(\overline{Q_{T}})}T}\|y_{0,x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)},

which completes the proof of Lemma
3.1
.

Remark 3.1

Estimate (
3.3
) implies that
\tilde{y}(\cdot,T)
is close to
y_{0}
in the
L^{2}
norm provided the time
T
is sufficiently small.

Now, consider the following controlled linear parabolic equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}y_{t}-(by_{x})_{x}=v(x,t)&\text{in }\omega\times(0,T),\\[5.69054pt]
y=0&\text{on }\partial\omega\times(0,T),\\[5.69054pt]
y(0)=y_{0}&\text{in }\omega,\end{array}\right.

(3.5)

where
\omega
is a fixed proper subinterval of
(0,1)
.

We have the following regional nonnegative controllability result for (
3.5
).

Lemma 3.2

Let
y_{0}\in L^{2}(\omega)
and denote by
y(\cdot;v)
the solution of (
3.5
). Then, for any
T>0
, the set

F:=\left\{y(T;v):v\in L^{2}_{+}(\omega\times(0,T))\right\}

is dense in
y(T;0)+L^{2}_{+}(\omega)
.

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.
Without loss of generality, we assume
y_{0}\equiv 0
. Arguing by contradiction, suppose that there exists
y_{d}\in L^{2}_{+}(\omega)
such that
y_{d}\notin\overline{F}
. Note that
\overline{F}
is a closed and convex set. By the Hahn-Banach theorem (in its geometrical form), we can separate
y_{d}
from
\overline{F}
, i.e., there exist
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
g\in L^{2}(\omega)
such that

\int_{\omega}y(T;v)g\,dx\leq 0<\alpha<\int_{\omega}y_{d}g\,dx\quad\forall v\in L^{2}_{+}(\omega\times(0,T)).

(3.6)

Let
q\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\omega))
be the solution of the auxiliary backward problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-q_{t}-(bq_{x})_{x}=0&\text{in }\omega\times(0,T),\\[5.69054pt]
q=0&\text{on }\partial\omega\times(0,T),\\[5.69054pt]
q(T)=g&\text{in }\omega.\end{array}\right.

(3.7)

Multiplying both sides of the first equation of (
3.7
) by
y
and integrating by parts, we obtain

0\geq\int_{\omega}gy(T;v)\,dx=\int_{\omega\times(0,T)}qv\,dxdt\quad\forall v\in L^{2}_{+}(\omega\times(0,T)).

This implies that
q\leq 0
a.e. in
\omega\times(0,T)
. In particular,
g=q(T)\leq 0
. This contradicts (
3.6
), because (
3.6
) implies that
\int_{\omega}y_{d}g\,dx>0
while
y_{d}\geq 0
and
g\leq 0
would force this integral to be non-positive.

Remark 3.2

By the classical convolution technique, we can deduce that there exists a sequence
\{v_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset C_{+}(\overline{\omega\times(0,T)})
such that
v_{k}\rightarrow v
in
L^{2}(\omega\times(0,T))
as
k\rightarrow+\infty
. Let
y
and
y_{k}
denote the unique solutions of (
3.5
) corresponding to the controls
v
and
v_{k}
, respectively. Subtracting the two equations, multiplying the resulting equation by the difference
y-{y_{k}}
, and integrating by parts yields

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\quad\frac{1}{2}\int_{\omega}(y(x,T)-y_{k}(x,T))^{2}dx+\int_{\omega\times(0,T)}b(x,t)\left((y(x,t)-y_{k}(x,t))_{x}\right)^{2}dxdt\\[9.95845pt]
=\displaystyle\int_{\omega\times(0,T)}(v(x,t)-v_{k}(x,t))(y(x,t)-y_{k}(x,t))dxdt,\end{array}

Using condition (
3.2
) and applying the Cauchy and Poincaré inequalities, we obtain

\|y(\cdot,T)-y_{k}(\cdot,T)\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\leq C(\rho,meas(\omega))\|v_{k}-v\|_{L^{2}(\omega\times(0,T))}.

Consequently, the
L^{2}
-norm of the difference between the solutions at time
T
can be made arbitrarily small by taking the
L^{2}
-norm of the difference of the controls sufficiently small.

Now, we are in a position to give a proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

First, let us fix
M
as the smallest natural number satisfying
M\cdot l\geq 1
, where
l
is the (fixed) length of the moving subinterval
\omega(t)
(see (
1.3
)).
Given any element
y_{d}\in L_{+}^{2}(0,1)
, we can decompose it into
M
“pieces” localized in disjoint subintervals of length at most
l
, as follows:

y_{d_{1}}(x)=\begin{cases}y_{d}(x),&x\in(0,l),\\
0,&x\in[l,1),\end{cases}

y_{d_{j}}(x)=\begin{cases}y_{d}(x),&x\in((j-1)l,jl),\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

for
j=2,\ldots,M-1
,

y_{d_{M}}(x)=\begin{cases}y_{d}(x),&x\in((M-1)l,1),\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Clearly, each
y_{d_{j}}\in L^{2}_{+}(0,1)
and

y_{d}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{M}y_{d_{j}}(x),\quad\text{a.e. }x\in(0,1).

Moreover, given
\epsilon>0
, there exist
\hat{y}_{d_{j}}\in H_{0}^{1}(0,1)\cap L^{2}_{+}(0,1)
for
j=1,\ldots,M
, with its support
\omega_{j}
contained in the corresponding one of each
y_{d_{j}}
and verifying

\sum_{j=1}^{M}\|\hat{y}_{d_{j}}-y_{d_{j}}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

We now present a sequential argument, using the controls provided by Lemma
3.2
, as follows: given
\hat{y}_{d_{j}}
,
\delta_{j}\in(0,\delta_{j-1}/2)
,
j=1,\ldots,M
, there exist controls

\hat{v}_{j}(x,t)=\begin{cases}0,&t\in(0,T-2\delta_{j}),\\
{v}_{j}(x,t),&t\in(T-2\delta_{j},T-\delta_{j}),\\
0,&t\in(T-\delta_{j},T),\end{cases}

for
j=1,\dots,M-1
, and

\hat{v}_{M}(x,t)=\begin{cases}0,&t\in(0,T-\delta_{M}),\\
{v}_{M}(x,t),&t\in(T-\delta_{M},T),\end{cases}

for which

\|\tilde{y}_{0,\hat{v}_{j},\omega_{j}}(\cdot,T)-\hat{y}_{d_{j}}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2M},\quad j=1,\dots,M.

We finish the proof by taking

v(x,t)=\sum_{j=1}^{M}\hat{v}_{j}(x,t),\qquad\tilde{y}(x,t)=\sum_{j=1}^{M}\tilde{y}_{0,\hat{v}_{j},\omega_{j}}(x,t)

and noticing that by the linearity

\displaystyle\|\tilde{y}(\cdot,T)-y_{d}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\tilde{y}(\cdot,T)-\sum_{j=1}^{M}\hat{y}_{d_{j}}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}+\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{M}\hat{y}_{d_{j}}-y_{d}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{M}\|\tilde{y}_{0,\hat{v}_{j},\omega_{j}}(\cdot,T)-\hat{y}_{d_{j}}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}+\sum_{j=1}^{M}\|\hat{y}_{d_{j}}-y_{d_{j}}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\leq\epsilon.

Note that in each time subinterval
(T-\delta_{j},T-\delta_{j+1})
, the control
v(x,t)
is equal to
\hat{v}_{j+1}(x,t)
and hence its support is included in
\omega(t)=(r(t),r(t)+l)
, with
r(t)\in PC[0,T]
given by

r(t)=\begin{cases}0,&t\in(0,T-\delta_{1}),\\
l,&t\in(T-\delta_{1},T-\delta_{2}),\\
2l,&t\in(T-\delta_{2},T-\delta_{3}),\\
\qquad\quad\vdots&\\
jl,&t\in(T-\delta_{j},T-\delta_{j+1}),\\
\qquad\quad\vdots&\\
(M-2)l,&t\in(T-\delta_{M-2},T-\delta_{M-1}),\\
1-l,&t\in(T-\delta_{M-1},T).\end{cases}

This implies that
v(x,t)=v(x,t)\chi_{\omega(t)}(x)
and
\tilde{y}=\tilde{y}_{0,v,r}
, as desired.

4
Controllability of linear parabolic equations governed by multiplicative control

In this section, we establish the controllability of the following linear parabolic equation:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}y_{t}-(by_{x})_{x}=u(x,t)y(x,t)\chi_{\omega(t)}(x),&\text{in }Q_{T},\\[5.69054pt]
y(0,t)=y(1,t)=0,&\text{in }(0,T),\\[5.69054pt]
y(x,0)=y_{0}(x),&\text{in }(0,1),\end{array}\right.

(4.1)

where
b\in C^{1,1}(\overline{Q_{T}})
satisfies the uniform ellipticity condition

b(x,t)\geq\rho>0\qquad\forall(x,t)\in\overline{Q_{T}},

(4.2)

and
\chi_{\omega(t)}
denotes the characteristic function of the time-dependent set
\omega(t)
defined in (
1.3
).

Combining Lemma
2.1
-(i), (iii) with Theorem
3.1
, and following the argument of Theorem 4.1 in
[
2
]
, we can prove the following approximate nonnegative controllability result for (
4.1
).

Lemma 4.2

Let
\sigma\in(0,1)
. Assume that
u\in C^{1}(\overline{Q_{T}})
with
u(x,t)\leq 0
for all
(x,t)\in\overline{Q_{T}}
and that

satisfies

for all

and the first order compatibility condition. Consider the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}y_{t}-(by_{x})_{x}=u(x,t)y(x,t),&\text{in }Q_{T},\\[5.69054pt]
y(0,t)=y(1,t)=0,&\text{in }(0,T),\\[5.69054pt]
y(x,0)=y_{0}(x),&\text{in }(0,1),\end{array}\right.

(4.5)

where
b\in C^{1,1}(\overline{Q_{T}})
satisfies
(
4.2
). Then, the classical solution to (
4.5
) possesses the following properties:

(a)

\|y_{t}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\leq\left[2\left(\|(b(\cdot,0)y^{\prime}_{0})^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,1)}+K_{2}\right)+K_{4}K_{3}Te^{K_{1}T}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\ \forall t\in[0,T]
, where
K_{1}
,
K_{2}
,
K_{3}
and
K_{4}
are positive constants depending only on
\|y_{0}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}
,
\|\sqrt{b(\cdot,0)}y^{\prime}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}
,
|b_{t}|_{C(\overline{Q_{T}})}
,
|u|_{C(\overline{Q_{T}})}
,
|u_{t}|_{C(\overline{Q_{T}})}
and

;

(b)

0\leq y_{x}(0,t)\leq e^{\Big(1+\frac{|b_{x}|_{C(\overline{Q_{T}})}}{\rho}\Big)}\cdot\max\limits_{x\in[0,1]}\left\{y_{0}^{\prime}(x)e^{y_{0}(x)}\right\}\quad\forall t\in[0,T]

Proof of Lemma
4.2
.

In the case
y_{0}\equiv 0
, the result is clearly valid because
y\equiv 0
. So, let us suppose that
y_{0}\not\equiv 0
.
The existence and uniqueness of a classical solution
y\in C^{2+\sigma,1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}(\overline{Q_{T}})
for problem (
4.5
) is a consequence of
[
4
, Theorem 5.2, p. 320]
. To prove (a), we first multiply both sides of the first equation of (
4.5
) by
y
, integrate over
(0,1)
, and utilize the assumption
u\leq 0
to obtain

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{1}y^{2}dx+\int_{0}^{1}by_{x}^{2}dx\leq 0.

In view of (
4.2
), it follows that

\|y(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\leq\|y_{0}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\quad\forall t\in[0,T].

(4.6)

Now, multiplying equation (
4.5
) by
y_{t}
and integrating over
(0,1)
, we deduce

\int_{0}^{1}y_{t}^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{1}by_{x}^{2}dx=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}b_{t}y_{x}^{2}dx+\int_{0}^{1}uyy_{t}dx.

Applying Cauchy’s inequality and noting that
b_{t}
and
u
are bounded, we arrive at

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{1}by_{x}^{2}dx\leq\|b_{t}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\int_{0}^{1}y_{x}^{2}dx+\|u(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\int_{0}^{1}y^{2}dx.

This, together with inequalities (
4.2
) and (
4.6
), yields

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{1}by_{x}^{2}dx\leq K_{1}\int_{0}^{1}by_{x}^{2}dx+K_{2},

where

K_{1}=\frac{|b_{t}|_{C(\overline{Q_{T}})}}{\rho}\quad\ \text{and}\quad\ K_{2}=|u|^{2}_{C(\overline{Q_{T}})}\|y_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,1)}.

By Gronwall’s inequality, we obtain

\|y_{x}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\leq\sqrt{K_{3}}e^{\frac{K_{1}t}{2}}\quad\forall t\in[0,T],

(4.7)

where

K_{3}=\frac{K_{2}+\|\sqrt{b(\cdot,0)}y^{\prime}_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,1)}}{\rho}.

Next, we differentiate (
4.5
) with respect to the time variable to deduce that
y_{t}
is the solution of the
problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}z_{t}-(bz_{x})_{x}-uz=(b_{t}y_{x})_{x}+u_{t}y&\text{in}\ Q_{T},\\[5.69054pt]
z(0,t)=z(1,t)=0&\text{in }(0,T),\\[5.69054pt]
z(x,0)=z_{0}(x)&\text{in}\ (0,1),\end{array}\right.

(4.8)

where
z_{0}(x)=\left(b(x,0)y^{\prime}_{0}(x)\right)^{\prime}+u(x,0)y_{0}(x)
.
Multiplying both sides of the first equation of (
4.8
) by
z
and integrating over
(0,1)
, we have

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{1}z^{2}dx+\int_{0}^{1}bz_{x}^{2}dx-\int_{0}^{1}uz^{2}dx=-\int_{0}^{1}b_{t}y_{x}z_{x}dx+\int_{0}^{1}u_{t}yzdx.

Since
u
is nonpositive and
b
satisfies (
4.2
), it follows that

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{1}z^{2}dx+\rho\int_{0}^{1}z_{x}^{2}dx\leq\int_{0}^{1}|b_{t}y_{x}z_{x}|dx+\int_{0}^{1}|u_{t}yz|dx.

(4.9)

We estimate the right-hand side terms of (
4.9
). First, an application of Cauchy’s inequality with parameter
\varepsilon=\rho
yields

\int_{0}^{1}|b_{t}y_{x}z_{x}|dx\leq\frac{\rho}{2}\int_{0}^{1}z_{x}^{2}dx+\frac{\|b_{t}(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}}{2\rho}\int_{0}^{1}y_{x}^{2}dx.

For the second term, using Cauchy’s inequality and Poincaré’s inequality, we obtain

\int_{0}^{1}|u_{t}yz|dx\leq\frac{\rho}{2}\int_{0}^{1}z_{x}^{2}dx+\frac{\|u_{t}(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}}{2\rho}\int_{0}^{1}y_{x}^{2}dx.

Substituting these two inequalities into (
4.9
) leads to

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{1}z^{2}dx\leq K_{4}\int_{0}^{1}y^{2}_{x}dx,

(4.10)

where

K_{4}=\frac{|b_{t}|^{2}_{C(\overline{Q_{T}})}+|u_{t}|^{2}_{C(\overline{Q_{T}})}}{\rho}.

Combining (
4.7
) with (
4.10
), we get

\|y_{t}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\leq\left[2\left(\|(b(\cdot,0)y^{\prime}_{0})^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,1)}+K_{2}\right)+K_{4}K_{3}Te^{K_{1}T}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\quad\forall t\in[0,T].

The lower bound in (b) follows directly from the definition, the homogeneous boundary condition and the nonnegativity of
y
. To derive the upper bound, we use Bernstein’s method (see
[
4
]
, pp. 414 and 537), by introducing the auxiliary function

w(x,t)=e^{y(x,t)}+Me^{1-\Big(1+\frac{|b_{x}|_{C(\overline{Q_{T}})}}{\rho}\Big)x}-1,

where
M=\max\limits_{x\in[0,1]}\big\{y^{\prime}_{0}(x)e^{y_{0}(x)}\big\}\frac{\rho}{\rho+{|b_{x}|_{C(\overline{Q_{T}})}}}e^{\frac{|b_{x}|_{C(\overline{Q_{T}})}}{\rho}}
. Note that
M>0
due to the hypotheses on
y_{0}
.

By direct calculation, it can be checked that
w\in C^{2,1}(\overline{Q_{T}})
and satisfies the following PDE:

\begin{array}[]{ll}&w_{t}(x,t)-(bw_{x})_{x}(x,t)\\[8.53581pt]
=&\displaystyle\left[u(x,t)y(x,t)-b(y_{x}(x,t))^{2}\right]e^{y(x,t)}\\[8.53581pt]
&-M\Big(1+\frac{|b_{x}|_{C(\overline{Q_{T}})}}{\rho}\Big)e^{1-\Big(1+\frac{|b_{x}|_{C(\overline{Q_{T}})}}{\rho}\Big)x}\left[\Big(1+\frac{|b_{x}|_{C(\overline{Q_{T}})}}{\rho}\Big)b-b_{x}\right].\end{array}

Let
(x_{0},t_{0})
be a point in
Q_{T}
where
w
attains its maximum. We will show that
x_{0}=0
. If
(x_{0},t_{0})\in Q_{T}
, then at this point we have
w_{t}(x_{0},t_{0})=0
,
w_{x}(x_{0},t_{0})=0
and
w_{xx}(x_{0},t_{0})\leq 0
. Substituting into the above PDE leads to the contradiction:

0\leq w_{t}(x_{0},t_{0})-(bw_{x})_{x}(x_{0},t_{0})\leq-M\Big(1+\frac{|b_{x}|_{C(\overline{Q_{T}})}}{\rho}\Big)e^{1-\Big(1+\frac{|b_{x}|_{C(\overline{Q_{T}})}}{\rho}\Big)x_{0}}b(x_{0},t_{0})<0.

The same reasoning can be used to exclude the case
t_{0}=T
, since the only difference is that
w_{t}(x_{0},t_{0})\geq 0
. Consequently,
(x_{0},t_{0})
must be a point on the parabolic boundary of
Q_{T}
, that is,
x_{0}=0
,
x_{0}=1
, or
t_{0}=0
.

The possibilities
x_{0}=1
and
t_{0}=0
can be excluded as follows. First, we observe that
w(0,t)\geq w(1,t)
. Second, at the initial time

, we have
w(x,0)=e^{y_{0}(x)}+Me^{1-\Big(1+\frac{|b_{x}|_{C(\overline{Q_{T}})}}{\rho}\Big)x}-1
. Given the choice of

, the spatial derivative at

is

w_{x}(x,0)=y^{\prime}_{0}(x)e^{y_{0}(x)}-M\Big(1+\frac{|b_{x}|_{C(\overline{Q_{T}})}}{\rho}\Big)e^{1-\Big(1+\frac{|b_{x}|_{C(\overline{Q_{T}})}}{\rho}\Big)x}\leq 0\quad\ \forall x\in[0,1].

Therefore,
w(x,0)
is non-increasing in
[0,1]
, so
w(x,0)\leq w(0,0)=Me
for all

. This implies that the maximum of

over
\overline{Q_{T}}
is attained at

, where

for all

. Hence,
w_{x}(0,t)\leq 0
, or equivalently,
y_{x}(0,t)\leq Me\Big(1+\frac{|b_{x}|_{C(\overline{Q_{T}})}}{\rho}\Big)
, for all

.

Following an argument similar to that of Corollary 4.4 in
[

]

(4.11)

&+\left[2\left(\|(b(\cdot,T_{1})y^{\prime}_{01})^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,1)}+K_{2}\right)+K_{4}K_{3}Te^{K_{1}T}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigg),\end{array}

of that lemma.

Under these conditions, we aim to prove that

\int_{0}^{2l}(y_{2}(x,T_{2}))^{2}dx\leq\frac{3\epsilon^{2}}{4(2M-1)}.

In view of inequality (
4.14
), it suffices to prove that

\int_{0}^{l}(y_{2}(x,T_{2}))^{2}dx\leq\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2(2M-1)}.

(4.17)

We multiply the PDE in (
4.13
) (with
m=m_{2}
) by
y_{2}
and integrate by parts over the domain
(0,l)\times(T_{1},T_{2})
. In this process, we use the boundary condition at
x=0
and note that the intervals
(0,l)
and
(l,2l)
are disjoint (so the source term vanishes on
(0,l)
), thus obtaining

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{l}(y_{2}(x,T_{2}))^{2}dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{l}(y_{01}(x))^{2}dx=&\displaystyle-\int_{T_{1}}^{T_{2}}\int_{0}^{l}b(x,t)\left((y_{2})_{x}(x,t)\right)^{2}dxdt\\[11.38109pt]
&\displaystyle+\int_{T_{1}}^{T_{2}}b(l,t)(y_{2})_{x}(l,t)y_{2}(l,t)dt.\end{array}

Combining this identity with the previous estimate (
4.12
), we derive the inequality:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{l}(y_{2}(x,T_{2}))^{2}dx&\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{l}(y_{01}(x))^{2}dx+2\int_{T_{1}}^{T_{2}}b(l,t)(y_{2})_{x}(l,t)y_{2}(l,t)dt\\[14.22636pt]
&\displaystyle\leq\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{4(2M-1)}+2\int_{T_{1}}^{T_{2}}b(l,t)(y_{2})_{x}(l,t)y_{2}(l,t)dt.\end{array}

Therefore, to verify (
4.17
), it is sufficient to prove that the boundary term satisfies:

2\int_{T_{1}}^{T_{2}}b(l,t)(y_{2})_{x}(l,t)y_{2}(l,t)dt\leq\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{4(2M-1)}.

(4.18)

We now verify this bound by exploiting the specific choice of
T_{2}
(see (
4.15
)–(
4.16
)). Using Hölder’s inequality and Corollary
4.2
(with
u(x)=-m_{2}\cdot\chi_{(l,2l)}(x)
, which is a bounded function) for problem (
4.13
), we find

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle b(l,t)(y_{2})_{x}(l,t)&\displaystyle=b(0,t)(y_{2})_{x}(0,t)+\int_{0}^{l}(b(y_{2})_{x})_{x}(x,t)dx\\[14.22636pt]
&\displaystyle=b(0,t)(y_{2})_{x}(0,t)+\int_{0}^{l}(y_{2})_{t}(x,t)dx\\[14.22636pt]
&\displaystyle\leq b(0,t)(y_{2})_{x}(0,t)+l^{\frac{1}{2}}\|(y_{2})_{t}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(0,l)}\\[14.22636pt]
&\displaystyle\leq|b(0,\cdot)|_{C([0,T])}e^{\Big(1+\frac{|b_{x}|_{C(\overline{Q_{T}})}}{\rho}\Big)}\max\limits_{x\in[0,1]}\left\{y_{01}^{\prime}(x)e^{y_{01}(x)}\right\}\\[14.22636pt]
&\quad+\left[2\left(\|(b(\cdot,T_{1})y^{\prime}_{01})^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,1)}+K_{2}\right)+K_{4}K_{3}Te^{K_{1}T}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\overset{\text{def}}{=}C_{2}.\end{array}

We can now combine this estimate with the fact that
0\leq y_{2}\leq\|y_{01}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}
(thanks to the maximum principle), to get

2\int_{T_{1}}^{T_{2}}b(l,t)(y_{2})_{x}(l,t)y_{2}(l,t)dt\leq 2\int_{T_{1}}^{T_{2}}C_{2}\cdot\|y_{01}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}dt=2(T_{2}-T_{1})C_{1}\leq\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{4(2M-1)},

which is exactly (
4.18
).

Finally, we can repeat the argument to select the constants
m_{j}
, the times
T_{j}\in[T_{j-1},T)
, and the solutions
y_{j}
, for
j=1,2,\dots,M-1
, verifying

\int_{0}^{jl}(y_{j}(x,T_{j}))^{2}dx\leq\frac{(2j-1)\epsilon^{2}}{4(2M-1)},

and arrive at

\int_{0}^{1}(y_{M}(x,T_{M}))^{2}dx\leq\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{4},

as desired.

5
Local controllability of system (
1.1
)

In this section, we prove the approximate controllability of the quasi-linear parabolic equation (
1.1
) using a fixed-point argument.
For this, let
R>0
be given and set

\mathcal{D}:=\left\{z\in C^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2},1+\frac{\sigma}{4}}(\overline{Q_{T}}):|z|_{C^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2},1+\frac{\sigma}{4}}(\overline{Q_{T}})}\leq R,\ z(0)=y_{0}\right\}.

Clearly,
\mathcal{D}
is a nonempty, convex, and compact subset of
L^{2}(Q_{T})
for small initial data
y_{0}
.

We consider the mapping
\Lambda
:
\mathcal{D}\rightarrow 2^{\mathcal{D}}
, defined as
follows: for any
z\in\mathcal{D}
,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\Lambda(z)=y,\end{array}

where
y
is the solution to the following linearized equation of (
1.1
):

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}y_{t}-(a(z)y_{x})_{x}=u(x,t)y(x,t)&\text{in}\ Q_{T},\\[5.69054pt]
y(0,t)=y(1,t)=0&\text{in}\ (0,T),\\[5.69054pt]
y(x,0)=y_{0}(x)&\text{in}\ (0,1).\end{array}\right.

(5.1)

By Theorem
4.1
, for any
z\in\mathcal{D}
, there exists a control
u\in L^{\infty}({Q_{T}})
(in fact, by smooth approximation, even

u\in C^{\frac{\sigma}{2},\frac{\sigma}{4}}(\overline{Q_{T}})
) such that the corresponding solution
y
of (
5.1
) satisfies

\|y(T)-y_{d}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\leq\varepsilon.

(5.2)

Now, we are in a position to give a proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.
We analyze the controllability of (
1.1
) in
the frame of classical solutions.
Specifically, we prove that
\Lambda
has a fixed point by Kakutani’s fixed point theorem, which yields the approximate controllability of system (
1.1
).

Step 1.
We show that
\Lambda(\mathcal{D})\subseteq\mathcal{D}
.
By the Schauder theory of second-order linear parabolic equations (see
[
13
, Theorem 7.2.24]
), we have the estimate

|y|_{C^{2+\frac{\sigma}{2},1+\frac{\sigma}{4}}(\overline{Q_{T}})}\leq C|y_{0}|_{C^{2+\frac{\sigma}{2}}([0,1])},

(5.3)

where
C
depends on
|a|_{C([-R,R])}
,
|u|_{C^{\frac{\sigma}{2},\frac{\sigma}{4}}(\overline{Q_{T}})}
and
T
. Moreover, by checking the proof of Proposition
4.1
, it follows that

|u|_{C^{\frac{\sigma}{2},\frac{\sigma}{4}}(\overline{Q_{T}})}\leq C(\varepsilon,\|y_{0}\|_{L^{2}(0,L_{0})}).

(5.4)

Consequently, there exists a sufficiently small constant
\gamma>0
such that
|y|_{C^{2+\frac{\sigma}{2},1+\frac{\sigma}{4}}(\overline{Q_{T}})}\leq R
provided that
|y_{0}|_{C^{2+\frac{\sigma}{2}}([0,1])}\leq\gamma
; that is,
y\in\mathcal{D}
.

Step 2.
We prove that the mapping
\Lambda
is upper semicontinuous.
Assume that
z_{n}\in\mathcal{D}
,
z_{n}\rightarrow z
,
y^{(n)}\in\Lambda(z_{n})
, and
y^{(n)}\rightarrow y
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. We aim to show that
y\in\Lambda(z)
.

For any
z_{n}\in D
, let us consider the following system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}y^{(n)}_{t}-(a(z_{n})y^{(n)}_{x})_{x}=u_{n}(x,t)y^{(n)}(x,t),&(x,t)\in Q_{T},\\[5.69054pt]
y^{(n)}(0,t)=0,\ y^{(n)}({1},t)=0,&t\in(0,T),\\[5.69054pt]
y^{(n)}(x,0)=y_{0}(x),&x\in(0,1).\end{array}\right.

(5.5)

From (
5.3
), we see that
\{y^{(n)}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is uniformly bounded in
{C^{2+\frac{\sigma}{2},1+\frac{\sigma}{4}}(\overline{Q_{T}})}
. Moreover,
\{u_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
satisfies (
5.4
). It follows from Arzelà-Ascoli theorem that there exists a function
u\in C^{\frac{\sigma}{2},\frac{\sigma}{4}}(\overline{Q_{T}})
such that
u_{n}\rightarrow u
in
C(\overline{Q_{T}})
. Furthermore,

\begin{array}[]{ll}z_{n}\rightarrow z\quad\text{in}\quad C^{1,1}(\overline{Q_{T}}),\\[8.53581pt]
y^{(n)}\rightarrow y\quad\text{in}\quad C^{2,1}(\overline{Q_{T}}).\end{array}

Letting
n\rightarrow\infty
in (
5.5
),
we find that the following equation holds:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}y_{t}-(a(z)y_{x})_{x}=u(x,t)y(x,t),&(x,t)\in Q_{T},\\[5.69054pt]
y(0,t)=0,\ y({1},t)=0,&t\in(0,T),\\[5.69054pt]
y(x,0)=y_{0}(x),&x\in(0,1).\end{array}\right.

(5.6)

Since equation (
5.6
) admits at most one classical solution, we conclude that
y\in\Lambda(z)
.

Step 3.
For any
z\in\mathcal{D}
,
\Lambda(z)
is a nonempty, convex and compact subset of
L^{2}(Q_{T})
.

Therefore, by Kakutani’s fixed point theorem, there exists a
y\in\mathcal{D}
such that
y\in\Lambda(y)
. This means that for system (
1.1
) we can find a control
u\in C^{\frac{\sigma}{2},\frac{\sigma}{4}}(\overline{Q_{T}})
with
\operatorname{supp}u(x,t)\subseteq\omega(t)\times[0,T]
such that the corresponding solution
y
satisfies (
5.2
). Moreover, the cost of the control
u
fulfills estimate (
5.4
).

Thus the proof of Theorem
1.2
is complete.
