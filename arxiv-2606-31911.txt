Trade-Offs in Decentralized Gigantic MIMO with Hard-Boundary Constraints
Juan Vidal Alegría, Joao Vieira, Ove Edfors

Abstract
To maintain the antenna apertures offered by 5G massive MIMO systems operating at the sub-6GHz band, known as FR1, 6G base stations (BSs) using the upper-mid band, FR3, should increase the number of antennas by a factor 4-8, giving rise to gigantic MIMO. This poses challenges in terms of processing complexity and interconnection bandwidth. The WAX framework, previously introduced for exploring trade-offs in decentralized architectures, may offer the flexibility needed to tackle these challenges. However, no results have been established on the applicability of this framework in the presence of hard-boundary constraints. The current work explores gigantic MIMO implementations based on a novel adaptation of the WAX framework, where the decentralized processing is performed by non-cooperating hardware modules. These modules may be implemented through state-of-the-art massive MIMO baseband units (BBUs). The results show the potential of the proposed framework towards exploiting trade-offs between complexity and performance in practical gigantic MIMO implementations.

II-A

Preliminaries on the WAX framework

In
[
9
,
10
]
, important theoretical results were derived for the WAX framework. We will summarize the main results upon which we will base our particularization to gigantic
MIMO
systems with hard boundaries.

In
[
9
]
, the achievability of information-lossless processing in the WAX framework was shown to be equivalent to the ability to decompose the channel matrix
\boldsymbol{H}
into the so-called WAX decomposition, given by
[
9
, Theorem 1]

\boldsymbol{H}=\boldsymbol{W}\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{X},

(4)

where the matrices have direct correspondence with the processing applied in (
2
), making it directly applicable to the system model described above. This decomposition could be thus exploited to characterize the information lossless trade-off between
L
and
T
, given in
[
9
, Theorem 1]
as

L>K\frac{M-T}{M}=K\frac{M_{\mathrm{P}}-T_{\mathrm{P}}}{M_{\mathrm{P}}},

(5)

which assumes an unstrucutred
\boldsymbol{A}
, e.g., a realization of an i.i.d. Gaussian matrix, and where we have ignored the regime where
T\geq K
since it trivially leads to information loss.

Taking into account the structure in (
3
), with the extra restriction of having full-rank
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{A}}_{\mathrm{T}}
,
[
10
, Corollary 1]
provides an alternative formulation of the WAX decomposition where
\boldsymbol{X}
is conveniently substituted. In such formulation, information-lossless processing can be attained by finding the decentralized filters
\{\boldsymbol{W}_{m}\}_{m=1}^{M_{\mathrm{P}}}
solving

\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{W}_{1}^{-1}&\dots&\boldsymbol{W}_{M_{\mathrm{P}}}^{-1}\end{bmatrix}\left(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{B}}\bullet\boldsymbol{H}\right)=\boldsymbol{0}_{L\times K(M_{\mathrm{P}}-T_{\mathrm{P}})},

(6)

where
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{B}}\bullet\boldsymbol{H}
is the face-splitting product between
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{B}}
and
\boldsymbol{H}
, whose rows are given by the Kronecker product of the rows of
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{B}}
with the
L\times K
row-blocks of
\boldsymbol{H}=[\boldsymbol{H}_{1}^{\mathrm{T}}\;\dots\;\boldsymbol{H}_{M_{\mathrm{P}}}^{\mathrm{T}}]^{\mathrm{T}}
, and where we have

\widetilde{\boldsymbol{B}}=\begin{bmatrix}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{A}}_{\text{B}}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{A}}_{\text{T}}^{-1}&-\mathbf{I}_{M_{\mathrm{P}}-T_{\mathrm{P}}}\end{bmatrix}^{\mathrm{T}}.

(7)

The matrix
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{B}}
captures the applicability of the respective combining module
\boldsymbol{A}
, as well as the restrictions on the available decentralized schemes to compute the decentralized filters
\{\boldsymbol{W}_{m}\}_{m=1}^{M_{\mathrm{P}}}
.

The alternative formulation of the WAX decomposition from (
6
) was exploited in
[
10
]
to define sparse constructions for
\boldsymbol{A}
based on sum modules. These constructions led to an achievable trade-off, corresponding to a degenerate version of (
5
) when fixing one of the decentralized filters, given by

L\geq K\frac{M_{\mathrm{P}}-T_{\mathrm{P}}}{M_{\mathrm{P}}-1}.

(8)

II-B

Problem formulation

The main goal of this work is to define a suitable combining module
\boldsymbol{A}
, or equivalently
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{B}}
, such that each decentralized module can find the decentralized filters that it should apply without cooperating with other decentralized modules. Specifically, decentralized module
p\in\{1,\dots,P\}
should be able to independently find the set of filters
\{\boldsymbol{W}_{m}\}_{m\in\mathcal{P}_{p}}
that give a solution to (
4
), equivalently to (
6
), where

\mathcal{P}_{p}=\{m\in\mathbb{N}:(p-1)N+1\leq m\leq pN)\}

(9)

corresponds to the set of indices of the decentralized filters applied at decentralized module
p
. We further consider that each decentralized module is in charge of estimating the channel matrix associated to its own antennas. Hence, decentralized module
p\in\{1,\dots,P\}
only has access to the estimates of the channel blocks
\{\boldsymbol{H}_{m}\}_{m\in\mathcal{P}_{p}}

Conclusion

We have studied an adaptation of the WAX framework to facilitate the implementation of gigantic
