Mr. Seward to Mr. Adams.

No. 492.]

Department of State, Washington, March 2, 1863.

Sir: You will present to Mr. William Trater, who presided over a meeting of the inhabitants of Galashiels held at the Corn Exchange on the 5th of February last, the profound acknowledgments of the President of the United States for the deep interest expressed by the workingmen assembled on that occasion in behalf of the cause of the United States in the painful domestic controversy in which they are now unhappily engaged. The President hopes and confidently trusts that the moral opinion of mankind, now happily awakened to the real nature and character of the contest, will relieve it of every of its past embarrassments, and be productive of benign results, favorable to the interests of all nations.

I am, sir, your obedient servant,

WILLIAM H. SEWARD.

Charles Francis Adams, Esq., &c., &c., &c.
