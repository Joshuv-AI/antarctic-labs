Section 1: Executive Summary
The primary objective of this project was to focus on conducting the American Society of
Heating, Refrigerating and Air-Conditioning Engineers (ASHRAE) Level 2 Energy Audits
of all the schools/school facilities in the portfolio, and on developing a comprehensive
Strategic Plan for implementing energy improvements. The overall aim of budget period
1 (BP1) was to implement Energy Audits and the Strategic Plan to provide a clear
pathway to prioritize energy improvements as well as allow the district to access private
sector funding (if needed) and/or tax credits and realize high-impact health and safety
benefits for Budget Period 2.

Unfortunately, due to immediate needs at the school district, Canton City School District
needed to take money that was going to be used as cost share on this award and apply
it to failing apparatus at other schools in the district. This, combined with a pause in
grant actions while the Renew America’s Schools program was under a programmatic
review by the new administration, led Canton to withdraw from the grant.

Section 2: Project Overview
Canton City School District (CCSD), in Canton, Ohio, has a storied history and a diverse
array of facilities that has catered to the changing educational needs of its students.
Over the years, CCSD has grown to become the 24th largest school district in Ohio,
which was attributable to its location in a U.S. manufacturing center. However, as the
manufacturing industry declined, so did CCSD’s student enrollment and financial
resources. Student enrollment has decreased by more than 66% since its peak of more
than 22,000 students in 1974. Today, the district serves approximately 8,000 students.
Decreasing enrollment has further compounded the district’s financial challenges,
prompting a strategic response to optimize operations and attract students and families.
Of the students they serve, most are from low-income families and face related
academic challenges. CCSD continuously faces financial deficit, leading to deferred
facility maintenance and a reevaluation of class sizes, academic programming,
headcount, and other resource allocations. CCSD is unable to invest in preventative

maintenance for their aging infrastructure. Today, CCSD operates 22 schools that
include an early childhood center, 12 elementary schools, three middle schools
(including a STEM focused school for grades 4-8), two high schools (including early
college advanced learning opportunities), and four alternative schools. CCSD is the
City’s largest employer with more than 1,400 FTEs. Investing in the district is essential
for the area.

Strategic Response: In 2022, Superintendent Jeff Talbert spearheaded a strategic
plan called “Design for Excellence” to address CCSD’s economic difficulties. The plan,
still in place today, aims to save the CCSD $7 million a year by streamlining staff and
operations. The plan calls for CCSD to establish eight K-6 elementary schools. It
suggests closing several facilities that are beyond repair and no longer needed due to
decreased student enrollment and constructing newer schools in areas with the most
need to ensure all CCSD students receive the best education possible. Under the plan,
CCSD would go from 22 schools to 16.

Critical portfolio: Although CCSD has a strategy for school buildings in dire need of
replacement and/or closure, there are multiple school buildings that are critical to the
Canton community and must be restored. The Eco-Schools Canton project aims to
restore these 15 school buildings by implementing energy-saving measures to create
safe and healthy environments for students, staff, and community members. Selected
facilities are in significant need of upgrades and retrofits to ensure students, teachers,
staff, and community members can learn in a safe, healthy, and comfortable
environment. Grant funding will allow CCSD to complete a comprehensive audit to
ensure that building upgrades will have the highest impact.

The fifteen schools chosen for this project: To revitalize and restore the
infrastructure of 15 CCSD schools by implementing energy-saving measures and
creating safe and healthy environments for students, staff, and community members.
The project portfolio consists of buildings with the greatest facility, financial, and
community need.

Needs Assessment:

HVAC: CCSD’s buildings are plagued with HVAC issues that compromise the comfort
and safety of our students and staff. For example, Timken High School's antiquated
steam heating system is a financial burden due to its age and the constant need for
repairs, failing to maintain consistent temperatures. Hartford Middle School's unit
ventilators and actuators, essential for proper air circulation and temperature control,
are in dire need of repair. Lehman Middle School's air handling units have broken

 Page 4 of 6

screens, and the variable frequency drives screen is non-functional, affecting the overall
HVAC performance.

Historic Status: McKinley High School, a building of historical significance, faces
unique challenges due to its historic status. The school requires sensitive upgrades to
preserve its heritage while enhancing comfort levels. The boiler controls are outdated,
the pneumatic controls are labor-intensive, and the Natatorium's lack of a pool cover
and humidity control in the heating and ventilation system leads to discomfort and
accelerated deterioration.

Roofing and Ceiling Issues: Several schools, including Worley Elementary and
Lehman Middle School, urgently require roof replacements and repairs. The peeling
paint on Worley Elementary's gym ceiling is not only unsightly but also poses a
significant safety risk to our students.

Window and Mechanical Piping Concerns: Harter Elementary's inefficient window
control system necessitates keeping windows open during winter to regulate
temperature, leading to discomfort and energy waste. Cedar Elementary's leaking
mechanical piping above the cafeteria demands immediate attention to prevent further
damage.

Ductwork and Boiler Controls: Hartford Middle School's rooftop unit ductwork suffers
from exterior condensation, which can be mitigated with proper sealing and insulation.
Clarendon Middle School's undersized ductwork creates noise distractions, and Belden
Elementary's non-functional boiler control system requires manual operation, which is
not sustainable.

Lighting System: The district's reliance on linear fluorescent lights and the partial
integration of the lighting control system underscores the need for modernization to
improve energy efficiency and reduce operational costs.

Thermostats While all the district school buildings have programmable thermostats, a
few buildings have pneumatic controls. Allen Patrick Elementary’s control system
requires replacement. The buildings controls system can shut off the chiller. The
existing variable air volume boxes can operate in heating mode when not required. The
temperature controls system required replacement through the buildings. These
controls provide a challenging learning environment for students and staff. The existing
boiler controls system only allows one boiler to operate at a time. The existing boiler
system combustion air is not designed properly to provide enough air. The existing
system does not allow all three boilers to operate. The existing boilers get too hot and
are a safety hazard. Cedar Elementary’s chiller controls system requires replacement.
The existing chiller system will shut off and requires manual operation to turn back on.
This issue occurs several times a month.

Section 3: Conclusion
Canton City School District (CCSD) has been on the front line of America’s offshoring of
manufacturing. As such Canton’s population has declined, reducing the tax base and
therefore the funding available for the school district. The school district has a
desperate need to upgrade its facilities and no budget available to do so. The Renew
America’s Schools program offered a much-needed lifeline to help Canton’s schools
modernize and serve its student population, 100% of whom qualify for free or reduced
lunch due to low family income. While the Renew America’s Schools program was the
subject of a programmatic review at the Department of Energy, Canton’s schools were
continuing to deteriorate. With uncertainty as to the status of the Renew America’s
Program, CCSD was forced to address immediate infrastructure needs using their cost
share funds. As such Canton used necessary cost share funding and forfeited their
opportunity to participate in the Renew America’s School’s grant program.
