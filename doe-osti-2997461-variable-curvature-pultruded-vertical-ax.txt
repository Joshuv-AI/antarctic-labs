 ABSTRACT

XFlow Energy is developing a floating offshore vertical-axis wind turbine (VAWT) enabling a 70%
reduction in the levelized cost of energy compared to floating horizontal-axis wind turbines (HAWTs).
While the wind industry has historically been successful at lowering the cost of terrestrial wind systems,
there are no scalable, economically viable floating wind solutions on the market with the levelized cost of
energy (LCOE) of current floating offshore solutions is $200/MWh (Musial 2022). Low-cost blade
production is one of the key technologies for realizing this target LCOE reduction. The proposed design
uses a constant cross-section blade, enabling the use of pultrusion, a continuous, automated, mold-free
production method. By employing blades with variable curvature, XFlow can minimize the bending stresses
in the blades, allowing for light-weighting. Constant curvature pultrusion has been demonstrated on hollow
sections. Variable curvature pultrusion has been demonstrated on solid sections. This project aims to
develop a method of variable curvature pultrusion for hollow sections, as required for mass-optimal VAWT
blades.

 1. VARIABLE CURVATURE PULTRUDED VERTICAL AXIS WIND BLADES

This phase 1 technical collaboration project (MDF-TC-2024-279) began on December 12, 2023 and
concluded on August 12, 2025.

The Phase I technical review revealed that the cross section of the target XFlow Energy blade design is
substantially larger than any known pultrusion design currently manufactured. Phase 1 economic
modeling determined that with current pultrusion technology, a 15 MW straight pultruded VAWT blade
would cost approximately 76% more vs a comparable 15 MW horizontal axis wind turbine blade. This is
in part due to the increased length needed for a VAWT blade compared to a HAWT blade with the same
resulting rotor swept area. The lowest cost recommended approach to produce a variable curvature
pultruded blade is to produce straight pultruded blades that could be deformed into desired shape during
installation (allowing for built in stress). While it may be possible that costs are being overestimated by
industry partners since this profile is outside of current capabilities, this project indicated that jumping to
an 15 MW blade design is not feasible at this time. It is recommended that XFlow explore opportunities
for a ~ 2.5 MW VAWT, since the pultrusion technology needed for this scale of turbine is commercially
available and cost competitive.

1.1 BACKGROUND

XFlow Energy develops technologies to reduce the cost of renewable energy. They design and
manufacture their own wind turbines and supply other OEMs with products and services to reduce the
cost of their products. XFlow is a small business.

Working with DOE MDF and CFTF at ORNL, this project will explore methods for producing single-piece,
variable curvature blades using pultrusion, an automated, low-cost-per-foot composites manufacturing
process. Despite having a longer blade length than a HAWT for a given swept area, success from this
project will ensure a reduced rotor cost by leveraging this advanced automated composites technique.

Because this process does not require large molds as with HAWT blades, blade production equipment can
be portside, and transportable between ports.

1.2 TECHNICAL RESULTS

The main goal in the initial phase of this proposed work was focused on 3 deliverables: 1) A thorough
background and literature search of both pultrusion manufacturing categories (CNC controlled adjustable
pultrusion dies and post variable curvature shaping) to fully understand the opportunities and challenges
and inform the research team to make appropriate down selection(s) of preferred approaches. 2) A high-
level baseline technoeconomic analysis to determine performance and cost targets of variable pultruded
VAWT wind blades that would be required to deliver a floating offshore wind product that could be
competitive with floating HAWT turbines. This analysis will also include consideration of both carbon
fiber and glass fiber based pultrusions. 3) A roadmap of the proposed down selected method(s) of
pultruding variable curvature wind blades and detailed manufacturing test plan.

Task 1.1: Detailed Literature Survey
Objective: Deep dive literature search and pultrusion supplier interviews on approaches to variable
curvature pultrusion and report out.
 ● ORNL will conduct a detailed literature survey and report out on variable curvature pultrusion
 manufacturing options.
Results: The team completed the background and literature search and published the following journal
publication: Segun Isaac Talabi, Jim Tobin, Benjamin Strom, Ian Brownstein, Vlastimil Kunc, Ahmed
Arabi Hassen, “Recent and future developments in pultrusion technology with consideration for curved
geometries: A review: (2024). Composites Part B: Engineering, Volume 283, 111678, ISSN 1359-8368,
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compositesb.2024.111678.
The key takeaways from this exercise relevant to the XFlow objectives for their desired variable curvature
pultruded blade were:
 ● The cross section of the target XFlow Energy blade design is substantially larger than any known
 pultrusion design currently manufactured in the world. Just making a straight pultruded blade
 contains significant technical and technoeconomic risk because of the lack of existing pultrusion
 equipment large enough for this part and unknown die making capability within the US or
 beyond.
 ● No practical approaches to variable pultrusion at the massive scale needed for the XFlow Energy
 design were discovered through the literature search. If the design were constant curvature there
 are some approaches that could be considered, but the practicality of making a straight profile,
 non-curved die at this scale is of substantial challenge itself that a constant curvature die adds
 significant risk at this scale. Post forming methods to shape the blade were deemed a more
 desirable approach after a thorough review of all options discovered in the literature review.

Task 1.2: Conceptual Wind Blade Designs

 5

Objective: Develop conceptual wind blade designs (1 carbon fiber based design and 1 glass fiber based
design) of 15 MW VAWT variable curvature pultruded blades. Blade designs will be used for cost
studies.

Results: XFlow developed conceptual glass blade designs (carbon was eliminated due to cost
considerations).

Task 1.3: Wind Blade Cost Estimations
 Objective: Develop Cost estimations of carbon and glass blade versions made with various preferred
approaches from literature search.

Results: Because the scale of XFlow Energy’s conceptual designs were much larger than what has ever
been pultruded in the industry, ORNL recruited additional industry experts from Crown Tooling, Kent
Automation and Creative Pultrusions to collaborate on the technoeconomic analysis, which concluded
that the desired pultrusions could not be manufactured at a price point near XFlow’s cost targets for a 15
MW turbine. However, the analysis suggests pultrusion could be economically viable for pultruded blade
designs for ~ 2.5 MW turbines, which is the next turbine size of interest for XFlow Energy’s business
future growth.

Task 1.4: Wind Blade Cost Target Analysis
Objective: Develop a high level technoeconomic analysis of required cost target for VAWT wind blades
to enable floating VAWT turbines to compete with floating HAWT turbines.
Results: XFlow’s pultruded blade cost targets for their business model are to provide US domestically
produced blades that are equivalent or less expensive in cost compared to conventional HAWT wind
blades that are produced by foreign sources. If the XFlow blades are produced domestically at an
equivalent HAWT foreign blade cost, additional cost savings of the rest of a VAWT turbine compared to
a HAWT turbine will give XFlow a cost competitive advantage while allowing for full US domestic
production.

The team developed a high level baseline technoeconomic analysis to determine performance and cost
targets of variable VAWT wind blades that would be required to deliver a floating offshore wind product
that could be competitive with floating HAWT turbines. Because the target design was so far beyond the
size on any known conventional pultrusions, the team reached out to pultrusion experts from Crown
Tooling (US based pultrusion die manufacturer), Kent Automation (pultrusion line equipment
manufacturer) and Creative Pultrusions (manufacturer of very large pultrusions) for assistance in
technoeconomic analysis. Working with these experts it was estimated that blade die would cost ~$1M,
the pultrusion line equipment would cost ~$15M for (one production line), and the pultruded blade would
cost ~$3k-4k/ft. The blade cost per unit length was determined using a rough design from XFlow which
could be optimized to become more cost competitive, but such optimization was outside the scope of this
project. Based on this design and extrapolations from analysis done by NREL (Stehly, T and Duffy, P.
“2021 Cost of Wind Energy Review.” National Renewable Energy Laboratory, 2021), the rotor cost
estimate for XFlow’s preliminary 15 MW design is 76% greater than an equivalent HAWT rotor. The
main increased cost drivers compared to HAWT blades are the significantly longer size of the blades
required and the relatively slow line speeds (approximately 6” per minute) of the pultrusion line
manufacturing process at this scale. The high automation of the pultrusion process does not overcome the
costs associated with the much longer blade and the slow line speed with XFlow’s current preliminary
blade design.

 6

Task 1.5: Manufacturing Approach Down Selection
Objective: Complete a down selection of preferred variable pultrusion approach(es) for testing and
evaluation in Phase 2 based on lessons learned in Tasks 1.1-1.4 above.

Results: The downselection for the preferred manufacturing approach is to do straight standard pultrusion
processing, with the exception of using DCPD resin to maximize pultrusion speed/reduce cost and deform
the blade to a variable curvature at installation. The blade design would be tailored to allow the straight
blade to be bent into the desired Troposkein shape via installation deformation and the designed built in
stress from this deformation would be accounted for in the fatigue life of the blade design. The primary
activity of the project would be to develop a cost effective pultrusion process using Perseus’ Materials
DCPD resin design, the pultrusion die currently used for XFlow Energy’s current 25kW pultruded blades
and to show the ability to deform the blade to the desired shape during an installation process.

1.3 IMPACTS

XFlow Energy has learned of the extreme cost challenges of moving from their current 25 kW pultruded
VAWT straight wind blade design scaling to a 15 MW variable curvature pultruded wind blade design.
However they have also learned that pultrusion technology and supply chain capability exists such that
developing a 2.5 MW pultruded VAWT wind blade design may be economically attractive for their future
business. If XFlow Energy does pursue a 2.5 MW turbine design and production, that manufacturing cell
could be leveraged to explore new technology that could be used to overcome the significant cost
challenges at 15 MW.

1.4 SUBJECT INVENTIONS

None.

1.5 CONCLUSIONS

The metric for success was not met for developing a 15 MW VAWT blade at this time. The extreme size
of a 15 MW pultruded VAWT blade (both in cross sectional size and length) is beyond the current
capabilities of the industry. The supply chain related challenges to produce pultrusion dies and pultrusion
line equipment of this size and the slow expected maximum line speed (6” per minute) would make it
difficult to efficiently use CAPEX associated with the die, pultrusion line, and large facilities needed.

XFlow Energy has learned of the extreme cost challenges of moving from their current 25 kW pultruded
VAWT straight wind blade design scaling to a15 MW variable curvature pultruded wind blade design.
However they have also learned that pultrusion technology and supply chain capability exists such that

could be leveraged to explore new technology that could be used to overcome the significant cost

 2. PARTNER BACKGROUND

XFlow Energy develops technologies to reduce the cost of renewable energy. They design and
manufacture our own wind turbines and supply other OEMs with products and services to reduce the cost
of their products.

XFlow’s flagship product is a 25 kW vertical-axis wind turbine (VAWT) which is being designed to
reduce the cost of wind energy generation. XFlow Energy’s unique rotor design eliminates the structural
failure modes common to VAWTs while simultaneously maintaining high efficiency and low
manufacturing costs.

XFlow also produces and licenses technologies to OEMs developing renewable energy products.
Additionally, XFlow offers engineering consulting, prototyping, wind tunnel testing, and field testing
services for renewable energy systems.
