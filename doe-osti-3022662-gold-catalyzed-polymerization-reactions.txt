1. Accomplishments
1.1 Project Goals
 The overarching goal of this project lied in the translation of gold (Au) catalyzed small
molecule synthetic transformations to the field of polymer science, the generation of new
macromolecular structures, and the application of the materials and methodologies produced
toward sustainable polymer development and chemical upcycling. Specifically, this project aimed
to polymerize monomers based on unsaturated precursors and access novel polymeric materials
through the extension of Au catalyzed intermolecular processes. The specific objectives of this
project comprised: (1) the extension of Au-catalyzed intermolecular reactions to bifunctional
monomers and the polycondensation of diverse nucleophiles; (2) to gain fundamental mechanistic
insight into Au-catalyzed polymerizations; (3) the application of these reactions to the synthesis
of new classes of aromatic polymers that can be readily and/or selectively deconstructed; and (4)
to apply these chemistries to post-polymerization modification (PPM) of commercially relevant
polyaromatic substrates, including post-industrial plastics and post-consumer waste.
 Specific tasks, activities, and completed milestones are shown in Table 1 along with the
originally stated goals of the entire project in Table 2. The project was abruptly terminated upon
PI Azoulay initiating his move from The University of Southern Mississippi to the Georgia
Institute of Technology in mid-2022 (~2 years into the project).

 In Aim 1 we proposed translating concepts from prototypical gold-catalyzed organic
reaction methodologies to polymer synthesis and understanding considerations relevant for the
efficient synthesis of high molecular weight materials. In Aim 1 we proposed developing
polymerization methodologies derived from prototypical Au catalyzed reaction protocols,
beginning with the Au catalyzed hydroarylation reaction (Task 1a). Next, we proposed the
investigation of substrate scope to afford pathways toward diverse macromolecular structures and
functional polymers which are otherwise unachievable (Task 1b). This task began in Year 1 and
spanned Year 2 (Table 1 and 2). In Aim 2 we proposed extending the polymerization reactions
that were developed to other prototypical Au catalyzed reactions containing heteronucleophiles
(Task 2a) to increase molecular complexity and enable routes toward polymers that are
isostructural with high-performance polymers that are currently non-recyclable. Optimized
reactions were proposed to be subject to comprehensive mechanistic and kinetic studies to better
understand catalytic features that influence these novel polymerization reactions and provide
routes for further advancements (Task 2b). Aim 3 aimed to employ the methods developed in
Aims 1–2 toward the synthesis of high-performance polymer materials (Task 3a) amenable to
diverse degradation mechanisms, to be screened, optimized, and the products comprehensively
characterized (Task 3b). This aimed to provide a rich multi-parameter chemical space for
application of machine learning algorithms that inform the design of subsequent generations of

degradable polymers (Task 3c). The intermolecular reactivity developed in Aims 1–2 will provide
an ideal platform for post-polymerization modification (PPM), and subsequent upcycling of
aromatic polymers. We will evaluate the Au-catalyzed intermolecular hydroarylation reaction
optimized in Aim 1 toward the PPM of commodity, specialty, and waste aromatic polymers (Task
4a). We will extend this PPM reactivity toward the heteronucleophile derived reactions developed
in Aim 2 while additionally developing sequential PPM protocols via coupling this chemistry to
prototypical PPM methods such as thiol-X and hydrothiolation chemistries (Task 4b).

 Our work has demonstrated new, remarkably efficient polymerization reactions, high
reactivities, new polymer structures with original macromolecular skeletons, a new means to
access advanced materials, and state-of-the-art PPM reactions. Compared to the more established
Pd, Cu, Ru, and Rh counterparts, the mild conditions under which Au can activate C–H bonds,
high reactivities, as well as the regioselectivity observed in the absence of directing groups
provides significant additional opportunities. In a broad context, this new reactivity from Au opens
access to a range of new polymer chemistries and offers promise to address core issues associated
with step-growth approaches such as multi-step monomer synthesis, intolerance to functionality,
 equilibrium effects associated with by-product formation,
 optimization of complex polymerization processes for
 individual monomers, and copolymerization reactions with
 disparate monomer reactivity and which result in
 anisotropic material properties. These initial results formed
 the basis for two manuscripts highlighted as cover features
 (see inset), additional manuscripts under preparation, two
 patents, > 10 student presentations, collaborative efforts
initiated with multiple research groups, and partnerships with industry leaders in aromatic and high
performance polymers.
 These studies establish the beginning of a knowledge base for catalyst reactivity parameters,
confirm the feasibility of our experimental design, demonstrate our competence in understanding
and optimizing the reactivity of these species, and indicate that ancillary ligand design plays a
highly important role. This motivates the next steps for making important catalyst discoveries for
the field of gold catalysis and polymer science.

1.3 Opportunities for Training and Professional Development

 Most research related to catalysis is focused on optimizing individual (small molecule)
reactions and the organometallic species that facilitate these reactions. Reactions to facilitate the
construction of contemporary macromolecular chemistries are often not addressed, nor at the
forefront of most catalyst research development. Our team combines expertise in organometallic
chemistry, polymer synthesis, synthetic methodology development, and polymer characterization
to address core issues toward translating new catalytic reactivity to macromolecular
transformations. The collaborative nature of the research and emphasis on the development of
advanced macromolecular structures that possess advanced properties, and which are sustainable
trains the next generation of scientists to solve significant challenges in polymer science.
 The project has provided support for 3 PhD students at The University of Southern Mississippi.
These students were trained in organometallic chemistry, polymer synthesis, methodology
development, and polymer characterization. Research conducted in this project requires close
collaboration with a post-doctoral researcher with significant expertise in organometallic
chemistry. One of these students received their PhD and transitioned to medicinal chemistry
focusing on photoredox catalysis at AstraZeneca. One of these students gave three oral
presentations at the 2020 Applied Polymer Technology Extension Consortium (APTEC) Research
Symposium, 2021 Susan A. Siltanen Graduate Research Symposium, and the ACS Spring 2021
National Conference; where in the first (APTEC) he was awarded 1st place for his oral presentation.
In addition, one of these students has given two oral presentations at ACS Spring 2022 National
Conference and MRS Spring 2022 National Conference. Additionally, one of these students was
recently awarded the USM Vice President for Research Innovation Award for the research and
published works supported from this project.
 All students routinely presented their work within our team meetings and receive feedback on
their work to improve communication and public speaking skills. Scientists are working closely
within and across teams to further refine polymerizations, PPM reactions, physical properties, and
application. Students are working with their faculty members on the preparation of patent
applications and manuscripts in relation to the work already completed.

4. Impact
4.1 Impact on the Development of the Principal Disciplines of the Project

 The discovery and development of a copolymerization method for diverse arene and alkyne
substrates through gold (Au)-catalysis represents a first of its kind reaction toward the synthesis
of highly functionalized aromatic polymers. This work has effectively established the utility of
homogenous gold complexes as a highly important catalytic species to effect next generation
polymeric materials and advanced macromolecular structures. This work additionally has
demonstrated unprecedented reactivity in Au-mediated intermolecular hydroarylations necessary
to effect these polymerizations, which has not been previously demonstrated in the literature.
These guidelines will inform the synthetic community toward further designing reactions with
heightened efficiency. Moreover, this work has stimulated the interest of the field toward
utilization of Au-catalysis in polymer synthesis, with our work being described as “a golden
opportunity to expand the scope of late-transition-metal-mediated polycondensations” by leaders
in the field (Synfacts 2021, 17, 0028). Additionally, the developed methodology generates products
structurally analogous to contemporary engineering thermoplastics (poly-p-phenylene, PEEK,
PSU, etc.); however, the polymerization protocol proceeds without heat, in the absence of
additives, and generates no corrosive/toxic byproducts, as is the case in the synthesis of these high-
performance materials. Accordingly, these results are of particular significance as they provide a
more environmentally benign methodology toward accessing these materials, which has
stimulated significant industrial interest. The functional group tolerance of the reaction allows for
the synthesis of high-performance polymers comprising diverse functionalities inaccessible
through contemporary approaches.
 The translation of these discoveries toward the post-polymerization modification (PPM) of
aromatic polymers represent the first instance of Au-catalysis toward PPM of polymer systems to
date. Accordingly, these results have established the subset of this field (polymer
functionalization) and have fundamentally contributed to the base knowledge and theory
associated with it. This work provides a basis for the one-step modification of commodity,
engineering, and waste aromatic polymers without the introduction of deleterious side reactions,
which detract from the advantageous properties of the parent material, under more facile
conditions than state-of-the-art approaches. This work has profound impacts toward the upcycling
of plastic waste, providing unexplored avenues to create value-added materials from waste
aromatic polymers, essentially granting a second life to discarded plastic waste (PS foam cups,
PET soda/water bottles, etc.). Accordingly, this work is expected to garner much attention from
the field and the plastic industry upon disclosure and will form the basis of further demonstrations
of this chemistry both from our group and others.

4.2 Impact on Technology Transfer
 The PPM work is anticipated to have a significant and immediate impact on commercial
technology toward the upcycling of contemporary aromatic polymers and utilization of plastic
waste. A patent has been submitted and we have developed an established connection with Solvay
(Atlanta, GA) where we have discussed potential commercialization routes of this chemistry and
discussed the possibility of a future start-up company utilizing this work.

4.3 Impact of Society Beyond Science and Technology
 Aromatic polymers–those that contain planar, rigid aromatic, or pseudo-aromatic
heterocycles–display robust chemical, mechanical, and thermal properties. This enables their
broad utility in commodity, specialty, and high-performance applications such as packaging,
textiles, transportation, electronics, construction, healthcare, energy, and many others. Synthetic

aromatic polymers are ubiquitous and indispensable to modern life, industry, and the global
economy. Completely new classes of materials will find utility in myriad applications. Exploiting
prototypical redox neutral mechanisms of Au, will motivate the chemistry, polymer, and materials
science communities to explore the novel reactivity of Au toward the development of new polymer
chemistries, upcycling, and recycling strategies.
 Plastic waste generation and utilization stands as a topic of paramount importance in society
today. With discouragingly low recycling rates of plastic in the United States (less than 10% of
plastics generated) developing new methods toward recycling and plastic waste utilization is of
dire importance toward sustainability efforts and in preventing an environmental catastrophe as a
large portion of plastic waste proliferates into the environment. Accordingly, the post-
polymerization modification work presented in the reporting period provides pathways toward the
utilization of plastic waste for the generation of value-added materials, providing a second
lifecycle of these plastics and effectively preventing their accumulation and proliferation into the
environment. Specifically, this work reports the modification of polystyrene (e.g., foam cups) and
polyethylene terephthalate (e.g., soda/water bottles), which stand among two of the most widely
consumed, and subsequently discarded, plastic products; this work provides avenues toward the
reutilization of these pivotal materials. The implications for the expansion of this work toward
more functional products and a myriad of aromatic plastic waste has the potential to exhibit a
profound impact on society and the environment.
