Skewness tunes the small-drift record rate
of random walks and Lévy flights

Abstract




1
Introduction

Record statistics organize how the running maximum of a stochastic process advances, and they appear across hydrology, finance, evolutionary biology, and the physics of disordered and extreme systems
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
. For symmetric random walk models a striking simplification holds: the statistics of the number and ages of records are
universal
, independent of the jump distribution, by virtue of the Sparre Andersen theorem
[
5
,
6
]
. This universality survives measurement error and noise, which rescale the pace of record setting but not its
\sqrt{n}
growth
[
7
]
, and extends to record ages under stochastic resetting
[
8
]
, but is a driftless phenomenon. Adding a drift makes the record rate depend on the step distribution, as for record temperatures under a warming trend.

Here we determine this small-drift dependence, for light- and heavy-tailed, symmetric and skewed steps alike. Let
S_{n}=\xi_{1}+\dots+\xi_{n}
be a random walk with i.i.d. steps of mean
{\mu=\mathbb{E}(\xi)>0}
, and let the record rate be the long-run fraction of times the walk reaches a new maximum (Fig.
1
),

\lambda(\mu)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\#\{n\leqslant N\colon S_{n}>\max(S_{0},\dots,S_{n-1})\}.

(1)

As
\mu\to 0
, its vanishing is governed by the walk’s fluctuations.

The physical origin of this vanishing is a competition of scales. When the centered steps lie in the domain of attraction of a stable law of index
1<\alpha\leqslant 2
and scale
c>0
, the deterministic drift contributes
\mu n
while the fluctuations grow only as
c\mkern 1.0mun^{1/\alpha}
, and the two balance at a crossover

n^{*}\asymp(c/\mu)^{1/\nu},\quad\nu=1-1/\alpha,

(2)

that recedes to infinity as
\mu\to 0
. Below
n^{*}
the walk is effectively driftless and stays above its start with the Sparre Andersen–Doney persistence probability
\sim n^{-(1-\rho)}
, the positivity parameter
\rho=\mathbb{P}(Y>0)
of the limiting stable law
Y
carrying the asymmetry of the steps
[
5
,
9
,
2
]
; above
n^{*}
the drift wins and records accrue at a fixed rate
[
1
,
10
]
. The record rate is the survival probability evaluated at the crossover,
\lambda(\mu)\asymp(n^{*})^{-(1-\rho)}\asymp\mu^{(1-\rho)/\nu}
, so the small-drift limit reads off the persistence of the underlying fluctuations and, through
\rho
, their skewness.

This heuristic fixes the exponent, but leaves the prefactor, the corrections, and their common analytic origin open. We identify that origin as a single Mellin transform. It yields the leading law

\lambda(\mu)\sim K\mu^{(1-\rho)/\nu},\quad\mu\to 0,

(3)

with the exponent
tuned continuously by the skewness
through
\rho
alone, sweeping the interval
{[1,\mkern 2.0mu1/(\alpha-1)]}
. The prefactor
K
is explicit for strictly stable steps, and the transform generates a correction ladder. In particular, the formula recovers the Gaussian linear law with slope
\sqrt{2}
and, for symmetric stable steps, the power
\mu^{\alpha/2(\alpha-1)}
. The same transform also gives the small-drift structure of the expected maximum, with the classical heavy-traffic constants as adjacent poles.

2
Spitzer–Baxter to Mellin poles

The record times of
S
are its strict ascending ladder epochs. The Spitzer–Baxter identity
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
expresses the record rate in terms of one-sided probabilities of the walk as

\lambda(\mu)=\exp\biggl[-\!\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\mkern 2.0mu\mathbb{P}(S_{n}\leqslant 0)\biggr]=\exp[-g(\mu)],

(4)

valid for any i.i.d. steps with
\mu>0
. Under stable scaling the centered partial sum
T_{n}=S_{n}-\mu n
obeys
T_{n}/c\mkern 1.0mun^{1/\alpha}\Rightarrow Y
, with
Y
standard stable and
c>0
the scale, so
\mathbb{P}(S_{n}\leq 0)
depends on
n
only through the single variable
\mu n^{\nu}
,

\mathbb{P}(S_{n}\leqslant 0)\longrightarrow h\Bigl(\frac{\mu n^{\nu}}{c}\Bigr),\quad h(x)=\mathbb{P}(Y\leqslant-x).

(5)

Here, the exact step in the argument is (
4
); the stable kernel in (
5
) is exact for Gaussian and strictly stable steps and governs the large-
n
terms that set the small-drift limit for general domains of attraction. Either way, the sum in (
4
) takes the harmonic-sum form

g(\mu)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\mkern 2.0muh\Bigl(\frac{\mu n^{\nu}}{c}\Bigr).

(6)

We see that the drift enters only through the single scaling variable
\mu n^{\nu}
, which sets the small-drift exponent.

Harmonic sums like (
6
) factorize under the Mellin transform
[
14

. With the base transform
h^{*}(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{s-1}h(u)\mkern 2.0mudu

u=\mu n^{\nu}/c
gives

G(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu^{s-1}g(\mu)\mkern 2.0mud\mu=c^{s}\mkern 2.0muh^{*}(s)\zeta(1+\nu s),

(7)

a base transform of the steps times a Riemann
\zeta
factor carrying the
1/n

weights. It is analytic in the fundamental strip
0<\Real{s}<\alpha

g
is recovered by inversion,

g(\mu)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c_{0}-i\infty}^{c_{0}+i\infty}G(s)\mu^{-s}\mkern 2.0muds,\quad 0<c_{0}<\alpha,

(8)

the small-
\mu
expansion following by displacing the contour to the left across the poles of
G
(Fig.
2

\alpha=2
) steps,

h^{*}(s)=\frac{2^{s/2-1}}{s\sqrt{\pi}}\mkern 2.0mu\Gamma\Bigl(\frac{s+1}{2}\Bigr),

(9)

from

in unit-variance normalization (the convention (
18

\mathcal{N}(0,2)
at
\alpha=2

c

3
The skew-tuned exponent

The leading law comes from the rightmost pole. Both factors of
G
are singular at
s=0
,

\displaystyle\zeta(1+\nu s)=\frac{1}{\nu s}+\gamma+O(s),

(10)

\displaystyle h^{*}(s)=\frac{1-\rho}{s}+h^{*}_{0}+O(s),

(11)

the second because
h(0)=\mathbb{P}(Y\leqslant 0)=1-\rho
. Their product is a double pole, whose residue against
\mu^{-s}
gives
g(\mu)=\frac{1-\rho}{\nu}\log(\frac{1}{\mu})+P+o(1)
and hence

\displaystyle\lambda(\mu)\sim K\mu^{(1-\rho)/\nu},\quad K=e^{-P},

(12)

\displaystyle P=(1-\rho)\gamma+\frac{h^{*}_{0}}{\nu}+\frac{1-\rho}{\nu}\log c.

(13)

This is the announced law (
3
). The skewness enters the exponent only through the positivity parameter, given for
{1<\alpha<2}
by the Zolotarev value
[
9
,
15
]

\rho=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\pi\alpha}\arctan(\beta\tan(\tfrac{1}{2}\pi\alpha))\in\Bigl[1-\frac{1}{\alpha},\mkern 2.0mu\frac{1}{\alpha}\Bigr],

(14)

so that the leading exponent runs over the whole interval

\frac{1-\rho}{\nu}\in\Bigl[1,\mkern 2.0mu\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\Bigr]

(15)

as the skewness
\beta
runs from
-1
to
+1
. At
\beta=-1
the heavier tail is on the descending side,
\rho=1/\alpha
, and the exponent is
1
. A walk with a heavy descending tail still has a record rate that vanishes linearly, because the ascending ladder structure that carries records is set by the lighter tail. At
\beta=+1
the heavier tail is on the ascending side,
\rho=1-1/\alpha
, and the exponent reaches its maximum
1/(\alpha-1)
. The effect runs counter to intuition: a heavier up-tail may favor larger record jumps, but at zero mean the heavy right tail is offset by a left-shifted bulk, so the median lies below the mean and
\rho=\mathbb{P}(Y>0)
falls below
1/2
. The persistence that controls record times then decays faster, so the small-drift record rate vanishes more rapidly. The leading exponent therefore varies continuously with skewness through
\rho
, forming a phase portrait (Fig.
3
). Invisible at
\mu=0
, skewness sets the exponent itself once a drift is present.

Two symmetric anchors bound the family. For finite-variance steps
Y
is Gaussian,
\rho=\nu=1/2
, and the exponent is again
1
, with the Gaussian slope

\lambda(\mu)\sim\sqrt{2}\mkern 1.0mu\mu/\sigma,

(16)

the reciprocal of the driftless ladder height
\mathbb{E}_{0}(H_{+})=\sigma/\sqrt{2}

[
16
,
17
]
, and recovering the coefficient used numerically in
[
1
]
. More generally, if
\tau_{+}
is the first strict ascending ladder epoch and
H_{+}=S_{\tau_{+}}
, then
\lambda(\mu)=1/\mathbb{E}(\tau_{+})=\mu/\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(H_{+})
. Thus finite-variance walks are linear whenever
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(H_{+})\to\mathbb{E}_{0}(H_{+})<\infty
; Gaussianity fixes the special value
\mathbb{E}_{0}(H_{+})=\sigma/\sqrt{2}
, while non-Gaussian details change only the slope. For symmetric heavy tails (
\rho=1/2
, any
\alpha
) the prefactor is unity in the natural scale and

\lambda(\mu)\sim\mu^{\alpha/2(\alpha-1)},\quad 1<\alpha<2,

(17)

an exponent exceeding
1
and falling to the diffusive value as
\alpha\to 2
. The renewal relation
\lambda=\mu/\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(H_{+})
still holds, but here
\mathbb{E}_{0}(H_{+})=\infty
: the ascending ladder height diverges as
\mu\to 0
, and the rate vanishes super-linearly. Between the anchors the skewed prefactor is also explicit. The limiting law
Y
has characteristic function
[
15
]

\varphi_{\alpha,\beta}(t)=\exp\bigl[-|t|^{\alpha}\bigl(1-i\beta\tan(\tfrac{1}{2}\pi\alpha)\operatorname{sgn}t\bigr)\bigr],

(18)

and the finite part of (
11
) is the one-sided logarithmic moment
h^{*}_{0}=\mathbb{E}[\log|Y|;Y\mkern 2.0mu{<}\mkern 2.0mu0]
, computable from the negative half of the limiting density. For the stable law, Zolotarev’s Mellin transform gives

h^{*}_{0}=(1-\rho)\Big[\gamma(\frac{1}{\alpha}-1)+\frac{1}{\alpha}\log R_{\alpha,\beta}\Big],

(19)

with
R_{\alpha,\beta}=|1-i\beta\tan(\tfrac{1}{2}\pi\alpha)|
the modulus of its bracket, so that (
12
) gives

P=\frac{1-\rho}{\nu}\log\bigl(c\mkern 1.0muR_{\alpha,\beta}^{1/\alpha}\bigr),\quad K=\bigl(c\mkern 1.0muR_{\alpha,\beta}^{1/\alpha}\bigr)^{-(1-\rho)/\nu},

(20)

which reduces to the symmetric cases through
R_{\alpha,0}=1
. At
\alpha=3/2
the spectrally one-sided endpoints are the exact numbers
K=2^{-1/3}
at
\beta=-1
and
K=2^{-2/3}
at
\beta=+1
. For strictly stable steps the skew-dependent exponent and constant are those of Hurvich and Reed’s all-time-maximum series
[
18
]
: by reflection the record rate is the atom at zero of the all-time maximum of the reflected walk,

\lambda(\mu)=\mathbb{P}(S_{n}>0\ \forall\mkern 2.0mun\geqslant 1)=\mathbb{P}\Bigl(\sup_{n\geqslant 1}(-S_{n})\leqslant 0\Bigr),

(21)

which their series expands. The leading pole of (
7
) carries the law to the whole domain of attraction, because that pole is fixed entirely by the universal value
h(0)=1-\rho
. The exponent
(1-\rho)/\nu
is therefore universal—set by the tail index
\alpha
and the asymmetry
\rho
alone, much as a critical exponent is—while the amplitude
K
, carried by the finite part
h^{*}_{0}
, is nonuniversal and explicit only for strictly stable steps. Fixed-
\alpha
cuts, confirmed by direct numerical evaluation of the Spitzer–Baxter sum (
4
), are shown in Fig.

4
The Cauchy point

The phase portrait has a singular boundary at
\alpha=1
. The separation of scales behind (
5
) (the drift
n\mu
against fluctuations
c\mkern 1.0mun^{1/\alpha}
) requires
\nu={1-1/\alpha>0}
; as
\alpha\downarrow 1
the crossover
n^{*}\asymp(c/\mu)^{1/\nu}
recedes faster than any power of
1/\mu
. At the Cauchy point the drift is a location shift of the same order as the fluctuations:
S_{n}/n
is Cauchy with location
\mu
and scale
c
for every
n
, so

\mathbb{P}(S_{n}\leqslant 0)=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\pi}\arctan\bigl(\frac{\mu}{c}\bigr)

(22)

is independent of
n
; the harmonic sum (
4
) then diverges logarithmically and the biased Cauchy walk has
no
positive record rate,
\lambda(\mu)=0
. Its records instead accumulate sublinearly, the mean number of records up to time
N
growing as

\langle R_{N}\rangle\sim\frac{N^{\rho(\mu)}}{\Gamma(1+\rho(\mu))},\quad\rho(\mu)=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\pi}\arctan\bigl(\frac{\mu}{c}\bigr),

(23)

with a positivity that now depends on the drift
[
19
,
20
]
. In the language of (
7
),
\nu\to 0
collapses the scaling variable
\mu n^{\nu}
, the kernel freezes, and the
\zeta
factor sticks at its pole: the fundamental strip closes and the power law gives way to a continuous family of exponents
\rho(\mu)
. This inverts the driftless picture, where symmetric Cauchy records are fully universal
[
6
]
. The Cauchy point is the one place where the step
scale
(invisible to the records at
\mu=0
, and confined to the prefactor
K
for
\alpha>1
) enters the exponent itself, through the ratio
\mu/c
. This dependence remains universal across the
\alpha=1
class
[
10
,
21
]
.

5
Correction ladder

Returning to
1<\alpha\leqslant 2
, the poles of
G
at the negative integers
s=-k
give the subleading corrections. Expanding the kernel near the origin,
h(u)=\sum_{k\geqslant 0}a_{k}u^{k}
with
a_{0}=1-\rho
and
a_{1}=-f_{Y}(0)
(
f_{Y}
the density of
Y
), each term
a_{k}u^{k}
produces a simple pole of
h^{*}
at
s=-k
. The
\zeta
factor is regular there, so for
k\geqslant 1
this pole contributes

\frac{a_{k}}{c^{k}}\mkern 1.0mu\zeta(1-\nu k)\mkern 1.0mu\mu^{k}

(24)

to
g(\mu)
, read off just as the leading law was. Since
a_{1}=-f_{Y}(0)<0
and
\zeta(1-\nu)<0
for
0<\nu\leqslant 1/2
, the first correction makes
\lambda(\mu)/K\mu^{(1-\rho)/\nu}
approach unity from below throughout the stable-kernel family. For the Gaussian walk, where the even coefficients vanish by symmetry, the sum reproduces the Chang–Peres
\zeta
-series
[
16
]
,

g(\mu)=\log\Big(\frac{\sigma}{\mu}\Bigr)-\frac{1}{2}\log{2}+\sum_{m\geqslant 0}a_{2m+1}\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}-m)\Bigl(\frac{\mu}{\sigma}\Bigr)^{2m+1},

(25)

and for strictly stable steps it gives the corresponding generalized
\zeta
-ladder; both are read off the same poles, beyond the reach of finite-variance corrected-diffusion expansions
[
22
,
23
,
24
]
. A related
\zeta
-ladder is also read from Mellin poles in
[
25
]
, but for the first positive position of symmetric driftless walks, a distinct object obtained there by a different mathematical route.

6
A companion functional

The same base transform treats the running maximum
M_{n}=\max(S_{0},\dots,S_{n})
. When the limiting lag is finite, Spitzer’s identity
[
11
]
gives
\mathbb{E}(M_{N})=\mu N+D(\mu)+o(1)
, where

D(\mu)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\mkern 2.0mu\mathbb{E}(S_{n}^{-}),

(26)

a harmonic sum with weight
n^{1/\alpha-1}
. Its kernel
\hat{h}(x)={\mathbb{E}[(x+Y)^{-}]}
obeys
\hat{h}^{\prime}(x)=-h(x)
, so in the common strip the two base transforms differ only by a unit shift,

\hat{h}^{*}(s)=\frac{h^{*}(s+1)}{s},

(27)

and the companion transform inherits the factorized form,

\widetilde{G}(s)=\frac{c^{1+s}}{s}\mkern 2.0muh^{*}(s+1)\mkern 1.0mu\zeta(\nu(1+s)).

(28)

Record rate and expected maximum are thus the
same
transform read at neighboring poles. For the Gaussian walk the leftward displacement gives

D(\mu)=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2\mu}+\frac{\zeta(\frac{1}{2})}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sigma+\frac{\mu}{4}+O(\mu^{2}),

(29)

whose first term is Kingman’s heavy-traffic law for queues
[
26
]
and the second, negative term is Siegmund’s corrected-diffusion constant
[
22
,
27
,
28
]
. Thus the Gaussian record-rate correction and Siegmund’s constant carry the same number (only with opposite signs),
\zeta(\frac{1}{2})/\sqrt{2\pi}
, read from neighboring poles of the shifted transform. This signed finite part sets the zero-drift expected maximum. Concretely, in the Gaussian case the constant
c
(not to be confused with the stable scale of the rest of this paper) in the zero-drift law
\mathbb{E}(M_{n})=\sigma\sqrt{2n/\pi}-c+o(1)
is minus the constant term in (
29
). For a general jump law, when the constant term exists, this Gaussian skeleton is shifted by a kernel-dependent contribution. For uniform jumps on
[-1,1]
, for example, the total constant is
c\simeq 0.29795

[
29
,
30
,
31
,
32
]
(interestingly related there to the Milne extrapolation length of radiative transfer theory), not the Gaussian value
\simeq 0.33636
obtained from its variance alone.

In the heavy-tailed regimes the fundamental strip of
\widetilde{G}
closes (the same mechanism as at the Cauchy point), marking the descending-tail obstruction to a finite limiting lag, so
{D(\mu)=\infty}
. With a heavy descending tail
h(x)\sim C_{-}x^{-\alpha}
the companion kernel decays as
{\hat{h}(x)\sim C_{-}x^{1-\alpha}/(\alpha-1)}
, so the
n
-sum requires
\Real{s}>1/(\alpha-1)
while the kernel transform requires
\Real{s}<\alpha-1
; for
\alpha<2
the two are incompatible, there is no common fundamental strip.

7
Conclusions

A single Mellin transform of the Spitzer–Baxter harmonic sum organizes the small-drift record rate of biased random walks and Lévy flights into one pole dictionary, in which the skewness of the steps tunes the record-rate exponent continuously over
[1,\mkern 2.0mu1/(\alpha-1)]
through the positivity parameter
\rho
alone (Fig.
3
). This closes the small-drift end of the biased record problem left open by the universal driftless theory and locates its boundary at the Cauchy point
\alpha=1
, where the power law gives way to a drift-tuned family of sublinear exponents. With the Mellin variable shifted by one, the same transform also gives the small-drift expansion of the expected maximum, placing Kingman’s and Siegmund’s constants in the same framework. Conversely, the small-drift record rate can be used to infer step asymmetry, providing an inverse route when records are observed more readily than the underlying increments. Full derivations and comprehensive numerical analyses will be reported elsewhere.
