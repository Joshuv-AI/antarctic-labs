Remark 1
.

There are several ways to generalise our notion of a family of knots.

1)

The usual notion of a knot can be extended by allowing
\gamma
to be an immersion with self-intersections; such knots are called
singular knots
in
[
3
]
. The normal bundle
N_{\gamma/M}
is still well-defined in this setting, and the complex structure
I_{[g]}
extends to the space of singular knots
\widehat{\operatorname{Kn}(M)}
, so the notion of a family of knots extends accordingly. Most of our results appear to carry over to this generality. We work with smooth knots throughout for three reasons: firstly, it simplifies the notation considerably and makes the proofs less technical, especially in Sections
5.2
and
5.3
; secondly, we are not aware of any positive-dimensional holomorphic family in which singular knots genuinely appear; thirdly, this completion of the space of knots is not unique, and for other completions of the space of knots the results of our paper may not hold. Whether positive-dimensional families other than ours exist at all in the completion by singular knots is an interesting open question.

2)

One could also allow the base
X
of a family to be a singular complex analytic space, which would be natural from the point of view of the intrinsic complex geometry of
\operatorname{Kn}(M)
. Finding a suitable definition of a finite-dimensional family over such a base would, however, require additional work. Such families seem nonetheless worth studying; see e.g. Remark
11
.

3)

Yet another extension would be to allow
X
to be a smooth complex orbifold, which would enable one to incorporate the remaining finite quotients of the round sphere into Theorem
C
. In a broader direction, if in Hitchin’s Theorem
1.3
one takes
M
to be a flat or hyperbolic
3
-manifold, the space of geodesics of
M
admits a structure of a complex analytic stack. Studying its properties and its relation to
\pi_{1}(M)
would be very interesting, though this lies well beyond the scope of the present paper.

1.3.
Organisation of the paper

In Section
2
, we introduce the notions of families and holomorphic families of knots. It is in fact equivalent to the notion of a finite-dimensional submanifold (resp. complex submanifold) in the infinite-dimensional space of knots. The only novelty is that we define it in purely finite-dimensional terms.

In Section
3
, we several examples of finite-dimensional families of knots. We genuinely give two sources of examples: one coming from projective geometry of the space of circles on the round sphere, the other one is associated with holomorphic line bundles on Riemann surfaces. We also introduce the notions of
real
and
flow-like
families and illustrate them through our examples.

In Section
4
, we remind the reader of LeBrun’s CR twistor spaces and Lempert’s space of transversal knots in a CR manifold. We use them to give a characterisation of holomorphic families of knots in terms of CR geometry.

In Section
5
, we study the geometry of compact complex families of knots. In particular, we prove Theorems
A
and
B
. We also describe the holomorphic subfamilies formed by knots that pass through a given point.

Finally, in Section
6
, we conclude with our proof of the converse to Hitchin’s theorem (theorem
C
above).

3.2.
Families obtained from Hermitian line bundles on complex curves

Now we discuss a different method of constructing holomorphic families of knots that is based on complex geometry and leads to families of knots in
3
-manifolds of more interesting topology. The idea is to come back to the initial example of the Hopf fibration
h\colon S^{3}\to\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}
and view it as the spherisation of the tautological line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}}(1)\to\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}
.

Let
X
be a Kähler manifold with a Kähler Riemannian metric
g
and
p\colon\mathcal{E}\to X
an holomorphic vector bundle on it equipped with an Hermitian metric
h
. The
Chern connection
is the unique Hermitian connection
\nabla_{h}
on
\mathcal{E}
such that
\nabla_{h}^{0,1}=\overline{\partial}_{\mathcal{E}}
(
[
24
, Proposition 3.12]
). The Chern connection splits the tangent bundle of the total space
\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{E})
into a direct sum of
vertical
and
horizontal
components

(2)

T\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{E})=\mathcal{V}\oplus\mathcal{H}^{\nabla},

where
\mathcal{V}=\operatorname{ker}dp
. There are natural isomorphisms
p^{*}\mathcal{E}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{V}
and
p^{*}TX\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{H}^{\nabla}
. One ends up with a Kähler Riemannian metric
g^{\nabla}=p^{*}(\operatorname{Re}h)\oplus p^{*}g
on
T\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{E})
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
(X,I_{X})
be a smooth complex curve and
g_{X}
a Kähler Riemannian metric on it. Let
p\colon\mathcal{L}\to X
be an holomorphic line bundle,
h
an Hermitian metric on
\mathcal{L}
and
\nabla
its Chern connection. Denote by
S^{1}\mathcal{L}:=\{(x,v)\in\mathcal{L}\ |\ ||v||_{h}=1\}
the bundle of unit circles in this metric. Let
\widetilde{g}
be the restriction of the metric

on
S^{1}\mathcal{L}\subset\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{L})
and
\pi:=p|_{S^{1}\mathcal{L}}

Then
\mathcal{X}=(X_{\bullet}=S^{1}\mathcal{L},X,\pi,\operatorname{id}_{S^{1}\mathcal{L}})
is an holomorphic family of knots in the three-dimensional Riemannian manifold
M=(S^{1}\mathcal{L},\widetilde{g})
.

Proof.

Since
\nabla
is Hermitian, the horizontal distribution
\mathcal{H}^{\nabla}
restricts to a distribution on
S^{1}\mathcal{L}\subset\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{L})
that we also denote
\mathcal{H}^{\nabla}
. One has
TS^{1}\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{V}\oplus\mathcal{H}^{\nabla}
with
\mathcal{V}=\operatorname{Ker}d\pi
.

Let
x\in X
be a point on the base and
F_{x}=\pi^{-1}(x)
a fibre over it. Then the bundle
N_{F_{x}/\mathcal{L}^{1}}=N_{\gamma/M}
can be identified with
\mathcal{H}^{\nabla}|_{F_{x}}
. The map
\delta_{x}\colon T_{x}X\to\Gamma(F_{x},N_{F_{x}/\mathcal{L}^{1}})
then identifies with the horizontal lift
v\mapsto\widetilde{v},\ \nabla\widetilde{v}=0
. In particular,
\delta_{x}
is injective; since the evaluation map is a bijection, this is indeed a family in the sense of Definition
1
.

Notice that for a point
y\in F_{x}
and an horizontal vector
u\in\mathcal{H}^{\nabla}_{y}
the inner product
\dot{\gamma}(y)\times_{\widetilde{g}}u
is again horizontal, because
\mathcal{V}\perp_{\widetilde{g}}\mathcal{H}^{\nabla}
. The operation
u\mapsto\dot{\gamma}(y)\times_{g}u
defines a complex structure operator on
\mathcal{H}^{\nabla}_{y}
that we denote by
I_{\nabla}
. This turns
\mathcal{H}^{\nabla}
into a complex rank one vector bundle over
S^{1}\mathcal{L}
.

The differential
d\pi
induces an isomorphism
d\pi\colon\mathcal{H}^{\nabla}\xrightarrow{\sim}\pi^{*}TX
. This is an isomorphism of real vector bundles of rank
2
. Both of these vector bundles are endowed with complex structure operators and to prove the holomorphicity of family
\mathcal{X}
one needs to show that this isomorphism commutes with the complex structure:

d\pi\circ I_{\nabla}=I_{X}\circ d\pi.

The isomorphism

is an isometry with respect to Hermitian metrics
\widetilde{g}|_{\mathcal{H}^{\nabla}}
and

. But on a given
2
-dimensional real vector space there exists only two complex structures which are Hermitian with respect to a given positive scalar product. Both of them should be elements of order
4
in
\operatorname{U}(1)
, and there are only two of such elements. Therefore
d\pi
sends
I_{\nabla}
either to
I_{X}
or to
-I_{X}
. The latter is ruled out by the fact that
\nabla^{0,1}=\overline{\partial}_{\mathcal{L}}
.
∎

Remark 5

Such families are neither real nor flow-like.

4.2.
Velocity map and CR geometry

Recall that an

on a smooth manifold

satisfying

. Alternatively, it is given by a distribution

and a complex structure operator
J\colon H\to H,\ J^{2}=-1
(the equivalence of two descriptions is given by
H:=(L+\overline{L})_{\mathbb{R}},\ L:=H^{1,0}
). An almost CR-structure is called

, if

. Equivalently, the Frobenius tensor

\Lambda^{2}H\to TN/H,\ \eta_{1}\wedge\eta_{2}\mapsto[\eta_{1},\eta_{2}]\mod H

is of
(1,1)
-type with respect to
J
. The
CR corank
of an almost CR-structure is defined as

\dim M-\operatorname{rk}H
.

A(n almost) CR structure of zero CR corank is just a(n almost) complex structure.

A map of almost CR manifolds
f\colon(N_{1},L_{1})\to(N_{2},L_{2})
is called

if
df(L_{1})\subseteq L_{2}
. Note that we do not require
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
to have equal CR codimension.

Let
\mathcal{X}=(X_{\bullet},X,\pi,\operatorname{ev})
be a family of knots in
M
. Let
\mathcal{H}
be the Ehresmann connection constructed in Proposition
4.2
. The map

is an isomorphism. Thus, we can pull back the complex structure operator

to

, obtaining an almost CR structure on
(\mathcal{H},\widetilde{I_{X}})
on
X_{\bullet}
.

This can be restated as follows.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=(X_{\bullet},X,\pi,\operatorname{ev})
be a family of knots in a
3
-dimensional conformal manifold
(M,[g])
. Then any almost complex structure

on

yields an almost CR structure
(\mathcal{H},\widetilde{I_{X}})
on
X_{\bullet}
in such a way that the projection
\pi\colon X_{\bullet}\to X
is CR holomorphic.

Note that even if
I_{X}
is integrable,
\widetilde{I_{X}}
does not have to be.

4.3.
LeBrun’s CR-twistor spaces

LeBrun (

yields an integrable CR structure on

with the CR distribution

is endowed with a fixed Riemannian metric

Let
m\in M
be a point and
S_{m}:=p^{-1}(m)
the spherisation of
T^{*}_{m}M
. Let
[\alpha]\in S_{m}
. The space
\Lambda_{m,[\alpha]}
fits into an exact sequence

0\to\operatorname{ker}dp|_{m,[\alpha]}\to\Lambda_{m,[\alpha]}\xrightarrow{dp}\alpha^{\perp}\to 0.

Here
\alpha^{\perp}
is the

The space
\operatorname{ker}dp|_{m,[\alpha]}
is the tangent space to

at the point

. Let

be the unique integrable complex structure on the
2

.

The space

is a

In other words, the exact sequence (

as an extension of two complex

(\operatorname{ker}dp|_{m,[\alpha]},I_{0})

Finally, the Levi-Civita connection on

gives an Ehresmann connection on the bundle

Theorem 4.4

(LeBrun,
[

)

The construction above gives a well-defined integrable CR structure

The CR

. They are, moreover, related by the velocity map
\operatorname{vel}\colon X_{\bullet}\to\mathbf{S}M
and from the very definition of
\mathcal{H}
it follows that
d\operatorname{vel}(\mathcal{H})\subseteq\Lambda
. Therefore, it is natural to ask, when the map
\operatorname{vel}
is CR holomorphic. As it turns out, this is equivalent to family
\mathcal{X}
being holomorphic (with respect to
I_{X}
). This result almost immediately follows from a result of Lempert (
[
17
]
) as we know explain.

A knot
\gamma\colon S^{1}\to\mathbf{S}M
is called
transverse
if it is everywhere transverse to
\Lambda
.
Observe that if
\gamma
is transverse, then
dp(\dot{\gamma})
is always non-zero.

Let
\gamma\colon S^{1}\to\mathbf{S}M
be a transverse knot. Let
\gamma(t)=(m,[\alpha])
. Here
[\alpha]
is an oriented line in
T^{*}_{m}M
, or, equivalently, an oriented plane
\operatorname{ker}\alpha\subset T_{m}M
. Let
(e_{1},e_{2})
be a positively oriented basis in
\operatorname{ker}\alpha
. Further,
\gamma
is
positive
if
(e_{1},e_{2},dp(\dot{\gamma}))
constitute a positive oriented basis in
T_{m}M
for some (equivalently, any)
t
and
(e_{1},e_{2})
.

We say that a family of knots in
\mathbf{S}M
is transverse (resp., positive) if every knot in this family is transverse (resp., positive).

The following proposition is
[
17
, Proposition 9.1]
.

Proposition 4.5
.

A knot
\gamma\colon S^{1}\to\mathbf{S}M
is an image of the velocity map of a knot in
M
if and only if it is transverse and positive.

Remark 6
.

The conventions of the paper
[
17
]
are dual to ours, so Lempert calls
Legendrian
what we call transverse and
negative
what we call positive.

Let
\gamma\colon S^{1}\to\mathbf{S}M
be a transverse knot. Then its normal bundle
N_{\gamma/\mathbf{S}M}
can be identified with
\gamma^{*}\Lambda
. The latter is a complex vector bundle with the complex structure operator
J_{g}
. This suggests that the space of transverse knots in
\mathbf{S}M
is an almost complex Fréchet manifold in the spirit of subsection
1.1
. Lempert proved that this is indeed the case, and, moreover, the resulting complex structure is formally integrable (
[
17
, Corollary 4.4]
).

Further, one can imitate the construction of subsection
2.2
and give a definition of a

holomorphic family of transverse knots
in
\mathbf{S}M
. Namely, let
\mathcal{Y}=(Y_{\bullet},Y,\pi_{\mathcal{Y}},\operatorname{ev}_{\mathcal{Y}})
be a family of transverse knots in
\mathbf{S}M
(i.e. the evaluation map
\operatorname{ev}_{\mathcal{Y}}
now goes from
Y_{\bullet}
to
\mathbf{S}M
). The transversality condition guarantees that
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{Y}}:=d\operatorname{ev}_{\mathcal{Y}}^{-1}(\Lambda)
is an Ehresmann connection on the fibration
Y_{\bullet}\xrightarrow{\pi_{\mathcal{Y}}}Y
. A tangent vector
v\in T_{y}Y
admits a unique lifting
\widehat{v}\in\Gamma(\pi_{\mathcal{Y}}^{-1}(y),(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{Y}})|_{\pi_{\mathcal{Y}}^{-1}(y)})
.

Let
I_{Y}
be a complex structure on the base
Y
. We say that a family
\mathcal{Y}
is
holomorphic
(with respect to
I_{Y}
) if

J_{g}\cdot d\operatorname{ev}_{\mathcal{Y}}(\widehat{v})=d\operatorname{ev}_{\mathcal{Y}}(\widehat{I_{Y}\cdot v)}

for every tangent vector
v\in T_{y}Y
.

Theorem 4.6

(Lempert)
.

Let
(M,[g])
be a conformal
3
-manifold. Let
\mathcal{X}=(X_{\bullet},X,\pi,\operatorname{ev})
be a family of knots in
M
. Consider the transverse family of knots in
\mathbf{S}M
given by

\mathcal{X}^{\operatorname{vel}}:=(X_{\bullet},X,\pi,\operatorname{vel}).

(Here the space of the family is the same, but we replace the evaluation map
\operatorname{ev}\colon X_{\bullet}\to M
with the velocity map
\operatorname{vel}\colon X_{\bullet}\to\mathbf{S}M
, thus treating it as a family of knots in
\mathbf{S}M
).

Let
I_{X}
be a complex structure on
X
. Then
\mathcal{X}
is an holomorphic family of knots in
(M,[g])
with respect to
I_{X}
if and only if
\mathcal{X}^{\operatorname{vel}}
is an holomorphic family of transverse knots in
(\mathbf{S}M,\Lambda,J_{g})
with respect to
I_{X}
.

Lempert phrases this theorem in a slightly different way. He proves (
[
17
, Corollary 9.6]
) that the velocity map determines a biholomorphism between the space of knots
\operatorname{Kn}(M)
and the space of transverse positive knots in
\mathbf{S}M
. Theorem
4.6
is obtained by restricting to finite-dimensional submanifolds of these Fréchet manifolds.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=(X_{\bullet},X,\pi,\operatorname{ev})
be a family of knots in a conformal
3
-manifold
(M,[g])
. Let
I_{X}
be a complex structure operator on
X
. The family
\mathcal{X}
is holomorphic with respect to

if and only if the velocity map

(X_{\bullet},\mathcal{H},\widetilde{I_{X}})\to(\mathbf{S}M,\Lambda,J_{g})

is CR-holomorphic.

This is an analogue of the Proposition
3.6
for holomorphic families with base of dimension greater than one.

5.
Geometry of holomorphic families of knots

5.1.
Radon transform and Kähler forms

Fix a smooth manifold
M
and a family of knots
\mathcal{X}=(X_{\bullet},X,\pi,\operatorname{ev})
in it. There is an important procedure that relates differential
k
-forms on
M
with differential
(k-1)
-forms on
X
. We refer to it as the
Radon transform
due to its similarity to the eponymous operation in integral geometry.

First, recall that if
\eta
is a
k
-form
\eta
on
X_{\bullet}
, one can construct
(k-1)
-form
\pi_{*}\eta
on
X
by integration along fibres:

(\pi_{*}\eta)_{x}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k-1})=\int_{F_{x}}\eta\left(\widetilde{v_{1}},\ldots,\widetilde{v_{k}},\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)dt,

where
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
is a positive-oriented trivialisation of
(\operatorname{ker}d\pi)|_{F_{x}}
and
\widetilde{v_{i}}\in\Gamma(\pi^{-1}(x),TX_{\bullet}|_{\pi^{-1}(x)})
are lifts of
v_{i}
.

Equivalently, one can associate to
\eta
an
(n-k)
-dimensional current
\Delta_{\eta}\colon\varphi\mapsto\int_{X_{\bullet}}\varphi\wedge\eta
. Then one can check that the pushforward
\pi_{*}\Delta_{\eta}
is again a regular current on
X
, so it can be represented by an integration against a form

\pi_{*}(\Delta_{\eta})\colon\psi\mapsto\int_{X}\psi\wedge\pi_{*}\eta.

The operation
\pi_{*}\colon\Omega^{\bullet}_{X_{\bullet}}\to\Omega^{\bullet-1}_{X}
sends closed forms to closed and descends to a map of the de Rham cohomology groups
H^{k}(X_{\bullet},\mathbb{R})\to H^{k-1}(X,\mathbb{R})
. If
X
is compact it coincides with the Gysin morphism. In particular, it sends integral cohomology classes to integral ones.

Definition 6
.

The
Radon transform
is the map

R\colon\Omega^{\bullet}_{M}\to\Omega^{\bullet-1}_{X},\ \eta\mapsto\pi_{*}\operatorname{ev}^{*}\eta.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a smooth
3
-dimensional Riemannian manifold and
\mathcal{X}=(X_{\bullet},(X,I_{X}),\pi,\operatorname{ev})
an holomorphic family of knots. Then the following holds:

(i)

the complex manifold
(X,I_{X})
is Kähler;

(ii)

if
X
and
M
are compact, then
X
is biholomorphic to (the analytification of) a smooth complex projective variety.

Proof.

(i)
. Let
\nu_{g}
be the Riemannian volume form on
M
and
\omega_{g}:=R(\nu_{g})
its Radon transform. We claim that
\omega_{g}
is a Kähler form on
(X,I_{X})
. If
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
are two vectors in
T_{x}X
, then
\omega_{g}(v_{1},v_{2})
can be computed as

\omega_{g}(v_{1},v_{2})=\int_{S^{1}}\gamma_{x}^{*}\nu_{g}(\delta_{x}(v_{1}),\delta_{x}(v_{2}),\dot{\gamma_{x}})dt.

The form
\omega_{g}
is closed and non-degenerate because
\nu_{g}
is. What is left to check is that
(u,v)\mapsto\omega_{g}(u,I_{X}v)
defines a positive symmetric
2
-form on
X
. Indeed, let
v
be a non-zero vector in
T_{x}X
. Then

\omega_{g}(v,I_{X}v)=\int_{F_{x}}{\nu_{g}}{\left(\delta(v),{\dot{\gamma_{x}}}{\times_{g}}{\delta(v)},\dot{\gamma_{x}}\right)}dt.

For any pair of non-zero vectors
u_{1},u_{2}
in the 3-dimensional Euclidean space the triple
(u,v\times u,v)
defines a positive-oriented basis. Thus the right-hand side of the equality above is positive.

(ii)
. Suppose now that
X
is compact. By rescaling
\nu_{g}
if necessary we may assume that the class
[\nu_{g}]\in H^{3}(M,\mathbb{R})
is integral. Therefore the class
[\omega_{g}]
is also integral. Thus,
X
admits a Kähler form with integral cohomology class. By Kodaira’s Embedding Theorem,
X
is biholomorphic to a projective variety (see e.g.
[
24
, Theorem 7.11]
).
∎

Remark 7
.

Let
\Sigma\subset M
be a smooth surface. To it one can associate the locus
L_{\Sigma}=\pi(\operatorname{ev}^{*}(\Sigma))\subset X
of knots lying on
\Sigma
. This locus is Lagrangian with respect to the symplectic form
\omega_{g}
, in particular it is never holomorphic.

An important corollary of Theorem
5.1
is the following.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact
3
-dimensional Riemannian manifold. Consider an holomorphic family of knots
(X_{\bullet},X,\pi,\operatorname{ev})
in it. Suppose that
X
is compact and
\dim X>0
. Then the evaluation map
\operatorname{ev}\colon X_{\bullet}\to M
is surjective.

Proof.

Suppose first that
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}X=1
. Then
\operatorname{ev}\colon X_{\bullet}\to M
is a smooth map between compact
3
-dimensional manifolds. To prove that it is surjective, it is enough to check that
\operatorname{ev}^{*}[\nu_{g}]\neq 0
where
[\nu_{g}]
is the cohomology class of the Riemannian volume form on
(M,g)
. But
\omega_{g}=R(\nu_{g})=\pi_{*}\operatorname{ev}^{*}\nu_{g}
is a Kähler form on the compact complex manifold
X
, so its cohomology class is non-zero. Therefore,
\operatorname{ev}^{*}[\nu_{g}]\neq 0
as well.

Assume
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}X>1
. Since
X
is projective, one can find a smooth closed holomorphic curve
Y\subset X
. Then the evaluation map is surjective already after restriction to
Y_{\bullet}:=\pi^{-1}(Y)
.
∎

If one drops the compactness assumption, the statement of Lemma
5.2
still holds locally.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
(M,g)
be a
3
-dimensional Riemannian manifold and
(X_{\bullet},X,\pi,\operatorname{ev})
an holomorphic family of knots,
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}X>0
. Let
m\in M
be a non-critical value of
\operatorname{ev}
. Then there exists a neighbourhood
U
of
m
such that
\operatorname{ev}^{-1}(U)\to U
is surjective.

Proof.

Let
x_{\bullet}=(x,t)
be a point in
X_{\bullet}
such that
\operatorname{ev}(x_{\bullet})=m
. In other words,
m=\gamma_{x}(t)
. It is sufficient to show that the differential
d\operatorname{ev}|_{x_{\bullet}}\colon T_{x_{\bullet}}X_{\bullet}\to T_{m}M
is surjective. Notice that
\operatorname{rk}d\operatorname{ev}|_{x_{\bullet}}\geq 1
, because it does not vanish restricted on
\operatorname{ker}d\pi|_{x_{\bullet}}=\mathbb{R}\dot{\gamma_{x}}
. Let
v\in T_{x}X
be a tangent vector such that
\delta(v)(t)\neq 0
. Such
v
exists because we assumed
m
to be non-critical. Then
\delta(v)(t),\dot{\gamma_{x}}
and
\delta(I_{X}v)(t)=\dot{\gamma_{x}}\times_{g}\delta(v)(t)
are three linearly independent vectors in
T_{m}M
in the image of
d\operatorname{ev}
. Thus
\operatorname{rk}d\operatorname{ev}|_{x_{\bullet}}\geq 3=\dim T_{m}M
.
∎

5.2.
Pinned loci

We say that an holomorphic family of knots
\mathcal{X}=(X_{\bullet},(X,I_{X}),\pi,\operatorname{ev})
in a Riemannian
3
-manifold
(M,g)
is
analytic
if there exist real analytic structures on
M
and
X_{\bullet}
such that
\operatorname{ev}\colon X_{\bullet}\to M
and
\pi\colon X_{\bullet}\to X
are analytic. Starting from now we restrict ourselves to study of analytic families of knots.

We are not aware of any example of a non-analytic holomorphic family of knots and such an assumption seems to be a minor limitation.

Recall the following result of Reiffen.

Theorem 5.4

(Reiffen;
[
20
]
, Satz 2’)
.

Let
X
be a compact manifold and
Y\subset X
a real analytic subvariety such that its smooth locus is a complex submanifold. Then
Y
is complex analytic.

As usual, for a point
x\in X
we denote
F_{x}:=\pi^{-1}(x)
and
\gamma_{x}:=\operatorname{ev}|_{F_{x}}

Definition 7
.

Fix a point
m\in M
. A
locus of knots pinned at
m
(or, simply, a
pinned locus
) is the set of the form

D_{m}:=\{x\in X\ |m\in\operatorname{im}\gamma_{x}\}.

Let
m
be a point in
M
and
\widetilde{D}_{m}:=\operatorname{ev}^{-1}(m)\subseteq X_{\bullet}
. Then
D_{m}=\pi(\widetilde{D}_{m})
.

Similarly, for a finite collection of pairwise distinct points
\mathbf{m}=\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}\}
in
M
one can define the
k
-pinned locus

D_{\mathbf{m}}=D_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}}
as the set of knots
[\gamma]\in X
that pass simultaneously through
m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}
. In other words,

D_{\mathbf{m}}=\bigcap_{m\in\mathbf{m}}D_{m}.

Clearly, if
\mathbf{m}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbf{m}
then
D_{\mathbf{m}}\subseteq D_{\mathbf{m}^{\prime}}.

Since
\widetilde{D}_{m}
is a fibre of the analytic map
\operatorname{ev}
and
D_{m}
is its image under the proper analytic map
\pi
, they both are analytic. Let
D^{\circ}_{m}\subseteq D_{m}
(respectively,
\widetilde{D}^{\circ}_{m}
) be their smooth loci.

Observe that
\widetilde{D}_{m}\to D_{m}
is a bijection and over a dense open analytic subset
\widetilde{D}^{\circ}_{m}\to D^{\circ}_{m}
is a real analytic isomorphism. Its inverse gives a section of the
S^{1}
-bundle
(D_{m})_{\bullet}:=\pi^{-1}(D_{m})\to D_{m}
.

Recall, that the complex structure operator
I_{X}
on
X
determines an almost CR structure
(\mathcal{H},\widetilde{I_{X}})
on
X_{\bullet}
, where the distribution
\mathcal{H}
is a certain Ehresmann connection on the fibre bundle
X_{\bullet}\to X
(Corollary
4.3
).

Lemma 5.5
.

The following holds:

(1)

\widetilde{D}^{\circ}_{m}
is a CR holomorphic submanifold of
(X_{\bullet},\mathcal{H},\widetilde{I_{X}})
of CR corank zero. This means that
T\widetilde{D}^{\circ}_{m}
is contained in the restriction of
\mathcal{H}
and preserved by
\widetilde{I_{X}}
. Moreover, the restriction of
\widetilde{I_{X}}
to
T\widetilde{D}^{\circ}_{m}
defines an integrable complex structure on
\widetilde{D}^{\circ}_{m}
and the projection
\widetilde{D}^{\circ}_{m}\to\pi(\widetilde{D}^{\circ}_{m})\subseteq X
is holomorphic;

(2)

D_{m}
is a closed complex analytic subvariety of
X
;

(3)

if
m\in M
is not a critical value of
\operatorname{ev}
, then
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}D_{m}=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}X-1
, i.e.
D_{m}
is a divisor;

(4)

if
X
is compact of complex dimension
n
and
m,m^{\prime}
are two non-critical values of
\operatorname{ev}
, then
[D_{m}]=[D_{m^{\prime}}]
in
H_{2n-2}(X,\mathbb{Q})
.

Proof.

(
1
). Since
\widetilde{D}^{\circ}_{m}
is an open dense subset of
\operatorname{ev}^{-1}(m)
, it is tangent to
\operatorname{ker}d\operatorname{ev}
. The latter is contained in
\mathcal{H}
.

Let
x_{\bullet}=(x,t)
be point in
\widetilde{D}^{\circ}_{m}
such that its image
x=\pi(x_{\bullet})
is in
D^{\circ}_{m}
. For a vector
v\in T_{x}X
denote by
\widetilde{v}
its horizontal lift via the connection
\mathcal{H}
. Then
\widetilde{v}_{x_{\bullet}}
is tangent to
\widetilde{D}^{\circ}_{m}
if and only if it belongs to
\operatorname{ker}d\operatorname{ev}
, that is
\delta(v)=0
. In this case,
\delta(I_{X}\cdot v)
also vanishes, thus
\widetilde{I_{X}}\cdot\widetilde{v}_{x_{\bullet}}
is again tangent to
\widetilde{D}^{\circ}_{m}
.

(
2
). The smooth locus of
D_{m}
is an holomorphic submanifold of
X
by (
1
). Therefore,
D_{m}
is complex analytic by Reiffen’s Theorem (Theorem
5.4
).

(
3
). If
m
is a non-critical value of
\operatorname{ev}
, then
\dim\widetilde{D}_{m}=\dim X_{\bullet}-\dim M
(recall that
\operatorname{ev}
is locally surjective by Proposition
5.3
). At the same time,

\dim_{\mathbb{R}}D_{m}=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{D}_{m}=\dim X_{\bullet}-\dim M=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}X+1-3=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}X-2.

(
4
). First of all
[\widetilde{D}_{m}]=[\widetilde{D}_{m^{\prime}}]
since these are two smooth fibers of a map between connected compact orientable manifolds, so they are both Poincaré dual to the pullback of the cohomology class of a point. At the same time,
[D_{m}]=\pi_{*}[\widetilde{D}_{m}]
and
[D_{m^{\prime}}]=\pi_{*}[\widetilde{D}_{m^{\prime}}]
.
∎

Remark 8
.

The assumption that
m
is non-critical is crucial in the item (
3
). Indeed, suppose that every knot
\gamma_{x}
in the family
X
passes through a point
m_{0}\in M
. Then
D_{m_{0}}=X
.

Remark 9
.

Lemma
5.5
can be generalised directly to
k
-pinned loci. We left as an exercise to the reader to check that for a generic choice of distinct

-points
\mathbf{m}=\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}\}
in

the

-pinned locus

is a complex subvariety of
X
of complex codimension

whose homology class does not depend on the choice of
\mathbf{m}
.

The following follows immediately from Lemma
5.5
and Corollary
4.7
.

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
m\in M
be a point. Then the velocity map
\operatorname{vel}\colon X_{\bullet}\to\mathbf{S}M
restricts to an holomorphic map

\widetilde{D}^{\circ}_{m}\to S_{m},

where the
2
-sphere
S_{m}=\mathbf{S}(T^{*}_{m}M)\simeq\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}
is endowed with the standard complex structure.

Let
(m,\xi)
be a point in
\mathbf{S}M
, that is,
m\in M
and
\xi
is a positive ray in
T^{*}_{m}M
. Let
\widetilde{D}_{m,\xi}:=\operatorname{vel}^{-1}(m,\xi)
and
D_{m,\xi}:=\pi(\widetilde{D}_{m,\xi})
. Explicitly,
D_{m,\xi}
can be described as the set of knots in our family passing through a given point
m
with the prescribed tangent vector at this point.

Proposition 5.7
.

Suppose that
(m,\xi)
is a non-critical value of
\operatorname{vel}
and
m
is a non-critical value of
m^{\prime}
. Then

(1)

\widetilde{D}_{m,\xi}\cap\widetilde{D}^{\circ}_{m}
is a closed codimension
1

\widetilde{D}^{\circ}_{m}
;

(2)

D_{m,\xi}
is a closed codimension
1
complex analytic subvariety of
D_{m}
;

Proof.

(

). By Corollary
5.6
it is a fibre of an holomorphic map to

;

(
2
). Arguments are similar to those in Lemma

.
∎

5.3.
The dimension bound

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian
3
-manifold and
\mathcal{X}=(X,X_{\bullet},\pi,\operatorname{ev})
a holomorphic family of knots in it. If
X
is compact, then
\dim X\leq 2
.

The idea of the proof is the following. We show that if
X
is compact then the loci
D_{m,\xi}
are either empty or zero-dimensional. At the same time, for a generic choice of
(m,\xi)
one has

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}D_{m,\xi}=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}D_{m}-1-1=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}X-2

by
(ii)
of Proposition
5.7
and
(iii)
of Lemma
5.5
. Thus, one concludes that
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}X\leq 2
.

Recall the following simple fact from Kähler geometry.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold and
f\colon X\to\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}
an holomorphic map. Let
Y\subseteq X
be a closed analytic subvariety and
\omega_{FS}
be the Fubini–Study form on
\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}
. Then
f|_{Y}
is locally constant if and only if
[(f^{*}{\omega_{FS}})|_{Y}]=0
in
H^{2}(Y,\mathbb{Q})

Proof.

Let
k=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}Y
. Choose a Kähler form
\omega
on
X
.

Suppose that
f|_{Y}
is not locally constant. Then
(f^{*}\omega_{FS})|_{Y}
is a non-negative
(1,1)
-form on
Y
which is positive in at least one point. Therefore,

\int_{Y}f^{*}\omega_{FS}\wedge\omega^{k-1}>0,

implying that
f^{*}\omega_{FS}
is not exact on
Y
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.8
.

Let
m
be generic enough, so that
D_{m}
is smooth. We may assume that
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}X\geq 2
. Therefore
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}D_{m}\geq 1
and the evaluation map
\operatorname{ev}\colon\pi^{-1}(D_{m})\to M
is surjective (Lemma
5.2
). The velocity map restricts to a surjective map
\operatorname{vel}\colon D_{m}=\widetilde{D}_{m}\to S_{m}=\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}
which is holomorphic by Corollary
5.6
.

A generic knot
\gamma\in D_{m}
is not contained inside the critical locus of
\operatorname{ev}|_{\pi^{-1}(D_{m})}
, i.e.
D_{m,z}=D_{m}\cap D_{z}
is smooth for generic
z\in\gamma
. Notice that
[D_{m,z_{1}}]
and
[D_{m,z_{2}}]
are homologous inside
D_{m}
for different
z_{1},\ z_{2}\in\gamma
. Moreover, we can assume that
D_{m,\xi}
is smooth for
\xi=\dot{\gamma}(m)
and the same is true for
D_{m,-\xi}
.

We use notation
D_{m,\pm\xi}:=D_{m,\xi}\cup D_{m,-\xi}
.

The idea of the proof is the following. If we take
z\in\gamma
and tend it to
m
, the subvarieties
D_{m,z}
tend to
D_{m,\xi}
. This implies that
\operatorname{vel}^{*}\omega_{FS}
integrates by zero over
D_{m,z}
, forcing it to be contained in a union of fibres of the velocity map.

More precisely, let
\Delta_{z}
denote the integration current of
D_{m,z}
in
D_{m}
. This is a closed integral current whose homology class does not depend on
z
. Moreover, since each
D_{m,z}
is a compact holomorphic submanifold inside compact Kähler manifold
D_{m}
, it is volume minimizing by Wirtinger inequality (see e.g.
[
6
]
). Therefore, the mass
\mathbf{M}(\Delta_{z})
is constant.

Further, Federer - Fleming Compactness Theorem (
[
5
]
) guarantees that there exists a limit
\Delta:=\lim_{z\to m}\Delta_{z}
that is an integral current in the same homology class.

We claim that
\Delta
is supported on
D_{m,\xi}\cup D_{m,\pm\xi}
.

Proof of the claim is the following. Let
z_{n}
be a sequence of points on
\gamma
that tend to
m
and
x_{n}
a sequence of points in
D_{m}
with
x_{j}\in D_{m,z_{j}}
. Each
x_{j}
corresponds to a knot
\gamma_{x_{j}}
that passes through
m
and intersects
\gamma
in at least one more point
z_{j}
. Suppose that
x
is a limit point of
(x_{n})
(such necessary exists by compactness). Then the knot
\gamma_{x}
passes through
m
and is tangent to
\gamma
there. This implies that either
\gamma_{x}\in D_{m,\xi}
or
\gamma_{x}\in D_{m,-\xi}
.

Choose a point
y\in X\setminus D_{m,\pm\xi}
. By the discussion above and compactness of
D_{m,z_{j}}
, we can find a small ball
B(y)
centered at
y
such that
B(y)\cap D_{m,z}
is empty for
z\in\gamma
sufficiently close to
m
. Thus
\operatorname{Supp}(\Delta)\cap B(y)
is empty.

Once we concluded that
\operatorname{Supp}(\Delta)\subseteq D_{m,\pm\xi}
, we can apply Lemma
5.9
. Namely, let
\omega:=\operatorname{vel}^{*}\omega_{FS}
be the pullback of Fubini–Study form on
S_{m}\simeq\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}
to
D_{m}
via the velocity map. For
z\in\gamma
we have

\int_{D_{m,z}}\omega=\langle\Delta_{z},\omega\rangle=\langle\Delta,\omega\rangle=0.

Here the second equality follows from the fact that
\Delta
and
\Delta_{z}
are homologeous and the third one from that
\Delta
is supported on a fibre of
\operatorname{vel}
.

By Lemma
5.9
this implies that connected components of
D_{m,z}
are contained in a fibre of
\operatorname{vel}\colon D_{m}\to S_{m}
for every
z\in\gamma
. The intersection of
D_{m,z}
and
D_{m,\xi}
is non-empty, as it contains
\gamma
. Thus,
D_{m,z}\subseteq D_{m,\pm\xi}
. Observe that by Proposition
5.7
these are both closed analytic subvarieties of the same dimension, hence
D_{m,z}\subseteq D_{m,\xi}
is a union of irreducible components.

Let
E\subseteq D_{m,\pm\xi}
be the irreducible component containing
{\gamma}
. Since we assumed that
D_{m,\xi}
is smooth, such component is unique and coincides with the connected component. As we shown, for every
z\in\gamma
one has
D_{m,z}=E
. This means, that every knot from
E
passes through every point of
\gamma
as well, i.e.
E=\{\gamma\}
.

Arguing similarly for knots in different connected components of
D_{m,\xi}
, we conclude that each of them is a point, i.e.
\dim D_{m,\xi}=0
.

At the same time,
\dim D_{m,\xi}=\dim X-2
, thus
X
is a surface.
∎

6.
Flow-like and real flow-like families

In this section we study flow-like and real flow-like families (Definitions
4
and
3
).

6.1.
Counting knots through pairs of points

Let
\mathcal{X}=(X_{\bullet},X,\pi,\operatorname{ev})
be a compact flow-like holomorphic family in a conformal
3
-manifold
(M,[g])
.

Recall that an algebraic variety is called
rationally connected
if every two points on it can be connected by a chain of rational curves.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=(X_{\bullet},X,\pi,\operatorname{ev})
be a compact holomorphic flow-like family in
(M,[g])
. Then
X
is a complex projective surface. For a point
m\in M
, the pinned locus
D_{m}\subset X
is a rational curve. Moreover,
X
is rationally connected.

Proof.

As we pointed out in Proposition
4.1
, the velocity map
\operatorname{vel}\colon X_{\bullet}\to\mathbf{S}M
is bijective. Since the family is compact, it is a homeomorphism. Therefore it identifies
X
with the base of an
S^{1}
-bundle whose total space in
\mathbf{S}M
, so
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}X=4
.

The restriction of the
S^{1}
-family
X_{\bullet}\to X
on the pinned locus
D_{m}
admits a canonical section
\widetilde{D}_{m}=\operatorname{ev}^{-1}(m)
. The velocity map maps it isomorphically to the sphere
\mathbf{S}_{m}
. Thus
D_{m}
has genus
0
.

It easily follows from surjectivity of evaluation map (Lemma
5.2
) that every two points on
X
can be connected by a chain of pinned curves
D_{m_{i}}
(in fact it is always sufficient to two such curves).
∎

Corollary 6.2
.

Under the same assumptions, the divisors
D_{m},D_{m^{\prime}}\subset X
are rationally equivalent.

Proof.

Since
X
is rationally connected,
H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=0
. Thus, rational equivalence of divisors is the same as cohomological equivalence (see e.g.
[
24
, Section 7.1.3.]
).
∎

This Corollary allows us to speak about
pinned divisor class

D
that corresponds to an holomorphic line bundle
\mathcal{O}(D)
. The pinned curves are obtained as zero sets of holomorphic sections of this bundle. Notice that the family of pinned curves
D_{m}
is parametrised by points
m\in M
, that is, has real dimension
3
. The space
\mathbf{P}(H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(D))
is therefore has complex dimension at least
2
, and for generic section of
\mathcal{O}(D)
its zero set is not a pinned curve, but rather its holomorphic deformation.

The main goal for now is to get a bound on the cohomological self-intersection
[D]^{2}\in H^{4}(X,\mathbb{Z})
. Namely, we prove the following lemma:

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=(X_{\bullet},X,\pi,\operatorname{ev})
be a flow-like holomorphic family on
(M,[g])
. Let
D_{m}\subseteq X
be a pinned curve. Then
[D_{m}]^{2}=2
.

The square
[D_{m}]^{2}
can be counted as the number of intersections of two generic pinned curves
D_{m_{1}}
and
D_{m_{2}}
. Geometrically, it can be interpreted as the number of knots from the family
\mathcal{X}
connecting
m_{1}
to
m_{2}
. Therefore Lemma
6.3
says that flow-like families share something in common with classical geometries: every two points can be connected by a preferred path, and there is at most two such paths.

Once we think about the number
[D_{m}]^{2}
as the number of knots connecting two generic points, it is easy to observe that it equals the degree of
bievaluation map

\operatorname{ev}^{(2)}\colon X_{\bullet}\times_{X}X_{\bullet}\to M\times M.

This map takes a triple
(\gamma_{x},t,s)
, where
t
and
s
are two points on the fiber
\gamma_{x}\subset X_{\bullet}
to
(\gamma_{x}(t),\gamma_{x}(s))\in M\times M
.

Notice that
\dim X_{\bullet}\times_{X}X_{\bullet}=\dim M\times M=6
and there exists a dense open subset of
M\times M
on which every point has exactly
d=[D_{m}]^{2}
preimages.

The idea is to connect the number
d
with the geometry of velocity map
\operatorname{vel}
seeing the latter as degeneration of
\operatorname{ev}^{(2)}
when the two points
t,\ s
tend to each other.

In order to do it let us first introduce the construction of
real blow-up
. This is an operation that is probably well-known, but we did not manage to find its discussion in the literature.

Let
L
be a smooth compact manifold and
K\subset L
a smooth closed submanifold. Let
U(K)
be a small tubular neighbourhood and
\mathring{U}(K):=U(K)\setminus K
. Consider the projectivisation of the normal bundle
\mathbf{P}N_{K/L}
. It is endowed with the tautological line bundle
\mathcal{O}(1)
. Let
P_{0}\subset\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{O}(1))
be the zero section and
V(P_{0})
its small tubular neighbourhood in
\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{O}(1))
. Let
\mathring{V}(P_{0})=V(P_{0})\setminus P_{0}
. By Tubular Neighbourhood Theorem there exists a diffeomorphism
\varphi\colon\mathring{U}(K)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathring{V}(P_{0})
.

Definition 8
.

The
real oriented blow-up
of
L
in
K
is the glueing

\Bl_{K}L:=(L\setminus K)\bigsqcup_{\varphi}V(P_{0}).

The resulting topological space is a smooth manifold that does not depend on the choice of
\varphi
.

It is endowed with a natural surjective map
\tau\colon\Bl_{K}L\to L
that is isomorphism on
\operatorname{Int}(\Bl_{K}L)\xrightarrow{\sim}L\setminus K
and on
P_{0}
acts as the projection
\mathbf{P}N_{K/L}\to K
.

We come back to study the bievaluation map
\operatorname{ev}^{(2)}
.

Remark that
X_{\bullet}
is embedded as a diagonal
\Delta_{X_{\bullet}}\subset X_{\bullet}\times_{X}X_{\bullet}
as a hypersurface. Similarly,
M
is embedded as a diagonal
\Delta_{M}\subset M\times M
and
\operatorname{ev}^{(2)}(\Delta_{X_{\bullet}})=\Delta_{M}
. The normal bundle
\mathcal{N}_{\Delta_{M}/M\times M}
is isomorphic to
T^{*}M
. Using the conformal structure we can identify
\mathbf{P}(T^{*}M)
with
\mathbf{P}(TM)=\mathbf{P}M
. The latter is obtained by taking quotienf of
\mathbf{S}M
by the natural involution
\pm 1
.

The key observation is that the map
\operatorname{ev}^{(2)}
admits a natural lift to the real blow-up of
M\times M
along the diagonal.

Proposition 6.4
.

There exists a smooth surjective map
\Phi
such that the diagram

\textstyle{\Bl_{\Delta_{M}}(M\times M)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\tau}
\textstyle{X_{\bullet}\times_{X}X_{\bullet}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\operatorname{ev}^{(2)}}
\scriptstyle{\Phi}
\textstyle{M\times M}

commutes. On the diagonal
\Delta_{X_{\bullet}}
it coincides with the composition map

X_{\bullet}\simeq\Delta_{X_{\bullet}}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{vel}}\mathbf{S}M\xrightarrow{\sigma}\mathbf{P}M,

where
\sigma
is the natural projection.

Proof.

Set-theoretically this map is already determined: it coincides with
\operatorname{ev}^{(2)}
on the complement of diagonal
\Delta_{X_{\bullet}}
and on the diagonal maps
(\gamma_{x},t,t)\mapsto(\gamma_{x}(t),[\dot{\gamma_{x}}(t)])\in\mathbf{P}M
.

The only thing that is needed is to check smoothness along the diagonal. By Boman’s theorem it is enough to check it on germs of smooth curves.

Let
c\colon{]}{-\varepsilon;\varepsilon}{[}\to X_{\bullet}\times_{X}X_{\bullet}
be a germ of a smooth path such that
c(0)
lies on diagonal
\Delta_{X_{\bullet}}
. We may assume that
c
is transverse to
\Delta_{X_{\bullet}}
and does not intersect it outside
c(0)
, otherwise the smoothness follows from smoothness of
\operatorname{vel}
and
\sigma
.

Let
c(\lambda)=(\gamma_{\lambda},t_{\lambda},s_{\lambda})
. By our assumption,
t_{0}=s_{0}=t
. Let

(p_{\lambda},q_{\lambda}):=\Phi(c(\lambda))=(\gamma_{\lambda}(t_{\lambda}),\gamma_{\lambda}(s_{\lambda}))\in M\times M.

The isomorphism
\varphi\colon\mathring{U}(\Delta_{M})\to\mathring{V}(P_{0})
allows us to write down a point
(p,q)
in a neighbourhood of
\Delta_{M}
as a point
(m,[v],r)\in V(P_{0})
, where
(m,[v])
is a point in
\mathbf{P}M
and
r\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
is the normal parameter. We are allowed to choose
\varphi
in such a way that
m=p
. Then, after applying diffeomorphism
\varphi
, we get

\Phi(\gamma_{\lambda},t_{\lambda},s_{\lambda})=(p_{\lambda},[v_{\lambda}],r_{\lambda})

and
r_{\lambda}v_{\lambda}=(s_{\lambda}-t_{\lambda})\dot{\gamma_{\lambda}}(t_{\lambda})+o(s_{\lambda}-t_{\lambda})
. Here
(s_{\lambda}-t_{\lambda})
is the distance between
s_{\lambda}
and
t_{\lambda}
taken with a sign depending on whether the shortest path between them goes along the orientation of the knot, or in the opposite direction.

As
\lambda\to 0
from either above to below,
r_{\lambda}\to 0
and
(s_{\lambda}-t_{\lambda})\to 0
. By L’Hôpital rule,

\lim_{\lambda\to 0}\frac{r_{\lambda}v_{\lambda}}{r_{\lambda}}=\lim_{\lambda\to 0}\frac{(s_{\lambda}-t_{\lambda})\dot{\gamma_{\lambda}}(t_{\lambda})+o(s_{\lambda}-t_{\lambda})}{s_{\lambda}-t_{\lambda}},

thus
\lim v_{0}=[\dot{\gamma_{0}}(t_{0})]
and the map is indeed continious.

The derivative of
\frac{d}{d\lambda}|_{\lambda=0}\Phi(c(\lambda))
is
\frac{d}{d\lambda}|_{\lambda=0}(s_{\lambda}-t_{\lambda})\cdot\dot{\gamma_{0}}(t)
is smooth in
\lambda
, and the same is for higher derivatives.
∎

Proof of Lemma
6.3
.

Degree of
\operatorname{ev}^{(2)}
equals degree of
\Phi
.

Let
(m,[v])\in P_{0}
be the point on the exceptional locus of
\Bl_{\Delta_{M}}(M\times M)
. It has exactly two preimages under
\Phi
. We need to check that the differential of
\Phi
is surjective at these preimages. This would mean that that
\Phi
is a 2-to-1 cover in a neighbourhood of
(m,[v])
. Since the number of preimages under the map
\operatorname{ev}^{(2)}
is constant on a dense open subset of
M\times M
, this would be enough.

Let
(\gamma,t,s)\in\Delta_{X_{\bullet}}
be a point from preimage of
(m,[v])
. The tangent space
T_{(\gamma,t,s)}(X_{\bullet}\times_{X}X_{\bullet})
is generated by the susbspace
T_{(\gamma,t,s)}\Delta_{X_{\bullet}}
and two vectors
\frac{\partial\gamma}{\partial t}
and
\frac{\partial\gamma}{\partial s}
(there is exactly one linear relation, namely
\frac{\partial\gamma}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial\gamma}{\partial s}\in T_{(\gamma,t,s)}\Delta_{X_{\bullet}}
).

The differential
d\Phi
is an isomorphism on the subspace
T_{(\gamma,t,s)}\Delta_{X_{\bullet}}
since there it coincides with
d\operatorname{vel}
. At the same time,
d\Phi(\frac{\partial\gamma}{\partial t})
and
d\Phi(\frac{\partial\gamma}{\partial s})
are non-zero because the evaluation map is immersive on
\gamma
. By dimension count
d\Phi
is surjecive at point
(\gamma,t,s)
.

∎

6.2.
Hirzebruch surfaces

Our goal is classification of holomorphic flow-like and flow-like real families parametrised by compact complex surfaces. It turns out that a base of such family necessary belongs to a certain kin of projective surfaces known as
Hirzebruch surfaces
. We briefly recall their main properties. The primary reference is
[
2
, Chapter V, Section 4]
.

Definition 9
.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}}(-1)
denote the anti-tautological bundle on
\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}
and
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}}(-n)
its
n
-th tensor power. The
n
-th Hirzebruch surface
is
\mathbf{F}_{n}:=\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}}(-n))
.

The surface
\mathbf{F}_{n}
is a smooth projective surface that is an holomorphic
\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}
-bundle over
\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}
. All Hirzebruch surfaces are rational. The surface
\mathbf{F}_{0}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}
. For
n\neq\pm m
the surfaces
\mathbf{F}_{n}
and
\mathbf{F}_{m}
are birational, but not biholomorphic. They are homeomorphic as smooth manifolds if and only if
n
and
m
have the same parity. The surface
\mathbf{F}_{1}
is isomorphic to the blow-up of
\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{2}
in a point.

There is a section
\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}\to\Sigma_{n}
that is given by sending a point to the fibre of of the line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}}(-n)
. Its image
\Sigma
is an holomorphic rational curve with self-intersection
[\Sigma]^{2}=-n
. It is the only irreducible holomorphic curve on
\mathbf{F}_{n}
with negative self-intersection.

Since
\mathbf{F}_{n}
is rational,
H^{1}(\mathbf{F}_{n},\mathbb{Z})=H^{3}(\mathbf{F}_{n},\mathbb{Z})=0
. The group
H^{2}(\mathbf{F}_{n},\mathbb{Z})
is generated by the class
f
of the fibre of the map
\mathbf{F}_{n}\to\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}
and the class
s
of
\Sigma
. The intersection pairing in these generators is given by the matrix

\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&-n\end{pmatrix}.

Vice versa, every rational surface with
b_{2}=2
is a Hirzebruch surface.

Recall that on a smooth projective surface the intersection number of two distinct complex curves is always non-negative.

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
\mathbf{F}_{n}
be a Hirzebruch surface, and
D\subseteq\mathbf{F}_{n}
a smooth curve. Suppose that
0<[D]^{2}\leq 2
. Then either
n=0
and
[D]=(f+s)
or
n=2
and
[D]=(2f+s)
.

Proof.

Let
[D]=(af+bs)
. First of all,
a>0
, otherwise
[D]^{2}=b^{2}s^{2}=-b^{2}n^{2}
is negative.

Since
D
is smooth and distinct from
\Sigma
, the product
[D].[\Sigma]
is non-negative. Thus

(af+bs)\cdot s=a-nb\geq 0,

which implies
a\geq nb
.

Observe that
b>0
(otherwise
[D]^{2}=0
). Therefore we conclude that
a\geq n
.

At the same time,

[D]^{2}=(af+bs)^{2}=2ab-nb^{2}=(2a-bn)b.

If
[D]^{2}=1
, then
b=1
Thus
b=1
or
b=2
. If latter, the right-hand side is divisible by
4
. Thus
b=1
, and
a=\frac{n}{2}+1\geqslant n
. Therefore
n=0,a=1
or
n=2,a=2
.
∎

6.3.
Flow-like and real flow-like families

In this subsection, we prove our classification of flow-like and real flow-like families of knots.

Theorem 6.6
.

Suppose
\mathcal{X}
is a flow-like compact holomorphic family of knots in surface of knots in
(M,[g])
. Then the base
X
of
\mathcal{X}
is biholomorphic to either
\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}
and
M
is diffeomorphic to a lens space.

Proof.

The flow-like condition guarantees that the velocity map
\operatorname{vel}\colon X_{\bullet}\to\mathbf{S}M
is a diffeomorphism.

Each pinned curve
D_{m}
is mapped isomorphically to
S_{m}\simeq\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}
via the velocity map. Thus, all the pinned curves are rational. This implies that
X
is rationally connected, and therefore rational by the Enriques–Kodaira classification
[
2
, Chapter VI]
. In particular,
\pi_{1}(X)
is trivial.

The exact sequence of homotopy groups for the fibration
X_{\bullet}\to X
reads:

(4)

0\to\pi_{2}(X_{\bullet})\to\pi_{2}(X)\xrightarrow{c_{1}}\pi_{1}(S^{1})\to\pi_{1}(X_{\bullet})\to 1.

We treat separately two cases, depending on whether
c_{1}=0
or not.

Case 1.

If the fibration admits a trivialisation,
c_{1}=0
and
\pi_{1}(X_{\bullet})=\mathbb{Z}
. Flow-likeness implies that
X_{\bullet}\cong M\times S^{2}
, hence
\pi_{1}(M)=\mathbb{Z}
and
M=S^{2}\times S^{1}
(see e.g.
[
10
, Proposition 3.4]
). Let
D_{m}\subset X
be a pinned curve and
(D_{m})_{\bullet}
its preimage in
X_{\bullet}
. Then
(D_{m})_{\bullet}
is also isomorphic to
S^{2}\times S^{1}
and
\operatorname{ev}\colon(D_{m})_{\bullet}\to M
is surjective. It is moreover of non-zero degree, since the Radon transform of the volume form on
M
integrates positively over
D_{m}
. At the same time, the sphere
\widetilde{D_{m}}\subset(D_{m})_{\bullet}
that corresponds to the natural section
\gamma\mapsto\gamma^{-1}(m)
is mapped to a point. Thus,
\operatorname{ev}_{*}
is not surjective on
H^{2}
, and by Poincaré duality
\operatorname{ev}^{*}
is not injective on
H^{2}
. Together with formula

f_{*}f^{*}\alpha=\deg(f)\alpha

for any
\alpha\in H^{\bullet}(M,\mathbb{Q})
this leads to a contradiction.

Case 2.
In this case,
H_{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})\cong\pi_{2}(X)\cong\pi_{2}(S^{2}\times S^{1}\times S^{2})=\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. A rational surface with
b_{2}=2
is a Hirzebruch surface, see
[
2
, Chapter VI]
.

If the fibration has degree
d\neq 0
, it follows that
\pi_{1}(M)\cong\mathbb{Z}/d
, and thus
M
is a lens space by Perelman- Hamilton solution of Thurston Geometrisation Conjecture. In this case,
H_{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})\cong\pi_{2}(X)\cong\pi_{2}(X_{\bullet})\oplus\operatorname{ker}c_{1}\cong\pi_{2}(M\times S^{2})\oplus\mathbb{Z}\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Thus
b_{2}(X)=2
regardless.

For a generic
m
, the pinned curve
D_{m}
is a smooth curve with self-intersection equal to
2
(Lemma
6.3
). By Proposition
6.5
there are only two cases: either
X\cong\mathbf{F}_{0}\cong\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}
, or
X\cong\mathbf{F}_{2}
.

Suppose
X\cong\mathbf{F}_{1}
. Then
[D_{m}]=(2f+s)
and
[\Sigma].[D_{m}]=s(2f+s)=0
. At the same time, this number equals the class of the fibre
\operatorname{ev}^{-1}(m)
for the evaluation map of the family
\Sigma_{\bullet}\to M
. This again contradicts this map having non-zero degree.
∎

Theorem 6.7
.

Suppose
X
is a flow-like real holomorphic surface of knots in
(M,[g])
. Then
M
is diffeomorphic to either
S^{3}
or
\mathbb{R}\mathbf{P}^{3}
,
X
is biholomorphic to
\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}
,
[g]
is the conformal class of the round metric, and
X
is a surface of geodesics for some round metric in this class.

Proof.

From the exact sequence (
4
) one sees that the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(M)\cong\pi_{1}(X_{\bullet})
is generated by that of the (image of the) fibre, that is, the class of the knot. In particular, if
X
is real, this class equals minus itself, as the knots passing along the vectors
v
and
-v
at one point
m
are homotopic and differ only by the orientation. This implies that either
\pi_{1}(M)=0
(and thus
M\cong S^{3}
) or
\pi_{1}(M)=\mathbb{Z}/2
(and
M\cong\mathbb{R}\mathbf{P}^{3}
).

We know that
X\cong\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}
or
X\cong\Sigma_{2}
. Any fixed-point-free involution on the Hirzebruch surface
\Sigma_{2}
preserves its negative-square section
\Gamma
, hence in the latter case
\mathcal{X}|_{\Gamma}
is also a real family. The number of knots in this family which pass through a generic point
m\in M
is given by the intersection number
[\Gamma].[D_{m}]=s(2f+s)=0
, which contradicts Proposition
5.2
.

Thus
X\cong\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}
is the only possible case. Consider the embedding
\varphi\colon X\to\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{3}
via the linear system of the pinned curves. It realises
X
as a smooth real quadric without real points inside a complex projective space with the standard real structure. Let
(V,q)
be the corresponding real quadratic space (
q
is only defined up to a scalar multiple, and thus may be considered positive definite). Define the map
\delta\colon M\to\mathbf{P}(V^{*})
by sending
m\in M
to the real plane which cuts out the corresponding pinned curve
D_{m}\subset X
. It is a diffeomorphism for
M\cong\mathbb{R}\mathbf{P}^{3}
and a double cover for
M\cong S^{3}
. If
M\supset\gamma\in X
is a knot from our family,
\delta(\gamma)\subset\mathbf{P}(V^{*})
is the set of all real planes in
V
which pass through the point
\varphi(\gamma)
, that is, a projective line in
\mathbf{P}(V^{*})
. The quadratic form
q
induces a metric
\tilde{q}
on
\mathbf{P}(V^{*})
(resp. its double cover) for which the family of straight lines (resp. great circles) is a surface of knots. The standard construction identifies this surface with
\mathbf{P}\{q(v,v)=0\}=\varphi(X)
, and, if the orientation on
V
is chosen correctly, the line
\delta(\gamma)
goes to the point
\gamma
.

Thus we are in the following situation: there exists a flow-like family of knots
\mathcal{X}
in
M
, and two metrics
g
and
\delta^{*}\tilde{q}
, for both of which this family is an holomorphic surface. For both complex structures
I_{g}
and
I_{\delta^{*}\tilde{q}}
, the pinned curves are holomorphic. In particular, these complex structures have infinitely many common complex lines at each point, which means that
I_{g}=\pm I_{\delta^{*}\tilde{q}}
. Flow-likeness implies that the pinned curves are holomorphically identified with
ST^{*}_{m}M
, on which the complex structure comes from the conformal class of
g_{m}
(resp.
(\delta^{*}\tilde{q})_{m}
). The conformality of the identity map between these means that the metrics
g_{m}
and
\delta^{*}\tilde{q}|_{m}
are homothetic, meaning that
\delta^{*}\tilde{q}\in[g]
.
∎

Remark 10
.

LeBrun proved
[
15
, Section 4]
that one can reconstruct a conformal structure on a Riemannian threefold from certain data on its CR twistor space—namely, a CR structure, its fibration by holomorphic
\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}
s, an anti-CR holomorphic involution preserving the fibration, and a distribution which LeBrun calls the “CR contact structure” (it is an odd-dimensional counterpart of the holomorphic contact structure on the Atiyah–Hitchin–Singer twistor space). In principle, one may employ this theorem to prove the above result. In particular, it explains why the assumption of the surface being preserved by the real structure on the space of knots is crucial.

Remark 11
.

If our definition of an holomorphic family were slightly more liberal, it would not be possible to exclude
\mathbf{F}_{2}
the way we did: one may happen that
\Sigma\subset\mathbf{F}_{2}
is contracted into a point. There exists an embedding of
\mathbf{F}_{2}\setminus\Sigma
into the space of round circles in
S^{3}
(it is the once-pinned locus inside the non-compact complex threefold of all circles, or, equivalently, the space of straight lines in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
). If one allows circles to degenerate into points, this would be the map from the Hirzebruch surface to a space of knots which contracts
\Sigma
into a point, much like the standard map
\mathbf{F}_{2}\to\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{3}
whose image is a quadratic cone does.

We end with a natural question that remains unanswered.

Question 1
.

Suppose that
(M,[g])
admits a compact holomorphic family of knots of complex dimension
2
. Does it mean that
M
is a sphere or
\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{3}
and
[g]
is the class of conformal metric? More generally, are there other examples of compact complex surfaces of knots except for those constructed in Proposition
3.4
?
