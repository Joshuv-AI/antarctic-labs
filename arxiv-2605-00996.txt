Introduction

We begin with some notation. For integers
a\leq b
, let

[a,b]:=\{a,a+1,\ldots,b\}
and
[b]:=[1,b].

For a set
X
and an integer
k
, we write
2^{X}
for the power set of
X
,
{X\choose k}
for the family of all
k
-element subsets of
X
, and
{X\choose\leq k}
for the family of all subsets of
X
of size at most
k
.

A
matching
is a collection of pairwise disjoint sets. For a family
\mathcal{F}
, let
\nu(\mathcal{F})
denote the maximum size of a matching contained in
\mathcal{F}
. The main object of this paper is the quantity

e(n,s):=\max\bigl\{|\mathcal{F}|:\mathcal{F}\subset 2^{[n]},\ \nu(\mathcal{F})<s\bigr\}.

This parameter was introduced by Erdős and Kleitman in the 1960s (see
[
7
]
). It was studied further in a series of papers by Frankl and the first author
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
, as well as in our earlier paper
[
9
]
. For a detailed and up-to-date account of the history of the problem and its connection to the Erdős Matching Conjecture
[
1
]
, we refer the reader to
[
9
]
.

A basic feature of the problem is that the behaviour of
e(n,s)
depends strongly on the residue class of
n
modulo
s
. Accordingly, we write

n=ms+c,\qquad 1\leq c\leq s,

and throughout the paper set

\ell:=s-c.

We now introduce four candidate extremal families avoiding
s
-matchings:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(m,s,\ell)

\displaystyle:=\{P\in 2^{[n]}:|P|+|P\cap[\ell-1]|\geq m+1\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{\prime}(m,s,\ell)

\displaystyle:=\bigcup_{i=m+1}^{n}{[n]\choose i}\ \cup\ {[m\ell-1]\choose m},

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(m,s,\ell)

\displaystyle:=\{P\in 2^{[n]}:|P|+|P\cap[ms-c-1]|\geq 2m\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(m,s,\ell)

\displaystyle:=\{P\in 2^{[n]}:|P\cap[ms-1]|\geq m\}.

The families
\mathcal{P}(m,s,\ell)
and
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}(m,s,\ell)
contain every set of size at least
m+1
, and their
m
-uniform layers are precisely the two standard candidate extremal families in the Erdős Matching Conjecture for
m
-uniform families avoiding an
\ell
-matching.

The third family
\mathcal{Q}(m,s,\ell)
can be thought of as another ‘clique’ counterpart of the family
\mathcal{P}(m,s,\ell)
, the first being
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}(m,s,\ell)
. It has a larger clique on the
m
’th layer in exchange for fewer sets on the upper layers.

The family
\mathcal{W}(m,s,\ell)
is obtained by applying the
doubling
operation
c+1
times to the extremal family for
n=ms-1
. Doubling turns a family
\mathcal{F}\subset 2^{[n]}
into the family
\mathcal{G}\subset 2^{[n+1]}
defined by

\mathcal{G}:=\{A\subset[n+1]:A\cap[n]\in\mathcal{F}\}.

This operation preserves the matching number. Kleitman proved in
[
7
]
that
e(ms-1,s),e(ms,s)
is attained on
\mathcal{W}(m,s,\ell)
. (In the former case, it is simply the family of all sets of size at least
m
.)

In the next section we discuss the sizes of these four families and show that each of them indeed avoids an
s
-matching.

For every pair
(n,s)
for which the value of
e(n,s)
is currently known, the extremal family is one of the four constructions above. In particular, in
[
9
]
we showed that for
n\leq 3s
each of these four families occur as extremal examples. Roughly speaking, when
c
is close to
s
, the extremal construction is of type
\mathcal{P}(m,s,\ell)
, whereas for small
c
the known extremal examples are of the type
\mathcal{Q}(m,s,\ell)
or
\mathcal{W}(m,s,\ell)
. The family
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}(m,s,\ell)
appears as the extremum somewhere in the middle.

This leads to the following general conjecture.

Conjecture 1
.

For all
n,s
,

e(n,s)=\max\bigl\{|\mathcal{P}(m,s,\ell)|,\ |\mathcal{P}^{\prime}(m,s,\ell)|,\ |\mathcal{Q}(m,s,\ell)|,\ |\mathcal{W}(m,s,\ell)|\bigr\}.

In
[
4
]
, the authors confirmed it for
s\geq m\ell+3\ell+3
, showing that
\mathcal{P}(m,s,\ell)
is extremal. The main result of this paper confirms the conjecture ‘on the other end’: for fixed
m,c
and all sufficiently large
s
.

Theorem 1
.

For every
m\geq 1
and every
c\geq 1
there exists
s_{0}
such that for all
s\geq s_{0}
,

e(n,s)=|\mathcal{Q}(m,s,\ell)|.

Moreover,

•

if
c\geq 2
, then
\mathcal{Q}(m,s,\ell)
is the unique shifted family attaining the maximum;

•

if
c=1
, then
\mathcal{Q}(m,s,\ell)
and
\mathcal{W}(m,s,\ell)
are the only shifted families attaining the maximum.

This result can be seen as a non-uniform analogue of the results on the Erdős Matching Conjecture in the regime when the clique is extremal, see
[
2
,
8
]
.

We recall the notion of shiftedness at the beginning of Section
3
. Throughout the paper, asymptotic notation (such as
O(),\Theta()
) is used with
m
and
c
fixed and
s,n\to\infty
. Since the value of
e(ms+c,s)
is already known for
m=1
by
[
4
]
, from this point on we assume
m\geq 2
.

Finally, we note that when
c=1
, the families
\mathcal{Q}(m,s,\ell)
and
\mathcal{W}(m,s,\ell)
coincide. This will be shown in the next section, where we also discuss further properties of the extremal families.

2
Extremal families

The families that we consider contain almost all subsets of
[n]
. Thus, rather than computing the sizes of the families themselves, it is more convenient to compute the sizes of their complements. For a family
\mathcal{F}\subset 2^{[n]}
we denote
\overline{\mathcal{F}}:=2^{[n]}\setminus\mathcal{F}.

Simple computations show that

\displaystyle|\overline{\mathcal{P}(m,s,\ell)}|

\displaystyle=\Theta(s^{m}),

\displaystyle|\overline{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}(m,s,\ell)}|

\displaystyle=((m+1)c+2){n\choose m-1}+O(s^{m-2}),

\displaystyle|\overline{\mathcal{Q}(m,s,\ell)}|

\displaystyle=(2c+2){n\choose m-1}+O(s^{m-2}),

\displaystyle|\overline{\mathcal{W}(m,s,\ell)}|

\displaystyle=2^{c+1}{n\choose m-1}+O(s^{m-2}).

The following simple observation is useful for proving upper bounds on the matching number.

Lemma 1
.

Let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy
x_{i}\geq 0
and
\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}<s
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family such that for every
F\in\mathcal{F}
we have
\sum_{i\in F}x_{i}\geq 1
. Then
\nu(\mathcal{F})<s
.

Proof.

We argue indirectly. Let
F_{1},\ldots,F_{s}
be an
s
-matching in
\mathcal{F}
. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\geq\sum_{i\in\bigcup_{j=1}^{s}F_{j}}x_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{s}\sum_{i\in F_{j}}x_{i}\geq s.

This contradicts the assumption
\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}<s
.
∎

Corollary 1
.

The families
\mathcal{P}(m,s,\ell)
,
\mathcal{P^{\prime}}(m,s,\ell)
,
\mathcal{Q}(m,s,\ell)
, and
\mathcal{W}(m,s,\ell)
do not contain an
s
-matching.

Proof.

We apply Lemma
1
. Specifically, we choose the weights
x_{i}
as follows:

\mathcal{P}(m,s,\ell)
: put
x_{i}=\frac{2}{m+1}
for
i\leq\ell-1
and
x_{i}=\frac{1}{m+1}
for
i\geq\ell
;

\mathcal{P}^{\prime}(m,s,\ell)
: put
x_{i}=\frac{1}{m}
for
i\leq m\ell-1
and
x_{i}=\frac{1}{m+1}
for
i\geq m\ell
;

\mathcal{Q}(m,s,\ell)
: put
x_{i}=\frac{1}{m}
for
i\leq ms-c-1
and
x_{i}=\frac{1}{2m}
for
i\geq ms-c
;

\mathcal{W}(m,s,\ell)
: put
x_{i}=\frac{1}{m}
for
i\leq ms-1
and
x_{i}=0
for
i\geq ms
.

∎

Lemma 2
.

We have
|\mathcal{Q}(m,s,s-1)|=|\mathcal{W}(m,s,s-1)|
.

Proof.

We will calculate
|\overline{\mathcal{Q}(m,s,s-1)}|
and
|\overline{\mathcal{W}(m,s,s-1)}|
.
Since
\mathcal{W}(m,s,s-1)
is obtained from
{[ms-1]\choose\geq m}
by iterating the doubling operation twice, we have

|\overline{\mathcal{W}(m,s,s-1)}|=4\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}{ms-1\choose i}.

(1)

To calculate
|\overline{\mathcal{Q}(m,s,s-1)}|
, note that

\overline{\mathcal{Q}(m,s,s-1)}=\{F\in 2^{[sm+1]}:2|F|-|F\cap\{ms-1,ms,ms+1\}|<2m\}.

Thus,
\overline{\mathcal{Q}(m,s,s-1)}
consists of

•

all sets of size less than
m
. There are
\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}{sm+1\choose i}
such sets;

•

sets of size
m
that intersect
\{sm-1,sm,sm+1\}
. There are
{sm+1\choose m}-{sm-2\choose m}
such sets;

•

sets of size
m+1
that contain
\{sm-1,sm,sm+1\}
. There are
{sm-2\choose m-2}
such sets.

Therefore,

|\overline{\mathcal{Q}(m,s,s-1)}|=\sum_{i=0}^{m}{sm+1\choose i}-{sm-2\choose m}+{sm-2\choose m-2}.

(2)

To prove that (
2
) is equal to (
1
), we express
{sm+1\choose i}
in terms of
{sm-1\choose i}
:

{sm+1\choose i}={sm-1\choose i}+2{sm-1\choose i-1}+{sm-1\choose i-2},

where, as usual, we set
{n\choose k}=0
for
k<0
. Summing over
i\in\{0,\ldots,m\}
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{m}{sm+1\choose i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{m}{sm-1\choose i}+2\sum_{i=1}^{m}{sm-1\choose i-1}+\sum_{i=2}^{m}{sm-1\choose i-2}

\displaystyle=4\sum_{i=0}^{m-2}{sm-1\choose i}+3{sm-1\choose m-1}+{sm-1\choose m}

\displaystyle=4\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}{sm-1\choose i}-{sm-1\choose m-1}+{sm-1\choose m}.

Finally, substituting this into (
2
), we obtain

\displaystyle|\overline{\mathcal{Q}(m,s,s-1)}|

\displaystyle=4\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}{sm-1\choose i}-{sm-1\choose m-1}+{sm-1\choose m}-{sm-2\choose m}+{sm-2\choose m-2}

\displaystyle=4\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}{sm-1\choose i}+\left({sm-1\choose m}-{sm-2\choose m}\right)-\left({sm-1\choose m-1}-{sm-2\choose m-2}\right)

\displaystyle=4\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}{sm-1\choose i}+{sm-2\choose m-1}-{sm-2\choose m-1}

\displaystyle=4\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}{sm-1\choose i}=|\overline{\mathcal{W}(m,s,s-1)}|.

∎

3
Overview of the proof

Let us recall the notion of shifting. We will denote a set
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\}
by
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})
if
a_{1}<\ldots<a_{k}
and we want to emphasize that its elements are listed in increasing order. We say that a set
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})
can be shifted to a set
(b_{1},\ldots,b_{k})
if
a_{i}\geq b_{i}
for all
i\in[k]
. A family
\mathcal{F}
is called shifted if, whenever
A\in\mathcal{F}
, we also have
B\in\mathcal{F}
for every
B
such that
A
can be shifted to
B
. It is well known
[
3
]
that the maximum size of a family of subsets of an
n
-element set avoiding an
s
-matching is attained by a shifted family. Therefore, it is enough to consider shifted families.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subset 2^{[n]}
be a family of subsets of
[n]
.
We denote by
\mathcal{F}^{(i)}
the
i
-th layer of
\mathcal{F}
, that is, the family
\mathcal{F}\cap{[n]\choose i}
. Let
y_{\mathcal{F}}(i)
be the number of
i
-element sets, which are not in
\mathcal{F}
, that is,
y_{\mathcal{F}}(i)={n\choose i}-|\mathcal{F}^{(i)}|
.

The main part of the proof is the analysis of
y(m)
and
y(m+1)
. In Section
4
we use a counting argument to prove that
y(m)+y(m+1)
is minimized by the family
\mathcal{Q}(m,s,\ell)
. Moreover, we establish a stability statement showing that if
\mathcal{F}
is a shifted family avoiding an
s
-matching and
\mathcal{F}\nsubseteq\mathcal{Q}(m,s,\ell)
, then

y_{\mathcal{F}}(m)+y_{\mathcal{F}}(m+1)\geq y_{\mathcal{Q}(m,s,\ell)}(m)+y_{\mathcal{Q}(m,s,\ell)}(m+1)+\Theta(s^{m-1}).

Since the family
\mathcal{Q}(m,s,\ell)
contains all sets of size at least
m+2
, except for
O(s^{m-2})
sets, this stability result immediately implies that
\mathcal{Q}(m,s,\ell)
is the largest family avoiding an
s
-matching among all families that do not contain sets of size exactly
m-1
. In Section
5
we prove that any family containing an
(m-1)
-element set is smaller than
\mathcal{Q}(m,s,\ell)
. To this end, we first use the stability result for
y(m)+y(m+1)
to show that
e(n,s)
cannot be much larger than
|\mathcal{Q}(m,s,\ell)|
. Then, assuming that a family
\mathcal{F}
with
\nu(\mathcal{F})<s
contains some
(m-1)
-element set
X
, we consider the family
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=\mathcal{F}\cap 2^{[n]\setminus X}
and using the stability statement conclude that
\overline{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}
is large, which in turn implies that
\mathcal{F}
is smaller than
\mathcal{Q}(m,s,\ell)
.

4
Missing sets on the layers
m
and
m+1

We have the following for the prospective extremal family:

y_{\mathcal{Q}(m,s,\ell)}(m)+y_{\mathcal{Q}(m,s,\ell)}(m+1)=(2c+1){n\choose m-1}+O(s^{m-2}).

In this section, we prove a stability theorem for
y(m)+y(m+1)
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
n=ms+c
,
m\geq 2
. Let
\mathcal{F}\subset 2^{[n]}
be a shifted family and
\nu(\mathcal{F})<s
. Then

y_{\mathcal{F}}(m)+y_{\mathcal{F}}(m+1)\geq(2c+2){n\choose m-1}+O(s^{m-2})

if one of the following holds:

1.

c\geq 2
and
\mathcal{F}\nsubseteq\mathcal{Q}(m,s,\ell)
;

2.

c=1
,
\mathcal{F}\nsubseteq\mathcal{Q}(m,s,\ell)
, and
\mathcal{F}\nsubseteq\mathcal{W}(m,s,\ell)
.

Note that the assumption
\mathcal{F}\nsubseteq\mathcal{W}(m,s,\ell)
in the second case of the theorem is crucial, since
\mathcal{W}(m,s,\ell)\nsubseteq\mathcal{Q}(m,s,\ell)
and

y_{\mathcal{W}(m,s,s-1)}(m)+y_{\mathcal{W}(m,s,s-1)}(m+1)=3{n\choose m-1}+O(s^{m-2}).

Theorem
2
immediately implies that for
c\geq 2
and
s\geq s_{0}(m,c)
the family
\mathcal{Q}(m,s,\ell)
has the smallest possible value of
y(m)+y(m+1)
among families avoiding an
s
-matching. However, Theorem
2
does not immediately imply Theorem
1
, even for
c\geq 2
, because
\overline{\mathcal{Q}(m,s,\ell)}
contains
{n\choose m-1}
sets of size
m-1
.

To prove Theorem
2
, we bound
y(m)
and
y(m+1)
separately in terms of the value
d(\mathcal{F})
, defined below.

Definition 1
.

The deletion number
d(\mathcal{F})
is the minimum number of elements of the ground set
[n]
that must be deleted in order to avoid an
\ell
-matching in
\mathcal{F}^{(m)}
. Formally,

d(\mathcal{F})=\min\Big\{|X|:\nu\Big(\mathcal{F}\cap{[n]\setminus X\choose m}\Big)<\ell\Big\}.

Note that if
\nu(\mathcal{F}^{(m)})<\ell
, then
d(\mathcal{F})=0
.

We will use the following simple lemma.

Lemma 3
.

If
\mathcal{F}
is shifted,
\ell\geq 1
, and
d(\mathcal{F})>d
, then for any integer
k
,
0\leq k\leq\frac{d}{m}
, and any
X\subset[d+m\ell]
with
|X|=d-km
, the family
\mathcal{F}\cap{[d+m\ell]\setminus X\choose m}
contains an
(\ell+k)
-matching.

Proof.

Assume that for some
X
of size
d-km
the family
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}:=\mathcal{F}\cap{[d+m\ell]\setminus X\choose m}
does not contain an
(\ell+k)
-matching. Take a largest matching
\mathcal{M}:=\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{\ell^{\prime}}\}
in
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
and consider the set

Y=X\cup X_{1}\cup\ldots\cup X_{k^{\prime}},

where
k^{\prime}=\min\{k,\ell^{\prime}\}
. Then, since
\mathcal{M}
is a largest matching, the family
\mathcal{F}\cap{[d+m\ell]\setminus Y\choose m}
does not contain an
\ell
-matching. At the same time,
|Y|\leq d
.

Now, if
\mathcal{F}\cap{[n]\setminus Y\choose m}
contains an
\ell
-matching, then, using the fact that
\mathcal{F}
is shifted, we can obtain an
\ell
-matching in
[d+m\ell]\setminus Y
. We conclude that
\mathcal{F}\cap{[n]\setminus Y\choose m}
does not contain an
\ell
-matching. This contradicts the assumption that
d(\mathcal{F})>d
.
∎

In the subsequent calculations we will use the following simple and standard estimate, which we state without proof.

Lemma 4
.

Let
n=ms+c
, where
m
and
c
are constant and
s\rightarrow\infty
. Then for any nonnegative integer functions
f(m,c)
and
g(m,c)
we have

{n-f(m,c)\choose m}-{n-f(m,c)-g(m,c)\choose m}=g(m,c){n\choose m-1}+O(n^{m-2}).

We will also use a result on the Erdős Matching Conjecture in the case
n\leq(k+\varepsilon(k))s
, namely, the following theorem due to Frankl from
[
2
]
.

Theorem 3
.

For every
k\geq 2
there exists a positive
\varepsilon(k)
such that for
ks\leq n\leq(k+\varepsilon(k))s
the family
{[ks-1]\choose k}
is the largest family in
{[n]\choose k}
without an
s
-matching.

The proof in
[
2
]
gives the bound
\varepsilon(k)=\frac{1}{2k^{2k+1}}
. Note that in
[
8
]
, Kolupaev and the first author obtained a much better dependence, namely
\varepsilon(k)=\frac{1}{100k}
for
k\geq 5
.

The next lemma bounds
y_{\mathcal{F}}(m)
in terms of
d(\mathcal{F})
.

Lemma 5
.

Let
m\geq 2
and
n=ms+c
. If
\mathcal{F}\subset 2^{[n]}
is a family with
d(\mathcal{F})\leq d
, then

y_{\mathcal{F}}(m)\geq((m+1)c-d+1){n\choose m-1}+O(s^{m-2}).

Proof.

Let
X
be a set of size
d
such that
\nu(\mathcal{F}\cap{[n]\setminus X\choose m})<\ell
, and let

\mathcal{F^{\prime}}=\mathcal{F}\cap{[n]\setminus X\choose m}.

Since
m
and
c
are constants and
s
tends to infinity, we may assume that

s>\frac{c(m+1)}{\varepsilon(m)}+c,

which is equivalent to
n<\ell(m+\varepsilon(m))
. Therefore, by Theorem
3
we get

|\mathcal{F^{\prime}}|\leq{m\ell-1\choose m}.

Since

\mathcal{F}^{(m)}\setminus\mathcal{F^{\prime}}\subset{[n]\choose m}\setminus{[n]\setminus X\choose m},

we obtain

|\mathcal{F}^{(m)}|\leq{n\choose m}-{n-d\choose m}+{m\ell-1\choose m}.

In particular,

|\mathcal{F}^{(m)}|\leq d{n\choose m-1}+{m\ell-1\choose m},

and by Lemma
4
,

\displaystyle y_{\mathcal{F}}(m)

\displaystyle\geq{n\choose m}-d{n\choose m-1}-{m\ell-1\choose m}

\displaystyle=\left({n\choose m}-{n-(m+1)c-1\choose m}\right)-d{n\choose m-1}

\displaystyle=((m+1)c-d+1){n\choose m-1}+O(s^{m-2}).

∎

Lemma
5
implies that if
d(\mathcal{F})<(m-1)c
, then

y_{\mathcal{F}}(m)\geq(2c+2){n\choose m-1}+O(s^{m-2}).

This bound alone is sufficient to prove Theorem
2
in this case. The next two lemmas allow us to lower bound the contribution of
y(m+1)
in the case when
d(\mathcal{F})>(m-1)c
and
d(\mathcal{F})=(m-1)c
, respectively.

Lemma 6
.

If
\mathcal{F}\subset 2^{[n]}
is a shifted family with
d(\mathcal{F})>(m-1)c
, then

y_{\mathcal{F}}(m+1)\geq(2c-1){n\choose m-1}+O(s^{m-2}).

Proof.

By Lemma
3
for
k=0
, for any

X\in{[m\ell+(m-1)c]\choose(m-1)c}={[ms-c]\choose(m-1)c},

the family
\mathcal{F}\cap{[ms-c]\setminus X\choose m}
contains an
\ell
-matching. For each
X
, fix one such matching
\pi_{X}
.

Put

\mathcal{G}:={[ms-c]\choose m-1}\times{[ms-c+1,n]\choose 2}.

Construct a random
s
-matching
\pi
in

\big(\mathcal{F}\cap{[ms-c]\choose m}\big)\cup\mathcal{G}

by the following procedure.

•

Choose
X\in{[ms-c]\choose(m-1)c}
uniformly at random.

•

Add
\pi_{X}
to
\pi
. Note that all sets added to
\pi
in this step belong to
\mathcal{F}
.

•

Choose a
c
-matching in
{X\choose m-1}\times{[ms-c+1,n]\choose 2}
uniformly at random and add it to
\pi
. Note that such matchings exist, since
|X|=(m-1)c
and
|[ms-c+1,n]|=2c
.

Let
\xi
be the number of sets from
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
in
\pi
. On the one hand,
\xi\geq 1
, since
\pi
is an
s
-matching and
\mathcal{F}
does not contain an
s
-matching. Therefore,
\mathbb{E}\xi\geq 1
. On the other hand, for any set
F\in\mathcal{G}
we have

\mathbb{P}[F\in\pi]=c{ms-c\choose m-1}^{-1}{2c\choose 2}^{-1}.

Therefore,

1\leq\mathbb{E}\xi=|\overline{\mathcal{F}}\cap\mathcal{G}|\cdot c{ms-c\choose m-1}^{-1}{2c\choose 2}^{-1}.

Combining these inequalities, we get

\begin{split}y_{\mathcal{F}}(m+1)&\geq|\overline{\mathcal{F}}\cap\mathcal{G}|\geq c^{-1}{ms-c\choose m-1}{2c\choose 2}\\
&=(2c-1){n-2c\choose m-1}=(2c-1){n\choose m-1}+O(s^{m-2}).\end{split}

∎

Proof of Theorem

In this section we prove the following stability version of Theorem

Theorem 4
.

Let

,

. Let
