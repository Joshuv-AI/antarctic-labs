Public Executive Summary
The project was led by the University of Wisconsin-Madison with Penn State and the University
of Illinois Chicago playing key roles. The objective of the project was to produce carbon-
negative concrete using cementitious materials generated by a carbon mineralization-based
direct air capture (DAC) process. The DAC process uses a novel aqueous carbonation cycle to
capture CO2 from the air at low cost. Simultaneously, the process upcycles industrial mineral
wastes as cementitious materials by enhancing their pozzolanic reactivity (ability to form
minerals that contribute to strength). The cementitious materials, storing the captured
atmospheric CO2 as solid carbonate (e.g., CaCO3), partially replace Portland cement as
supplementary cementitious material (SCM) or fully replace Portland cement via an alkali
activation cementation method. The resulting carbon-negative concrete offers enhanced
durability at equivalent strength and similar cost and can be formed into reusable building
components or recycled as carbon-negative aggregates at end of life.

Project Activities

The objective of this project was to develop a carbon-negative building material that fully
replaces Portland cement via alkali activation cementation. The resulting carbon-negative
concrete offers enhanced durability at equivalent strength and similar cost. A focus of the
project was to develop methods of addressing variability in the composition of feedstock
material, so that we can guarantee the performance of the concrete product (e.g., strength and
workability). We have developed a set of rapid tests that can be used to evaluate mineral waste
materials as feedstocks. We have also developed a machine learning model trained with data
from thousands of tests run during the project, which given a set of requirements, successfully
prescribes the concrete mixture that delivers the desired performance.
