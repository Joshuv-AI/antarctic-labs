Executive Summary of CRADA Work:

The National Renewable Energy Laboratory initiated a Prize with support from Argonne
National Laboratory (ANL), Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL), and Pacific Northwest
National Laboratory (PNNL), and sponsored by the U.S. Department of Energy Water Power
Technologies Office (DOE WPTO) to encourage ideas to reduce the time to commissioning for
PSH projects. As a result, nine finalists have been chosen to develop their concepts in advance of
the FAST Prize Pitch Contest to be held on October 7, 2019. The National Labs will provide
technical and business advisement to the noted FAST Prize finalists in preparation for this Pitch
Contest.

Summary of Research Results:

The intent of this project was to support Team Thompson, and the Southwest Research Institute
investigate the application of wastewater from fracking for accelerating the development of new
pumped-storage hydro (PSH) projects in anticipation of the Furthering Advancements to Shorten
the Time (FAST) to Commissioning for Pumped Storage Hydropower (PSH) Prize Pitch Contest
which was held on October 8, 2019. Specifically, National Laboratory staff assisted the
wastewater team by (1) helping to develop innovations to the PSH design, (2) improving the
organization and structure of the business case, and (3) preparing the designated pitch man to
address questions from the expert panel.

Task 1: Technical and business support provided by the National Laboratories to develop finalist
concepts in advance of Pitch Day. The technical advisement includes narrowing the scope of
concept, light economic feasibility analysis support, light modeling support, and connecting with
appropriate industry partners. Completed by October 7, 2019.

Task 1 Results: Technical and business support was provided by the team at the National
Renewable Energy Laboratory (NREL). This support included narrowing the scope of the
concept, conducting a light economic feasibility analysis, offering preliminary modeling
assistance, and facilitating connections with relevant industry partners. NREL helped refine the
project scope by focusing on closed-loop pumped storage hydropower (PSH) applications in
Texas and across the United States. The economic feasibility support involved developing a
conceptual schedule, identifying wastewater challenges, and gathering information on the power
sector in Texas and nationally. Modeling support included creating a conceptual model of a 20
MW PSH project with an efficiency of 85% and 300 feet of head. Finally, NREL facilitated
introductions to relevant industry partners, including Japan’s Yanbaru PSH and Australia’s North
Head Hydropower.

Task 2: Support with preparation of the Pitch Day materials including the technical volume and
the presentation and associated pitch. The business pitch support includes coaching and
preparation for Pitch Day and connecting with appropriate industry partners.

Task 2 Results: The NREL team assisted in preparing both a technical volume and a
presentation for Pitch Day. The technical volume included a problem statement, innovation
section, technical feasibility section, and market feasibility section. The problem statement
identified the challenge: closed-loop pumped storage hydropower (PSH) projects currently
appear less attractive compared to other investment opportunities in Texas and across the U.S.
To address this, the proposed innovation involved developing modular reservoirs, using
wastewater as a reservoir fill source, and outlining a conceptual project schedule. The Technical
Feasibility section covered simplified engineering and construction approaches, wastewater
management strategies, case studies of closed-loop hydropower, and mapping of West Texas
topography. The Market Feasibility section focused on Texas energy capacity, challenges related
