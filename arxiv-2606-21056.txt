On m m -order logarithmic Schrödinger operator
Jorge J. Betancor 1, Estefanía Dalmasso 2, Pablo Quijano 2, Lourdes Rodríguez-Mesa 1

Abstract
. In this paper we study the logarithm of order m m of the Schrödinger operator ℒ V \mathcal{L}_{V} in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , for certain nonnegative potentials V V . First, the operator log m ⁡ ℒ V \log^{m}\mathcal{L}_{V} , m ∈ ℕ m\in\mathbb{N} , is defined by using the spectral measure associated with the self-adjoint operator ℒ V \mathcal{L}_{V} on a suitable subspace of L 2 ​ ( ℝ d ) L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) . Then, the semigroup of operators { T t V } t > 0 \{T_{t}^{V}\}_{t>0} generated by ℒ V \mathcal{L}_{V} allows us to extend the definition of log m ⁡ ℒ V \log^{m}\mathcal{L}_{V} to a wider class of Lipschitz functions. By using logarithmic operators log m ⁡ ℒ V \log^{m}\mathcal{L}_{V} , m ∈ ℕ m\in\mathbb{N} , we prove Taylor expansions for the fractional powers ℒ V s \mathcal{L}_{V}^{s} and ℒ V − s \mathcal{L}_{V}^{-s} with respect to the order s ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) s\in(0,1) , where the convergence is understood in L p ​ ( ℝ d ) L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) , 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty .

In

, Chen and Weth defined the logarithmic Laplacian operator

(\log(-\Delta)f)(x)=\frac{d}{ds}(-\Delta)^{s}f(x)\big|_{s=0},

for every

, the space of

\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and established the following pointwise representation of
\log(-\Delta)

Theorem A

(
[
9

)
.

Let
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then,

\displaystyle(\log(-\Delta)f)(x)=c_{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{f(x)\mathcal{X}_{B(x,1)}(y)-f(y)}{|x-y|^{d}}dy+\alpha_{d}f(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where

, where
\gamma

\psi=\Gamma^{\prime}/\Gamma

Moreover, in
[
5

m

\mathcal{L}_{m}
, for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
, which is the operator with the symbol
(2\log|x|)^{m}
with respect to the Fourier transform. Motivated by the following well-known asymptotic expansion for
r>0
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

r^{s}=1+\sum_{n=1}^{m}\frac{s^{n}}{n!}(\log r)^{n}+o(s^{m}),\quad\mbox{ as }s\rightarrow 0^{+},

and using the logarithmic Laplacian of higher order, Chen
established asymptotic expansions for the fractional powers of the Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}
, (see
[

), and for the Riesz potentials
(-\Delta)^{-s}
, (see
[
5

.

In this paper we study the
m
-order logarithm of the Schrödinger operator
\mathcal{L}_{V}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
V
is a nonnegative potential (the first order logarithm of
\mathcal{L}_{V}
has been analyzed in
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
). Our objective is to obtain asymptotic expansions for the positive and negative fractional powers of the Schrödinger operator
\mathcal{L}_{V}
through its higher order logarithmic operators.

We consider a nonnegative measurable function
V\in L^{1}_{\textup{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and define the following closed and positive sesquilinear form
T
by

T(f,g)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla f(x)\overline{\nabla g(x)}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V(x)f(x)\overline{g(x)}dx,\quad f,g\in D(T),

where
D(T)=\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\nabla f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\mbox{ and }\sqrt{V}f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\}
. Note that
D(T)
is dense in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. According to
[
26
, Theorem VIII.15]
, there exists a self-adjoint operator
\mathcal{L}_{V}:D(\mathcal{L}_{V})=D(T)\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\langle\mathcal{L}_{V}f,g\rangle=T(f,g),\quad f,g\in D(\mathcal{L}_{V}).

Here
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the usual inner product in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Note that
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset D(\mathcal{L}_{V})
, and
\mathcal{L}_{V}\phi=(-\Delta+V)\phi
, for every
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Since
V\geq 0
, the spectrum
\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{V})
of
\mathcal{L}_{V}
is contained in
[0,\infty)
. Moreover, if
V(x)>0
, a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
0
is not an eigenvalue of
\mathcal{L}_{V}
. We assume in the sequel that
V(x)>0
, a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then, there exists a unique spectral measure
E_{V}
whose support is contained in
\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{V})
such that

\mathcal{L}_{V}f=\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda dE_{V}(\lambda)f,\quad f\in D(\mathcal{L}_{V}),

and
D(\mathcal{L}_{V})=\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{2}d\mu_{f,f}^{V}(\lambda)<\infty\}
. Here, for every
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\mu_{f,g}^{V}
denotes the Borel measure defined by
\mu_{f,g}^{V}(U)=\langle E_{V}(U)f,g\rangle
, for each Borel set
U\subset(0,\infty)
.

If
\Phi
is a Borel measurable function in
\mathbb{R}
, we define the operator
\Phi(\mathcal{L}_{V})
by

\Phi(\mathcal{L}_{V})f=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi(\lambda)dE_{V}(\lambda)f,\quad f\in D(\Phi(\mathcal{L}_{V})),

where
D(\Phi(\mathcal{L}_{V}))=\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\int_{0}^{\infty}|\Phi(\lambda)|^{2}d\mu_{f,f}^{V}(\lambda)<\infty\}
. In particular, we define, for every
s\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
, the
s
-power
\mathcal{L}_{V}^{s}
of
\mathcal{L}_{V}
by

\mathcal{L}_{V}^{s}f=\int^{\infty}_{0}\lambda^{s}dE_{V}(\lambda)f,\quad f\in D(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{s}),

being
D(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{s})=\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{2s}d\mu_{f,f}^{V}(\lambda)<\infty\}
, and the logarithmic Schrödinger operator
\log\mathcal{L}_{V}
by

(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})f=\int_{0}^{\infty}\log\lambda dE_{V}(\lambda)f,\quad f\in D(\log\mathcal{L}_{V}),

where
D(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})=\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\int_{0}^{\infty}|\log\lambda|^{2}d\mu_{f,f}^{V}(\lambda)<\infty\}
.

For every
t>0
, we define the operator
T_{t}^{V}
by

T_{t}^{V}f=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}dE_{V}(\lambda)f,\quad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

The family
\{T_{t}^{V}\}_{t>0}
is a semigroup of contractions in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
whose infinitesimal generator is
\mathcal{L}_{V}
. For every
t>0
, there exists a measurable function
T_{t}^{V}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
such that

T_{t}^{V}(f)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}T_{t}^{V}(x,y)f(y)dy,\quad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(1.1)

The integral representation (
1.1
) allows us to extend, for every
t>0
, the operator
T_{t}^{V}
from
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as a contraction from
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
into itself, for each
1\leq p<\infty
. Thus,
\{T_{t}^{V}\}_{t>0}
is a semigroup of contractions in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
1\leq p<\infty
.

We denote by
T_{t}(z)
,
t>0
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the Euclidean heat kernel, that is,
T_{t}(z)=(4\pi t)^{-d/2}e^{-|z|^{2}/(4t)}
. By the Feynman-Kac formula we have

0\leq T_{t}^{V}(x,y)\leq T_{t}(x-y),\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mbox{ and }t>0.

(1.2)

In
[
1
, Theorem 1.2]
, a pointwise representation of the logarithmic operator
\log\mathcal{L}_{V}
for certain potentials
V
was established. We say that
V\in L^{1}_{\textup{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfies the
q
-reverse Hölder inequality, with
1<q<\infty
, denoted by
V\in\textup{RH}_{q}
, when there exists
C>0
such that

\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}V(x)^{q}dx\right)^{1/q}\leq\frac{C}{|B|}\int_{B}V(x)dx,

for every ball
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Functions in
\textup{RH}_{q}
appeared in the studies of Gehring
[
20
]
and Coifman and Fefferman
[
12
]
. It is common for the condition
V\in\textup{RH}_{q}
to arise when harmonic analysis is developed in the Schrödinger setting. This can be seen, for instance, in
[
3
,
4
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
19
,
28
,
30
]
.

Suppose in the sequel that
d\geq 3
and
V\in\textup{RH}_{q}
with
q>d/2
. We consider (see
[
27
]
and
[
28

) the function
\rho:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
given by

\rho(x)=\sup\left\{r>0:\frac{1}{r^{d-2}}\int_{B(x,r)}V(y)dy\leq 1\right\}.

This function
\rho
, also called the critical radius function, plays an important role in the study of harmonic analysis associated with Schrödinger operators. It holds that
\rho(x)>0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
(see
[
28
, §1]
).

When
V\in\textup{RH}_{q}
, with
q>d/2
, the estimate (
1.2
) can be improved as follows (see
[
15

). For every
N\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
C_{N}>0
such that

0\leq T_{t}^{V}(x,y)\leq\frac{C_{N}}{t^{d/2}}e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{5t}}\left(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(y)}\right)^{-N},\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mbox{ and }t>0.

(1.3)

A pointwise representation for the logarithmic Schrödinger operator
\log\mathcal{L}_{V}
was established in
[
1
, Theorem 1.2]
.

Theorem B
.

Let
d\geq 3
and
q>d/2
. Suppose that
V\in\textup{RH}_{q}
and
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We have that

\displaystyle(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})(f)(x)

\displaystyle=-\int_{B(x,1)}(f(y)-f(x))\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t}dtdy

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(x,1)}f(y)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t}dtdy-K(x)f(x),

for almost all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where

\displaystyle K(x)

\displaystyle=2\log\rho(x)+\int_{0}^{\rho(x)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)-T_{t}(x-y)}{t}dydt

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\rho(x)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(x,1)}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t}dydt+\int_{\rho(x)^{2}}^{\infty}\int_{B(x,1)}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t}dydt+\gamma,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
\gamma
denotes the Euler–Mascheroni constant.

Note that in contrast with the result in Theorem
A
, the correction factor in Theorem
B
is not constant. This difference is due to the fact that the semigroup
\{T_{t}^{V}\}_{t>0}
is not Markovian, that is,
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}T_{t}^{V}(x,y)dy
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, does not coincide with the constant function
g(x)=1
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, when
t>0
.

We now consider the operator
\mathbb{L}{\rm og}\,\mathcal{L}_{V}
given by

(\mathbb{L}{\rm og}\,\mathcal{L}_{V})f=\frac{d}{ds}(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{s}f)\big|_{s=0^{+}}=\lim_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\mathcal{L}_{V}^{s}f-f}{s},\quad f\in D(\mathbb{L}{\rm og}\,\mathcal{L}_{V}),

where the limit is understood in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
being

\displaystyle D(\mathbb{L}{\rm og}\,\mathcal{L}_{V})

\displaystyle=\Bigg\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):f\in D(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{\beta}),\mbox{ for some }\beta\in(0,1),

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\mbox{ and }\lim_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\mathcal{L}_{V}^{s}f-f}{s}\mbox{ exists in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\Bigg\}.

In
[
1

it was proved that
(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})f=(\mathbb{L}{\rm og}\,\mathcal{L}_{V})f
, provided that

, for some

\lim_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-s}f-f}{s}=-(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})f,\quad\mbox{ in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

for every
f\in D(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-s_{0}})\cap D(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})
, for some
s_{0}>0

[
1

the following result was established .

Theorem C
.

Let
d\geq 3
and
q>d/2
. Suppose that
V\in\textup{RH}_{q}
and
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then, for every

\lim_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\mathcal{L}_{V}^{s}f-f}{s}=(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})f,\quad\mbox{ in }L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

We can write the above properties as follows:

\mathcal{L}_{V}^{s}f-f-s(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})f=o(s),\quad\mbox{ as }s\rightarrow 0^{+},\mbox{ in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

(1.4)

when

, for some
s_{0}\in(0,1)
, and

\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-s}f-f+s(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})f=o(s),\quad\mbox{ as }s\rightarrow 0^{+},\mbox{ in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

(1.5)

when
f\in D(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-s_{0}})\cap D(\log\mathcal{L}_{V})
, for some
s_{0}\in(0,1)
.

We now aim to extend the asymptotic expansions in (
1.4
) and (
1.5

We define, for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
, the operator
\log^{m}\mathcal{L}_{V}
by

(\log^{m}\mathcal{L}_{V})f=\int_{0}^{\infty}(\log\lambda)^{m}dE_{V}(\lambda)f,\quad f\in D(\log^{m}\mathcal{L}_{V}),

being
D(\log^{m}\mathcal{L}_{V})=\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\int_{0}^{\infty}(\log\lambda)^{2m}d\mu_{f,f}^{V}(\lambda)<\infty\}

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
s_{0}\in(0,1]
, and
V
be a measurable function such that
V(x)>0
a.e.

.

(a)

If
f\in D(\log^{m+1}\mathcal{L}_{V})\cap D(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{s_{0}})

\mathcal{L}_{V}^{s}f-\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{s^{k}}{k!}(\log^{k}\mathcal{L}_{V})f=o(s^{m}),\quad\mbox{ as }s\rightarrow 0^{+},\;\mbox{ in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(b)

If
f\in D(\log^{m+1}\mathcal{L}_{V})\cap D(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-s_{0}})
, then

\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-s}f-\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{k}\frac{s^{k}}{k!}(\log^{k}\mathcal{L}_{V})f=o(s^{m}),\quad\mbox{ as }s\rightarrow 0^{+},\;\mbox{ in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Let
s\in(0,1)
. For every

and

, the function

(a)

Suppose that

n\in\mathbb{N}

\lim_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{s}f-f-\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{s^{k}}{k!}(\mathbb{L}\textup{og}^{k}\mathcal{L}_{V})f\right)\frac{n!}{s^{n}}=(\mathbb{L}\textup{og}^{n}\mathcal{L}_{V})f,\quad\mbox{ in }L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(1.8)

(b)

Suppose that
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap{\textup{Lip}}\,^{\theta}_{\textup{loc},\textup{uni}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
and every compact set
\mathbb{K}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\lim_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{s}f-f-\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{s^{k}}{k!}(\mathbb{L}\textup{og}^{k}\mathcal{L}_{V})f\right)\frac{n!}{s^{n}}=(\mathbb{L}\textup{og}^{n}\mathcal{L}_{V})f,\quad\mbox{ in }L^{\infty}(\mathbb{K}).

(1.9)

Theorem 1.3
.

Let

,
q>d/2
,

, and

(a)

Suppose that

. Then, for every

\lim_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-s}f-f-\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{(-1)^{k}s^{k}}{k!}(\mathbb{L}\textup{og}^{k}\mathcal{L}_{V})f\right)\frac{(-1)^{n}n!}{s^{n}}=(\mathbb{L}\textup{og}^{n}\mathcal{L}_{V})f,\;\mbox{ in }L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(b)

Suppose that
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap{\textup{Lip}}\,^{\theta}_{\textup{loc},\textup{uni}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
and every compact set
\mathbb{K}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\lim_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-s}f-f-\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{(-1)^{k}s^{k}}{k!}(\mathbb{L}\textup{og}^{k}\mathcal{L}_{V})f\right)\frac{(-1)^{n}n!}{s^{n}}=(\mathbb{L}\textup{og}^{n}\mathcal{L}_{V})f,\;\mbox{ in }L^{\infty}(\mathbb{K}).

f\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

|u_{f,s}(x)|\leq C\int_{B(0,R)}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}dy\leq\frac{C}{|x|^{d-2s}}\int_{B(0,R)}|f(y)|dy,\quad|x|\geq 2R.

We first study the behavior of the positive power

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
d\geq 3
,
q>d/2
, and
V\in{\rm RH}_{q}
. For every
f\in{\textup{Lip}}_{\textup{loc}}^{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, with
\theta\in(0,1)
,

\lim_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\Gamma(-s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(f)(x)-f(x)}{t^{s+1}}dt=f(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(2.2)

being the integral absolutely convergent for each
0<s<\min\left\{\tfrac{\theta}{2},\tfrac{\delta}{2}\right\}
, where
{\delta=2-d/q}
.

Proof.

Assume that
f\in{\textup{Lip}}_{\textup{loc}}^{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Observe that, since
f\in L^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, using (
1.2
) we obtain that

\displaystyle|T_{t}^{V}f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{t^{d/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4t}}|f(y)|dy

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{1}{t^{d/2}}\int_{|y|\leq 2|x|}|f(y)|dy+\int_{|y|>2|x|}\frac{|f(y)|}{(1+|x-y|)^{d}}dy\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{(1+|x|)^{d}}{t^{d/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|f(y)|}{(1+|y|)^{d}}dy+\int_{|y|>2|x|}\frac{|f(y)|}{(1+|y|)^{d}}dy\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(1+|x|)^{d}\left(\frac{1}{t^{d/2}}+1\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|f(y)|}{(1+|y|)^{d}}dy<\infty,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mbox{ and }t>0.

On the other hand, since
T_{t}(1)(x)=1
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t>0
, we can write

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(f)(x)-f(x)}{t^{s+1}}dt

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(f)(x)-T_{t}^{V}(1)(x)f(x)}{t^{s+1}}dt+f(x)\int_{0}^{\rho(x)^{2}}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(1)(x)-T_{t}(1)(x)}{t^{s+1}}dt

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt\quad+f(x)\int_{\rho(x)^{2}}^{\infty}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(1)(x)}{t^{s+1}}dt-f(x)\int_{\rho(x)^{2}}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{s+1}}

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(f(y)-f(x)\mathcal{X}_{B(x,1)}(y)\big)\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t^{s+1}}dydt

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt\quad-f(x)\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(x,1)}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t^{s+1}}dydt

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt\quad+f(x)\int_{0}^{\rho(x)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)-T_{t}(x-y)}{t^{s+1}}dydt

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt\quad+f(x)\int_{\rho(x)^{2}}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t^{s+1}}dydt-f(x)\frac{\rho(x)^{-2s}}{s}

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(f(y)-f(x)\mathcal{X}_{B(x,1)}(y)\big)\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t^{s+1}}dydt

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt\quad+f(x)\int_{0}^{\rho(x)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)-T_{t}(x-y)}{t^{s+1}}dydt

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt\quad+f(x)\left(\int_{\rho(x)^{2}}^{\infty}\int_{B(x,1)}-\int_{0}^{\rho(x)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(x,1)}\right)\frac{T_{t}^{V}(x,y)}{t^{s+1}}dydt-f(x)\frac{\rho(x)^{-2s}}{s}

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt=:\sum_{j=1}^{4}I_{j}(x,s),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mbox{ and }s\in(0,1).

(2.3)

It is clear that

\lim_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\Gamma(-s)}I_{4}(x,s)=\lim_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{f(x)\rho(x)^{-2s}}{\Gamma(1-s)}=f(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(2.4)

We study now
I_{j}
,
j=1,2,3
. Using (
1.2
) we obtain

\displaystyle|I_{1}(x,s)|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{|x-y|\leq 1}|f(y)-f(x)|\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4t}}}{t^{d/2+s+1}}dtdy\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.+\int_{|x-y|>1}|f(y)|\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4t}}}{t^{d/2+s+1}}dtdy\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{|x-y|\leq 1}\frac{|f(y)-f(x)|}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}dy+\int_{|x-y|>1}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}dy\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{|x-y|\leq 1}|x-y|^{\theta-d-2s}dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|f(y)|}{(1+|x-y|)^{d}}dy\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{1}{\theta-2s}+(1+|x|)^{d}\right),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

provided that
0<s<\tfrac{\theta}{2}
. Then

\lim_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\Gamma(-s)}I_{1}(x,s)=0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(2.5)

Now, according to
[
16
, Proposition 2.16]
, there exists a nonnegative function
\phi
in the Schwartz class
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

|T_{t}^{V}(x,y)-T_{t}(x-y)|\leq\left(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(x)}\right)^{\delta}\phi_{t}(x-y),\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mbox{ and }t>0,

(2.6)

where
\delta=2-d/q
, and
\phi_{t}(z)=t^{-d/2}\phi(z/\sqrt{t})
,
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t>0
.
Using this estimate we get

\displaystyle|I_{2}(x,s)|

\displaystyle\leq C|f(x)|\int_{0}^{\rho(x)^{2}}\frac{1}{t^{s+1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(x)}\right)^{\delta}|\phi_{t}(x-y)|dydt

\displaystyle=C\frac{|f(x)|}{\rho(x)^{\delta}}\int_{0}^{\rho(x)^{2}}\frac{dt}{t^{s-\tfrac{\delta}{2}+1}}=C\frac{|f(x)|}{\rho(x)^{2s}(\tfrac{\delta}{2}-s)},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

provided that
0<s<\tfrac{\delta}{2}
.
Thus, we deduce that

\lim_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\Gamma(-s)}I_{2}(x,s)=0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(2.7)

Finally, using (
1.3
) for
N=1
we get

\displaystyle|I_{3}(x,s)|

\displaystyle\leq C|f(x)|\rho(x)\left(\int_{\rho(x)^{2}}^{\infty}\int_{|x-y|<1}+\int_{0}^{\rho(x)^{2}}\int_{|x-y|\geq 1}\right)\frac{e^{-c\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t}}}{t^{d/2+s+3/2}}dydt

\displaystyle\leq C|f(x)|\rho(x)\left(\int_{\rho(x)^{2}}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{1}^{\infty}\right)\frac{e^{-c\frac{r^{2}}{t}}r^{d-1}}{t^{d/2+s+3/2}}drdt

\displaystyle\leq C|f(x)|\rho(x)\left(\int_{\rho(x)^{2}}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{s+3/2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-u}u^{d/2-1}du+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{dr}{r^{2s+2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C|f(x)|\rho(x)\left(1+\rho(x)^{-2s-1}\right),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mbox{ and }s\in(0,1).

It follows that

\lim_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\Gamma(-s)}I_{3}(x,s)=0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(2.8)

From (
2
), (
2.4
), (
2.5
), (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) we deduce that (
2.2
) holds.
∎

We now establish some properties for the negative power
\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-s}
,
s\in(0,1)
, of
\mathcal{L}_{V}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

If
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is such that
\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{s-1}\|T_{t}^{V}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}dt<\infty
with
s\in(0,1)
, then
f\in D(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-s})
and

\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-s}f=\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{s-1}T_{t}^{V}(f)dt
, where the integral is understood in the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-Bochner sense.

Proof.

Suppose that
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{s-1}\|T_{t}^{V}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}dt<\infty
, with
s\in(0,1)
. We are going to see that
f\in D(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-s})
, i.e.,
\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-s}dE_{V}(\lambda)f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
or, equivalently,
\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-2s}d\mu_{f,f}(\lambda)<\infty
.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we define
J_{n}=\int_{1/n}^{\infty}\lambda^{-s}dE_{V}(\lambda)f
. We have that

\|J_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\left(\int_{\frac{1}{n}}^{\infty}\lambda^{-2s}d\mu_{f,f,}(\lambda)\right)^{1/2}\leq n^{2s}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Let
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We get, for
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
,
m<n
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(J_{n}-J_{m})(x)g(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\frac{1}{n}}^{\frac{1}{m}}\lambda^{-s}dE_{V}(\lambda)f\right)(x)g(x)dx

\displaystyle\hskip-113.81102pt=\int_{\frac{1}{n}}^{\frac{1}{m}}\lambda^{-s}d\mu_{f,g}(\lambda)=\int_{\frac{1}{n}}^{\frac{1}{m}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-t\lambda}t^{s-1}}{\Gamma(s)}dtd\mu_{f,g}(\lambda)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{s-1}}{\Gamma(s)}\int_{\frac{1}{n}}^{\frac{1}{m}}e^{-t\lambda}d\mu_{f,g}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\hskip-113.81102pt=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{s-1}}{\Gamma(s)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(x)\left(\int_{\frac{1}{n}}^{\frac{1}{m}}e^{-t\lambda}dE_{V}(\lambda)f\right)(x)dxdt

(2.9)

\displaystyle\hskip-113.81102pt=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(x)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{s-1}}{\Gamma(s)}\left(\int_{\frac{1}{n}}^{\frac{1}{m}}e^{-t\lambda}dE_{V}(\lambda)f\right)(x)dtdx.

(2.10)

Note that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{s-1}}{\Gamma(s)}\int_{\frac{1}{n}}^{\frac{1}{m}}e^{-t\lambda}d|\mu_{f,g}|(\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{t}{n}}t^{s-1}dt\int_{0}^{\infty}d|\mu_{f,g}|(\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\quad n,m\in\mathbb{N},\,m<n,

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{s-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|g(x)|

\displaystyle\left|\left(\int_{\frac{1}{n}}^{\frac{1}{m}}e^{-t\lambda}dE_{V}(\lambda)f\right)(x)\right|dxdt

\displaystyle\leq\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{s-1}\left\|\int_{\frac{1}{n}}^{\frac{1}{m}}e^{-t\lambda}dE_{V}(\lambda)f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}dt

\displaystyle\leq\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{s-1}\left(\int_{\frac{1}{n}}^{\frac{1}{m}}e^{-2t\lambda}d\mu_{f,f}(\lambda)\right)^{1/2}dt

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{s-1}e^{-\frac{t}{n}}dt,\quad n,m\in\mathbb{N},\,m<n,

and the interchanges of order of integration in (
2.9
) and (
2.10
) are justified.

We deduce that, for every
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
, with
m<n
,

(J_{n}-J_{m})(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{s-1}}{\Gamma(s)}\left(\int_{\frac{1}{n}}^{\frac{1}{m}}e^{-t\lambda}dE_{V}(\lambda)f\right)(x)dt,\quad\text{ a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Then,

\displaystyle\|J_{n}-J_{m}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{s-1}}{\Gamma(s)}\left\|\int_{\frac{1}{n}}^{\frac{1}{m}}e^{-t\lambda}dE_{V}(\lambda)f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{s-1}\left(\int_{\frac{1}{n}}^{\frac{1}{m}}e^{-2t\lambda}d\mu_{f,f}(\lambda)\right)^{1/2}dt,\quad n,m\in\mathbb{N},\,m<n.

On the other hand, we can write

\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{s-1}\|T_{t}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{s-1}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-2t\lambda}d\mu_{f,f}(\lambda)\right)^{1/2}dt<\infty,

and, for each
t>0
,

\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{n}}e^{-2t\lambda}d\mu_{f,f}(\lambda)\leq\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{n}}d\mu_{f,f}(\lambda)=\langle E_{V}(0,\tfrac{1}{n})f,f\rangle\longrightarrow 0,\mbox{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

Monotone convergence theorem leads to

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{s-1}\left(\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{n}}e^{-2t\lambda}d\mu_{f,f}(\lambda)\right)^{1/2}dt=0.

Hence, for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\|J_{n}-J_{m}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\varepsilon,\quad n,m\in\mathbb{N},\,n_{0}<m<n.

Thus we have proved that
\{J_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then, there exists
F\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for which
J_{n}\longrightarrow F
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We conclude that
f\in D(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-s})
and that

\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-s}f=\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{s-1}T_{t}^{V}fdt.\qed

Note that from Proposition
2.2
we deduce that, for every
s\in(0,1)
,

(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-s}f)(x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{s-1}T_{t}^{V}(f)(x)dt,\quad a.e.\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

provided that
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{s-1}\|T_{t}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}dt<\infty
.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
d\geq 3
,
q>d/2
, and
V\in{\rm RH}_{q}
. If
f\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
s\in(0,1)
, then
f\in D(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-s})
, and

\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-s}f=\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{s-1}T_{t}^{V}(f)dt,

where the integral is understood in the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-Bochner sense.

Proof.

Let
f\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
s\in(0,1)
. We choose
R>0
such that
\supp(f)\subset B(0,R)
. According to (
1.2
) we have that

|T_{t}^{V}(f)(x)|\leq Ct^{-d/2},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\quad and\quad t>0.

(2.11)

By (
1.3
) and
[
28
, Lemma 1.4(b)]
we get

\displaystyle|T_{t}^{V}(f)(x)|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B(0,R)}|f(y)|\frac{e^{-c\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t}}}{t^{d/2}}\frac{\rho(y)}{\sqrt{t}}dy

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{e^{-c|x|^{2}/t}}{t^{(d+1)/2}}\int_{B(0,R)}|f(y)|\rho(y)dy

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{e^{-c|x|^{2}/t}}{t^{(d+1)/2}},\quad x\notin B(0,2R)\quad and\quad t>0.

(2.12)

It follows from (
2.11
) and (
2
) that

\displaystyle\|T_{t}^{V}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{B(0.2R)}|T_{t}^{V}(f)(x)|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(0,2R)}|T_{t}^{V}(f)(x)|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(t^{-d}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(0,2R)}\frac{e^{-c|x|^{2}/t}}{t^{d+1}}dx\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(t^{-d/2}+t^{-(d+2)/4}\right)\leq Ct^{-(d+2)/4},\quad t\geq 1.

(2.13)

By using (
2.11
) and (
2
) we deduce that

\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{s-1}\|T_{t}^{V}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}dt\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{1}t^{s-1}dt+\int_{1}^{\infty}t^{s-1-\frac{d+2}{4}}dt\right)<\infty.

According to Proposition
2.2
we conclude that
f\in D(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-s})
.
∎

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
d\geq 3
,
q>d/2
, and
V\in{\rm RH}_{q}
. For every
f\in{\textup{Lip}}_{\textup{loc}}^{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{1}_{-\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, with
\theta\in(0,1)
and
\varepsilon\in(0,d)
,

\lim_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}T_{t}^{V}(f)(x)t^{s-1}dt=f(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(2.14)

being the integral absolutely convergent for each
0<s<\min\left\{\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2},\tfrac{1}{4}\right\}
.

Proof.

Consider
f\in{\textup{Lip}}^{\theta}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{1}_{-\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, for some
\varepsilon>0
. We can write

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle T_{t}^{V}(f)(x)t^{s-1}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}(T_{t}^{V}(f)(x)-f(x)T_{t}^{V}(1)(x))t^{s-1}dt+f(x)\int_{\rho(x)^{2}}^{\infty}T_{t}^{V}(1)(x)t^{s-1}dt

\displaystyle\quad+f(x)\left(\int_{0}^{\rho(x)^{2}}(T_{t}^{V}(1)(x)-T_{t}(1)(x))t^{s-1}dt+\int_{0}^{\rho(x)^{2}}T_{t}(1)(x)t^{s-1}dt\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(f(y)-f(x)\mathcal{X}_{B(x,1)}(y)\big)T_{t}^{V}(x,y)t^{s-1}dydt

\displaystyle\quad+f(x)\int_{0}^{\rho(x)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(T_{t}^{V}(x,y)-T_{t}(x-y))t^{s-1}dt

\displaystyle\quad+f(x)\left(\int_{\rho(x)^{2}}^{\infty}\int_{B(x,1)}-\int_{0}^{\rho(x)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(x,1)}\right)T_{t}^{V}(x,y)t^{s-1}dt+\frac{f(x)\rho(x)^{2s}}{s}

\displaystyle=:\sum_{j=1}^{4}R_{j}(x,s),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mbox{ and }s\in(0,\min\left\{\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2},\tfrac{1}{4}\right\}).

(2.15)

Indeed, by proceeding as in the proof of Proposition
2.1
we get

\displaystyle|R_{1}(x,s)|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{|x-y|\leq 1}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}dy+\int_{|x-y|>1}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{d-2s}}dy\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{|x-y|\leq 1}|x-y|^{\theta-d+2s}dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|f(y)|}{(1+|x-y|)^{d-\varepsilon}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{1}{\theta}+(1+|x|)^{d-\varepsilon}\right),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mbox{ and }s\in\left(0,\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\right).

Also, considering (
2.6
), we deduce that, for each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
s\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle|R_{2}(x,s)|

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{|f(x)|}{\rho(x)^{\delta}}\int_{0}^{\rho(x)^{2}}t^{s+\tfrac{\delta}{2}-1}dt\leq C\frac{|f(x)|\rho(x)^{2s}}{2s+\delta}

\displaystyle\leq C|f(x)|\rho(x)^{2s}\leq C|f(x)|(1+\rho(x))^{2},

and, from (
1.3
) with
N=1
, we obtain

\displaystyle|R_{3}(x,s)|

\displaystyle\leq C|f(x)|\rho(x)\left(\int_{\rho(x)^{2}}^{\infty}\int_{|x-y|<1}+\int_{0}^{\rho(x)^{2}}\int_{|x-y|\geq 1}\right)\frac{e^{-c\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t}}}{t^{d/2-s+3/2}}dydt

\displaystyle\leq C|f(x)|\rho(x)\left(\int_{\rho(x)^{2}}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{1}^{\infty}\right)\frac{e^{-c\frac{r^{2}}{t}}r^{d-1}}{t^{d/2-s+3/2}}drdt

\displaystyle\leq C|f(x)|\rho(x)\left(\int_{\rho(x)^{2}}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{-s+3/2}}+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{dr}{r^{-2s+2}}\right)

\displaystyle=C|f(x)|\frac{\rho(x)^{2s}+\rho(x)}{1-2s}

\displaystyle\leq C|f(x)|(\rho(x)^{2s}+\rho(x))\leq C|f(x)|(1+\rho(x)),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mbox{ and }s\in\left(0,\tfrac{1}{4}\right).

Thus, the integral in (
2.14
) is absolutely convergent. We conclude that

\lim_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)}\sum_{j=1}^{4}R_{j}(x,s)=\lim_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sum_{j=1}^{4}\frac{sR_{j}(x,s)}{\Gamma(1+s)}=\lim_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}f(x)\rho(x)^{2s}=f(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.\qed

(4.11)

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
s\in(0,\min\left\{\tfrac{\theta}{2},\tfrac{\delta}{2}\right\})
.

Finally, taking into account the estimation for
h_{\rho(x)}
in Lemma
4.1
, we obtain that, for each
\varepsilon>0
,

|\Omega_{f,4}(x,s)|\leq Cs|f(x)|\left(\frac{\mathcal{X}_{(0,1]}(\rho(x))}{\rho(x)^{2+\varepsilon}}+\rho(x)^{2\varepsilon}\mathcal{X}_{(1,\infty)}(\rho(x)\right),

(4.12)

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
s\in(0,\min\left\{\tfrac{\theta}{2},\tfrac{\delta}{2}\right\})
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a compact in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We can find
x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\mathbb{K}\subset\cup_{i=1}^{\ell}B(x_{i},\rho(x_{i}))
. By using
[
15
, Proposition 1]
we deduce that, for a certain
C>1
,

\frac{\rho(x_{i})}{C}\leq\rho(y)\leq C\rho(x_{i}),\quad y\in B(x_{i},\rho(x_{i})),\,i=1,\ldots,\ell.

Then, there exists
0<A<B
such that
A\leq\rho(y)\leq B
,
y\in\mathbb{K}
.

From (
4
), (
4.10
), (
4.11
) and (
4.12
), we deduce that, for every
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap{\textup{Lip}}^{\theta}_{{\rm loc,uni}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\left|\sum_{j=1}^{4}\Omega_{f,j}(x,s)\right|\leq Cs(1+|f(x)|),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\;s\in\left(0,\min\left\{\tfrac{\theta}{2},\tfrac{\delta}{4}\right\}\right).

(4.13)

If
f\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap{\textup{Lip}}^{\theta}_{{\rm loc,uni}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and
\supp f\subset B(0,R)
, with
R>1
, then by using (
4
), (
4.10
), (
4.11
) and (
4.12
), we get that, for each
\varepsilon>0
,

\left|\sum_{j=1}^{4}\Omega_{f,j}(x,s)\right|=|\Omega_{f,1}(x,s)|\leq Cs|f(x)||x|^{-(d-\varepsilon)},

(4.14)

provided that
|x|\geq 2R
, and
s\in(0,\min\{\tfrac{\theta}{2},\delta/4\})
. Here
C>0
does not depend on
x
nor
s
.

Therefore, if
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap{\textup{Lip}}^{\theta}_{{\rm loc,uni}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by virtue of (
4.13
) we get
\|\Omega_{V}(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq Cs
, and consequently,

\lim_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}\|\Omega_{f}(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=0.

Let us consider now
1<p<\infty
and suppose that
f\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap{\textup{Lip}}^{\theta}_{{\rm loc,uni}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Choose
R>1
such that
{\rm supp}\,f\subset B(0,R)
. Using (
4.13
) and (
4.14
) we deduce that, when
0<\varepsilon<d(1-1/p)
,

\displaystyle\|\Omega_{f}(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Omega_{f}(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{p}(B(0,2R))}+\|\Omega_{f}(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(0,2R))}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})})s\Big(1+\Big(\int_{|x|\geq 2R}\frac{dx}{|x|^{(d-\varepsilon)p}}\Big)^{1/p}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq Cs\Big(1+\Big(\int_{2R}^{\infty}r^{d(1-p)+\varepsilon p-1}dr\Big)^{1/p}\Big)\leq Cs,\quad s\in\big(0,\min\{\tfrac{\theta}{2},\tfrac{\delta}{4}\}\big).

Thus, it follows that

\lim_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}\|\Omega_{f}(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=0.

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

We argue as in the proof of Theorem
1.2
. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and assume that
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap{\textup{Lip}}^{\theta}_{{\rm loc},{\rm uni}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

We define
\Lambda_{f}(x,s)
, for each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
s\in(0,1/4)
, as

\Lambda_{f}(x,s):=\frac{1}{s^{n}}\Big(\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-s}(f)(x)-f(x)-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(-s)^{k}}{k!}(\mathbb{L}\textup{og}^{k}\mathcal{L}_{V})f(x)\Big).

Our aim is to establish that
\lim_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}\Lambda_{f}(\cdot,s)=0
, in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
1<p<\infty
, when
f\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap{\textup{Lip}}_{\textup{loc},\textup{uni}}^{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{K})
, for every compact
\mathbb{K}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, provided that
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap{\textup{Lip}}_{\textup{loc},\textup{uni}}^{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,d)
. According to Remark
2.5
we have that, for every
s\in(0,\min\left\{\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2},\tfrac{1}{4}\right\})
,

\mathcal{L}_{V}^{-s}(f)(x)-f(x)=\frac{s}{\Gamma(1+s)}\sum_{j=1}^{3}R_{j}(x,s)+f(x)\Big(\frac{\rho(x)^{2s}}{\Gamma(1+s)}-1\Big),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
R_{j}(x,s)
,
j=1,2,3
, are the terms in (
2
).

Consider
a_{k,\ell}
,
k=1,\ldots,n
,
\ell=0,\ldots,k
, as in (
4.3
) and decompose
\Lambda_{f}
as
\Lambda_{f}=\sum_{j=1}^{4}\Lambda_{f,j}
, where, for
j=1,2,3
,

\Lambda_{f,j}(x,s)=\frac{1}{s^{n}}\Big(\frac{s}{\Gamma(1+s)}R_{j}(x,s)-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(-s)^{k}}{k!}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\ell a_{k,\ell}J_{j}^{(\ell-1)}(x,0)\Big),

when
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
s\in(0,\min\left\{\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2},\tfrac{1}{4}\right\})
, and

\Lambda_{f,4}(x,s)=\frac{f(x)}{s^{n}}\Big(\frac{\rho(x)^{2s}}{\Gamma(1+s)}-1-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(-s)^{k}}{k!}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}a_{k,\ell}(2\log\rho(x))^{\ell}\Big),

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
s\in(0,1)
. Here
J_{j}^{(r)}(\cdot,0)
,
r\in\mathbb{N}\cap\{0\}
, is given by (
2
) (for
s=0
).

Let
G
and
H
the functions given by (
4.4
) and (
4.5
), respectively. We have that, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
s\in(0,\min\left\{\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2},\tfrac{1}{4}\right\})
,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{f,1}(x,s)=-\frac{1}{s^{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(f(y)-f(x)\mathcal{X}_{B(x,1)}(y)\big)T_{t}^{V}(x,y)dyG(-s,t)dt,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{f,2}(x,s)=-\frac{f(x)}{s^{n}}\int_{0}^{\rho(x)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(T_{t}^{V}(x,y)-T_{t}(x-y))dyG(-s,t)dt,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{f,3}(x,s)=-\frac{f(x)}{s^{n}}\left(\int_{\rho(x)^{2}}^{\infty}\int_{B(x,1)}-\int_{0}^{\rho(x)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(x,1)}\right)T_{t}^{V}(x,y)dyG(-s,t)dt,

and

\Lambda_{f,4}(x,s)=\frac{f(x)}{s^{n}}H(-s,x),\quad s\in(0,1).

In order to estimate
\Lambda_{f,j}
,
j=1,2,3
, we use Lemma
4.1
. By (
1.2
) we obtain, when
0<\varepsilon<\theta
, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
s\in(0,(d-\varepsilon)/2)
,

\displaystyle|\Lambda_{f,1}(x,s)|

\displaystyle\leq Cs\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(y)-f(x)\mathcal{X}_{B(x,1)}(y)|

\displaystyle\hskip-34.14322pt\quad\times\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4t}}}{t^{d/2+1+\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}}}dt+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{5t}}}{t^{d/2-s+1-\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}}}dt\right)dy

\displaystyle\hskip-34.14322pt\leq Cs\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(y)-f(x)\mathcal{X}_{B(x,1)}(y)|\Big(|x-y|^{-d-\varepsilon}+|x-y|^{-d+2s+\varepsilon}\Big)dy

\displaystyle\hskip-34.14322pt\leq Cs\left(\|f\|_{{\textup{Lip}}^{\theta}_{\rm loc,uni}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\int_{B(x,1)}|x-y|^{\theta-d-\varepsilon}dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(x,1)}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{d-2s-\varepsilon}}dy\right)

\displaystyle\hskip-34.14322pt\leq Cs\left(\|f\|_{{\textup{Lip}}^{\theta}_{\rm loc,uni}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\int_{0}^{1}r^{\theta-\varepsilon-1}dy+\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip-34.14322pt\leq Cs\big(\|f\|_{{\textup{Lip}}^{\theta}_{\rm loc,uni}(\mathbb{R}^{d})})+\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\big).

On the other hand, according to (
2.6
) we can write, for
0<\varepsilon<\delta=2-d/q
, and each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
s\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle|\Lambda_{f,2}(x,s)|

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{|f(x)|}{s^{n}\rho(x)^{\delta}}\int_{0}^{\rho(x)^{2}}t^{\tfrac{\delta}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\phi_{t}\left(\frac{x-y}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\right||G(-s,t)|dydt

\displaystyle\leq Cs\frac{|f(x)|}{\rho(x)^{\delta}}\left(\mathcal{X}_{(0,1]}(\rho(x))\int_{0}^{\rho(x)^{2}}t^{\tfrac{\delta}{2}-1-\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}}dt\right.

\displaystyle\quad+\left.\mathcal{X}_{(1,\infty)}(\rho(x))\Big(\int_{0}^{1}t^{\tfrac{\delta}{2}-1-\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}}dt+\int_{1}^{\rho(x)^{2}}t^{\tfrac{\delta}{2}+s+\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}-1}dt\Big)\right)

\displaystyle\leq Cs|f(x)|\big(\mathcal{X}_{(0,1]}(\rho(x))\rho(x)^{-\varepsilon}+\mathcal{X}_{(1,\infty)}(\rho(x))\rho(x)^{2+\varepsilon}\big).

For
\Lambda_{f,3}
we have the following estimation,

\displaystyle|\Lambda_{f,3}(x,s)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|f(x)|}{s^{n}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\int_{B(x,1)}\int_{\rho(x)^{2}}^{\infty}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(x,1)}\int_{0}^{\rho(x)^{2}}\right)T_{t}^{V}(x,y)|G(-s,t)|dtdy.

Assume that
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\rho(x)\leq 1
. By (
1.2
), for every
\varepsilon\in(0,d)
and
s\in(0,(d-\varepsilon)/4)
,

\displaystyle|\Lambda_{f,3}(x,s)|

\displaystyle\leq Cs|f(x)|\left(\int_{B(x,1)}\Big(\int_{\rho(x)^{2}}^{1}\frac{e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4t}}}{t^{d/2+1+\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}}}dt+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4t}}}{t^{d/2-s+1-\varepsilon/2}}dt\Big)dy\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(x,1)}\int_{0}^{\rho(x)^{2}}\frac{e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4t}}}{t^{d/2+1+\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}}}dtdy\right)

\displaystyle\leq Cs|f(x)|\left(\rho(x)^{-(d+\varepsilon)}+\frac{1}{d-2s-\varepsilon}+\int_{|x-y|\geq 1}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{d+\varepsilon}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq Cs|f(x)|\Big(1+\rho(x)^{-(d+\varepsilon)}\Big)\leq Cs|f(x)|\rho(x)^{-(d+\varepsilon)}.

In the case that
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfies
\rho(x)>1
, we get, for
s\in(0,\varepsilon/4)
, with
\varepsilon\in(0,d/2)
,

\displaystyle|\Lambda_{f,3}(x,s)|

\displaystyle\leq Cs|f(x)|\left(\int_{B(x,1)}\Big(\int_{\rho(x)^{2}}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4t}}}{t^{d/2-s+1-\varepsilon/2}}dt\Big)dy\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(x,1)}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4t}}}{t^{d/2+1+\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}}}dt+\int_{1}^{\rho(x)^{2}}\frac{e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4t}}}{t^{d/2-s+1-\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}}}dt\Big)dy\right)

\displaystyle\leq Cs|f(x)|\left(\rho(x)^{-(d-2s-\varepsilon)}+\rho(x)^{2\varepsilon}\int_{|x-y|\geq 1}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{d-2s+\varepsilon}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq Cs|f(x)|\Big(\rho(x)^{-(d-2s-\varepsilon)}+\rho(x)^{2\varepsilon}\Big)\leq Cs|f(x)|\rho(x)^{3\varepsilon}.

Hence, for each
\varepsilon\in(0,d/2)
,

|\Lambda_{f,3}(x,s)|\leq Cs|f(x)|\big(\mathcal{X}_{(0,1]}(\rho(x))\rho(x)^{-(d+\varepsilon)}+\mathcal{X}_{(1,\infty)}(\rho(x))\rho(x)^{3\varepsilon}\big),

provided that
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
s\in(0,\varepsilon/4)
.

Finally, we observe that, for each
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle|\Lambda_{f,4}(x,s)|

\displaystyle=\frac{|f(x)||H(-s,x)|}{s^{n}}

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\leq Cs|f(x)|\Big(\rho(x)^{-\varepsilon}\mathcal{X}_{(0,1]}(\rho(x))+\rho(x)^{2+\varepsilon}\mathcal{X}_{(1,\infty)}(\rho(x)\Big),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,s\in(0,1).

The above estimations allow us to establish the assertions in Theorem
1.3
by proceeding as in the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
