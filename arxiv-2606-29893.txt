AdaGrad does not adapt to Hölder-smoothness for composite objectives
Matia Bojović 1,2 Saverio Salzo 1,3 Massimiliano Pontil 1,4 1 Computational Statistics and Machine Learning, Istituto Italiano di Tecnologia, Genoa, Italy 2 Department of Mathematics, University of Genoa, Genoa, Italy 3 DIAG, Sapienza University of Rome, Rome, Italy 4 Department of Computer Science, University College London, London, UK

Abstract
We exhibit a simple deterministic one-dimensional convex composite optimization problem for which AdaGrad scheme does not achieve the classical convergence rate 𝒪 ( n − ( 1 + ν ) / 2 ) \mathcal{O}(n^{-(1+\nu)/2}) associated with Hölder-smooth objectives. The example highlights a basic mismatch between classical AdaGrad accumulation and composite optimality. A main insight is that the gradient of the smooth term may not vanish at the optimum, causing AdaGrad to keep reducing its stepsize excessively and converge more slowly. We also discuss why alternative accumulation mechanisms based on gradient mappings or on successive gradient differences, avoid this pathology.



1
Introduction

Adaptive gradient methods are among the standard tools for training machine learning models. Their appeal is that they reduce the need to tune a fixed learning rate by adjusting the effective stepsize using information observed along the optimization trajectory.
AdaGrad, introduced by
3
, is a prototypical example: it rescales the update by the square root of the cumulative sum of past squared subgradients, coordinate by coordinate. The method was originally proposed for nonsmooth Lipschitz-continuous composite convex optimization, achieving the optimal rate
\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{n})
in the objective gap.

Later works considered the smooth setting and asked whether AdaGrad can adapt to the unknown smoothness level of the objective, while attaining the corresponding standard rate. This question is closely related to the notion of universality introduced by
5
, namely the ability of an algorithm to achieve the optimal rate without prior knowledge of the smoothness level.
For AdaGrad, an early result in this direction was given by
4
, who, to the best of our knowledge, is the first to show that AdaGrad
also adapts to smooth problems with Lipschitz-continuous gradients, achieving the standard rate
\mathcal{O}(1/n)
. The settings described above are the endpoints of a broader class of functions, which is that of Hölder-smoothness
C^{1,\nu}
,
\nu\in[0,1]
. For this class of functions,
6
has proved that AdaGrad achieves the rate
\mathcal{O}(n^{-(1+\nu)/2})
, fully interpolating between
\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{n})
and
\mathcal{O}(1/n)
.

However, all these results are formulated in the noncomposite setting, where the update reduces to a gradient step with an adaptive stepsize or diagonal scaling.
In this simplified setting, if
x_{\star}
minimizes a differentiable convex function
f
over the whole space, then

\nabla f(x_{\star})=0.

Thus, along a convergent deterministic trajectory, the quantities accumulated by AdaGrad, namely the squared gradient norms, are expected to vanish. The growth of the accumulator is therefore controlled by progress toward stationarity: as the iterates approach a minimizer, AdaGrad keeps adding smaller and smaller terms, allowing for the appropriate convergence rates in the given class of functions.

Many problems in machine learning and statistics are naturally formulated in composite form,

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}F(x):=f(x)+\varphi(x),

(1)

where
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is convex and smooth, while
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is proper, convex and lower semicontinuous. In applications,
\varphi
often represents nonsmooth regularization, such as an
\ell_{1}
-penalty, or constraints through an indicator function. The point we emphasize, however, is not nonsmoothness itself, but the splitting of the objective. In a composite problem, stationarity is not measured by the gradient of the smooth part alone. The first-order optimality condition is

0\in\nabla f(x_{\star})+\partial\varphi(x_{\star}),

where
\partial\varphi(x_{\star})
denotes the subdifferential of
\varphi
at
x_{\star}
. Thus, the gradient of
f
does not vanish at a minimizer, since it may be balanced by the first-order contribution of
\varphi
. This creates a mismatch for AdaGrad when its metric is driven only by
\lVert\nabla f(x_{n})\rVert
: even if the iterates approach a composite minimizer, the accumulated gradient norms may keep growing linearly. Consequently, the effective stepsizes may decay as if the method were not approaching stationarity.

The purpose of this note is to make this obstruction explicit. We construct a deterministic one-dimensional convex composite problem for which the smooth part is Hölder-smooth, but the gradient of the smooth part remains bounded away from zero along the AdaGrad trajectory. The example uses an affine
\varphi
, showing that the obstacle is not caused by nonsmoothness of the regularizer, but by gradient’s accumulation applied only to one component of the objective. As a consequence, the AdaGrad weights grow at least as the square root of the iteration counter, and the resulting effective stepsize becomes too small to retain the appropriate Hölder-smooth composite rate.

Contributions.
Our main result shows that for every
\nu\in\left]0,1\right]
and
\alpha\in\left]1/{2},(1+\nu)/{2}\right[
, there exists a one-dimensional convex composite objective
F=f+\varphi
, with
f\in C^{1,\nu}(\mathbb{R})
,
\varphi\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{R})
, such that for every
\varepsilon\geq 0
and every base stepsize
\eta
in a suitable range around zero, the corresponding AdaGrad iterates

x_{n+1}=\operatorname{prox}_{\frac{\eta}{w_{n}}\varphi}\left(x_{n}-\frac{\eta}{w_{n}}f^{\prime}(x_{n})\right),\quad{\rm where}\,\,w_{n}=\varepsilon+\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}|f^{\prime}(x_{k})|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

(2)

satisfy

F(x_{n})-F_{\star}\geq\kappa n^{-\alpha},

for every

. This rules out the expected rate
\mathcal{O}(n^{-(1+\nu)/2})

for AdaGrad in the deterministic composite setting, marking a conceptual difference with the unconstrained scenario.

Related works.
The issue of the accumulation mechanism of the gradients in AdaGrad has already been considered in the literature. Indeed, there exist works proposing different accumulation schemes.

Proposition 1

(Hölder-smoothness)
.

The function
f
given in (
3
), with
p>2
and
c\in\left]0,1\right[
, is convex and continuously differentiable. Moreover,

\nu\in\left]0,1\right]

(\forall\,x,y\in\mathbb{R})\quad\lvert{f^{\prime}(x)-f^{\prime}(y)}\rvert\leq L_{\nu}|x-y|^{\nu}

for every
\nu\in\left]0,1\right]
.

Proof.

Let
p>2
and
c\in\left]0,1\right[
.
The function
g^{\prime}_{p,c}
given in (
4
) is continuous on
\mathbb{R}
and differentiable on
\mathbb{R}

with the exception of
\pm 1
and it holds

g^{\prime\prime}_{p,c}(x)=\begin{cases}c(p-1)|x|^{p-2}&\text{if }x\in\left]-1,1\right[\\
0&\text{if}\ \mathbb{R}\setminus[-1,1].\end{cases}

This shows that
\lvert{g^{\prime\prime}_{p,c}(x)}\rvert\leq c(p-1)
, for every

, and hence,

is Lipschitz continuous with constant

.
Consequently, recalling (

Also, it follows from (

Thus

The case

. Let
\nu\in\left]0,1\right[

Remark 1
.

The derivative of the smooth part of

does not vanish at the minimizer:

f^{\prime}(0)=g^{\prime}_{p,c}(0)-1=-1\neq 0.

This is the key feature of the construction. The point

Analysis of the lower bound

We now analyze the iterate (
2
) for the special objective components
f
and
\varphi
given in (
3
).
We first note that, directly from the definition the proximity operator, it follows that

(\forall\,\gamma>0)(\forall\,v\in\mathbb{R})\qquad\operatorname{prox}_{\gamma\varphi}(v)=v-\gamma.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
If we denote with
\gamma_{n}=\eta/w_{n}
, recalling that
f^{\prime}(x)=g^{\prime}_{p,c}(x)-1
, we have

\displaystyle x_{n+1}

\displaystyle=x_{n}-\gamma_{n}f^{\prime}(x_{n})-\gamma_{n}

\displaystyle=x_{n}-\gamma_{n}(g^{\prime}_{p,c}(x_{n})-1)-\gamma_{n}

\displaystyle=x_{n}-\gamma_{n}g^{\prime}_{p,c}(x_{n}).

Thus, the affine part of
f
is exactly cancelled out by the proximal step associated with
\varphi
. However, it remains in the AdaGrad denominator, which is built from
f^{\prime}(x_{n})=g^{\prime}_{p,c}(x_{n})-1
.
By construction,
|g^{\prime}_{p,c}(x)|\leq c<1
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Hence, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
f^{\prime}(x)\leq 0
and

1-c\leq|f^{\prime}(x)|\leq 1+c.

Consequently,

\varepsilon+(1-c)\sqrt{n+1}\leq w_{n}\leq\varepsilon+(1+c)\sqrt{n+1}.

Lemma 1

(Convergence to the minimizer)
.

Let
F=f+\varphi
, with
f
and
\varphi
defined in (
3
), with
p>2
and
c\in\left]0,1\right[
.
Let
\eta
be such that

0<\eta<\frac{1-c}{c(p-1)}

and let
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be generated by AdaGrad algorithm (
2
), with
x_{0}\neq 0
.
Then
x_{n}\to 0
. Moreover, the sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is nonincreasing and with constant sign.

Proof.

Set
L:=c(p-1)
. By Proposition
1
,
g^{\prime}_{p,c}
is Lipschitz-continuous with constant
L
, and
g^{\prime}_{p,c}(0)=0
.
Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
w_{n}\geq 1-c
, we have

\gamma_{n}=\frac{\eta}{w_{n}}\leq\frac{\eta}{1-c}<\frac{1}{c(p-1)}=\frac{1}{L}\ \Rightarrow\ \gamma_{n}L<1.

Suppose first that
x_{n}>0
. Since
0\leq g^{\prime}_{p,c}(x_{n})\leq Lx_{n}
, we get

x_{n+1}=x_{n}-\gamma_{n}g^{\prime}_{p,c}(x_{n})\geq x_{n}-\gamma_{n}Lx_{n}>0.

Moreover,
g^{\prime}_{p,c}(x_{n})\geq 0
, and therefore
x_{n+1}\leq x_{n}
. Hence
0<x_{n+1}\leq x_{n}
.
Similarly, if
x_{n}<0
, then
g^{\prime}_{p,c}(x_{n})\leq 0
and
|g^{\prime}_{p,c}(x_{n})|\leq L|x_{n}|
. Thus

x_{n+1}=x_{n}-\gamma_{n}g^{\prime}_{p,c}(x_{n})\leq x_{n}-\gamma_{n}Lx_{n}<0,

while also
x_{n+1}\geq x_{n}
. Hence
x_{n}\leq x_{n+1}<0
.
Therefore the sign of the iterates is preserved. In addition,
r_{n}:=|x_{n}|
is nonincreasing, and so
r_{n}
converges to some
\bar{r}\geq 0
. We prove that
\bar{r}=0
. Suppose by contradiction that
\bar{r}>0
. Since the sign of
x_{n}
is fixed and
r_{n}\in[\bar{r},r_{0}]
, where
r_{0}:=|x_{0}|
, continuity of
g^{\prime}_{p,c}
and the fact that
g^{\prime}_{p,c}(x)\neq 0
for
x\neq 0
imply

a:=\inf_{\bar{r}\leq|x|\leq r_{0}}|g^{\prime}_{p,c}(x)|>0.

Thus
|g^{\prime}_{p,c}(x_{n})|\geq a
for every

. Using

w_{n}\leq\varepsilon+(1+c)\sqrt{n+1},

we obtain

r_{n}-r_{n+1}=\gamma_{n}|g^{\prime}_{p,c}(x_{n})|\geq\frac{\eta a}{\varepsilon+(1+c)\sqrt{n+1}}.

Summing this inequality gives a contradiction, since

\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon+(1+c)\sqrt{n+1}}=+\infty,

but the left-hand side telescopes:

\sum_{n=0}^{N}(r_{n}-r_{n+1})=r_{0}-r_{N+1}\leq r_{0}.

Hence
\bar{r}=0
, and therefore
x_{n}\to 0
.

Theorem 1

(Lower bound)
.

Let
F=f+\varphi
, with
f
and
\varphi
defined in (
3
), with
p>2
and
c\in\left]0,1\right[
.
Let
\eta
be such that

0<\eta<\frac{1-c}{c(p-1)}

and let
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be generated by AdaGrad algorithm (
2
), with
x_{0}\neq 0
. Then, there exists
\kappa>0

such that

(\forall\,n\in\mathbb{N})\quad F(x_{n})-F_{\star}\geq\kappa\,n^{-\frac{p}{2(p-2)}}.

Proof.

Let
r_{n}:=|x_{n}|
. By Lemma
1
,
r_{n}\downarrow 0
, and the sign of
x_{n}
is constant. By oddness of
g^{\prime}_{p,c}
, the recursion becomes

r_{n+1}=r_{n}-\gamma_{n}g^{\prime}_{p,c}(r_{n}).

Set
q:=p-2
. Since
r_{n}\to 0
, there exists
\bar{n}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
0<r_{n}\leq 1
for every
n\geq\bar{n}
. Hence

(\forall\,n\geq\bar{n})\quad g^{\prime}_{p,c}(r_{n})=cr_{n}^{p-1}.

Increasing
\bar{n}
, if necessary, we may also assume that

(\forall\,n\geq\bar{n})\quad\theta_{n}:=\gamma_{n}\frac{g^{\prime}_{p,c}(r_{n})}{r_{n}}\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Indeed,
g^{\prime}_{p,c}(r_{n})/r_{n}=cr_{n}^{p-2}
,
r_{n}\to 0
, and
\gamma_{n}\leq\eta/(1-c)
.
Now, let
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geq\bar{n}
.
Since
0\leq\theta_{n}\leq 1/2
, the mean value theorem applied to
\theta\mapsto(1-\theta)^{-q}
gives

(1-\theta_{n})^{-q}-1\leq q2^{q+1}\theta_{n}.

Therefore, since
r_{n+1}=r_{n}(1-\theta_{n})
, we have

\displaystyle r_{n+1}^{-q}-r_{n}^{-q}

\displaystyle=r_{n}^{-q}\left((1-\theta_{n})^{-q}-1\right)

\displaystyle\leq q2^{q+1}r_{n}^{-q}\theta_{n}

\displaystyle=q2^{q+1}\gamma_{n}\frac{g^{\prime}_{p,c}(r_{n})}{r_{n}^{q+1}}.

Since
q+1=p-1
and
g^{\prime}_{p,c}(r_{n})=cr_{n}^{p-1}
, we get

r_{n+1}^{-q}-r_{n}^{-q}\leq q2^{q+1}c\,\gamma_{n}.

Moreover,

w_{n}\geq\varepsilon+(1-c)\sqrt{n+1}\geq(1-c)\sqrt{n+1}.

Thus

\gamma_{n}=\frac{\eta}{w_{n}}\leq\frac{\eta}{(1-c)\sqrt{n+1}}.

Consequently, for every
n\geq\bar{n}
,

r_{n+1}^{-q}-r_{n}^{-q}\leq\frac{q2^{q+1}c\eta}{(1-c)\sqrt{n+1}}.

Summing from
\bar{n}
to
n-1
, we obtain

\displaystyle r_{n}^{-q}

\displaystyle\leq r_{\bar{n}}^{-q}+\frac{q2^{q+1}c\eta}{1-c}\sum_{k=\bar{n}}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{k+1}}

\displaystyle\leq r_{\bar{n}}^{-q}+\frac{2q2^{q+1}c\eta}{1-c}\sqrt{n}.

In the end there exists
A>0
such that, for every integer
n\geq\bar{n}
,

r_{n}^{-q}\leq A\sqrt{n},

and hence

r_{n}\geq A^{-1/q}n^{-\frac{1}{2q}}=A^{-\frac{1}{p-2}}n^{-\frac{1}{2(p-2)}}.

Finally, using the above lower bound on
r_{n}
, we obtain

F(x_{n})-F_{\star}=g_{p,c}(x_{n})=g_{p,c}(r_{n})=\frac{c}{p}r_{n}^{p}\geq C_{1}\,n^{-\frac{p}{2(p-2)}},

for every integer
n\geq\bar{n}
, where

C_{1}:=\frac{c}{p}A^{-\frac{p}{p-2}}>0.

It remains to absorb the finite number of iterations before
\bar{n}
. Since
x_{0}\neq 0
, Lemma
1
shows that the sign of
x_{n}
is preserved, and in particular
x_{n}\neq 0
for every
n
. Therefore
F(x_{n})-F_{\star}>0
for every
n
. Define

C_{2}:=\min_{1\leq n<\bar{n}}n^{\frac{p}{2(p-2)}}\bigl(F(x_{n})-F_{\star}\bigr)>0,

with the convention that
C_{2}=+\infty
if
\bar{n}\leq 1
. Setting

\kappa:=\min\{C_{1},C_{2}\}

gives,

F(x_{n})-F_{\star}\geq\kappa\,n^{-\frac{p}{2(p-2)}},

for every
n\geq 1
, and the statement follows.
∎

Corollary 1

(Failure of AdaGrad for every Hölder-smooth class)
.

Fix
\nu\in\left]0,1\right]
and
\alpha

such that

\frac{1}{2}<\alpha<\frac{1+\nu}{2}.

Then there exists
f\in C^{1,\nu}(\mathbb{R})
and
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
affine such that,
if
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is generated by AdaGrad algorithm (

(\forall\,n\in\mathbb{N})\qquad F(x_{n})-F_{\star}\geq\kappa n^{-\alpha}.

Remark 2

(Averaged iterate)
.

The same lower bound holds for the averaged iterate

\bar{x}_{n}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}x_{k}.

Indeed, the sign of
x_{n}
is preserved and
|x_{n}|
is nonincreasing. Hence

|\bar{x}_{n}|\geq|x_{n}|.

Since
F=g_{p,c}
is even and nondecreasing as a function of

, we have

F(\bar{x}_{n})-F_{\star}\geq F(x_{n})-F_{\star}.

Thus, for every integer

4
Discussion: what should be accumulated?

The lower bound above is not due to stochastic noise, nonconvexity, or lack of smoothness of
f
. Rather, it arises from using the wrong stationarity measure in a composite problem. In the construction of the pathological example, since the derivative of the smooth component does not vanish, we force the accumulator to grow linearly. Consequently, the effective stepsize satisfies

\frac{\eta}{w_{n}}=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\right),

even though the iterates approach the minimizer. This is the critical issue: as an example, in the Lipschitz-smooth setting, the textbook proximal gradient method uses a constant stepsize, whereas here the decay of the effective stepsize is unavoidable and prevents the standard convergence rates from being recovered.

A more suitable quantity for the accumulation mechanism in the composite setting is the gradient mapping, introduced in this context by
1
;
7
. For a fixed
\gamma>0
, define the gradient mapping

\mathcal{G}_{\gamma}(x):=\frac{1}{\gamma}\left(x-\operatorname{prox}_{\gamma\varphi}\bigl(x-\gamma f^{\prime}(x)\bigr)\right).

This quantity satisfies

\mathcal{G}_{\gamma}(x_{\star})=0

whenever
x_{\star}
is a minimizer of the composite objective
f+\varphi
and the gradient mapping is expected to vanish as the iterates converge to a solution. Thus an adaptive mechanism that accumulates

\sum_{k=0}^{n}|\mathcal{G}_{\eta/w_{k}}(x_{k})|^{2}

would not keep adding a nonzero constant merely because
f^{\prime}(x_{\star})\neq 0
.

An alternative way to avoid the same pathology is to accumulate gradient differences rather than gradient magnitudes. This is the idea behind AdaGrad-Diff in
(
2
)
. In the scalar case, the difference-based metric takes the form

w_{n}^{\mathrm{diff}}=\varepsilon+\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}|f^{\prime}(x_{k})-f^{\prime}(x_{k-1})|^{2}\right)^{1/2},\qquad f^{\prime}(x_{-1})=0.

The crucial distinction is that, even if the gradient magnitudes
|f^{\prime}(x_{n})|
do not vanish, the successive differences still decay at the scale needed for appropriate convergence rate. In the Lipschitz-smooth case, these differences are square-summable, so the corresponding stepsize remains of constant order. In the Hölder-smooth case
f\in C^{1,\nu}
, it is not difficult to show, following the ideas in
(
2
)
, that the growth is instead

{w_{n}^{\mathrm{diff}}}=\mathcal{O}\left(n^{\frac{1-\nu}{2}}\right).

This is precisely aligned with the stepsize decay associated with the Hölder-smooth setting for unconstrained optimization problems considered in
(
6
)
.
Therefore the metric is not forced to grow linearly simply because the limiting gradient of the smooth part is nonzero.

In this sense, both gradient-mapping accumulation and AdaGrad-Diff-type accumulation target quantities that are better aligned with composite optimality. The example above shows that this distinction is not merely cosmetic: for standard gradient accumulation, the mismatch can rule out the standard
\mathcal{O}(n^{-(1+\nu)/2})
rate even in a deterministic one-dimensional convex Hölder-smooth composite problem.

5
Conclusion

We have provided an example showing that Adagrad does not adapt to the level of Hölder-smoothness of the objective function in a composite optimization setting.
The critique relies on the fact that the gradient of the smooth part may not vanish at a minimizer for a composite problem.
Our example suggests that, in the composite setting, adaptive mechanisms should accumulate quantities consistent with the composite optimality condition. Two natural candidates are the gradient mapping and successive gradient differences. The gradient mapping vanishes at composite minimizers, while successive gradient differences do not accumulate a nonzero limiting gradient. Both therefore avoid the failure mechanism identified here: the linear growth of the gradient accumulator even as the iterates converge to a composite minimizer with
f^{\prime}(x_{\star})\neq 0
.
