1.0 Summary
 This request was for physical samples collected through the U.S. Department of Energy (DOE)
 Atmospheric Radiation Measurement (ARM) User Facility tethered balloon system (TBS) deployments
 and DOE Ice Nucleating Particle (INP) Mentor Program during the Surface Atmosphere Integrated Field
 Laboratory (SAIL) campaign from 2021 to 2023 near Crested Butte, Colorado. Additional analysis was
 performed on these samples to quantify and type INPs using the same methods as the DOE INP Mentor
 Program. The overall goals of the associated project (DE-SC0024202) funded by DOE Atmospheric
 System Research (ASR) are:
 1. Quantify the annual cycle of INP concentrations (as functions of temperature) by source type and in
 the context of cloud condensation nuclei (CCN) and total aerosol populations in the SAIL
 environment, with a special focus on measuring biological particles and biological INPs, and episodes
 of biomass burning and dust transport influences.
 2. Distinguish and quantify the contributions of cloud seeding and snowmaking activities to the
 observed INPs in winter, especially artificial INPs active at small supercooling.
 3. Determine vertical profiles of total aerosols, CCN, and INPs, to link surface-based observations to
 those most relevant at cloud base and therefore most relevant to cloud microphysical evolution.
 4. Synthesize data into new parameterizations describing INP populations and their relationships to
 ambient aerosols so that the results can be applied to any region, and to transfer these descriptions to
 collaborators who will incorporate them into numerical models for study of the corresponding
 aerosol-cloud-precipitation interactions.

 INP analysis was performed successfully, with significant results described below in section 2.0.
 Additional analysis was attempted on several of these samples to quantify silver iodide seeding materials
 through high-sensitivity inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-MS) measurements of silver
 content. We had some success on precipitation and source samples using this method, but silver content
 of the aerosol samples was too low to quantify.

2.0 Results
 In total, 69 analyses were performed on DOE INP mentor samples from the SAIL campaign under this
 request, including 25 untreated samples, 33 heat treatments, and 11 hydrogen peroxide treatments. Two
 TBS samples were analyzed without treatment. Data for 25 INP mentor samples and 2 TBS samples have
 been uploaded to ARM Data Discovery, together with the other SAIL INP mentor samples, for easy
 access by other users. The remaining INP data will be uploaded to ARM Data Discovery soon after
 quality checks and data compilation.

 Based on the samples analyzed under this project and the SAIL INP mentor data set, the major scientific
 results are summarized below. The primary findings have been published in Zhou et al. (2026).

 1

1. Seasonal variability of natural INPs in the Rocky Mountain region.
 Nearly two years of observations provided a long-term characterization of natural INPs in the Upper
 Colorado River Basin (Figure 1). After excluding samples influenced by artificial cloud seeding and
 snowmaking activities, natural INP concentrations showed a clear seasonal cycle, with low
 concentrations in winter, increasing in spring, peaking in summer, decreasing in autumn, and
 returning to low levels in winter.

Figure 1. INP concentrations (sL-1) at temperatures from −7.5 °C to −27.5 °C (interval of 2.5°C) for all
 samples analyzed during the SAIL campaign. The grey shadows show samples collected
 during cloud seeding and affected by snowmaking activities. Reproduced from Zhou et al.
 (2026) under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 License.

2. Contributions of biological/heat-labile and organic INPs.
 Heat and H2O2 treatments identified the contributions of different INP types. Heat treatment reduced
 INP concentrations at warm temperatures (> −15 °C) by 90% on average, indicating that INPs active
 at these temperatures were dominated by biological/heat-labile INPs. H2O2 treatment resulted in an
 average 91% reduction in INP concentrations across the measured temperature range, suggesting a
 substantial contribution from organic INPs.

Figure 2. Comparison of INP temperature spectra from base (untreated), heat-treated, and H2O2-treated
 analyses. Reproduced from Zhou et al. (2026) under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0
 License.

 2

 3. Development of an INP parameterization.
 The long-term INP observation data set was used to develop an ice nucleation active surface site
 density (ns)-based parameterization using the surface area of particles larger than 500 nm. The
 parameterization could be useful for representing INPs dominated by organic-containing soil dust in
 other remote continental or mountainous environments.
 4. Impacts of artificial cloud seeding and snowmaking activities.
 Several winter samples were collected during periods that overlapped with ground-based cloud
 seeding activities in nearby areas. These samples showed higher INP concentrations at cold activation
 temperature ranges than natural winter samples. In addition, eight samples showed INP spectra
 similar to those of Snomax, a commercial ice nucleating product used for snowmaking. P. syringae
 was detected by quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) in some of these samples.
 5. Linking biological particles to INPs
 INP measurements under this request were also used in a collaborative SAIL study combining
 single-particle microscopy with machine learning. Higher abundances of biological particles with
 substantial reductions in INP concentrations after heat treatment provided additional evidence for the
 contribution of biological particles to INPs during the SAIL campaign.

3.0 Further Research Opportunities
 Further research could identify the specific biological components responsible for the observed biological
 INPs and determine whether they are associated with soil dust. Additional analyses combining cloud
 seeding-affected samples with seeding source samples and precipitation samples could help better
 characterize the sources, transport, and spatial distribution of seeding-related INPs, and better constrain
 the contribution of seeding materials in the regions.

5.0 Lessons Learned
 Having access to collected samples from the INP Mentor Program, even those that were not analyzed,
 proved very valuable in this case to support the generation of a higher-quality public data set that allowed
 for the results and publications described above, as well as future works.
