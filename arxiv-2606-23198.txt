Incremental Learning in Mirror Flows
Raphaël Berthier, Loucas Pillaud-Vivien

Abstract
We study mirror flows generated by a convex quadratic loss and a general convex lower semicontinuous mirror potential. We show that, when initialized near the boundary of the domain of the mirror potential, their rescaled trajectories converge to a limiting mirror flow whose potential is the indicator function of the domain. In this limit, the primal variable minimizes the loss over a time-dependent hypothesis set: the subdifferential of the support function of the domain, evaluated at the dual variable. This characterization provides a general mechanism for incremental learning in mirror flows.



1
Introduction

Context.

Neural networks trained with gradient descent often learn solutions of increasing complexity: the model first captures simple structure, then progressively incorporates finer details
[
AJB+17
,
KKN+19
,
ZSL25
]
. This incremental learning phenomenon, often visible as plateaus in the training loss separated by rapid transitions, has attracted significant theoretical attention.

The most detailed analyses of incremental learning have been carried out for diagonal linear networks, including precise descriptions of transition times and plateau levels
[
Ber23
,
PF23
]
. This level of detail is possible because the training dynamics of these networks reduce to a mirror flow
[
WGL+20
]
. Mirror flows themselves feature incremental learning when initialized near the boundary of the domain of the mirror potential. This paper gives a rigorous description of this phenomenon for a broad class of mirror flows, thereby generalizing the previous analyses of diagonal linear networks.

Contributions.

We study mirror flows associated with a convex quadratic loss and a general convex lower semicontinuous mirror potential. We prove that, when the mirror flow is initialized near the boundary of the domain of the mirror potential, the rescaled dynamics converge to a limiting mirror flow whose potential is the indicator function of the domain. We establish existence and uniqueness for these limiting dynamics, building on the subgradient-flow theory of
[
Bré73
]
. We show that, in the limit, the primal variable minimizes the loss over a time-varying hypothesis set—the subdifferential of the support function of the domain, evaluated at the dual variable—which evolves throughout training. This evolution is the mechanism behind incremental learning. The result yields several insights.

First, it provides a unified and more rigorous treatment of incremental learning in diagonal linear networks as a special case of mirror flows on the non-negative orthant with entropic potential.

Second, it enables the study of new geometries. We analyze the von Neumann entropic potential on the positive semidefinite cone. When the mirror flow is initialized near the boundary point
0
, we obtain an incremental learning procedure through successive rank increases. Notably, the incremental learning regime on the positive semidefinite cone is qualitatively different from the non-negative orthant case: the primal variable evolves continuously within each fixed-rank phase, rather than jumping between stationary points. This shows that incremental learning can arise from an alternation of slow and rapid dynamics, beyond the saddle-to-saddle dynamics that have already been widely studied
[
JGŞ+21
,
LLL21
]
.

We compare this mirror flow on the positive semidefinite cone with matrix factorization, which also features incremental learning through successive rank increases and for which a precise description is notoriously difficult
[
LLL21
,
GWB+17
,
ACHL19
,
RC20
]
.

Overall, diagonal linear networks are a special case of both mirror flows—for the entropic potential on the non-negative orthant—and matrix factorization—in the diagonal case. This paper suggests that the mirror-flow viewpoint is the sharper framework for analyzing their incremental learning behavior. Consequently, one should be cautious when extrapolating results from diagonal linear networks to matrix factorization or to other neural architectures.

, and has been

. Beyond linear

[

]

, and for transformers learning in-context

-grams

Convex-analysis reminders and notation

Convex analysis.

Let
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a convex set. We denote by
\aff C
the affine hull of
C
, the smallest affine set containing
C
. We denote by
\ri C
the relative interior of
C
, the interior of
C
relative to
\aff C
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be a convex function. We denote by
\dom f=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid f(x)<+\infty\}
the domain of
f
. We say that
f
is proper if
\dom f\neq\emptyset
. We say that
f
is lower semicontinuous if, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and all sequences
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converging to
x
,
f(x)\leqslant\liminf_{n\to\infty}f(x_{n})
. We denote by
\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the set of convex, proper, lower semicontinuous functions from
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to
\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
. We now fix
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

We denote by
\partial f(x)=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},f(y)\geqslant f(x)+\langle z,y-x\rangle\}
the subdifferential of
f
at
x
. We denote by
\dom\partial f=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\partial f(x)\neq\emptyset\}
the domain of
\partial f
. The subdifferential generalizes the gradient: if
f
is differentiable at
x
, then
\partial f(x)=\{\nabla f(x)\}

[
BC17
, Prop. 17.31]
.

The Fenchel conjugate of
f
is the convex function
f^{*}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
defined by
f^{*}(w)=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle w,x\rangle-f(x)
. Fenchel conjugacy preserves
\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and is an involution on this class: if
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then
f^{*}\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
(f^{*})^{*}=f

[
Roc70
, Coro. 12.2.1]
. Moreover, for all
x,w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
w\in\partial f(x)
if and only if
x\in\partial f^{*}(w)

[
BC17
, Coro. 16.30]
.

Let
C
be a closed nonempty convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We denote by
\iota_{C}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
the indicator function of
C
, defined as
\iota_{C}(x)=0
if
x\in C
and
\iota_{C}(x)=+\infty
otherwise. We denote by
\sigma_{C}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
the support function of
C
, defined as
\sigma_{C}(w)=\sup_{x\in C}\langle w,x\rangle
. The indicator and support functions are proper, convex, and lower semicontinuous. Moreover, they are conjugate to each other:
\iota_{C}^{*}=\sigma_{C}
and
\sigma_{C}^{*}=\iota_{C}

[
Roc70
, Thm. 13.2]
.

Other notation.

We denote by
\Argmin_{x\in C}f(x)
the set of minimizers of
f
on
C
. If this set is a singleton, we denote by
\argmin_{x\in C}f(x)
its unique element.

If
M
is a positive semidefinite matrix, we denote by
M^{\dagger}
its Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse, by
M^{1/2}
its positive semidefinite square root, by
\Span M
the span of its columns, and by
\ker M
its kernel.

For
p\in\{1,2\}
, an interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, and a measure
\mu
on
I
, we denote by
L^{p}(I,\mathbb{R}^{d},\mu)
the space of functions
f:I\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\int_{I}\|f(t)\|^{p}\mathrm{d}\mu(t)<+\infty
. When
\mu
has density
g(t)
with respect to the Lebesgue measure, we write
\mu=g(t)\mathrm{d}t
.

We write
x\geqslant 0
(resp.
x>0
) to mean that all coordinates of
x
are nonnegative (resp. positive). If
x
is a real number, we denote by
x_{+}=\max(x,0)
its positive part and by
x_{-}=\max(-x,0)
its negative part.

When
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we denote by
\varphi(x)=(\varphi(x_{1}),\dots,\varphi(x_{d}))
the componentwise application of
\varphi
to
x
. This convention is used, for instance, for
\varphi=\log
,
\varphi=\exp
,
\varphi=(\cdot)_{+}
, and
\varphi=(\cdot)_{-}
.

We denote by
\mathbf{1}=(1,\dots,1)
the vector of ones, whose dimension is implicit from the context.

If
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we denote by
x\circ y
the componentwise product of
x
and
y
.

3
Incremental learning in mirror flows

Let
\ell
denote a convex quadratic function

\ell(x)=\frac{1}{2}\langle x,Mx\rangle-\langle q,x\rangle+c\,,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,,\qquad d\geqslant 1\,,

where
M\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is a positive semidefinite matrix,
q\in\Span M
(so that
\ell
is bounded below), and
c\in\mathbb{R}
.
Consider
h\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We denote
C=\dom h
. We assume that
C
is closed. The functions
\ell
and
h
will be referred to as the loss and the mirror potential, respectively.

3.1
Mirror flows

Let
w:\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
. If
h
is a smooth function, we say that
w
is a solution of the
mirror flow
with mirror potential
h
and loss
\ell
if there exists
x:\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that for all
t\geqslant 0
,
w
is differentiable at
t
and

(3.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}w}{\mathrm{d}t}(t)=-\nabla\ell(x(t))\,,

\displaystyle w(t)=\nabla h(x(t))\,.

See
[
NY83
]
for the historical introduction. The mirror flow can be seen as a generalization of the gradient flow constrained to
C
, through a change of geometry induced by the mirror potential
h
.

Example 3.1

(logistic differential equation)
.

As a first illustration of the results of this work, we use the logistic differential equation as a running example:

\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t}(t)=x(t)(1-x(t))\,,

where
x(t)\geqslant 0
.

This equation corresponds to a mirror flow. For
x\in\mathbb{R}
, define the loss
\ell(x)=\frac{1}{2}(x-1)^{2}
and the entropic mirror potential
h(x)=x\log x-x
for
x\geqslant 0
(with the convention
0\log 0=0
) and
h(x)=+\infty
otherwise. The logistic differential equation can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}w}{\mathrm{d}t}(t)=1-x(t)\,,

\displaystyle w(t)=\log x(t)\,,

which corresponds to Eq. (
3.1
):
w(t)=\log x(t)
is a solution of the mirror flow with mirror potential
h
and loss
\ell
.

The logistic differential equation can be solved in closed form: its positive solutions are
x(t)=f(t-t_{0})
for some
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, where
f(t)=1/(1+e^{-t})
is the logistic function. Thus little is gained by applying our results to this equation, but it provides a simple running example. More substantial examples are given in Section
4
.

In this paper, we need to generalize the definition of a mirror flow. We no longer assume that
h
is smooth.

Definition 3.2

(mirror flow)
.

Let
w:\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We say that
w
is a solution of the
mirror flow
with mirror potential
h
and loss
\ell
if there exists
x:\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that for almost every (a.e.)
t\geqslant 0
,
w
is differentiable at
t
and

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}w}{\mathrm{d}t}(t)=-\nabla\ell(x(t))\,,\qquad w(t)\in\partial h(x(t))\,.

We refer to
w
as the
dual variable
and
x
as a
primal variable
of the mirror flow.

Example 3.3

(logistic differential equation, continued)
.

Consider the following mirror flow related to the logistic differential equation. For
x\in\mathbb{R}
, take the same loss
\ell(x)=\frac{1}{2}(x-1)^{2}
as in Example
3.1
, but replace the entropic mirror potential by
h(x)=\iota_{\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}}(x)
. Then

\partial h(x)=\partial\iota_{\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}}(x)=\begin{cases}\{0\}&\text{if }x>0\,,\\
\mathbb{R}_{\leqslant 0}&\text{if }x=0\,,\\
\emptyset&\text{if }x<0\,.\end{cases}

As a consequence,
w
is a solution of the mirror flow with mirror potential
h
, loss
\ell
, and primal variable
x
if and only if for a.e.
t\geqslant 0
,
w
is differentiable at
t
and

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}w}{\mathrm{d}t}(t)=1-x(t)\,,

\displaystyle w(t)\leqslant 0\,,

\displaystyle x(t)\geqslant 0\,,

\displaystyle w(t)x(t)=0\,.

Here, the differential inclusion condition of the mirror flow takes the form of sign constraints on
w
and
x
with a complementarity slackness condition. A solution of this mirror flow initialized from
w(0)=-1
is given by
w(t)=\min(-1+t,0)
, with associated primal variable

(3.2)

x(t)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }t<1\,,\\
1&\text{if }t\geqslant 1\,.\end{cases}

In Theorem
3.5
below, we show that this solution
w(t)
is the unique solution among Lipschitz functions and that the associated primal variable
x(t)
is essentially unique.

In this example, the primal solution
x(t)
of the mirror flow is the limit of the time-rescaled sigmoid function
x^{\mu}(t)=f(\mu(t-1))
as
\mu\to+\infty
(pointwise, for all
t\neq 1
). This turns out to be a general phenomenon: mirror flows associated with non-smooth mirror potentials naturally arise as limits of mirror flows with smooth mirror potentials. This statement is made concrete in Section
3.2
below and motivates the generalized definition of mirror flows proposed in Definition
3.2
.

As the function
\ell
is quadratic, a mirror flow can be seen as a subgradient flow in the dual variable.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
w:\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Assume that
w(0)\in\ri\dom h^{*}+\Span M
. The following statements are equivalent.

(a)

w
is a solution of the mirror flow with mirror potential
h
and loss
\ell
.

(b)

For a.e.
t\geqslant 0
,
w
is differentiable at
t
and

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}w}{\mathrm{d}t}(t)+M\partial\Phi(w(t))\ni 0\,,

\displaystyle\text{where }\Phi(w)=h^{*}(w)-\langle M^{\dagger}q,w\rangle\,.

(c)

Define
u(t)=(M^{\dagger})^{1/2}(w(t)-w(0))
. For a.e.
t\geqslant 0
,
u
is differentiable at
t
and

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}t}(t)+\partial\varphi(u(t))\ni 0\,,

\displaystyle\text{where }\varphi(u)=h^{*}\left(w(0)+M^{1/2}u\right)-\left\langle(M^{\dagger})^{1/2}q,u\right\rangle\,.

Statement
(b)
shows that the dual variable
w
follows a differential inclusion. When
M
is positive definite, this differential inclusion is a preconditioned subgradient flow. However, the connection to subgradient flows is not straightforward when
M
is not positive definite. As we want to encompass this latter case, we prefer statement
(c)
for technical reasons:
w
can be written as
w(0)+M^{1/2}u
where
u
follows a subgradient flow (see Def.
5.2
for a definition of the subgradient flow).

This proposition is useful for building intuition about the dual dynamics; it is also key to our analyses of mirror flows and their limits, as it allows us to leverage corresponding results on subgradient flows. For instance, this reduction yields the following result.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
w_{0}\in\dom\partial h^{*}\cap(\ri\dom h^{*}+\Span M)
. There exists a unique Lipschitz solution
w:\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
of the mirror flow with mirror potential
h
and loss
\ell
such that
w(0)=w_{0}
.

Let
x
be an associated primal variable. Then for a.e.
t\geqslant 0
,

x(t)\in\underset{y\in\partial h^{*}(w(t))}{\Argmin}\ell(y)\,.

If
h^{*}
is differentiable on
\dom\partial h^{*}
, the minimization statement is vacuous as
\partial h^{*}(w(t))=\{\nabla h^{*}(w(t))\}
and
x(t)=\nabla h^{*}(w(t))
: we get no additional property from the definition of a mirror flow. However, we will study scalings of mirror flows that converge to mirror flows for which
h^{*}
is not differentiable and thus this statement is not vacuous: typically,
\partial h^{*}(w(t))
will not be a singleton. This result will be key in showing that mirror flows perform incremental learning in these scalings: they minimize
\ell
over a hypothesis set
\partial h^{*}(w(t))
that varies through training (typically with some sharp transitions).

While there exists a solution such that the dual variable
w
is Lipschitz, the associated primal variable
x
might not even be continuous (see Example
3.3
).

We note that the primal variable
x(t)
is not necessarily unique. However,
\nabla\ell(x(t))
is a.e. unique as it is a.e. the derivative of the unique dual variable
w(t)
. Similarly, by the minimization property above,
\ell(x(t))
is a.e. unique.

In the case where
M
is invertible, the initialization requirement is
w_{0}\in\dom\partial h^{*}
. Moreover, as
\ell
is strictly convex in this case, the solution to
\min_{y\in\partial h^{*}(w(t))}\ell(y)
is unique, so that the primal variable
x
is uniquely defined a.e.

By
[
BC17
, Corollary 16.18]
,
\ri\dom h^{*}\subset\dom\partial h^{*}
; hence
w_{0}\in\ri\dom h^{*}
is a sufficient condition for the theorem above.

Example 3.6

(logistic differential equation, continued)
.

We return to the setting of Example
3.3
where
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
\ell(x)=\frac{1}{2}(x-1)^{2}
and
h(x)=\iota_{\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}}(x)
. The unique Lipschitz solution is
w(t)=\min(-1+t,0)
. We analyze the minimization property of Theorem
3.5
.

We have
h^{*}=\iota_{\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}}^{*}=\sigma_{\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}}=\iota_{\mathbb{R}_{\leqslant 0}}
. Thus

\partial h^{*}(w)=\begin{cases}\{0\}&\text{if }w<0\,,\\
\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}&\text{if }w=0\,,\\
\emptyset&\text{if }w>0\,.\end{cases}

By Theorem
3.5
, we have:

•

for a.e.
t<1
,
w(t)<0
and thus
x(t)\in\partial h^{*}(w(t))=\{0\}
, i.e.
x(t)=0
.

•

for a.e.
t\geqslant 1
,
w(t)=0
and thus
x(t)=\argmin_{y\in\partial h^{*}(w(t))}\ell(y)=\argmin_{y\geqslant 0}\ell(y)=1
.

These statements are consistent with the solution found in Eq. (
3.2
).

3.2
Limiting dynamics

We now assume that
\dom h^{*}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
; note that by
[
BC17
, Coro. 16.18]
we also have
\dom\partial h^{*}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
\varepsilon>0
be a small parameter, and let
w^{\varepsilon}
be the unique solution of the mirror flow with mirror potential
h
and loss
\ell
, initialized from
w^{\varepsilon}(0)=w^{\varepsilon}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
x^{\varepsilon}
denote an associated primal variable. The reader may think of
h
as smooth, as is the case in all our examples; smoothness is, however, not required for the result below.

Assume that
\mu^{\varepsilon}:=\|w^{\varepsilon}_{0}\|\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\to 0]{}+\infty
. This assumption is often associated with a primal initialization converging to the boundary of
C
.

Example 3.7

(logistic differential equation, continued)
.

We consider the logistic differential equation introduced in Example
3.1
, which is a mirror flow with entropic mirror potential
h(x)=x\log x-x
for
x\geqslant 0
and
h(x)=+\infty
otherwise. The domain of the mirror potential is
C=\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}
. We initialize the primal variable as
x^{\varepsilon}_{0}=\varepsilon
, which converges to the boundary of
C
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. This gives the dual initialization
w^{\varepsilon}_{0}=\log\varepsilon
, so that, if
\varepsilon\leqslant 1
,
\mu^{\varepsilon}=|w^{\varepsilon}_{0}|=\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\to 0]{}+\infty
.

More generally, when
h
is essentially smooth
[
Roc70
, Sec. 26]
, the divergence of
\mu^{\varepsilon}
is tied to the convergence of the primal initialization to the boundary of
C
. By definition,
h
is essentially smooth if
C
has nonempty interior,
h
is differentiable on the interior of
C
, and for any sequence
(x_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in the interior of
C
converging to its boundary,
\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\nabla h(x_{k})\|=+\infty
. Under this assumption, it is sufficient to initialize
x^{\varepsilon}_{0}
in the interior of
C
and let it converge to the boundary of
C
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. A sufficient condition for essential smoothness is Legendre type. Example
3.7
and all examples in Section
4
are of Legendre type.

We return to our general discussion on the limiting dynamics. In the asymptotic regime
\varepsilon\to 0
, we have
\mu^{\varepsilon}\to+\infty
, and we rescale the primal and dual variables in time and space as follows:

\displaystyle\overline{w}^{\varepsilon}(s)=\frac{1}{\mu^{\varepsilon}}w^{\varepsilon}(\mu^{\varepsilon}s)\,,

\displaystyle\overline{x}^{\varepsilon}(s)=x^{\varepsilon}(s\mu^{\varepsilon})\,,

\displaystyle s\geqslant 0\,.

This time rescaling is the one required to obtain a nontrivial limit and incremental learning. Indeed, the rescaled functions themselves follow a mirror flow: for a.e.
s\geqslant 0
,
\overline{w}^{\varepsilon}
is differentiable at
s
and

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\overline{w}^{\varepsilon}}{\mathrm{d}s}(s)=-\nabla\ell(\overline{x}^{\varepsilon}(s))\,,

\displaystyle\overline{w}^{\varepsilon}(s)\in\partial\left(\frac{1}{\mu^{\varepsilon}}h\right)(\overline{x}^{\varepsilon}(s))\,,

see Section
5.3
for the computations. Informally, taking the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
, we have
\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu^{\varepsilon}}h\to\iota_{C}
, and thus it is natural to expect convergence to the mirror flow with mirror potential
\iota_{C}
and loss
\ell
, initialized at
\overline{w}_{0}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\overline{w}^{\varepsilon}(0)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\mu^{\varepsilon}}w^{\varepsilon}_{0}
: for a.e.
s\geqslant 0
,
\overline{w}
is differentiable at
s
and

(3.3)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\overline{w}}{\mathrm{d}s}(s)=-\nabla\ell(\overline{x}(s))\,,

\displaystyle\overline{w}(s)\in\partial\iota_{C}(\overline{x}(s))\,.

The following theorem specifies under which assumptions and in which sense the above derivation is true.

Theorem 3.8
.

Denote
\overline{w}_{0}^{\varepsilon}=\overline{w}^{\varepsilon}(0)=\frac{1}{\mu^{\varepsilon}}w^{\varepsilon}_{0}
. We assume that:

(i)

as
\varepsilon\to 0
,
\overline{w}^{\varepsilon}_{0}
converges to some
\overline{w}_{0}\in\dom\partial\sigma_{C}\cap\left(\ri\dom\sigma_{C}+\Span M\right)
,

(ii)

for
\varepsilon>0
small enough,
\overline{w}_{0}^{\varepsilon}-\overline{w}_{0}\in\aff\dom\sigma_{C}
.

Then there exists a unique Lipschitz solution
\overline{w}
of the mirror flow with mirror potential
\iota_{C}
and loss
\ell
, initialized at
\overline{w}(0)=\overline{w}_{0}
, as written in Eq. (
3.3
). Let
\overline{x}
be an associated primal variable. Let
S>0
. Then:

(a)

\overline{w}^{\varepsilon}\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\to 0]{}\overline{w}
uniformly on
[0,S]
.

(b)

We have the convergences

\displaystyle\nabla\ell(\overline{x}^{\varepsilon}(s))

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\to 0]{}\nabla\ell(\overline{x}(s))

\displaystyle\text{in }L^{2}([0,S],\mathbb{R}^{d},s\mathrm{d}s),

\displaystyle\ell(\overline{x}^{\varepsilon}(s))

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\to 0]{}\ell(\overline{x}(s))=\min_{y\in\partial\sigma_{C}(\overline{w}(s))}\ell(y)

\displaystyle\text{in }L^{1}([0,S],\mathbb{R},s\mathrm{d}s).

(c)

Let
\overline{y}:[0,S]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an accumulation point of
\overline{x}^{\varepsilon}
in the sense of essential pointwise convergence: there exists a subsequence
\varepsilon_{k}\xrightarrow[k\to\infty]{}0
such that, for a.e.
s\in[0,S]
,
\overline{x}^{\varepsilon_{k}}(s)\xrightarrow[k\to\infty]{}\overline{y}(s)
. Then, for a.e.
s\in[0,S]
,
\overline{y}(s)\in\underset{y\in\partial\sigma_{C}(\overline{w}(s))}{\Argmin}\ell(y)
.

As before,
\overline{w}_{0}\in\ri\dom\sigma_{C}
is a sufficient condition for requirement
(i)
of the theorem.

In the case where
M
is invertible, requirement
(i)
reduces to
\overline{w}_{0}\in\dom\partial\sigma_{C}
. Moreover, in this case, the primal variable
\overline{x}(s)
is uniquely defined and
\overline{x}^{\varepsilon}
converges to
\overline{x}
in
L^{2}([0,S],\mathbb{R}^{d},s\mathrm{d}s)
by statement
(b)
.

Requirement
(ii)
is automatic whenever
\aff\dom\sigma_{C}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
(in particular, it holds in all three examples of Section
4
); it is best viewed as a non-degeneracy assumption on the approach of
\overline{w}_{0}^{\varepsilon}
to
\overline{w}_{0}
.

Example 3.9

(logistic differential equation, continued)
.

Theorem
3.8
shows that the solution of the logistic differential equation, when initialized at
x^{\varepsilon}_{0}=\varepsilon
and rescaled in time as
\overline{x}^{\varepsilon}(s)=x^{\varepsilon}(\mu^{\varepsilon}s)
with
\mu^{\varepsilon}=\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
, converges to the primal solution of the mirror flow with mirror potential
h=\iota_{\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}}
given in Eq. (
3.2
). In this example, the convergence can also be derived by elementary means. Recall that
x^{\varepsilon}(t)=f(t-t_{0}^{\varepsilon})
where
f
is the logistic function and
t_{0}^{\varepsilon}
satisfies
f(-t_{0}^{\varepsilon})=\varepsilon
. This implies
t_{0}^{\varepsilon}\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\to 0]{}+\infty
, and thus
\varepsilon=f(-t_{0}^{\varepsilon})=\frac{1}{1+e^{t_{0}^{\varepsilon}}}=e^{-t_{0}^{\varepsilon}}(1+o(1))
. Taking logarithms, we get
t_{0}^{\varepsilon}=\mu^{\varepsilon}+o(1)
. We thus obtain

\overline{x}^{\varepsilon}(s)=x^{\varepsilon}(\mu^{\varepsilon}s)=f(\mu^{\varepsilon}s-t_{0}^{\varepsilon})=f((s-1)\mu^{\varepsilon}+o(1))\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\to 0]{}\begin{cases}0&\text{if }s<1\,,\\
1&\text{if }s>1\,.\end{cases}

In other words, the rescaled primal variable
\overline{x}^{\varepsilon}
converges a.e. pointwise to the primal solution of the limiting mirror flow, provided in Eq. (
3.2
). A similar computation shows the convergence of the rescaled dual variable.

4.1
Optimization on the non-negative orthant
\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}^{d}

4.1.1
Limiting dynamics

We consider the minimization of the loss
\ell(x)=\frac{1}{2}\langle x,Mx\rangle-\langle q,x\rangle+c
, with
M
positive semidefinite and
q\in\Span M
, via the mirror flow with entropic mirror potential
h(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}(x_{i}\log x_{i}-x_{i})
and domain
\dom h=\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}^{d}
. We also have
\nabla h(x)=\log x
, with inverse
\nabla h^{*}(w)=e^{w}
, and Fenchel conjugate
h^{*}(w)=\sum_{k=1}^{d}e^{w_{k}}
, with
\dom h^{*}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Support function.

We have
C=\dom h=\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}^{d}
,
\sigma_{C}=\sigma_{\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}^{d}}=\iota_{\mathbb{R}_{\leqslant 0}^{d}}
, and
\dom\sigma_{C}=\dom\partial\sigma_{C}=\mathbb{R}_{\leqslant 0}^{d}
. Requirement
(i)
of Theorem
3.8
is satisfied if
\overline{w}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{<0}^{d}
. Moreover,
\aff\dom\sigma_{C}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, so requirement
(ii)
of Theorem
3.8
is automatically satisfied.

The limiting dynamics.

We initialize the mirror flow at
x_{0}^{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon\mathbf{1}
, that is,
w_{0}^{\varepsilon}=(\log\varepsilon)\mathbf{1}
where
\mathbf{1}=(1,\ldots,1)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Thus
\mu^{\varepsilon}=\|w_{0}^{\varepsilon}\|=\sqrt{d}\log(1/\varepsilon)\to+\infty
and
\overline{w}_{0}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}w_{0}^{\varepsilon}/\mu^{\varepsilon}=-\mathbf{1}/\sqrt{d}
. Hence requirements
(i)
and
(ii)
are satisfied, and applying Theorem
3.8
, the limiting mirror flow reads:

(4.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\overline{w}}{\mathrm{d}s}(s)=-\nabla\ell(\overline{x}(s))\,,

\displaystyle\overline{x}(s)\in\partial\iota_{\mathbb{R}_{\leqslant 0}^{d}}(\overline{w}(s))\,.

Primal dynamics and sparsity.

For
w\leqslant 0
, the set
\partial\iota_{\mathbb{R}_{\leqslant 0}^{d}}(w)
is the convex set of vectors complementary to
w
in the following sense:

\partial\iota_{\mathbb{R}_{\leqslant 0}^{d}}(w)=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}^{d}\,\middle|\,\langle x,w\rangle=0\right\}\,.

Therefore, the limiting primal trajectory satisfies
\overline{x}_{i}(s)=0
whenever
\overline{w}_{i}(s)<0
, and
\overline{x}_{i}(s)\geqslant 0
whenever
\overline{w}_{i}(s)=0
. Define the
active set

I(w)=\{i\mid w_{i}=0\}
; then
\overline{x}(s)
is the minimizer of
\ell
restricted to coordinates in
I(\overline{w}(s))
with the constraint
x\geqslant 0
:

\overline{x}(s)\in\Argmin\left\{\ell(y)\,\middle|\,y\geqslant 0,\,\supp(y)\subset I(\overline{w}(s))\right\}\,.

The trajectory
\overline{w}(s)
is piecewise linear, and the active set
I(\overline{w}(s))
is piecewise constant, with changes at the times when some coordinate
\overline{w}_{i}(s)
hits
0
. Consequently, the limiting primal trajectory
\overline{x}(s)
can be chosen piecewise constant; in each phase it is the solution of a constrained quadratic optimization problem.

Implementation.

We simulate the original mirror flow for two values
\varepsilon\in\{10^{-20},10^{-100}\}
and compare it to the limiting dynamics in Eq. (
4.1
). The result is shown in Figure
1
. In this simulation,
M\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, with
d=8
, is generated randomly as a Wishart matrix with
n=6
degrees of freedom and scale matrix
\I_{d}
, that is
M=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}a_{i}^{\top}
, where the
a_{i}
are i.i.d. vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
distributed according to
\mathcal{N}(0,\I_{d})
. The vector
q\in\Span M
is generated as
q=Mz
, where
z
is a standard Gaussian vector. The visualization of the dual trajectory of
\overline{w}
is given in Appendix
C.4.1
. Further implementation details are given in Appendix
C.1
.

4.1.2
Connection to diagonal linear networks

Mirror flows on the non-negative orthant with the entropic mirror potential arise in the study of diagonal linear networks (DLNs)
[
LWLA22
]
. This connection has been central to analyses of incremental learning in DLNs
[
Ber23
,
PF23
]
. We recall it here and interpret our results in this context.

A two-layer DLN implements a linear relation
b=\langle x,a\rangle
between its input
a\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and its output
b\in\mathbb{R}
. However, the regression parameter
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is itself parametrized as the componentwise product
x=v\circ u
of the outer-layer weights
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and the inner-layer diagonal weights
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. When minimizing the quadratic loss
\ell(x)
, the gradient-flow dynamics are run in terms of the weights
u
and
v
, rather than in terms of
x
itself:

(4.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}t}=-\nabla_{u}\left(\ell(v\circ u)\right)\,,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}t}=-\nabla_{v}\left(\ell(v\circ u)\right)\,.

The induced dynamics on
x
can be understood through a mirror flow, and the results of this paper describe the incremental learning dynamics in the limit of small initialization.

Symmetric case.

We start with the symmetric case
u=v
. This can be interpreted as a weight-tied DLN in which both layers share the same weight; more importantly for us, it is a convenient theoretical intermediate setting in which the main ideas can be derived before turning to the asymmetric case
[
Ber25
]
.

In this case, Eqs. (
4.2
) become

(4.3)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}t}=-\nabla_{u}\left(\ell(u\circ u)\right)=-2u\circ\nabla\ell(u\circ u)\,.

The induced dynamics on
x=u\circ u
are

(4.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t}=2u\circ\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}t}=-4u\circ u\circ\nabla\ell(u\circ u)=-4x\circ\nabla\ell(x)\,,

that is,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}w}{\mathrm{d}t}=-\nabla\ell(x)\,,\qquad w=\nabla h(x)\,,

\displaystyle\text{where }h(x)=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{d}(x_{i}\log x_{i}-x_{i})\,,\qquad x\geqslant 0\,.

In words,
x
is the primal variable of a mirror flow with the entropic mirror potential (up to a multiplicative constant).

Incremental learning occurs when the primal variable
x
of the mirror flow is initialized close to the boundary of
C=\dom h=\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}^{d}
. This corresponds to (at least some of) the weights
u
being initialized at a small value. Let
\varepsilon>0
be a small parameter and
u^{\varepsilon}
denote the solution of the DLN dynamics (
4.3
) initialized from
u^{\varepsilon}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Denote
x^{\varepsilon}=u^{\varepsilon}\circ u^{\varepsilon}
the solution of the corresponding mirror flow (
4.4
), initialized from
x^{\varepsilon}_{0}=u^{\varepsilon}_{0}\circ u^{\varepsilon}_{0}
. The application of Theorem
3.8
readily gives the following result.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that, for all
i\in\{1,\dots,d\}
,
u^{\varepsilon}_{0,i}\neq 0
, and

\mu^{\varepsilon}:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\log^{2}|u^{\varepsilon}_{0,i}|\right)^{1/2}\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\to 0]{}+\infty\,.

Denote
\overline{x}^{\varepsilon}(s)=x^{\varepsilon}(\mu^{\varepsilon}s)
,
s\geqslant 0
. We assume that
\frac{1}{2\mu^{\varepsilon}}\log|u^{\varepsilon}_{0}|
converges to some limit
\overline{w}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{\leqslant 0}^{d}\cap\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{<0}+\Span M\right)
. Under this assumption, there exists a unique Lipschitz solution
\overline{w}
of the mirror flow with mirror potential
\iota_{\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}^{d}}
and loss
\ell
, initialized at
\overline{w}_{0}
. Let
\overline{x}
be an associated primal variable. Let
S>0
. Then:

(a)

We have the convergences

\displaystyle\nabla\ell(\overline{x}^{\varepsilon}(s))

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\to 0]{}\nabla\ell(\overline{x}(s))

\displaystyle\text{in }L^{2}([0,S],\mathbb{R}^{d},s\mathrm{d}s),

\displaystyle\ell(\overline{x}^{\varepsilon}(s))

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\to 0]{}\ell(\overline{x}(s))=\min_{y\geqslant 0\,,\,\langle\overline{w}(s),y\rangle=0}\ell(y)

\displaystyle\text{in }L^{1}([0,S],\mathbb{R},s\mathrm{d}s).

(b)

Let
\overline{y}:[0,S]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an accumulation point of
\overline{x}^{\varepsilon}
in the sense of essential pointwise convergence. Then, for a.e.
s\in[0,S]
,
\overline{y}(s)\in\underset{y\geqslant 0\,,\,\langle\overline{w}(s),y\rangle=0}{\Argmin}\ell(y)
.

To illustrate the results, one can choose, for instance,
u^{\varepsilon}_{0}=\sqrt{\varepsilon}\mathbf{1}
, in which case we obtain
\overline{w}_{0}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\mathbf{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{<0}\subset\mathbb{R}_{\leqslant 0}^{d}\cap\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{<0}+\Span M\right)
.

A similar description was obtained in
[
Ber23
]
for a specific class of Hessians
M
in the so-called underparameterized setting. Our generalization also covers the overparameterized setting.

The asymmetric case.

We now turn to the case
u\neq v
. Let
u^{\varepsilon}
and
v^{\varepsilon}
be the solutions of the DLN dynamics (
4.2
), initialized from
u^{\varepsilon}_{0}
and
v^{\varepsilon}_{0}
, respectively, and let
x^{\varepsilon}=v^{\varepsilon}\circ u^{\varepsilon}
be the linear parameter. The connection to mirror flows is more involved in this case; we state the result directly and postpone the derivation to the proof.

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume that, for all
i\in\{1,\dots,d\}
,
u^{\varepsilon}_{0,i}\neq\pm v^{\varepsilon}_{0,i}
, and

\mu^{\varepsilon}:=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left[\log^{2}\left|\frac{u^{\varepsilon}_{0,i}+v^{\varepsilon}_{0,i}}{2}\right|+\log^{2}\left|\frac{u^{\varepsilon}_{0,i}-v^{\varepsilon}_{0,i}}{2}\right|\right]\right)^{1/2}\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\to 0]{}+\infty\,.

Denote
\overline{x}^{\varepsilon}(s)=x^{\varepsilon}(\mu^{\varepsilon}s)
,
s\geqslant 0
. We assume that

•

\frac{1}{\mu^{\varepsilon}}\log\left|\frac{u^{\varepsilon}_{0}+v^{\varepsilon}_{0}}{2}\right|
converges to some limit
\overline{w}_{0}^{\text{pos}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{\leqslant 0}
,

•

\frac{1}{\mu^{\varepsilon}}\log\left|\frac{u^{\varepsilon}_{0}-v^{\varepsilon}_{0}}{2}\right|
converges to some limit
\overline{w}_{0}^{\text{neg}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{\leqslant 0}
, and

•

there exists
a\in\Span M
such that
\overline{w}_{0}^{\text{pos}}+a<0
and
\overline{w}_{0}^{\text{neg}}-a<0
.

We write
\overline{w}_{0}=\frac{\overline{w}_{0}^{\text{pos}}-\overline{w}_{0}^{\text{neg}}}{2}
and
\lambda=-\frac{\overline{w}_{0}^{\text{pos}}+\overline{w}_{0}^{\text{neg}}}{2}
. The third assumption above implies
\lambda>0
. Under these assumptions, there exists a unique Lipschitz solution
\overline{w}
of the mirror flow with mirror potential
h(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}|x_{i}|
and loss
\ell
, with initialization
\overline{w}(0)=\overline{w}_{0}
. Let
\overline{x}
be an associated primal variable. Let
S>0
. Then:

(a)

We have the convergences

\displaystyle\nabla\ell(\overline{x}^{\varepsilon}(s))

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\to 0]{}\nabla\ell(\overline{x}(s))

\displaystyle\text{in }L^{2}([0,S],\mathbb{R}^{d},s\mathrm{d}s),

\displaystyle\ell(\overline{x}^{\varepsilon}(s))

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\to 0]{}\ell(\overline{x}(s))

\displaystyle\text{in }L^{1}([0,S],\mathbb{R},s\mathrm{d}s),

where

\ell(\overline{x}(s))=\min\left\{\ell(y)\,\middle|\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ satisfying }\begin{cases}\text{if }\overline{w}_{i}(s)=\lambda_{i},&y_{i}\geqslant 0\,,\\
\text{if }\overline{w}_{i}(s)\in(-\lambda_{i},\lambda_{i}),&y_{i}=0\,,\\
\text{if }\overline{w}_{i}(s)=-\lambda_{i},&y_{i}\leqslant 0\,.\end{cases}\right\}\,.

(b)

Let
\overline{y}:[0,S]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an accumulation point of
\overline{x}^{\varepsilon}
in the sense of essential pointwise convergence. Then, for a.e.
s\in[0,S]
,

\overline{y}(s)\in\Argmin\left\{\ell(y)\,\middle|\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ satisfying }\begin{cases}\text{if }\overline{w}_{i}(s)=\lambda_{i},&y_{i}\geqslant 0\,,\\
\text{if }\overline{w}_{i}(s)\in(-\lambda_{i},\lambda_{i}),&y_{i}=0\,,\\
\text{if }\overline{w}_{i}(s)=-\lambda_{i},&y_{i}\leqslant 0\,.\end{cases}\right\}\,.

The proof of the theorem is given in Appendix
B
. To illustrate the theorem, take for instance
u^{\varepsilon}_{0}=2\varepsilon\mathbf{1}
and
v^{\varepsilon}_{0}=0
. Then
\mu^{\varepsilon}=\sqrt{2d}\log(1/\varepsilon)
,
\overline{w}_{0}^{\text{pos}}=\overline{w}_{0}^{\text{neg}}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2d}}\mathbf{1}
, hence
\overline{w}_{0}=0
and
\lambda=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2d}}\mathbf{1}
. The limiting dynamics correspond to a mirror flow whose mirror potential is the
\ell_{1}
-norm, up to a multiplicative constant:

(4.5)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\overline{w}}{\mathrm{d}s}(s)=-\nabla\ell(\overline{x}(s))\,,

\displaystyle\overline{w}(s)\in\frac{1}{\sqrt{2d}}\partial\left(\|\cdot\|_{1}\right)(\overline{x}(s))\,.

This corresponds to the results derived in
[
PF23
]
under a certain “general position” assumption that we do not need here.

The dynamics (
4.5
) correspond to the inverse scale space method
[
BOXG05
,
BRH07
,
BMBO13
]
. This flow is valued in sparse recovery as an unbiased alternative to the Lasso regularization path
[
ORX+16
]
. It is remarkable that diagonal linear networks implement an inverse scale space flow implicitly, through a product parametrization of the regressor and an infinitesimal initialization.

4.2
Optimization on the positive semidefinite cone
\mathcal{S}^{d}_{+}

4.2.1
Limiting dynamics

We now consider the matrix analogue. Let
\mathcal{S}^{d}
be the space of
d\times d
real symmetric matrices endowed with the trace inner product
\langle A,B\rangle=\Tr(AB)
. We consider the loss
\ell(X)=\frac{1}{2}\langle X,\mathcal{M}(X)\rangle-\langle Q,X\rangle+c
, where
\mathcal{M}:\mathcal{S}^{d}\to\mathcal{S}^{d}
is a self-adjoint positive semidefinite linear operator and
Q
is in the image of
\mathcal{M}
. Lifting
\log
and
\exp
to spectral functions on
\mathcal{S}^{d}
(acting on the eigenvalues), we define the
von Neumann entropic mirror potential
by

h(X)=\begin{cases}\Tr(X\log X-X)&\text{if }X\in\mathcal{S}^{d}_{+}\,,\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

This mirror potential is convex
[
Bha09
, Theorem 4.3.5]
. The domain of
h
is the cone of positive semidefinite matrices, i.e.
\dom h=\mathcal{S}^{d}_{+}
. We also have
\nabla h(X)=\log X
, with inverse
\nabla h^{*}(W)=\exp W
, and Fenchel conjugate
h^{*}(W)=\Tr(\exp W)
, with
\dom h^{*}=\mathcal{S}^{d}
.

Support function.

We have
C=\dom h=\mathcal{S}^{d}_{+}
and
\sigma_{C}=\sigma_{\mathcal{S}^{d}_{+}}=\iota_{\mathcal{S}^{d}_{-}}
, where
\mathcal{S}^{d}_{-}=\{V\in\mathcal{S}^{d}\mid V\preceq 0\}
is the cone of negative semidefinite matrices. Thus
\dom\sigma_{C}=\dom\partial\sigma_{C}=\mathcal{S}^{d}_{-}
. Requirement
(i)
of Theorem
3.8
is satisfied if
\overline{W}_{0}\in\{V\in\mathcal{S}^{d}\mid V\prec 0\}
, the cone of negative definite matrices. Moreover,
\aff\dom\sigma_{C}=\mathcal{S}^{d}
, so requirement
(ii)
of Theorem
3.8
is automatically satisfied.

The limiting dynamics.

We initialize the primal variable of the mirror flow at
X^{\varepsilon}_{0}=\varepsilon\I_{d}
, that is,
W^{\varepsilon}_{0}=(\log\varepsilon)\I_{d}\in\mathcal{S}^{d}_{-}
. Then
\mu^{\varepsilon}=\|W^{\varepsilon}_{0}\|=\sqrt{d}\log(1/\varepsilon)\to+\infty
and
\overline{W}_{0}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}W^{\varepsilon}_{0}/\mu^{\varepsilon}=-\I_{d}/\sqrt{d}
. Hence requirements
(i)
and
(ii)
are satisfied, and applying Theorem
3.8
, the limiting dual flow reads:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\overline{W}}{\mathrm{d}s}(s)=-\nabla\ell(\overline{X}(s))\,,

\displaystyle\overline{X}(s)\in\partial\iota_{\mathcal{S}^{d}_{-}}(\overline{W}(s))\,.

Primal dynamics and low-rank bias.

For
W\in\mathcal{S}^{d}_{-}
, the set
\partial\iota_{\mathcal{S}^{d}_{-}}(W)
is the convex set of matrices complementary to
W
in the following sense:

\partial\iota_{\mathcal{S}^{d}_{-}}(W)=\left\{X\in\mathcal{S}^{d}_{+}\,\middle|\,\Span X\subset\ker W\right\}\,.

Denoting
r(s)=\dim\ker\overline{W}(s)
, the limiting primal variable
\overline{X}(s)
is positive semidefinite with
\rank\overline{X}(s)\leqslant r(s)
and minimizes
\ell
over this convex set, that is,

\overline{X}(s)\in\Argmin\left\{\ell(Y)\,\middle|\,Y\succeq 0,\,\Span Y\subset\ker\overline{W}(s)\right\}\,.

The trajectory
\overline{W}(s)
is no longer piecewise linear. Slow dynamics occur within each fixed-rank set of predictions, and the limiting primal trajectory
\overline{X}(s)
is not piecewise constant, although it exhibits discontinuities when a new eigenvalue activates. This illustrates the
incremental learning
of the iterates, as the rank of the predictions increases through training.

Implementation.

We simulate the original mirror flow for two values
\varepsilon\in\{10^{-20},10^{-100}\}
and compare it to the limiting dynamics. The result is shown in Figure
2
. Here,
\mathcal{M}
is a Wishart operator on
\mathcal{S}^{d}
, i.e.
\mathcal{M}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}\otimes A_{i}
, where the
(A_{i})_{i\leqslant n}
are i.i.d. GOE matrices: symmetric matrices with independent
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
diagonal entries and
\mathcal{N}(0,1/2)
off-diagonal entries. The matrix
Q
is generated as
Q=\mathcal{M}(Z)
, where
Z
is an independent GOE matrix. We take
n=6
and
d=8
. The visualization of the dual trajectory of
\overline{W}
is given in Appendix
C.4.2
. Further implementation details are given in Appendix
C.1
.

4.2.2
Connection to the matrix factorization problem

We have seen in Section
4.1.2
that the mirror flow on the non-negative orthant with the entropic mirror potential corresponds to the parametrization
x=u\circ u
and a gradient flow in
u
. The matrix analogue does
not
hold: the mirror flow on
\mathcal{S}^{d}_{+}
with the von Neumann entropic mirror potential is
not
equivalent to a gradient flow in
U\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
for the parametrization
X=UU^{\top}
. Both dynamics nevertheless share a striking qualitative feature: when initialized infinitesimally close to the boundary of
\mathcal{S}^{d}_{+}
, both exhibit
incremental learning
through successive rank increases. We illustrate this empirically in Appendix
C.2
, where we run both flows side by side on the same instance of the matrix problem for four random seeds. While the qualitative incremental-rank picture is shared by both flows, the precise quantitative dynamics—the values of the intermediate saddles, the order in which eigenvalues activate, and the pacing of the activations—can differ between the two.

4.3
Optimization on the probability simplex
\Delta_{d}

To illustrate the generality of our results, we also consider mirror flows on sets with affine constraints. Consider the same quadratic loss
\ell
as in Section
4.1
, but now on the probability simplex
\Delta_{d}=\mathbb{R}^{d}_{\geqslant 0}\cap\mathcal{S}_{1}
, where
\mathcal{S}_{1}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\sum_{k=1}^{d}x_{k}=1\}
. To reflect the structure of the problem, we optimize
\ell
via the mirror flow with entropic mirror potential
h(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{d}(x_{k}\log x_{k}-x_{k})+\iota_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}(x)
, with domain
\Delta_{d}
. For
x\in\ri\Delta_{d}
, we have
\partial h(x)=\log x+\mathbb{R}\mathbf{1}
,
\dom h^{*}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\nabla h^{*}(w)=e^{w}/\|e^{w}\|_{1}
; see Lemma
C.1
for the conjugate calculation.

Support function.

We have
C=\Delta_{d}
and, for all
\overline{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\sigma_{\Delta_{d}}(\overline{w})=\max_{i}\overline{w}_{i}
. Moreover,
\dom\sigma_{\Delta_{d}}=\dom\partial\sigma_{\Delta_{d}}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Hence requirements
(i)
and
(ii)
of Theorem
3.8
are automatically satisfied.

The limiting dynamics.

We initialize the mirror flow near the first canonical vector:

x_{0}^{\varepsilon}=\left(1-\varepsilon,\frac{\varepsilon}{d-1},\ldots,\frac{\varepsilon}{d-1}\right).

Equivalently,

w_{0}^{\varepsilon}=\left(\log(1-\varepsilon),\log\frac{\varepsilon}{d-1},\ldots,\log\frac{\varepsilon}{d-1}\right),

so
\mu^{\varepsilon}=\|w_{0}^{\varepsilon}\|\sim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sqrt{d-1}\log(1/\varepsilon)
and

\overline{w}_{0}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{w_{0}^{\varepsilon}}{\mu^{\varepsilon}}=-\left(0,\frac{1}{\sqrt{d-1}},\ldots,\frac{1}{\sqrt{d-1}}\right).

Applying Theorem
3.8
, the limiting dual flow reads:

\frac{\mathrm{d}\overline{w}}{\mathrm{d}s}(s)=-\nabla\ell(\overline{x}(s))\,,\qquad\overline{x}(s)\mathrel{\in}\partial\sigma_{C}(\overline{w}(s))\,.

Primal dynamics and sparsity.

For all
w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we can define its active set of coordinates as
I(w)=\{k\mid w_{k}=\max_{i}w_{i}\}
. We have the following expression:

\partial\sigma_{\Delta_{d}}(w)=\left\{\sum_{k\in I(w)}\alpha_{k}e_{k}\,\middle|\,\sum_{k\in I(w)}\alpha_{k}=1,\,\alpha_{k}\geqslant 0\right\},

which is the convex hull of
(e_{k})_{k\in I(w)}
, where the
e_{k}
are the canonical vectors. We prove this expression in Lemma
C.2
.
Therefore
\overline{x}(s)
is the minimizer of
\ell
restricted to coordinates in
I(\overline{w}(s))
under the simplex constraint:

\overline{x}(s)\in\Argmin\left\{\ell(y)\,\middle|\,y\in\Delta_{d},\,\supp(y)\subset I(\overline{w}(s))\right\}\,.

The trajectory
\overline{w}(s)
is piecewise affine, and the active set
I(\overline{w}(s))
is piecewise constant, with changes at the times when a new coordinate of
\overline{w}(s)
reaches the maximum value of the vector. Consequently, the limiting primal trajectory
\overline{x}(s)
can be chosen piecewise constant; in each phase it is the solution of a constrained quadratic optimization problem.

\ell
as in Section

. The result is shown in Figure

\overline{w}
is given in Appendix

. Further implementation details are given in Appendix

.

5.2.1
Existence and uniqueness of subgradient flows

The existence and uniqueness result for subgradient flows that we use here was proved in
[

]
, which studies more generally the evolution equations associated with maximal monotone operators. This includes our setting: subgradients of convex, proper, lower semicontinuous functions are maximal monotone operators

, Ex. 2.3.4]

Definition 5.2
.

Let
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

. We say that
u

if, for almost all

,
u
is differentiable at
t
and

\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}t}(t)+\partial f(u(t))\ni 0\,.

For all

,
\partial f(u)
is a closed convex set
[
Bré73
, p. 28]
. When it is nonempty, we denote by
(\partial f)^{\circ}(u)=\argmin_{z\in\partial f(u)}\|z\|^{2}
its element of minimal norm.

Theorem 5.3

(
[

, Thm. 3.1]
)
.

Let
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
u_{0}\in{\dom\partial f}
. There exists a unique Lipschitz solution
u
of the subgradient flow associated with
f
such that
u(0)=u_{0}
.
Moreover, for a.e.
t\geqslant 0
,
u
is differentiable at
t
and

(5.1)

\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}t}(t)+(\partial f)^{\circ}(u(t))=0\,.

5.2.2
Proof of Theorem

Uniqueness.

Minimization property.

By definition of the mirror flow, for a.e.

. We therefore only need to show that, for a.e.

and all

By Theorem

, for a.e.

is differentiable at

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}t}(t)+(\partial\varphi)^{\circ}(u(t))=0\,,

\displaystyle(\partial\varphi)^{\circ}(u)=\underset{z\in\partial\varphi(u)}{\argmin}\|z\|^{2}\,.

Recall the formula for

given by Lemma

(5.2)

Note that in the case where

5.3.1
Evolutionary convergence of subgradient flows

We first introduce Mosco convergence, which is the variational convergence notion behind the evolutionary convergence of subgradient flows.

Definition 5.4
.

Let
f^{\varepsilon}
,
\varepsilon>0
, and
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We say that
f^{\varepsilon}

Mosco converges
to

as
\varepsilon\to 0

\displaystyle f^{\varepsilon}\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\to 0]{\mathsf{M}}f

•

(
\liminf
inequality) for all

and all

converging to
u

\mathbb{R}^{d}

f(u)\leqslant\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}f^{\varepsilon}(u^{\varepsilon})\,,

•

(
\limsup
inequality) for all
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, there exists
(u^{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
converging to
u
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
such that

f(u)\geqslant\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}f^{\varepsilon}(u^{\varepsilon})\,.

Example 5.5
.

Recall that
C=\dom h
is closed. For
\varepsilon>0
, define
f^{\varepsilon}={\varepsilon}h
. Then
f^{\varepsilon}\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\to 0]{\mathsf{M}}\iota_{C}
.

Indeed, we check both inequalities.

•

(
\liminf
inequality) Let
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
(u^{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
converge to
u
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. If

, then
\iota_{C}(u)=+\infty
and, since
C
is closed,

\varepsilon>0
small enough. Thus
f^{\varepsilon}(u^{\varepsilon})=+\infty=\iota_{C}(u)
for
\varepsilon>0
small enough, and the
\liminf
inequality holds. If
u\in C
, then
\iota_{C}(u)=0
and, since the convex function

is locally bounded below
[
BC17
, Coro. 16.18]
,
\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}f^{\varepsilon}(u^{\varepsilon})\geqslant 0=\iota_{C}(u)
.

•

(
\limsup
inequality) Let
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We choose
u^{\varepsilon}=u
for all
\varepsilon>0
. Independently of whether
u
is in
C
or not, we have
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}f^{\varepsilon}(u^{\varepsilon})=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon h(u)=\iota_{C}(u)
and the
\limsup
inequality holds.

We now recall the evolutionary convergence result under Mosco convergence of the associated functions.

Theorem 5.6

(
[

]
)
.

Let

,

Proof of Theorem

The proof strategy is to reduce the rescaled mirror flows to subgradient flows and then apply the evolutionary convergence result of the previous section.

Recall that
w^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\overline{w}^{\varepsilon}}{\mathrm{d}s}(s)=\frac{\mu^{\varepsilon}}{\mu^{\varepsilon}}\frac{\mathrm{d}w^{\varepsilon}}{\mathrm{d}t}(\mu^{\varepsilon}s)=-\nabla\ell({x}^{\varepsilon}(\mu^{\varepsilon}s))=-\nabla\ell(\overline{x}^{\varepsilon}(s))\,,

\displaystyle\overline{w}^{\varepsilon}(s)=\frac{1}{\mu^{\varepsilon}}w^{\varepsilon}(\mu^{\varepsilon}s)\in\frac{1}{\mu^{\varepsilon}}\partial h(x^{\varepsilon}(\mu^{\varepsilon}s))=\partial\left(\frac{1}{\mu^{\varepsilon}}h\right)(\overline{x}^{\varepsilon}(s))\,.

\overline{w}^{\varepsilon}

\overline{w}_{0}
as

We now transform these mirror flows into subgradient flows to apply evolutionary convergence results. By Proposition
3.4
,
u^{\varepsilon}=(M^{\dagger})^{1/2}(\overline{w}^{\varepsilon}-\overline{w}_{0}^{\varepsilon})

By Example

(h^{\varepsilon})^{*}\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\to 0]{\mathsf{M}}\sigma_{C}
. Since simultaneously

\varphi^{\varepsilon}\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\to 0]{\mathsf{M}}\varphi
, where

Lemma 5.9
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem

\varphi^{\varepsilon}\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\to 0]{\mathsf{M}}\varphi
.

Using this lemma, we apply Theorem

. Let

(B.4)

\displaystyle\nabla\widetilde{\ell}\left(\overline{x}^{\text{pos},\varepsilon}(s),\overline{x}^{\text{neg},\varepsilon}(s)\right)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\to 0]{}\nabla\widetilde{\ell}\left(\overline{x}^{\text{pos}}(s),\overline{x}^{\text{neg}}(s)\right)\,,

\displaystyle\text{in }L^{2}([0,S],\mathbb{R}^{2d},s\mathrm{d}s)\,,

(B.5)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\ell}\left(\overline{x}^{\text{pos},\varepsilon}(s),\overline{x}^{\text{neg},\varepsilon}(s)\right)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\to 0]{}\widetilde{\ell}\left(\overline{x}^{\text{pos}}(s),\overline{x}^{\text{neg}}(s)\right)\,,

\displaystyle\text{in }L^{1}([0,S],\mathbb{R},s\mathrm{d}s)\,,

where

\widetilde{\ell}(\overline{x}^{\text{pos}}(s),\overline{x}^{\text{neg}}(s))=\min\left\{\widetilde{\ell}(y^{\text{pos}},y^{\text{neg}})\,\middle|\,\begin{subarray}{c}y^{\text{pos}},y^{\text{neg}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{\geqslant 0},\\
\langle\overline{w}^{\text{pos}}(s),y^{\text{pos}}\rangle=\langle\overline{w}^{\text{neg}}(s),y^{\text{neg}}\rangle=0\end{subarray}\right\}\,.

(c)

Let
(\overline{y}^{\text{pos}},\overline{y}^{\text{neg}}):[0,S]\to\mathbb{R}^{2d}
be an accumulation point of
(\overline{x}^{\text{pos},\varepsilon},\overline{x}^{\text{neg},\varepsilon})
in the sense of essential pointwise convergence. Then, for a.e.
s\in[0,S]
,

(B.6)

(\overline{y}^{\text{pos}}(s),\overline{y}^{\text{neg}}(s))\in\Argmin\left\{\widetilde{\ell}(y^{\text{pos}},y^{\text{neg}})\,\middle|\,\begin{subarray}{c}y^{\text{pos}},y^{\text{neg}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{\geqslant 0},\\
\langle\overline{w}^{\text{pos}}(s),y^{\text{pos}}\rangle=\langle\overline{w}^{\text{neg}}(s),y^{\text{neg}}\rangle=0\end{subarray}\right\}\,.

We need to show that these consequences of Theorem
3.8
imply Theorem
4.2
. Denote
\widetilde{w}=\frac{\overline{w}^{\text{pos}}-\overline{w}^{\text{neg}}}{2}
and
\widetilde{x}=\overline{x}^{\text{pos}}-\overline{x}^{\text{neg}}
. We show that
\widetilde{w}
is a Lipschitz solution of the mirror flow with loss
\ell
and mirror potential
h(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}|x_{i}|
, initialized at
\widetilde{w}(0)=\overline{w}_{0}
, with primal variable
\widetilde{x}
. Indeed, by the definition of
\overline{w}_{0}
,
\widetilde{w}(0)=\frac{\overline{w}_{0}^{\text{pos}}-\overline{w}_{0}^{\text{neg}}}{2}=\overline{w}_{0}
. Further,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\widetilde{w}}{\mathrm{d}s}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\overline{w}^{\text{pos}}}{\mathrm{d}s}(s)-\frac{\mathrm{d}\overline{w}^{\text{neg}}}{\mathrm{d}s}(s)\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\nabla_{x^{\text{pos}}}\widetilde{\ell}(\overline{x}^{\text{pos}}(s),\overline{x}^{\text{neg}}(s))-\nabla_{x^{\text{neg}}}\widetilde{\ell}(\overline{x}^{\text{pos}}(s),\overline{x}^{\text{neg}}(s))\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\nabla\ell(\overline{x}^{\text{pos}}(s)-\overline{x}^{\text{neg}}(s))+\nabla\ell(\overline{x}^{\text{pos}}(s)-\overline{x}^{\text{neg}}(s))\right)

\displaystyle=-\nabla\ell(\widetilde{x}(s))\,.

Note that a similar computation gives
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\left(-\frac{\overline{w}^{\text{pos}}(s)+\overline{w}^{\text{neg}}(s)}{2}\right)=0
. Thus, for all
s\geqslant 0
,

(B.7)

-\frac{\overline{w}^{\text{pos}}(s)+\overline{w}^{\text{neg}}(s)}{2}=-\frac{\overline{w}^{\text{pos}}(0)+\overline{w}^{\text{neg}}(0)}{2}=\lambda>0\,.

We now show
\widetilde{w}(s)\in\partial h(\widetilde{x}(s))
. Recall that
(\overline{w}^{\text{pos}}(s),\overline{w}^{\text{neg}}(s))\in\partial\iota_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}_{\geqslant 0}}(\overline{x}^{\text{pos}}(s),\overline{x}^{\text{neg}}(s))
, that is,
\overline{x}^{\text{pos}}(s),\overline{x}^{\text{neg}}(s)\geqslant 0
,
\overline{w}^{\text{pos}}(s),\overline{w}^{\text{neg}}(s)\leqslant 0
and
\langle\overline{w}^{\text{pos}}(s),\overline{x}^{\text{pos}}(s)\rangle=\langle\overline{w}^{\text{neg}}(s),\overline{x}^{\text{neg}}(s)\rangle=0
. Let
i\in\{1,\dots,d\}
.

•

If
\overline{x}_{i}(s)>0
, then we must have
\overline{x}_{i}^{\text{pos}}(s)>0
. Thus
\overline{w}_{i}^{\text{pos}}(s)=0
. Thus, by Eq. (
B.7
),
\widetilde{w}_{i}(s)=-\frac{\overline{w}_{i}^{\text{neg}}(s)}{2}=\lambda_{i}
.

•

If
\overline{x}_{i}(s)<0
, then we must have
\overline{x}_{i}^{\text{neg}}(s)>0
. Thus
\overline{w}_{i}^{\text{neg}}(s)=0
. Thus, by Eq. (
B.7
),
\widetilde{w}_{i}(s)=\frac{\overline{w}_{i}^{\text{pos}}(s)}{2}=-\lambda_{i}
.

•

In all cases,

\left|\widetilde{w}_{i}(s)\right|=\left|\frac{\overline{w}_{i}^{\text{pos}}(s)-\overline{w}_{i}^{\text{neg}}(s)}{2}\right|\leqslant\frac{\left|\overline{w}_{i}^{\text{pos}}(s)\right|+\left|\overline{w}_{i}^{\text{neg}}(s)\right|}{2}=\lambda_{i}\,.

These points show that
\widetilde{w}(s)\in\partial h(\widetilde{x}(s))
, and thus complete the proof that
\widetilde{w}(s)
is a Lipschitz solution of the mirror flow with loss
\ell
and mirror potential
h
, such that
\widetilde{w}(0)=\overline{w}_{0}
. As such a solution is unique, we can conclude that

\overline{w}(s)=\widetilde{w}(s)=\frac{\overline{w}^{\text{pos}}(s)-\overline{w}^{\text{neg}}(s)}{2}\,.

Note that, as we do not have uniqueness of the primal solution, we do not have
\overline{x}(s)=\widetilde{x}(s)
a priori. However, as explained below Theorem
3.5
, we must have
\nabla\ell(\overline{x}(s))=\nabla\ell(\widetilde{x}(s))
and
\ell(\overline{x}(s))=\ell(\widetilde{x}(s))
a.e.

We are now ready to prove the statements of the theorem.

(a)

By Eq. (
B.4
), we have the following convergence in
L^{2}([0,S],\mathbb{R}^{d},s\mathrm{d}s)
:

\displaystyle\nabla\ell(\overline{x}^{\varepsilon}(s))

\displaystyle=\nabla\ell(\overline{x}^{\text{pos},\varepsilon}(s)-\overline{x}^{\text{neg},\varepsilon}(s))

\displaystyle=\nabla_{x^{\text{pos}}}\widetilde{\ell}(\overline{x}^{\text{pos},\varepsilon}(s),\overline{x}^{\text{neg},\varepsilon}(s))

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\to 0]{}\nabla_{x^{\text{pos}}}\widetilde{\ell}(\overline{x}^{\text{pos}}(s),\overline{x}^{\text{neg}}(s))

\displaystyle=\nabla\ell(\overline{x}^{\text{pos}}(s)-\overline{x}^{\text{neg}}(s))

\displaystyle=\nabla\ell(\widetilde{x}(s))=\nabla\ell(\overline{x}(s))\,.

This proves the first statement. Further, by Theorem
3.5
,

\displaystyle\ell(\overline{x}(s))=\min\left\{\ell(y)\,\middle|\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ satisfying }\begin{cases}\text{if }\overline{w}_{i}(s)=\lambda_{i},&y_{i}\geqslant 0\,,\\
\text{if }\overline{w}_{i}(s)\in(-\lambda_{i},\lambda_{i}),&y_{i}=0\,,\\
\text{if }\overline{w}_{i}(s)=-\lambda_{i},&y_{i}\leqslant 0\,.\end{cases}\right\}\,.

Finally, by Eq. (
B.5
), we have the following convergence in
L^{1}([0,S],\mathbb{R},s\mathrm{d}s)
:

\ell(\overline{x}^{\varepsilon}(s))=\widetilde{\ell}(\overline{x}^{\text{pos},\varepsilon}(s),\overline{x}^{\text{neg},\varepsilon}(s))\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\to 0]{}\widetilde{\ell}(\overline{x}^{\text{pos}}(s),\overline{x}^{\text{neg}}(s))=\ell(\widetilde{x}(s))=\ell(\overline{x}(s))\,.

This finishes the proof of part
(a)
.

(b)

Let
\overline{y}:[0,S]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an accumulation point of
\overline{x}^{\varepsilon}
in the sense of essential pointwise convergence. To use the lifted result above, we need to show that
(\overline{y}_{+}(s),\overline{y}_{-}(s))
is an accumulation point of
(\overline{x}^{\text{pos},\varepsilon},\overline{x}^{\text{neg},\varepsilon})
in the same sense.

Recall that, from Eq. (
B.3
),
x^{\text{pos}}\circ x^{\text{neg}}
is a constant of the dynamics. As a consequence,

\overline{x}^{\text{pos},\varepsilon}(s)\circ\overline{x}^{\text{neg},\varepsilon}(s)=\overline{x}^{\text{pos},\varepsilon}_{0}\circ\overline{x}^{\text{neg},\varepsilon}_{0}=e^{2w_{0}^{\text{pos},\varepsilon}}\circ e^{2w_{0}^{\text{neg},\varepsilon}}=e^{2\mu^{\varepsilon}(\overline{w}_{0}^{\text{pos},\varepsilon}+\overline{w}_{0}^{\text{neg},\varepsilon})}\,,

where
\mu^{\varepsilon}\to\infty
and
\overline{w}_{0}^{\text{pos},\varepsilon}+\overline{w}_{0}^{\text{neg},\varepsilon}\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\to 0]{}\overline{w}_{0}^{\text{pos}}+\overline{w}_{0}^{\text{neg}}=-2\lambda<0
. Thus we obtain, for all
s\geqslant 0
,

\overline{x}^{\text{pos},\varepsilon}(s)\circ\overline{x}^{\text{neg},\varepsilon}(s)\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\to 0]{}0\,.

Moreover, there exists
\varepsilon_{k}\to 0
such that
\overline{x}^{\text{pos},\varepsilon_{k}}(s)-\overline{x}^{\text{neg},\varepsilon_{k}}(s)=\overline{x}^{\varepsilon_{k}}(s)\xrightarrow[k\to\infty]{}\overline{y}(s)
for a.e.
s\in[0,S]
. Combining these two convergences, we have that

\left(\overline{x}^{\text{pos},\varepsilon_{k}}(s),\overline{x}^{\text{neg},\varepsilon_{k}}(s)\right)\xrightarrow[k\to\infty]{}(\overline{y}_{+}(s),\overline{y}_{-}(s))\,,

for a.e.
s\in[0,S]
. This proves that
(\overline{y}_{+},\overline{y}_{-})
is an accumulation point of
(\overline{x}^{\text{pos},\varepsilon},\overline{x}^{\text{neg},\varepsilon})
in the sense of essential pointwise convergence. By Eq. (
B.6
), for a.e.
s\in[0,S]
,

(\overline{y}_{+}(s),\overline{y}_{-}(s))\in\Argmin\left\{\widetilde{\ell}(y^{\text{pos}},y^{\text{neg}})\,\middle|\,\begin{subarray}{c}y^{\text{pos}},y^{\text{neg}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{\geqslant 0},\\
\langle\overline{w}^{\text{pos}}(s),y^{\text{pos}}\rangle=\langle\overline{w}^{\text{neg}}(s),y^{\text{neg}}\rangle=0\end{subarray}\right\}\,.

Using the same reasoning as above,
\overline{y}(s)=\overline{y}_{+}(s)-\overline{y}_{-}(s)
satisfies

\begin{cases}\text{if }\overline{w}_{i}(s)=\lambda_{i},&\overline{y}_{i}(s)\geqslant 0\,,\\
\text{if }\overline{w}_{i}(s)\in(-\lambda_{i},\lambda_{i}),&\overline{y}_{i}(s)=0\,,\\
\text{if }\overline{w}_{i}(s)=-\lambda_{i},&\overline{y}_{i}(s)\leqslant 0\,.\end{cases}

Moreover, if
y
satisfies

\begin{cases}\text{if }\overline{w}_{i}(s)=\lambda_{i},&y_{i}\geqslant 0\,,\\
\text{if }\overline{w}_{i}(s)\in(-\lambda_{i},\lambda_{i}),&y_{i}=0\,,\\
\text{if }\overline{w}_{i}(s)=-\lambda_{i},&y_{i}\leqslant 0\,,\end{cases}

then
(y_{+},y_{-})
satisfies

\begin{cases}y_{+}\geqslant 0,y_{-}\geqslant 0\,,\\
\langle\overline{w}^{\text{pos}}(s),y_{+}\rangle=\langle\overline{w}^{\text{neg}}(s),y_{-}\rangle=0,\end{cases}

and thus

\ell(\overline{y}(s))=\widetilde{\ell}(\overline{y}_{+}(s),\overline{y}_{-}(s))\leqslant\widetilde{\ell}(y_{+},y_{-})=\ell(y)\,.

This finishes the proof of part
(b)
.

C.1
Details on the implementation

Mirror flows.

We first discuss simulating the mirror flows for finite
\varepsilon
. To approximate the mirror flow, we directly implement its discretization, known as the mirror descent algorithm:

\displaystyle\overline{w}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\overline{w}_{k}-\eta\nabla\ell(\overline{x}_{k})

\displaystyle\overline{x}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\nabla h^{*}(\overline{w}_{k+1})\,.

In the simulations, we use a small step size
\eta\in[10^{-4},10^{-3}]
.

Limiting mirror flow.

Simulating the limiting mirror flow is more involved because
h^{*}
, being the support function of a convex set
C
, may be non-smooth and have a restricted domain. We use the minimization property of Theorem
3.5
through the following algorithm.

\displaystyle\overline{v}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\overline{w}_{k}-\eta\nabla\ell(\overline{x}_{k})

(C.1)

\displaystyle\overline{w}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\Pi_{\dom\partial\sigma_{C}}\left[\overline{v}_{k+1}\right]

(C.2)

\displaystyle\overline{x}_{k+1}

\displaystyle\in\Argmin_{x\in\partial\sigma_{C}(\overline{w}_{k+1})}\ell(x)\,,

Here
\Pi_{\dom\partial\sigma_{C}}
denotes the Euclidean projection onto
\dom\partial\sigma_{C}
in Step (
C.1
). In all cases, Step (
C.2
) requires a convex optimization solver to minimize
\ell
over
\partial\sigma_{C}(\overline{w}_{k+1})
.

We detail the steps of the algorithm in the three examples of Section
4
. All routines we refer to are implemented in Python using the
SciPy
library:

•

In the case of the non-negative orthant,
\dom\partial\sigma_{C}=\mathbb{R}_{\leqslant 0}^{d}
. Hence the projection is
w\mapsto\min\{0,w\}
. As explained in the main text, the constrained minimization requires minimizing
\ell
over vectors
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{\geqslant 0}
supported on the zero coordinates of
\overline{w}_{k+1}
. We implement this by calling a non-negative least-squares solver on these coordinates, namely the
scipy.optimize.nnls
routine.

•

For optimization on positive semidefinite matrices, the projection onto
\mathcal{S}^{d}_{-}=\dom\partial\sigma_{C}
sets the positive eigenvalues of
\overline{W}_{k+1}
to zero. The set
\partial\sigma_{C}(\overline{W}_{k+1})
consists of positive semidefinite matrices whose range is a subspace of the kernel of
\overline{W}_{k+1}
; equivalently, it is encoded by the linear equality
\langle-\overline{W}_{k+1},X\rangle=0
on
X\succeq 0
. The minimization of
\ell
over
\partial\sigma_{C}(\overline{W}_{k+1})
is then a semidefinite program, which we model in Python via the
CVXPY
library—a domain-specific language for convex optimization that canonicalizes the problem into a standard conic form—and solve using the SCS backend (Splitting Conic Solver), a first-order ADMM-based solver for cone programs. SCS is warm-started at each iteration from the previous iterate
\overline{X}_{k}
to accelerate convergence.

•

In the case of the simplex, there is no projection as
\dom\partial\sigma_{C}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The set
\partial\sigma_{C}(\overline{w}_{k+1})
is the convex hull of the vertices of the simplex corresponding to the coordinates that are maximal in
\overline{w}_{k+1}
, which we identify in practice through a small tolerance on the gap to the maximum. As
\ell
is quadratic in this example, the minimization of
\ell
over
\partial\sigma_{C}(\overline{w}_{k+1})
reduces to a quadratic program with non-negativity and sum-to-one constraints, which we solve by calling
scipy.optimize.minimize
with the SLSQP method (Sequential Least Squares Programming).

In all figures, we identify
s=t/\mu^{\varepsilon}=k\eta/\mu^{\varepsilon}
.

We numerically compare the matrix mirror flow studied in Section

X=UU^{\top}
,
for the same matrix loss

The matrix mirror flow with the von Neumann potential satisfies

\dot{W}=-\nabla\ell(X)
,

, initialized at

W^{\varepsilon}_{0}=(\log\varepsilon)\,\I_{d}
.
The matrix factorization gradient flow optimizes
\ell
directly through the

\frac{\mathrm{d}U}{\mathrm{d}t}=-\nabla_{U}\ell(UU^{\top})=-2\big(\mathcal{M}(UU^{\top})-Q\big)\,U\,,\qquad U(0)=\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,\I_{d}\,,

so that
X(0)=\varepsilon\I_{d}
in both cases.

Time rescaling.

We plot trajectories against the rescaled time

, with
\mu^{\varepsilon}=\log(1/\varepsilon)
.
For the mirror flow we set

multiplies the rate

relative to the mirror flow; to align the time axes we therefore plot

We use the same measurement model as in Section

C.3
Additional lemmas

C.3.1
Conjugate of the entropic mirror potential on the simplex

We state and prove the following lemma, which is used in the simulation of Section
4.3
. In this setting, the mirror potential is

h(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}(x_{i}\log x_{i}-x_{i})+\iota_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}(x)

if
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{\geqslant 0}
, and
h(x)=+\infty
otherwise, where
\mathcal{S}_{1}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}=1\}
.

Lemma C.1
.

The conjugate function of
h
is given by
\displaystyle h^{*}(w)=1+\log\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}e^{w_{i}}\right)
, whose domain is
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Moreover, for all
w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\displaystyle\nabla h^{*}(w)=e^{w}/\|e^{w}\|_{1}
, where the exponential is taken coordinatewise.

Proof.

The potential
h=f+\iota_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}
can be decomposed as a sum of two functions, where in this proof
f
denotes the entropic potential with domain
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{\geqslant 0}
. We first note that
f^{*}(w)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}e^{w_{i}}
, with domain
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Second, we compute

\displaystyle\iota_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}^{*}(w)=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{1}}\langle w,x\rangle=\begin{cases}\lambda,&\text{if }w=\lambda\mathbf{1}\,,\\
+\infty,&\text{otherwise}\,.\end{cases}

Since
0\in\ri(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{\geqslant 0}-\mathcal{S}_{1})
, we can apply
[
BC17
, Theorem 15.3]
, so that

\displaystyle h^{*}(w)

\displaystyle=\min_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}f^{*}(w-u)+\iota_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}^{*}(u)=\min_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}e^{w_{i}-\lambda}+\lambda=1+\log\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}e^{w_{i}}\right)\,.

The expression of
\nabla h^{*}
is then immediate.
∎

C.3.2
Subdifferential of the support function of the simplex

Recall that for all
w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we can define its active set of coordinates as
I(w)=\{k\mid w_{k}=\max_{i}w_{i}\}
. We prove here the following expression, which is used in the simulation of Section
4.3
.

Lemma C.2
.

For all
w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\partial\sigma_{\Delta_{d}}(w)=\left\{\sum_{k\in I(w)}\alpha_{k}e_{k}\,\middle|\,\sum_{k\in I(w)}\alpha_{k}=1,\,\alpha_{k}\geqslant 0\right\},

where
(e_{k})_{1\leqslant k\leqslant d}
are the canonical vectors.

Proof.

In this proof, for any vector
w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we write
w_{\max}:=\max_{i}w_{i}=\sigma_{\Delta_{d}}(w)
. Note that

\displaystyle p\in\partial\sigma_{\Delta_{d}}(w)

\displaystyle\iff\forall v\in\mathbb{R}^{d},v_{\max}\geqslant w_{\max}+\langle p,v-w\rangle

We show the lemma by double inclusion.

First, let
p
be of the form
\sum_{k\in I(w)}\alpha_{k}e_{k}
where
\sum_{k\in I(w)}\alpha_{k}=1
and
\alpha_{k}\geqslant 0
. Let
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We have

\displaystyle\langle p,v-w\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in I(w)}\alpha_{k}(v_{k}-w_{k})=\sum_{k\in I(w)}\alpha_{k}v_{k}-w_{\max}\leqslant v_{\max}-w_{\max}\,,

hence
\partial\sigma_{\Delta_{d}}(w)\supseteq\left\{\sum_{k\in I(w)}\alpha_{k}e_{k}\,\middle|\,\sum_{k\in I(w)}\alpha_{k}=1,\,\alpha_{k}\geqslant 0\right\}
.

Second, let
p\in\partial\sigma_{\Delta_{d}}(w)
.

We first show that
p\in\Delta_{d}
. Let
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, take
v=w+tu
for some
t>0
. We have that

\displaystyle\max_{i}\{w_{i}+tu_{i}\}

\displaystyle\geqslant w_{\max}+t\langle p,u\rangle\,,

Dividing by
t
and letting
t\to\infty
, as
\max_{i}\{w_{i}+tu_{i}\}/t\to\max_{i}u_{i}
, we obtain
\langle p,u\rangle\leqslant\max_{i}u_{i}
. Since this holds for all
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, evaluating the inequality at
u=-e_{k}
gives
p\in\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}^{d}
, and evaluating it at
u=\pm\mathbf{1}
gives
p\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
. Hence
p\in\Delta_{d}
.

We now show that
p
is supported on the active coordinates of
w
. Taking
v=0
, we obtain
\langle p,w\rangle\geqslant w_{\max}
. Since
p\in\Delta_{d}
, we also have
\langle p,w\rangle\leqslant w_{\max}
. Hence
\langle p,w\rangle=w_{\max}
. Since
p\in\Delta_{d}
, this can happen only if
p
is supported on the active coordinates of
w
. This finishes the proof of the second inclusion, and thus of the lemma.
∎
