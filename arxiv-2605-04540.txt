Hierarchical entanglement transitions and hidden area-law sectors in quantum many-body dynamics
Tarun Grover

Abstract
Chaotic many-body dynamics typically generates volume-law entanglement from initially low-entangled states. We reveal an intricate, hierarchical entanglement structure in local quantum quenches, both in the canonical purification of locally quenched Gibbs states and in a companion pure-state circuit model. In either setting, the full state exhibits a Rényi-index-tuned transition: at long times, S α > 1 S_{\alpha>1} obeys an area law, while S α ≤ 1 S_{\alpha\leq 1} is volume-law. More strikingly, the response linear in the quench strength is carried by only an O ⁡ ( 1 ) O(1) -dimensional dominant Schmidt sector; the corresponding states exhibit their own area-to-volume-law transitions at critical indices α c < 1 \alpha_{c}<1 , implying polynomial-bond-dimension approximability in one dimension. We provide evidence that this hierarchy persists recursively: upon bipartitioning the dominant Schmidt states, their leading Schmidt sectors exhibit analogous structure. We derive the mechanism analytically in the circuit model, prove the S α > 1 S_{\alpha>1} area law for locally quenched Gibbs states, and support the hierarchy by exact diagonalization of random circuits and locally quenched Gibbs states of chaotic spin chains.

A circuit model.
The Hilbert space of our model consists of
N

q
-dimensional qudits on a
d
-dimensional lattice, with local basis
\{|0\rangle,|1\rangle,...,|q-1\rangle\}
. Consider a unitary circuit
U(t)
consisting of geometrically local gates, and evolve a local, traceless, unitary operator
O
as
O(t)=U(t)OU^{\dagger}(t)
. For now, we assume absence of any global symmetries (we will later briefly discuss a circuit with U(1) symmetry). The state of our interest is:

|\psi(t)\rangle=\frac{(\mathbbm{1}+\epsilon O(t))|0\rangle}{\sqrt{\mathcal{N}_{t}}},

(3)

where
0<\epsilon<1
is a real number,
|0\rangle
is any product state, which will take to be the all-0 state (
|00...0\rangle
), and
\mathcal{N}_{t}=1+\epsilon^{2}+2\epsilon\Re{a_{t}}
is the normalization with

a_{t}=\langle 0|O(t)|0\rangle
. Similar to the aforementioned discussion, for any operator
O^{\prime}
,
\langle\psi(t)|O^{\prime}|\psi(t)\rangle

\langle 0|\left(O^{\dagger}(t)O^{\prime}+O^{\prime}O(t)\right)|0\rangle-\langle 0|O^{\dagger}(t)|0\rangle\langle 0|O^{\prime}|0\rangle-\langle 0|O^{\prime}|0\rangle\langle 0|O(t)|0\rangle
at linear order in
\epsilon
.

We now bipartition the system into two contiguous halves
A,B
and study
\rho_{A}=\operatorname{tr}_{B}|\psi(t)\rangle\!\langle\psi(t)|
. Decomposing

O(t)|0\rangle

O(t)|0\rangle=|x\rangle_{A}\otimes|0_{B}\rangle+|y\rangle_{AB}

\langle 0_{B}|y\rangle=0

\rho_{A}

\rho_{A}=(1-\mu)|v\rangle\!\langle v|+\mu\,\omega,

(4)

where
\mu=\epsilon^{2}\langle y|y\rangle/\mathcal{N}_{t}
,
|v\rangle=\frac{|0_{A}\rangle+\epsilon|x\rangle}{\sqrt{\mathcal{N}_{t}(1-\mu)}}

4

for all time

4

A

(a) For
\alpha>1

(b)

(c) For
\alpha<1

Discussion.
We expect that the phenomena described here should extend to Gibbs states of generic interacting quantum field theories, including holographic ones. Although related local-quench and purification setups have been studied
Nozaki et al. 2014
;
Nozaki 2014
;
Nozaki et al. 2013
;
Asplund et al. 2015
;
Caputa et al. 2014
;
Dutta and Faulkner 2021
;
Kusuki and Tamaoka 2021
;
Kudler-Flam et al. 2021
;
Bianchi et al. 2025
, we are not aware of a treatment of the locally quenched canonical purification in Eq. (

) for a strongly interacting field theory. Such a calculation may be subtle. Even for local quenches on ground states, the analytic continuation from Rényi entropies to obtain
S_{1}
can involve order-of-limits issues
Caputa et al. 2014
. In the present setting, this issue is especially sharp: although
S_{\alpha}
is smooth as
\alpha\to 1
at any finite system size, our results imply that the limits
\alpha\to 1
and
L\to\infty
do not commute, leading to an area law for
S_{\alpha>1}(\rho_{A_{1/2}})
but a volume-law component in
S_{\alpha\leq 1}(\rho_{A_{1/2}})
.

We emphasize that our results on area-law approximants are statements about the entanglement structure and representability—we showed the existence of such a representation by truncating the
exact state
obtained using ED. This does not imply the existence of a general-purpose classical algorithm for finding the approximant with polynomial resources! This distinction is important: low bond-dimension by itself does not guarantee that one can
find
such a representation efficiently. Indeed, there are one-dimensional Hamiltonians whose ground states are MPS, yet finding the relevant MPS can be computationally hard

;
Jiang 2025

;

\alpha_{c}=1
is consistent with complexity-theoretic expectations. Similarly, with regard to our circuit model, BQP/PromiseBQP computations can be formulated as constant-gap estimates of local observables such as

Appendix A
Details of inequalities for Rényi entropies

Our starting point is Eq.
4
in the main text:

\rho_{A}=(1-\mu)|v\rangle\!\langle v|+\mu\,\omega,

(7)

where
\mu=\epsilon^{2}\langle y|y\rangle/\mathcal{N}_{t}
,
|v\rangle=\frac{|0_{A}\rangle+\epsilon|x\rangle}{\sqrt{\mathcal{N}_{t}(1-\mu)}}
is a unit norm vector,
\omega=\operatorname{tr}_{B}|y\rangle\!\langle y|/\langle y|y\rangle
is a normalized density matrix (with the constraint
\langle x|x\rangle+\langle y|y\rangle=1
). We are interested in point-wise (i.e. for a single realization of the circuit
U(t)
), as well as ensemble-averaged bounds on the Rényi entropies
S_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log(\operatorname{tr}(\rho^{\alpha}_{A}))
. Let’s write the eigendecomposition of
\rho_{A}
as
\rho_{A}=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}|\lambda_{i}\rangle\!\langle\lambda_{i}|
with
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\lambda_{3}\dots
. We consider the three cases of interest in turn: (a)
\alpha>1
, (b)
\alpha=1
, and (c)
\alpha<1
.

1.

S_{\alpha>1}
: First, we notice that
\lambda_{1}\geq\langle v|\rho_{A}|v\rangle\geq(1-\mu)
. Therefore,
\operatorname{tr}(\rho^{\alpha}_{A})=\sum_{i}\lambda^{\alpha}_{i}\geq\lambda^{\alpha}_{1}\geq(1-\mu)^{\alpha}
, and therefore, one obtains
S_{\alpha>1}\leq\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\log(1/(1-\mu))
. Further, one can show that
\mu\leq\epsilon^{2}
(see next paragraph), which implies that

S_{\alpha>1}\leq\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\log(1/(1-\epsilon^{2})).

(8)

Note that this bound holds point-wise and therefore is also true for
\mathbb{E}\,S_{\alpha>1}
, the ensemble averaged Rényi entropies.

It remains to prove
\mu\leq\epsilon^{2}
. Since
\|x\|^{2}:=\langle x|x\rangle=1-\|y\|^{2}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{t}

\displaystyle=\||0_{A}\rangle+\epsilon|x\rangle\|^{2}+\epsilon^{2}\|y\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq(1-\epsilon\|x\|)^{2}+\epsilon^{2}\|y\|^{2}

\displaystyle=(1-\epsilon\sqrt{1-\|y\|^{2}})^{2}+\epsilon^{2}\|y\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|y\|^{2}+(\sqrt{1-\|y\|^{2}}-\epsilon)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\|y\|^{2}.

Hence
\mu=\frac{\epsilon^{2}\|y\|^{2}}{\mathcal{N}_{t}}\leq\epsilon^{2}
.

2.

S_{1}
: We apply the standard Holevo-type inequality
\sum_{i}p_{i}\log(1/p_{i})+\sum_{i}p_{i}S(\rho_{i})\geq S(\sum_{i}p_{i}\rho_{i})\geq\sum_{i}p_{i}S(\rho_{i})
to Eq.
7
:

\mu S(\omega)\leq S(\rho_{A})\leq h_{2}(\mu)+\mu S(\omega),

(9)

where
h_{2}(x):=-x\log x-(1-x)\log(1-x)
.
Taking the ensemble average of these pointwise inequalities, one obtains,
\mathbb{E}\,[\mu S(\omega)]\leq\mathbb{E}\,[S(\rho_{A})]\leq\mathbb{E}\,[h_{2}(\mu)]+\mathbb{E}\,[\mu S(\omega)]
.

These inequalities imply that the volume law coefficient of
S(\rho_{A})
is given by
\lim_{N\to\infty}\mu S(\omega)/N
, and is therefore upper bounded by
\epsilon^{2}\log(q)
.

3.

S_{\alpha<1}
: We now show that when
\alpha<1
,

\mu^{\alpha}\operatorname{tr}\omega^{\alpha}\leq\operatorname{tr}\rho^{\alpha}_{A}\leq(1-\mu)^{\alpha}+\mu^{\alpha}\operatorname{tr}\omega^{\alpha}.

(10)

Equivalently,

S_{\alpha}(\omega)+\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\log\mu\leq S_{\alpha}(\rho_{A}(t))\leq\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\Big[(1-\mu)^{\alpha}+\mu^{\alpha}e^{(1-\alpha)S_{\alpha}(\omega)}\Big].

(11)

Proof.

For
0<\alpha<1
, the map
x\mapsto x^{\alpha}
is operator monotone increasing. Since
\rho_{A}\geq\mu\omega
, monotonicity gives

\rho_{A}^{\alpha}\geq(\mu\omega)^{\alpha}=\mu^{\alpha}\omega^{\alpha}.

(12)

Taking the trace gives the lower bound in Eq.
11
. The upper bound follows from the Rotfel’d inequality
Bhatia 2013
:

\operatorname{tr}(A+B)^{\alpha}\leq\operatorname{tr}A^{\alpha}+\operatorname{tr}B^{\alpha}\qquad(0<\alpha<1),

(13)

applied to
A=(1-\mu)|v\rangle\!\langle v|
and
B=\mu\omega
.

|v\rangle

\lambda_{2}=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\textrm{any}\,\,|\phi\rangle\\
\|\phi\|=1\end{subarray}}\ \max_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\psi\rangle\perp|\phi\rangle\\
\|\psi\|=1\end{subarray}}\langle\psi|\rho_{A}|\psi\rangle.

The optimal
|\phi\rangle

7

\lambda_{2}\leq\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}|v\rangle\perp|\psi\rangle\\
\|\psi\|=1\end{subarray}}\mu\langle\psi|\omega|\psi\rangle\leq\mu\|\omega\|_{\infty}=\mu\beta

(16)

Therefore,

m-\lambda_{2}\geq m-\mu\beta=1-\mu+\mu a-\mu\beta=\Delta>0.

(17)

. A simple calculation gives

\|Q\rho_{A}|v\rangle\|^{2}=\mu^{2}\langle v|\omega Q\omega|v\rangle=\mu^{2}\bigl(\langle v|\omega^{2}|v\rangle-a^{2}\bigr).

(18)

Because
\omega
is a density matrix with eigenvalues
\leq 1

\omega^{2}\leq\omega
. This implies
\langle v|\omega^{2}|v\rangle\leq\langle v|\omega|v\rangle=a

Notice that using the definition
\langle v|\rho_{A}|v\rangle=m

\|Q\rho_{A}|v\rangle\|^{2}=\|(\rho_{A}-m)|v\rangle\|^{2}

\displaystyle\|Q\rho_{A}|v\rangle\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 1}|c_{k}|^{2}(\lambda_{k}-m)^{2}

(19)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 2}|c_{k}|^{2}(\lambda_{k}-m)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\Delta^{2}\sum_{k\geq 2}|c_{k}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta^{2}(1-|c_{1}|^{2}),

where the second inequality in Eq.

Finally using
\|Q\rho_{A}|v\rangle\|^{2}\leq\mu^{2}a
and noticing that

1-|\langle\lambda_{1}|v\rangle|^{2}\leq\frac{\|Q\rho_{A}|v\rangle\|^{2}}{\Delta^{2}}\leq\frac{\mu^{2}a}{\Delta^{2}}.

(20)

∎

is a normalized density matrix, and
\mu_{1/4}=\|y_{A_{1/2}}\|^{2}
. Recall that the key point, as mentioned in the main text and discussed in more detail in Appendix
C
, is that
\mu_{1/4}=\|y_{A_{1/2}}\|^{2}
will generically be exponentially small in the system size which will lead to
\alpha_{c}<1
for
\rho_{A_{1/4}}
. This is because
\|x\|^{2}=\|x_{A_{1/4}}\|^{2}+\|y_{A_{1/2}}\|^{2}
, and in Eq.
34
, by our assumption,
\|x\|^{2}=\|\langle 0_{B}|O(t)|0\rangle\|^{2}
is exponentially small in the system size.

Due to the above self-similar structure, the hierarchy can now be defined iteratively.

At level
j
, one obtains

\rho_{A_{2^{-j}}}=(1-\mu_{2^{-j}})|v_{2^{-j}}\rangle\!\langle v_{2^{-j}}|+\mu_{2^{-j}}\,\omega_{2^{-j}},

(38)

where
|A_{2^{-j}}|=N/2^{j}
and
N
is the total number of qudits with local Hilbert-space dimension
q
.
To determine the entropies, we need the scaling of
\mu_{2^{-j}}
with system size. Under the aforementioned assumption,
\mu_{2^{-j}}
has the same scaling as
\|x_{A_{2^{-(j-1)}}}\|^{2}
, where

\|x_{A_{2^{-(j-1)}}}\|^{2}\sim\prod_{r=1}^{j-1}q^{-N/2^{r}}=q^{-N(1-2^{-(j-1)})}.

(39)

Although the product above is defined only for
j>1
, the final expression is valid also for
j=1
.

Indeed, at each step we write

|x_{A_{2^{-(j-1)}}}\rangle=|x_{A_{2^{-j}}}\rangle\otimes|0_{B_{2^{-j}}}\rangle+|y_{A_{2^{-(j-1)}}}\rangle,\qquad\langle 0_{B_{2^{-j}}}|y_{A_{2^{-(j-1)}}}\rangle=0,

(40)

and conditioning on region
B_{2^{-j}}
contributes a factor of
q^{-N/2^{j}}
.

Further, under the same assumption as stated in the first paragraph of this subsection, the state
\omega_{2^{-j}}
is approximately the identity density matrix on
N/2^{j}
qudits, and therefore

\operatorname{tr}(\omega_{2^{-j}}^{\alpha})\sim q^{(1-\alpha)N/2^{j}}.

(41)

Using Eq. (
38
), the
0<\alpha<1
Rényi moment is then controlled by

\operatorname{tr}(\rho_{A_{2^{-j}}}^{\alpha})\sim c_{1}+c_{2}\,\mu_{2^{-j}}^{\alpha}\operatorname{tr}(\omega_{2^{-j}}^{\alpha})\sim c_{1}+c_{2}\,q^{-\alpha N(1-2^{-(j-1)})}\,q^{(1-\alpha)N/2^{j}}=c_{1}+c_{2}\,q^{\frac{N}{2^{j}}\left[1-(2^{j}-1)\alpha\right]},

(42)

where
c_{1},c_{2}=O(1)
. Hence the second term is exponentially large for
\alpha<1/(2^{j}-1)
and exponentially small for
\alpha>1/(2^{j}-1)
, which yields

\alpha_{c,j}=\frac{1}{2^{j}-1}.

(43)

Thus
\alpha_{c,1}=1
for the top level, as expected from the discussion in the main text, while the next two levels give
\alpha_{c,2}=1/3
and
\alpha_{c,3}=1/7
.

Appendix E
Robustness of Rényi transitions across circuit ensembles

In the main text we used Haar-random two-qubit gates as a convenient strongly scrambling circuit ensemble. To demonstrate explicitly that the observed hierarchy is not tied to Haar architecture, we also studied two other circuit ensembles described below. The first of them is a nearest-neighbor circuit built from a finite universal gate set, while the second one is a circuit that has a
U(1)
symmetry.

E.1
Hierarchical Rényi entropies for a circuit made of finite universal gate-set

Figure 4:
Saturated Rényi entropies in the random circuit model with Clifford+T architecture, with
L_{A}=L/2,L_{A_{1/4}}=\lfloor L/4\rfloor
, at
\epsilon=0.1
. The plotted entropies are averaged over thirty samples for both (a) and (b). The inset of (b) shows the Rényi entropies of the second highest Schmidt state in the second hierarchy.

The circuit acts on a one-dimensional chain of
L
qubits with open boundary conditions. As in the Haar circuit, we study

|\psi(t)\rangle=\frac{(1+\epsilon O(t))|0\rangle}{\sqrt{\mathcal{N}_{t}}},\qquad O(t)=U(t)OU^{\dagger}(t),\qquad O=Z_{L/2}.

(44)

The unitary
U(t)
is a depth-
t
random Clifford+
T
brickwork circuit. One timestep consists of two layers of single-site gates interleaved with two layers of nearest-neighbor CZ gates. More explicitly, for each timestep we choose with probability
1/2
whether the odd or even CZ layer is applied first. If
\sigma_{t}\in\{\mathrm{odd},\mathrm{even}\}
denotes this choice and
\bar{\sigma}_{t}
the complementary parity, then

U_{t}=U_{\mathrm{CZ}}^{\bar{\sigma}_{t}}\,R^{(2)}_{t}\,U_{\mathrm{CZ}}^{\sigma_{t}}\,R^{(1)}_{t}.

(45)

Here
U_{\mathrm{CZ}}^{\mathrm{odd}}
applies CZ gates on bonds

(1,2),(3,4),\ldots
, while
U_{\mathrm{CZ}}^{\mathrm{even}}
applies CZ gates on bonds

(2,3),(4,5),\ldots
. The random choice of
\sigma_{t}
helps in restoring statistical translational invariance.

Each single-site layer
R^{(a)}_{t}
is a product of independent one-qubit gates. On every site we first choose a gate uniformly from the finite Clifford subset
\{\mathds{1},H,S,S^{\dagger},X,Z\}

and then independently apply a non-Clifford gate with probability
p_{T}
. When a non-Clifford gate is applied, it is chosen with equal probability from

T=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&e^{i\pi/4}\end{pmatrix},\qquad T^{\dagger}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&e^{-i\pi/4}\end{pmatrix}.

(46)

In the numerics we use
p_{T}=\frac{1}{2}
.
For any fixed
p_{T}>0
, this gives a local circuit drawn from a finite universal gate set. The case
p_{T}=0
reduces to a Clifford circuit.

We computed the same diagnostics as in the Haar-random circuit ensemble. First, we formed

\rho_{A_{1/2}}
for the half-chain cut and computed its Rényi entropies. Second, we extracted the two largest Schmidt vectors of
\rho_{A_{1/2}}
, bipartitioned the corresponding half-chain state into
A_{1/4}|B_{1/4}
, and computed the resulting nested Rényi entropies. The results are shown in Fig.
4
. They are qualitatively the same as for the Haar-random circuit: the full state (i.e., the top hierarchy) exhibits an area-to-volume-law transition at
\alpha_{c}=1
, while the leading Schmidt state (i.e., the top state in the second hierarchy) exhibits a transition at
\alpha_{c}<1
. The second Schmidt state in the second hierarchy instead shows volume-law behavior for all Rényi indices studied, consistent with it belonging to the scrambled state
\omega
.

E.2
Hierarchical Rényi entropies in a U(1) symmetric circuit

Figure 5:
Saturated Rényi entropies of the top Schmidt state corresponding to the top two hierarchies for the random circuit model with U(1) symmetry discussed in Appendix
E.2

\epsilon=0.4
,
L_{A}=L/2,L_{A_{1/4}}=\lfloor L/4\rfloor
. The plots are obtained by averaging over ten instances of the random circuit.

The circuit we consider is based on Ref.
Khemani et al. 2018
.
The circuit is composed of nearest-neighbor two-site gates in the brickwork structure (i.e. one first applies gates on all odd bonds and then on even bonds). Each two-site gate conserves
\sum_{i}Z_{i}
. In the basis
\{|00\rangle,|01\rangle,|10\rangle,|11\rangle\}
,
the gate has the block form

G=e^{i\theta_{00}}|00\rangle\langle 00|+e^{i\theta_{11}}|11\rangle\langle 11|+\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in\{01,10\}}V_{\alpha\beta}|\alpha\rangle\langle\beta|,

(47)

where
\theta_{00},\theta_{11}
are random phases and
V\in U(2)
is Haar-random.

Similar to the case without symmetry, for a given
U(1)
preserving circuit
U(t)
, we first define the Heisenberg operator
O(t)=U(t)\,Z_{L/2}\,U^{\dagger}(t)
, where
Z_{L/2}
is the
Pauli
Z
operator on site
L/2
, and using this, we define the state of our main interest:

|\psi_{\Phi}(t)\rangle=\frac{(\mathbbm{1}+\epsilon O(t))|\Phi\rangle}{\sqrt{\mathcal{N}_{t}}},\qquad\mathcal{N}_{t}=1+\epsilon^{2}+2\epsilon a_{t},

(48)

where
|\Phi\rangle
is a product state, and
a_{t}=\langle\Phi|O(t)|\Phi\rangle
. In addition to the randomness in the circuit due to
U(2)
Haar-random gates
V
, we also allow initial state
|\Phi\rangle
to have randomness in the following sense: the center site is fixed in the
+1
eigenstate of
Z_{L/2}
, namely
|0\rangle
, and every other site is chosen independently from the
X
-basis states
|+\rangle,|-\rangle
:

|\Phi\rangle=|0\rangle_{L/2}\otimes\bigotimes_{j\neq L/2}|\eta_{j}\rangle_{j},\qquad\eta_{j}\in\{+,-\}.

One advantage of this ensemble of initial states is that the ensemble average of
a_{t}
is precisely the infinite-temperature autocorrelator of
Z_{L/2}
which is expected to show diffusive behavior:

\overline{a_{t}}=\overline{\langle\Phi|Z_{L/2}(t)|\Phi\rangle}=\frac{1}{2^{L}}\operatorname{tr}\!\big[(\mathbbm{1}+Z_{L/2})Z_{L/2}(t)\big]=\frac{1}{2^{L}}\operatorname{tr}\!\big[Z_{L/2}Z_{L/2}(t)\big].

The results for the Rényi entropies for the first two hierarchies are shown in Fig.
5
. They are again consistent with
\alpha_{c}=1
for the top hierarchy, and
\alpha_{c}<1
for the second hierarchy.

Appendix F
Area-law Rényi entropies
S_{\alpha>1}
of
|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta,\theta}(t)}\rangle
at
\beta,\theta=O(1)

In this section we show that, assuming only that the Gibbs canonical purification
|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta}}\rangle
has area-law Rényi entropy
S_{\alpha}
for Rényi-index
\alpha=1/2
, the canonical purification
|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta,\theta}(t)}\rangle
of the quenched density matrix necessarily satisfies an area law of entanglement for Rényi indices
\alpha>1
:
S_{\alpha>1}\sim cL^{d-1}_{A}
for all
t
.

The basic idea of the proof is that the overlap between
|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta}}\rangle
and
|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta,\theta}(t)}\rangle
remains non-zero and
O(1)
for all times even in the thermodynamic limit, which implies that there always exists one large Schmidt eigenvalue of
|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta,\theta}(t)}\rangle
for all times
t
. This large eigenvalue then leads to area-law Rényi entropy
S_{\alpha>1}
for the quenched state.

F.1
O(1)
time-independent overlap between
|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta}}\rangle
and
|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta,\theta}(t)}\rangle

Using the definition of the canonical purification, the overlap between two canonical purifications
|\sqrt{\rho_{1}}\rangle
and
|\sqrt{\rho_{2}}\rangle
is equal to
\operatorname{tr}(\sqrt{\rho_{1}}\sqrt{\rho_{2}})
. Therefore the overlap of interest is:

\displaystyle\langle\sqrt{\rho_{\beta}}|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta,\theta}(t)}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}(\sqrt{\rho_{\beta}}\sqrt{\rho_{\beta,\theta}(t)})

(49)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Z(\beta)}\operatorname{tr}\!\left(e^{-\beta H/2}e^{iHt}U^{\dagger}_{\theta}(x)e^{-\beta H/2}U_{\theta}(x)e^{-iHt}\right)

(50)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Z(\beta)}\operatorname{tr}\!\left(e^{-\beta H/2}U_{\theta}^{\dagger}(x)e^{-\beta H/2}U_{\theta}(x)\right).

(51)

Note that the time-dependence completely drops out after taking trace.

Due to
U_{\theta}(x)
being a local unitary, one may write

U_{\theta}^{\dagger}(x)HU_{\theta}(x)=H+\Delta H,\qquad\Delta H:=U_{\theta}^{\dagger}(x)HU_{\theta}(x)-H.

where
\left\lVert\Delta H\right\rVert=O(1)
.
Then

\langle\sqrt{\rho_{\beta}}|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta,\theta}(t)}\rangle=\frac{1}{Z(\beta)}\operatorname{tr}\!\left(e^{-\beta H/2}e^{-\beta(H+\Delta H)/2}\right).

(52)

Proposition 2
.

For any local unitary
U_{\theta}(x)
,

\langle\sqrt{\rho_{\beta}}|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta,\theta}(t)}\rangle\geq e^{-\beta\left\lVert\Delta H\right\rVert/2}.

Since
\left\lVert\Delta H\right\rVert=O(1)
, this implies that the overlap
\langle\sqrt{\rho_{\beta}}|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta,\theta}(t)}\rangle
is bounded from below by an
O(1)
time-independent constant for all time
t
.

Proof.

Let’s write the right-hand side of Eq.
52
in the eigenbasis
\{|n\rangle\}
of
H
, with
H|n\rangle=E_{n}|n\rangle
. Then

\frac{1}{Z(\beta)}\operatorname{tr}\!\left(e^{-\beta H/2}e^{-\beta(H+\Delta H)/2}\right)=\frac{1}{Z(\beta)}\sum_{n}e^{-\beta E_{n}/2}\,\langle n|e^{-\beta(H+\Delta H)/2}|n\rangle.

(53)

Using the convexity of the exponential, for any Hermitian
X
and normalized vector
|\psi\rangle
,

\langle\psi|e^{X}|\psi\rangle\geq e^{\langle\psi|X|\psi\rangle},

Applying this to Eq.
53
with
X=-\frac{\beta}{2}(H+\Delta H),\,\,|\psi\rangle=|n\rangle
,
gives

\langle n|e^{-\beta(H+\Delta H)/2}|n\rangle\geq e^{-\beta\langle n|H+\Delta H|n\rangle/2}.

Since

\langle n|H+\Delta H|n\rangle=E_{n}+\langle n|\Delta H|n\rangle\leq E_{n}+\left\lVert\Delta H\right\rVert,

we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\sqrt{\rho_{\beta}}|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta,\theta}(t)}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Z(\beta)}\operatorname{tr}\!\left(e^{-\beta H/2}e^{-\beta(H+\Delta H)/2}\right).

(54)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Z(\beta)}\sum_{n}e^{-\beta E_{n}/2}\,\langle n|e^{-\beta(H+\Delta H)/2}|n\rangle

(55)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Z(\beta)}\sum_{n}e^{-\beta E_{n}/2}e^{-\beta(E_{n}+\left\lVert\Delta H\right\rVert)/2}

(56)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\beta\left\lVert\Delta H\right\rVert/2}.

(57)

∎

Remark 1
.

In the mixed-field Ising model used in the numerics in the main text,
H=\sum_{i=1}^{L-1}Z_{i}Z_{i+1}+\sum_{i=1}^{L}(gX_{i}+hZ_{i})+Z_{1}/4-Z_{L}/4
, and
U_{\theta}=e^{i\theta Z_{x_{0}}}
, one finds

\Delta H=g\left(U_{\theta}^{\dagger}X_{x_{0}}U_{\theta}-X_{x_{0}}\right),

A simple calculation yields

\left\lVert\Delta H\right\rVert=2|g|\left|\sin(\theta)\right|,

and therefore

\langle\sqrt{\rho_{\beta}}|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta,\theta}(t)}\rangle\geq\exp\!\left[-\beta|g|\left|\sin(\theta)\right|\right].

F.2
Area-law Rényis
S_{\alpha>1}
for
|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta,\theta}(t)}\rangle

We now show that the time-independent overlap established in Eq.
57
directly implies an area-law upper bound on the Rényi entropies
S_{\alpha>1}
of
|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta,\theta}(t)}\rangle
. The argument is similar to that used in Ref.
Muth et al. 2011
for operator entanglement, but we repeat it here for completeness in our present notation.

We bipartition the Hilbert space into the standard
A_{s}A_{a}|B_{s}B_{a}
. Let

|\phi\rangle=\sum_{i}\sqrt{q_{i}}\,|s_{i}\rangle_{A}|t_{i}\rangle_{B},\qquad|\psi\rangle=\sum_{j}\sqrt{p_{j}}\,|u_{j}\rangle_{A}|v_{j}\rangle_{B}

be the Schmidt decompositions of two bipartite pure states, with
q_{1}\geq q_{2}\geq\cdots
and
p_{1}\geq p_{2}\geq\cdots
.

Lemma 2
.

For any
\alpha>1
,

S_{\alpha}(\rho_{A}^{\psi})\leq S_{1/2}(\rho_{A}^{\phi})-\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha-1}\log|\langle\phi|\psi\rangle|,

where
\rho_{A}^{\phi}=\operatorname{tr}_{B}|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|
and
\rho_{A}^{\psi}=\operatorname{tr}_{B}|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|
.

Proof.

Writing
|\phi\rangle,|\psi\rangle
in a product basis on
A,B
as

|\phi\rangle=\sum_{ij}\Phi_{ij}|a_{i}\rangle|b_{j}\rangle,\qquad|\psi\rangle=\sum_{ij}\Psi_{ij}|a_{i}\rangle|b_{j}\rangle,

their overlap is

\langle\phi|\psi\rangle=\operatorname{tr}(\Phi^{\dagger}\Psi).

The singular values of
\Phi,\Psi
are
\{\sqrt{q_{i}}\}
and
\{\sqrt{p_{i}}\}
respectively, so by the von Neumann trace inequality,

|\langle\phi|\psi\rangle|=|\operatorname{tr}(\Phi^{\dagger}\Psi)|\leq\sum_{i}\sqrt{q_{i}p_{i}}.

Let

Q:=\sum_{i}\sqrt{q_{i}},\qquad w_{i}:=\frac{\sqrt{q_{i}}}{Q},\qquad\sum_{i}w_{i}=1.

Then

|\langle\phi|\psi\rangle|\leq Q\sum_{i}w_{i}\,p_{i}^{1/2}.

Since
\alpha>1
, the function
x^{1/\alpha}
is concave on
x\geq 0
, and Jensen’s inequality gives

\sum_{i}w_{i}\,p_{i}^{1/2}=\sum_{i}w_{i}\,(p_{i}^{\alpha/2})^{1/\alpha}\leq\left(\sum_{i}w_{i}\,p_{i}^{\alpha/2}\right)^{1/\alpha}.

Therefore

|\langle\phi|\psi\rangle|\leq Q\left(\sum_{i}w_{i}\,p_{i}^{\alpha/2}\right)^{1/\alpha}.

Using the definition of
w_{i}
and then Cauchy–Schwarz,

\sum_{i}w_{i}\,p_{i}^{\alpha/2}=\frac{1}{Q}\sum_{i}\sqrt{q_{i}}\,p_{i}^{\alpha/2}\leq\frac{1}{Q}\left(\sum_{i}q_{i}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{i}p_{i}^{\alpha}\right)^{1/2}.

Since
\sum_{i}q_{i}=1
, this becomes

\sum_{i}w_{i}\,p_{i}^{\alpha/2}\leq\frac{1}{Q}\left(\sum_{i}p_{i}^{\alpha}\right)^{1/2}.

Substituting back, we obtain

|\langle\phi|\psi\rangle|\leq Q^{1-1/\alpha}\left(\sum_{i}p_{i}^{\alpha}\right)^{1/(2\alpha)}.

Now,

Q^{2}=\left(\sum_{i}\sqrt{q_{i}}\right)^{2}=e^{S_{1/2}(\rho_{A}^{\phi})},\qquad\sum_{i}p_{i}^{\alpha}=e^{(1-\alpha)S_{\alpha}(\rho_{A}^{\psi})}.

Hence

|\langle\phi|\psi\rangle|\leq\exp\!\left[\frac{\alpha-1}{2\alpha}S_{1/2}(\rho_{A}^{\phi})-\frac{\alpha-1}{2\alpha}S_{\alpha}(\rho_{A}^{\psi})\right].

Taking logarithms and rearranging proves

S_{\alpha}(\rho_{A}^{\psi})\leq S_{1/2}(\rho_{A}^{\phi})-\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha-1}\log|\langle\phi|\psi\rangle|.

∎

We now apply the Lemma to

|\phi\rangle=|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta}}\rangle,\qquad|\psi\rangle=|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta,\theta}(t)}\rangle.

Then

\rho_{A}^{\phi}=\rho_{A,\beta},\qquad\rho_{A}^{\psi}=\rho_{A,\beta,\theta}(t),

and therefore

S_{\alpha}(\rho_{A,\beta,\theta}(t))\leq S_{1/2}(\rho_{A,\beta})-\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha-1}\log\!\left|\langle\sqrt{\rho_{\beta}}|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta,\theta}(t)}\rangle\right|.

Using the overlap bound from Eq.
57
,

\left|\langle\sqrt{\rho_{\beta}}|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta,\theta}(t)}\rangle\right|\geq e^{-\beta\left\lVert\Delta H\right\rVert/2},

we obtain

S_{\alpha}(\rho_{A,\beta,\theta}(t))\leq S_{1/2}(\rho_{A,\beta})+\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\beta\left\lVert\Delta H\right\rVert.

We now assume the following:

Assumption 1

(Area law for the Gibbs canonical purification)
.

For fixed
\beta<\infty
, the reduced density matrix
\rho_{A,\beta}=\operatorname{tr}_{B}|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta}}\rangle\langle\sqrt{\rho_{\beta}}|
for the canonical purification of the Gibbs state satisfies

S_{1/2}(\rho_{A,\beta})\leq cL^{d-1}_{A}

uniformly in
L_{A}
across the cut
A_{s}A_{a}|B_{s}B_{a}
, where
c=O(1)
.

Corollary 1
.

Assume that the Gibbs canonical purification satisfies Assumption
1
, namely

S_{1/2}(\rho_{A,\beta})\leq cL_{A}^{d-1}.

Then for every
\alpha>1
,

S_{\alpha}(\rho_{A,\beta,\theta}(t))\leq cL_{A}^{d-1}+\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\beta\left\lVert\Delta H\right\rVert.

In particular,
|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta,\theta}(t)}\rangle
satisfies an area law for all Rényi indices
\alpha>1
, uniformly in time.

Corollary 2

(Large Schmidt eigenvalue)
.

Let
p_{1}
denote the largest eigenvalue of

\rho_{A,\beta,\theta}(t)=\operatorname{tr}_{B}|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta,\theta}(t)}\rangle\langle\sqrt{\rho_{\beta,\theta}(t)}|.

Under Assumption
1
,

p_{1}\geq\exp\!\left[-cL_{A}^{d-1}-\beta\left\lVert\Delta H\right\rVert\right].

In particular, in one spatial dimension, for a fixed cut one has

p_{1}\geq e^{-c-\beta\left\lVert\Delta H\right\rVert}=O(1),

uniformly in time.

Proof.

For any density matrix, the Rényi entropies are monotone decreasing in
\alpha
, and

S_{\infty}=-\log p_{1}.

Therefore, taking the limit
\alpha\to\infty
in the preceding corollary gives

S_{\infty}(\rho_{A,\beta,\theta}(t))\leq cL_{A}^{d-1}+\beta\left\lVert\Delta H\right\rVert.

Since
S_{\infty}(\rho_{A,\beta,\theta}(t))=-\log p_{1}
, this implies

p_{1}\geq\exp\!\left[-cL_{A}^{d-1}-\beta\left\lVert\Delta H\right\rVert\right].

In
d=1
,
L_{A}^{d-1}=1
, so for a fixed cut the lower bound is
O(1)
.
∎

Remark 2
.

The preceding corollary shows that in
d=1
the Schmidt spectrum of
|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta,\theta}(t)}\rangle
necessarily contains a time-independent
O(1)
leading weight, consistent with the numerics discussed in the main text.

Appendix H
Perturbative treatment of the locally quenched Gibbs state at high temperatures and arbitrary quench angle

In this appendix we show that, in the regime
\beta\ll 1
with
\theta=O(1)
, the canonical purification of the locally quenched Gibbs state reduces perturbatively to a product state across the cut plus a contribution from an operator that crosses the cut. This provides the precise connection to the circuit model discussed in the main text. We will only keep terms to linear order in
\beta
to illustrate the idea.

We begin from

\rho_{\beta,\theta}(t)=\frac{e^{iHt}U_{\theta}^{\dagger}e^{-\beta H}U_{\theta}e^{-iHt}}{Z(\beta)},\qquad Z(\beta)=\operatorname{tr}(e^{-\beta H}),

(58)

whose canonical purification is

|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta,\theta}(t)}\rangle=\frac{e^{iHt}U_{\theta}^{\dagger}e^{-\beta H/2}U_{\theta}e^{-iHt}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{a}}{\sqrt{Z(\beta)}}|\Phi\rangle.

(59)

Here
|\Phi\rangle=\sum_{i}|i\rangle_{s}|i\rangle_{a}
is the unnormalized maximally entangled state between the system and ancilla. We again define

\Delta H:=U_{\theta}^{\dagger}HU_{\theta}-H,\qquad\Delta H(t):=e^{iHt}\Delta H\,e^{-iHt}.

(60)

Then one has

e^{iHt}U_{\theta}^{\dagger}e^{-\beta H/2}U_{\theta}e^{-iHt}=e^{-\beta(H+\Delta H(t))/2}.

(61)

Without loss of generality, we can assume
\operatorname{tr}(H)=0
so that
Z(\beta)=|\mathcal{H}_{S}|+O(\beta^{2})
where
|\mathcal{H}_{S}|
is the Hilbert-space dimension of the system. Therefore, perturbatively in
\beta
,

|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta,\theta}(t)}\rangle=\frac{|\Phi\rangle}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{H}_{S}|}}-\frac{\beta}{2\sqrt{|\mathcal{H}_{S}|}}(H+\Delta H(t))\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{a}\,|\Phi\rangle+O(\beta^{2}).

(62)

We now bipartition the doubled Hilbert space as
A_{s}A_{a}|B_{s}B_{a}
, with
A_{s},B_{s}
two contiguous halves of the physical system. Since
|\Phi\rangle
factorizes across this cut,

|\Phi\rangle=|\Phi_{A}\rangle\otimes|\Phi_{B}\rangle,

(63)

we also have

\frac{|\Phi\rangle}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{H}_{S}|}}=\frac{|\Phi_{A}\rangle}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{H}_{A_{S}}|}}\otimes\frac{|\Phi_{B}\rangle}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{H}_{B_{S}}|}},

(64)

where
|\mathcal{H}_{A_{S}}|
and
|\mathcal{H}_{B_{S}}|
are the Hilbert-space dimensions of the system degrees of freedom in
A_{s}
and
B_{s}
, respectively. It is useful to decompose the operator
H+\Delta H(t)
into a part supported entirely in
A
, a part supported entirely in
B
, and a part with support on both sides of the cut:

H+\Delta H(t)=H_{A}(t)+H_{B}(t)+O_{\partial}(t).

(65)

Here
H_{A}(t)
and
H_{B}(t)
contain all terms supported strictly within
A_{s}
and
B_{s}
respectively, while
O_{\partial}(t)
contains all terms whose support intersects both sides of the cut.

A simple but important point is that the terms supported only in
A
or only in
B
do not generate new Schmidt structure at linear order in
\beta
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\left[1-\frac{\beta}{2}(H_{A}(t)+H_{B}(t))\right]\frac{|\Phi_{A}\rangle}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{H}_{A_{S}}|}}\frac{|\Phi_{B}\rangle}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{H}_{B_{S}}|}}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\Phi_{A}\rangle}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{H}_{A_{S}}|}}\frac{|\Phi_{B}\rangle}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{H}_{B_{S}}|}}-\frac{\beta}{2}H_{A}(t)\frac{|\Phi_{A}\rangle}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{H}_{A_{S}}|}}\frac{|\Phi_{B}\rangle}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{H}_{B_{S}}|}}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\beta}{2}\frac{|\Phi_{A}\rangle}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{H}_{A_{S}}|}}H_{B}(t)\frac{|\Phi_{B}\rangle}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{H}_{B_{S}}|}}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{\beta}{2}H_{A}(t)\right)\frac{|\Phi_{A}\rangle}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{H}_{A_{S}}|}}\otimes\left(1-\frac{\beta}{2}H_{B}(t)\right)\frac{|\Phi_{B}\rangle}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{H}_{B_{S}}|}}+O(\beta^{2}).

(66)

Thus, up to corrections of order
\beta^{2}
, the action of
H_{A}(t)+H_{B}(t)
merely dresses the product state
|\Phi_{A}\rangle|\Phi_{B}\rangle
.

Using Eqs. (
62
)–(
66
), one obtains

|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta,\theta}(t)}\rangle=|\phi_{A}(t)\rangle\otimes|\phi_{B}(t)\rangle-\frac{\beta}{2}O_{\partial}(t)\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{a}\,\frac{|\Phi\rangle}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{H}_{S}|}}+O(\beta^{2}),

(67)

where

|\phi_{A}(t)\rangle:=\left(1-\frac{\beta}{2}H_{A}(t)\right)\frac{|\Phi_{A}\rangle}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{H}_{A_{S}}|}},\qquad|\phi_{B}(t)\rangle:=\left(1-\frac{\beta}{2}H_{B}(t)\right)\frac{|\Phi_{B}\rangle}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{H}_{B_{S}}|}}.

(68)

Since

\frac{|\Phi\rangle}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{H}_{S}|}}=|\phi_{A}(t)\rangle\otimes|\phi_{B}(t)\rangle+O(\beta),

one may also write,

|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta,\theta}(t)}\rangle=\left(\mathbbm{1}-\frac{\beta}{2}O_{\partial}(t)\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{a}\,\right)|\phi_{A}(t)\rangle\otimes|\phi_{B}(t)\rangle+O(\beta^{2}).

(69)

Eq. (
69
) is the precise analogue of the circuit-model state discussed in the main text, Eq.
3
. The only difference is that the time-independent reference state
|0\rangle
is replaced by the dressed, time-dependent product state
|\phi_{A}(t)\rangle\otimes|\phi_{B}(t)\rangle
, which differs from it only by terms supported locally within
A
and
B
.

We now follow the same idea used in the circuit model in the main text and write

O_{\partial}(t)\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{a}\,\frac{|\Phi\rangle}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{H}_{S}|}}=|x(t)\rangle_{A}\otimes\frac{|\Phi_{B}\rangle}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{H}_{B_{S}}|}}+|y(t)\rangle,\qquad\left(\mathbbm{1}_{A}\otimes\frac{\langle\Phi_{B}|}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{H}_{B_{S}}|}}\right)|y(t)\rangle=0.

(70)

Since
|\phi_{B}(t)\rangle=|\Phi_{B}\rangle/\sqrt{|\mathcal{H}_{B_{S}}|}+O(\beta)
, substituting Eq. (
70
) into Eq. (
69
) gives, up to
O(\beta^{2})
corrections,

|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta,\theta}(t)}\rangle=\left(|\phi_{A}(t)\rangle-\frac{\beta}{2}|x(t)\rangle_{A}\right)\otimes|\phi_{B}(t)\rangle-\frac{\beta}{2}|y(t)\rangle+O(\beta^{2}).

(71)

Tracing out region
B
one obtains

\rho_{A}(t)=\operatorname{tr}_{B}|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta,\theta}(t)}\rangle\langle\sqrt{\rho_{\beta,\theta}(t)}|=|v(t)\rangle\langle v(t)|+O(\beta^{2}),

(72)

where

|v(t)\rangle=|\phi_{A}(t)\rangle-\frac{\beta}{2}|x(t)\rangle_{A}+O(\beta^{2}).

(73)

Let’s write

\rho_{A}(t)=|v(t)\rangle\langle v(t)|+R_{A}(t),\qquad\|R_{A}(t)\|_{1}=O(\beta^{2}).

(74)

To make entropy-density statements we now assume that
\|R_{A}(t)\|_{1}\leq C(t)\beta^{2}
where
C(t)
is independent of the subsystem size. This implies that
T_{A}(t):=\frac{1}{2}\|\rho_{A}(t)-|v(t)\rangle\langle v(t)|\|_{1}\leq\frac{C(t)}{2}\beta^{2}
. The Fannes–Audenaert continuity bound
Fannes 1973
;
Audenaert 2007
then implies

S_{1}(\rho_{A}(t))\leq T_{A}(t)\log(\dim\mathcal{H}_{A}-1)+h_{2}(T_{A}(t)),

(75)

where
h_{2}(x)=-x\log x-(1-x)\log(1-x)
. Since the doubled region
A=A_{s}A_{a}
has local Hilbert-space dimension
q^{2}
per physical site,
\log\dim\mathcal{H}_{A}=2|A_{s}|\log q
. Therefore, the volume law coefficient of
S_{1}
is bounded as

s_{1}=\lim_{|A_{s}|\to\infty}\frac{S_{1}(\rho_{A}(t))}{|A_{s}|}\leq C(t)\beta^{2}\log q.

(76)

Thus the volume-law coefficient of the top-level von Neumann entropy vanishes at least quadratically in
\beta
.

Appendix I
Perturbative treatment of the locally quenched Gibbs state at small quench angle and
\beta=O(1)

In this appendix we consider the regime
\theta\ll 1
at fixed
\beta=O(1)
. We show that, upon approximating the Gibbs canonical purification by a finite-bond-dimension state, the locally quenched purification takes a form directly analogous to the circuit-model state discussed in the main text.

Let

U_{\theta}=e^{i\theta Q_{x}},

(77)

where
Q_{x}
is a local Hermitian operator. The canonical purification of the quenched Gibbs state is

|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta,\theta}(t)}\rangle=\frac{e^{iHt}U_{\theta}^{\dagger}e^{-\beta H/2}U_{\theta}e^{-iHt}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{a}}{\sqrt{Z(\beta)}}|\Phi\rangle.

(78)

Expanding to linear order in
\theta
,

U_{\theta}^{\dagger}e^{-\beta H/2}U_{\theta}=e^{-\beta H/2}+i\theta[e^{-\beta H/2},Q_{x}]+O(\theta^{2}).

(79)

Since
e^{-\beta H/2}
commutes with
H
, this implies

|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta,\theta}(t)}\rangle=|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta}}\rangle+\frac{i\theta}{\sqrt{Z(\beta)}}|[e^{-\beta H/2},Q_{x}(t)]\rangle+O(\theta^{2}),

(80)

where

Q_{x}(t)=e^{iHt}Q_{x}e^{-iHt}.

(81)

Equivalently, using the vectorization identities

|AQ\rangle=(\mathbbm{1}\otimes Q^{T})|A\rangle,\qquad|QA\rangle=(Q\otimes\mathbbm{1})|A\rangle,

(82)

Eq. (
80
) may be written as

|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta,\theta}(t)}\rangle=|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta}}\rangle+i\theta\,\bigl(\mathbbm{1}\otimes Q_{x}(t)^{T}-Q_{x}(t)\otimes\mathbbm{1}\bigr)|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta}}\rangle+O(\theta^{2}).

(83)

We now assume that the Gibbs canonical purification is well approximated by a finite-bond-dimension state across the cut
A_{s}A_{a}|B_{s}B_{a}
:

|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta}}\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{D}\sqrt{p_{i}}\,|\Phi_{A}^{i}\rangle|\Phi_{B}^{i}\rangle,

(84)

with
D=O(1)
.

Similar to the circuit model in the main text, we now decompose the term linear in
\theta
in Eq. (
83
) into the part that remains inside the Schmidt sector of
|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta}}\rangle
on the
B
side, and the orthogonal remainder:

\bigl(\mathbbm{1}\otimes Q_{x}(t)^{T}-Q_{x}(t)\otimes\mathbbm{1}\bigr)\sum_{i=1}^{D}\sqrt{p_{i}}\,|\Phi_{A}^{i}\rangle|\Phi_{B}^{i}\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{D}|x_{A}^{i}(t)\rangle|\Phi_{B}^{i}\rangle+|y(t)\rangle,

(85)

with
\langle\Phi_{B}^{i}|y(t)\rangle=0

for all
i
. Substituting Eq. (
85
) into Eq. (
83
), we find

|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta,\theta}(t)}\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{D}\left(\sqrt{p_{i}}\,|\Phi_{A}^{i}\rangle+i\theta|x_{A}^{i}(t)\rangle\right)|\Phi_{B}^{i}\rangle+i\theta|y(t)\rangle+O(\theta^{2}).

(86)

Since
|y(t)\rangle
is orthogonal to all
|\Phi_{B}^{i}\rangle
, tracing out region
B
leads to the following expression for the density matrix on
A
:

\rho_{A}(t)=\operatorname{tr}_{B}|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta,\theta}(t)}\rangle\langle\sqrt{\rho_{\beta,\theta}(t)}|=\sum_{i=1}^{D}\left(\sqrt{p_{i}}\,|\Phi_{A}^{i}\rangle+i\theta|x_{A}^{i}(t)\rangle\right)\left(\sqrt{p_{i}}\,\langle\Phi_{A}^{i}|-i\theta\langle x_{A}^{i}(t)|\right)+O(\theta^{2}).

(87)

Thus, to linear order in
\theta
, the reduced density matrix is controlled entirely by the same number of
O(1)
Schmidt states as the finite-bond-dimension approximation to the thermal purification. In this sense, the small-
\theta
expansion has the same structure as the circuit-model state discussed in the main text, with the one-dimensional sector replaced by the
D
-dimensional Schmidt sector of
|\sqrt{\rho_{\beta}}\rangle
.

Following the same argument as in Appendix
H
, if one again assumes that the one-norm of the
O(\theta^{2})
correction in Eq. (
87
) is uniformly bounded by a system-size independent constant, then the Fannes–Audenaert continuity bound
Fannes 1973
;
Audenaert 2007
implies that the volume law coefficient of
S_{1}(\rho_{A})
vanishes at least quadratically in
\theta
.
