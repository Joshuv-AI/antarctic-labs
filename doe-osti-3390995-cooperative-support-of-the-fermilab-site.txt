 Abstract of CRADA work:

 Under the prime contract with the Department of Energy, Fermi Research Alliance, LLC (FRA)
 is responsible for effective stewardship of the Fermi National Accelerator Laboratory (Fermilab)
 6,800-acre site, to assure that Fermilab is renewed and enhanced to meet future mission needs.
 As the steward of the Fermilab site, FRA is responsible for maintaining the natural areas for the
 site (e.g., prairie restoration sites, woodlands, etc.), which are essential for protecting the health
 and safety of personnel working on or visiting the site, while supporting the scientific and
 educational missions of Fermilab.

 Fermilab Natural Areas (FNA) is a not-for-profit corporation organized and existing under the
 laws of the State of Illinois. FNA was founded in 2006 and incorporated in 2008 for the
 purposes of studying, conserving, and restoring the Fermilab Site’s natural areas in a manner
 consistent with the science mission of Fermilab and enhancing the awareness and visibility of
 the natural amenities of Fermilab to neighboring communities.

 Through this CRADA, FRA and FNA will continue to collaborate for the purposes of sustaining
 and expanding restoration, conservation, management, and enhancement of the Fermilab site’s
 natural areas in support of Fermilab’s Environmental Management System, collecting ecological
 data, including plants and wildlife, that is needed to evaluate the environmental impacts of
 laboratory operations and new construction projects, and supporting educational opportunities in
 ecological science.

 Summary of Research Results:

 Over the course of the CRADA from 2023 to 2025, FNA contributed greatly to help achieve
 goals set forth by the ELM Committee. They contributed over 12,000 hours of natural areas
 work during this time period averaging about 4,000 hours per year. During this CRADA they
 also hired seven Robert F. Betz interns to assist with daily ecology activities. Three of these
 occurred during the summer and four occurred during fall.

 There were many activities that were carried out during this time. FNA cut and removed
 invasive brush as well as conducted weed control on herbaceous non-native plants. They
 would also collect, process, and sort native seed so that the Fermilab Ecology group could
 reseed areas that needed to have an increase of native plant diversity. Plant monitors recorded
 population sizes of uncommon to rare plant species and even cross pollinated several species
 to increase the likelihood of producing viable seed. Wildlife monitors surveyed many wildlife
 groups including insects, amphibians, reptiles, birds, and mammals. Notable observations
 during this period include the number of Osprey nests increasing from three to five nests.

 In addition, several grants have been applied for and received during this CRADA. DuPage
 Foundation Grant and the Nature Conservancy Volunteer Stewardship Network Grant. These
 grants have helped to supply needed equipment to conduct ecological work, hire the interns
 mentioned above, and carry out portions of habitat restoration project prioritized by the ELM
 Committee.
