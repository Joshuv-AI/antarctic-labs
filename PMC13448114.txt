Bridging the Great Discordance: Ontological Intelligence as the New Paradigm in Spine Surgery
Authors: Jin-Sung Kim
Published: 2026

INTRODUCTION: THE EPISTEMOLOGICAL CRISIS IN SPINE CARE

We propose Ontological Intelligence as a new paradigm for spine surgery. Analogous to enterprise ontology platforms such as Palantir Foundry, deployed at scale across defense, manufacturing, and financial services to fuse fragmented data streams into unified decision-logic models, this artificial intelligence (AI) framework moves beyond static data to encapsulate the active decision-making logic of clinical care. It maps real-world entities (patients, pathologies, implants, outcomes) alongside their semantic relationships and kinetic actions—when to operate, when to wait, how to escalate—into a living, machine-readable knowledge system. This architecture directly addresses two unresolved epistemological crises.

Ontological Intelligence is conceptually distinct from related approaches: unlike a static knowledge graph that represents factual relationships [1], a conventional clinical decision support system that applies predefined rules [2], or a digital twin that models physical or biomechanical entities [3], it integrates these paradigms by virtualizing the cognitive process of clinical judgment itself.

First, Clinicoradiological Discordance. Among all musculoskeletal pathologies, spinal disorders exhibit the most profound disparity between imaging findings and patient symptoms. Patients with severe radiological abnormalities—high-grade stenosis, massive disc extrusion—may remain asymptomatic, while those with subtle structural changes suffer debilitating impairment [4,5]. Current guidelines, being linear and algorithmic, cannot accommodate the high-dimensional integration of biomechanics, comorbidities, and psychosocial context that optimal decisions require.

Second, Attrition of Heuristic Knowledge. Spine surgery depends heavily on tacit knowledge—the nuanced judgment accumulated by master surgeons over decades. Unlike codified guidelines, these heuristics (such as delaying surgery based on physiological reserve or frailty despite clear imaging indications) are rarely documented, remaining sequestered within individual minds and evaporating upon retirement.

THE ONTOLOGICAL IMPERATIVE: FROM DATA TO DECISION

In information science, an ontology is not a static classification (such as International Classification of Diseases, 10th Revision) but a formal specification of a shared conceptualization that explicitly defines concepts and their semantic relationships [6]. A Spinal Ontology— building upon foundational efforts such as the Korean e-Spine database and ontology project [7]—would encode, for example, that “L4–5 spondylolisthesis” may be associated with “mechanical instability,” conditionally necessitating “fusion” only when defined clinical thresholds are satisfied. This transforms fragmented data into a structured, machine-readable format, enabling AI to reason with genuine clinical context.

Analogous frameworks have already been explored in highstakes industries. Enterprise ontology platforms such as Palantir Foundry illustrate how fusing fragmented data streams into a unified decision-logic model—a Digital Twin of the organization— can transform a System of Record into a System of Insight. A Spinal Intelligence Platform built on the same principle would integrate imaging (picture archiving and communication system), electronic medical records, and patient-reported outcomes into a unified model of the optimal therapeutic pathway [8].

VISION: THE COGNITIVE COLLECTIVE INTELLIGENCE AVATAR

Building upon this ontological foundation, we envision a ‘Cognitive Collective Intelligence Avatar’—a system that virtualizes the collective expertise of the world’s leading spine surgeons, going beyond the Physical Digital Twin that models biomechanics alone [3]. By employing swarm intelligence algorithms and Bayesian Truth Serum by employing swarm intelligence for consensus aggregation and Bayesian Truth Serum for the truthful elicitation of subjective judgments, the Avatar aggregates expert judgment while filtering individual bias and noise. This concept is grounded in emerging evidence: a digital swarm intelligence platform applied to radiological consensus increased interreader reliability, with an absolute increase in Cohen κ of up to 0.30 compared with individual expert judgment [9], and Bayesian Truth Serum has been validated for eliciting truthful subjective opinions when objective ground truth is unavailable [10]. Built on established algorithms, the Avatar functions as a perpetual global Grand Rounds, delivering consensus-based, explainable recommendations to both surgeon and patient.

We acknowledge that the generalizability of these findings— derived primarily from radiological consensus tasks—to the multidimensional decision-making of spine surgery requires rigorous validation. Realizing this Avatar in practice will demand prospective benchmarking against expert surgical judgment and careful attention to interpretability before clinical deployment.

CONCLUSION: A PARADIGM SHIFT FOR FUTURE RESEARCH

Ontological Intelligence in spine surgery demands three converging shifts. First, from static taxonomy to dynamic ontology: research must prioritize causal relationships among clinical variables over mere labeling, building living knowledge graphs that evolve in real time. Second, from eminence-based to intelligencebased medicine: while preserving the rigor of evidence-based medicine, this paradigm extends it by integrating algorithmically aggregated expert consensus that complements—and ultimately surpasses—the intuition of individual key opinion leaders. Third, from outcome reporting to process encoding: capturing the decision-making heuristics behind each clinical choice transforms the art of medicine into a permanent, accessible scientific asset.

By bridging the gap between imaging and clinical reality, Ontological Intelligence redefines precision in spine care, ensuring that collective wisdom translates directly into clinical action and fulfills medicine's mandate to alleviate suffering with the utmost precision.
