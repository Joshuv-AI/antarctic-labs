Abstract
. In this paper, we study the structure of double cyclic codes of length ( γ , δ ) (\gamma,\delta) over 𝔽 q + u ​ 𝔽 q , u 2 = 0 \mathbb{F}_{q}+u\mathbb{F}_{q},u^{2}=0 . We also study the dual of double cyclic code of length ( γ , δ ) (\gamma,\delta) and give a minimal spanning set of double cyclic codes. Moreover, we study the necessary and sufficient conditions for a double cyclic code to be reversible and reversible-complement double cyclic code and with the help of these codes, we constructed DNA codes over 𝔽 4 + u ​ 𝔽 4 , u 2 = 0 \mathbb{F}_{4}+u\mathbb{F}_{4},u^{2}=0 . We also constructed some optimal codes to support our results.

Preliminaries

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a finite field of
q

q=p^{e},\ e\in\mathbb{N}
and
p
is any prime. Let
\mathscr{R}=\mathbb{F}_{q}+u\mathbb{F}_{q},\ u^{2}=0
then,
\mathscr{R}
is a finite commutative ring with unity of characteristic
p
and cardinality
q^{2}
. The ring
\mathscr{R}
is a principal ideal chain ring with unique maximal ideal
\langle u\rangle
hence
\mathscr{R}
is a local ring. The Gray map from
\mathscr{R}
to
\mathbb{F}^{2}_{q}
is defined as

\displaystyle\vartheta:\mathscr{R}\to

\displaystyle\mathbb{F}^{2}_{q}

\displaystyle\vartheta(a+ub)=

\displaystyle(a,a+b),\ a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}.

This map can naturally be extended to
\Phi:\mathscr{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}^{2n}_{q}
. Recall that a linear code
\mathbf{C}
of length
n
over any commutative ring
\mathbf{R}
is an
\mathbf{R}
-submodule of
\mathbf{R}
-module
\mathbf{R}^{n}
. A linear code
\mathbf{C}
is called cyclic if
\mathbf{C}
is closed under cyclic shift i.e., for

(c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-1})\in\mathbf{C}\implies(c_{n-1},c_{0},\ldots,c_{n-2})\in\mathbf{C}\ \forall(c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-1})\in\mathbf{C}.

For any
b=(b_{0},b_{1},\ldots,b_{n-1})\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
, we can identify
b
by a polynomial
b(x)=b_{0}+b_{1}x+\cdots+b_{n-1}x^{n-1}
in
\frac{\mathbf{R}[x]}{\langle x^{n}-1\rangle}
. Then, any linear code
\mathbf{C}
of length
n
over
\mathbf{R}
is cyclic if and only if
\mathbf{C}
is an ideal in
\frac{\mathbf{R}[x]}{\langle x^{n}-1\rangle}
.

The following result gives the complete structure of cyclic code over the ring
\mathscr{R}
of length
n
. For further insight readers may refer to
[
20
]

Theorem 2.1
.

[

Let

.

1.

If

(a)

(b)

\gamma

we have from equation (
3.1
) that
t(x)=\xi_{1}(x)(g_{1}(x)+ua_{1}(x))
then

\displaystyle(t(x)|g_{2}(x)+ua_{2}(x))=

\displaystyle(\xi_{1}(x)(g_{1}(x)+ua_{1}(x))|g_{2}(x)+ua_{2}(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi_{1}(x)\star(g_{1}(x)+ua_{1}(x)|0)+(0|g_{2}(x)+ua_{2}(x))\in\mathit{C}.

When
\xi_{2}(x)=u\frac{x^{\delta}-1}{g_{2}(x)}\nu_{1}(x)
we have from equation (
3.1
) that
t(x)=\xi_{1}(x)(g_{1}(x)+ua_{1}(x))+u\frac{x^{\delta}-1}{g_{2}(x)}\nu_{1}(x)t(x)
and from Lemma (
3.3
)
u\frac{x^{\delta}-1}{g_{2}(x)}\nu_{1}(x)t(x)=d(x)(g_{1}(x)+ua_{1}(x))\text{ mod }x^{\gamma}-1
for some
d(x)\in\mathscr{R}[x]
then

\displaystyle(t(x)|g_{2}(x)+ua_{2}(x))=

\displaystyle((\xi_{1}(x)+d(x))(g_{1}(x)+ua_{1}(x))|g_{2}(x)+ua_{2}(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\xi_{1}(x)+d(x))\star(g_{1}(x)+ua_{1}(x)|0)+(0|g_{2}(x)+ua_{2}(x))\in\mathit{C}.

Finally, for
\xi_{2}(x)=\frac{x^{\delta}-1}{a_{2}(x)}\nu_{2}(x)
we have from equation (
3.1
) that
t(x)=\xi_{1}(x)(g_{1}(x)+ua_{1}(x))+\frac{x^{\delta}-1}{a_{2}(x)}\nu_{2}(x)t(x)
and from Lemma (
3.3
)
\frac{x^{\delta}-1}{a_{2}(x)}\nu_{2}(x)t(x)=f(x)(g_{1}(x)+ua_{1}(x))\text{ mod }x^{\gamma}-1
for some
f(x)\in\mathscr{R}[x]
then

\displaystyle(t(x)|g_{2}(x)+ua_{2}(x))=

\displaystyle((\xi_{1}(x)+f(x))(g_{1}(x)+ua_{1}(x))|g_{2}(x)+ua_{2}(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\xi_{1}(x)+f(x))\star(g_{1}(x)+ua_{1}(x)|0)+(0|g_{2}(x)+ua_{2}(x))\in\mathit{C}.

Hence,
\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathit{C}
and therefore
\mathit{C}=\mathscr{C}
. We conclude that
\mathscr{C}=\langle(g_{1}(x)+ua_{1}(x)|0),(0|g_{2}(x)+ua_{2}(x))\rangle
.
∎

Let
\mathscr{C}

over
\mathscr{R}
, where

and
t(x)
satisfy the conditions in theorem
3.1
and

4.
Minimal spanning set

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a double cyclic code of length
(\gamma,\delta)
over
\mathscr{R}
, where
g_{1}(x),a_{1}(x),g_{2}(x),a_{2}(x)
and
t(x)
are as in Theorem
3.1
such that
x^{\gamma}-1=g_{1}(x)h_{1}(x)
,
x^{\delta}-1=g_{2}(x)h_{2}(x)
with
deg(g_{1}(x))=r_{1},\ deg(a_{1}(x))=r_{2},\ deg(g_{2}(x))=s_{1},\ deg(a_{2}(x))=s_{2}
. Consider the sets

\displaystyle N_{1}=

\displaystyle\bigcup\limits_{k=0}^{\gamma-r_{1}-1}\big\{x^{k}\star(g_{1}(x)+ua_{1}(x)|0)\big\}

\displaystyle N_{2}=

\displaystyle\bigcup\limits_{k=0}^{r_{1}-r_{2}-1}\big\{x^{k}\star(uh_{1}(x)a_{1}(x)|0)\big\}

\displaystyle N_{3}=

\displaystyle\bigcup\limits_{k=0}^{\delta-s_{1}-1}\big\{x^{k}\star(t(x)|g_{2}(x)+ua_{2}(x))\big\}

\displaystyle N_{4}=

\displaystyle\bigcup\limits_{k=0}^{s_{1}-s_{2}-1}\big\{x^{k}\star(h_{2}(x)t(x)|uh_{2}(x)a_{2}(x))\big\}.

Then
N_{1}\cup N_{2}\cup N_{3}\cup N_{4}
forms a minimal generating set for
\mathscr{C}
as an
\mathscr{R}
module. Moreover,
\mathscr{C}
has
q^{2\gamma+2\delta-r_{1}-s_{1}-r_{2}-s_{2}}
codewords.

Proof.

Let
c(x)\in\mathscr{C}
then there exist polynomials
p(x),q(x)\in\mathscr{R}[x]
such that

c(x)=p(x)\star(g_{1}(x)+ua_{1}(x)|0)+q(x)\star(t(x)|g_{2}(x)+ua_{2}(x)).

If
deg(p(x))\leq\gamma-r_{1}-1
then we have
p(x)\star(g_{1}(x)+ua_{1}(x)|0)\in span(N_{1})
. Otherwise by division algorithm, we have

p(x)=h_{1}(x)p_{1}(x)+l_{1}(x),

where
p_{1}(x),l_{1}(x)\in\mathscr{R}[x]
and
l_{1}(x)=0
or
deg(l_{1}(x))\leq\gamma-r_{1}-1
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle p(x)\star(g_{1}(x)+ua_{1}(x)|0)=

\displaystyle(h_{1}(x)p_{1}(x)+l_{1}(x))\star(g_{1}(x)+ua_{1}(x)|0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{1}(x)\star(uh_{1}(x)a_{1}(x)|0)+l_{1}(x)\star(g_{1}(x)+ua_{1}(x)|0).

If
deg(p_{1}(x))\leq r_{1}-r_{2}-1
then,
p_{1}(x)\star(uh_{1}(x)a_{1}(x)|0)\in Span(N_{2})
. Otherwise by division algorithm

p_{1}(x)=\frac{x^{\gamma}-1}{h_{1}(x)a_{1}(x)}p_{2}(x)+l_{2}(x),

where
p_{2}(x),l_{2}(x)\in\mathscr{R}[x]
and
l_{2}(x)=0
or
deg(l_{2}(x))\leq r_{1}-r_{2}-1
. Therefore we have

\displaystyle p_{1}(x)\star(uh_{1}(x)a_{1}(x)|0)=

\displaystyle\big(\frac{x^{\gamma}-1}{h_{1}(x)a_{1}(x)}p_{2}(x)+l_{2}(x)\big)\star(uh_{1}(x)a_{1}(x)|0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle l_{2}(x)\star(uh_{1}(x)a_{1}(x)|0)\in Span(N_{2}).

Therefore,
p(x)\star(g_{1}(x)+ua_{1}(x)|0)\in Span(N_{1}\cup N_{2})
.

Now if
deg(q(x))\leq\delta-s_{1}-1
, then
q(x)\star(t(x)|g_{2}(x)+ua_{2}(x))\in Span(N_{3})
. Otherwise from division algorithm

q(x)=h_{2}(x)q_{1}(x)+l_{3}(x),

where
q_{1}(x),l_{3}(x)\in\mathscr{R}[x]
and
l_{3}(x)=0
or
deg(l_{3}(x))\leq\delta-s_{1}-1
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle q(x)\star(t(x)|g_{2}(x)+ua_{2}(x))=

\displaystyle(h_{2}(x)q_{1}(x)+l_{3}(x))\star(t(x)|g_{2}(x)+ua_{2}(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q_{1}(x)\star(h_{2}(x)t(x)|uh_{2}(x)a_{2}(x))+l_{3}(x)\star(t(x)|g_{2}(x)+ua_{2}(x)).

If
deg(q_{1}(x))\leq s_{1}-s_{2}-1
, then
q_{1}(x)\star(h_{2}(x)t(x)|uh_{2}(x)a_{2}(x))\in Span(N_{4})
. Otherwise using division algorithm we get

q_{1}(x)=\frac{x^{\delta}-1}{h_{2}(x)a_{2}(x)}q_{2}(x)+l_{4}(x),

where
q_{2}(x),l_{4}(x)\in\mathscr{R}[x]
and
l_{4}(x)=0
or
deg(l_{4}(x))\leq s_{1}-s_{2}-1
. Therefore,

\displaystyle q_{1}(x)\star(h_{2}(x)t(x)|uh_{2}(x)a_{2}(x))=

\displaystyle\big(\frac{x^{\delta}-1}{h_{2}(x)a_{2}(x)}q_{2}(x)+l_{4}(x)\big)\star\big(h_{2}(x)t(x)|uh_{2}(x)a_{2}(x)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q_{2}(x)\star\big(\frac{x^{\delta}-1}{a_{2}(x)}t(x)|0\big)+l_{4}(x)\star\big(h_{2}(x)t(x)|uh_{2}(x)a_{2}(x)\big).

From Lemma
3.3
,
q_{2}(x)\star\big(\frac{x^{\delta}-1}{a_{2}(x)}t(x)|0\big)\in Span(N_{1}\cup N_{2})
and
l_{4}(x)\star\big(h_{2}(x)t(x)|uh_{2}(x)a_{2}(x)\big)\in Span(N_{4}).
Therefore
N_{1}\cup N_{2}\cup N_{3}\cup N_{4}
is a spanning set for
\mathscr{C}
. Since no element in
N_{1}\cup N_{2}\cup N_{3}\cup N_{4}
is linearly dependent with other elements so, it is a minimal spanning set for
\mathscr{C}
. Clearly
\mathscr{C}
has
q^{2\gamma+2\delta-r_{1}-s_{1}-r_{2}-s_{2}}
codewords.
∎

5.
Dual

In this section, we find the relation between the generator polynomials of double cyclic code and its dual code. For a double cyclic code
\mathscr{C}=\langle(g_{1}(x)+ua_{1}(x)|0),(t(x)|g_{2}(x)+ua_{2}(x))\rangle
with generators as in Theorem
3.1
of length
(\gamma,\delta)
over
\mathscr{R}
, let us denote
g_{1}(x)+ua_{1}(x)
by
F_{1}(x)
and
g_{2}(x)+ua_{2}(x)
by
G_{1}(x)
for simplicity and we also assume
F_{1}(x)\text{ and }G_{1}(x)
to be monic over
\mathscr{R}[x]
. Then
\mathscr{C}=\langle(F_{1}(x)|0),(t(x)|G_{1}(x))\rangle
.

From
[
10
]
, we have that the dual
\mathscr{C}^{\perp}
of
\mathscr{C}
is also a double cyclic code of length
(\gamma,\delta)
over
\mathscr{R}
, hence we denote
\mathscr{C}^{\perp}=\langle(\widehat{F_{1}}(x)|0),(\widehat{t}(x)|\widehat{G_{1}}(x))\rangle
.
For any polynomial
f(x)\in\mathscr{R}[x]
of degree
e
, the reciprocal polynomial
f^{*}(x)
of
f(x)
is defined as
f^{*}(x)=x^{e}f(x^{-1})
. The polynomial
f(x)
is said to be self-reciprocal if
f^{*}(x)=f(x)
.

Lemma 5.1
.

[
7
]

Let
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Then
x^{mn}-1=(x^{n}-1)\theta_{m}(x^{n})
, where
\theta_{m}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}x^{i}
.

Let
\xi=lcm(\gamma,\delta)
, motivated by Borges et al.,
[
7
]
we define the following map:

\varphi:\mathscr{R_{\gamma,\delta}}\times\mathscr{R_{\gamma,\delta}}\to\frac{\mathscr{R}[x]}{\langle x^{\xi}-1\rangle},

such that for any two elements
\textbf{v}(x)=(v(x)|v^{\prime}(x))
and
\textbf{w}(x)=(w(x)|w^{\prime}(x))
of
\mathscr{R_{\gamma,\delta}}
, we have

\varphi(\textbf{v}(x),\textbf{w}(x))=v(x)\theta_{\frac{\xi}{\gamma}}(x^{\gamma})x^{\xi-1-deg(w(x))}w^{*}(x)+v^{\prime}(x)\theta_{\frac{\xi}{\delta}}(x^{\delta})x^{\xi-1-deg(w^{\prime}(x))}w^{\prime*}(x).

\varphi
is a bilinear map between
\mathscr{R}[x]
-modules.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
v
and
w
be elements of
\mathscr{R^{\gamma}}\times\mathscr{R^{\delta}}
with associated polynomials
\textbf{v}(x)=(v(x)|v^{\prime}(x))
and
\textbf{w}(x)=(w(x)|w^{\prime}(x))
respectively. Then
v
is orthogonal to
w
and all of its cyclic shifts if and only if
\varphi(\textbf{v}(x),\textbf{w}(x))=0
.

Proof.

For
\textbf{v}=(v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{\gamma-1}|v^{\prime}_{0},v^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,v^{\prime}_{\delta-1})
and
\textbf{w}=(w_{0},w_{1},\ldots,v_{\gamma-1}|w^{\prime}_{0},w^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,w^{\prime}_{\delta-1})
in
\mathscr{R^{\gamma}}\times\mathscr{R^{\delta}}
, let
\textbf{v}^{(i)}=(v_{0+i},v_{1+i},\ldots,v_{\gamma-1+i}|v^{\prime}_{0+i},v^{\prime}_{1+i},\ldots,v^{\prime}_{\delta-1+i})
be the
i^{th}
double cyclic shift of
v
. Then,
\textbf{w}\cdot\textbf{v}^{(i)}=0
if and only if
\sum_{j=0}^{\gamma-1}w_{j}v_{j+i}+\sum_{d=0}^{\delta-1}w^{\prime}_{d}v^{\prime}_{d+i}=0.
Let
S_{i}=\sum_{j=0}^{\gamma-1}w_{j}v_{j+i}+\sum_{d=0}^{\delta-1}w^{\prime}_{d}v^{\prime}_{d+i}
, then

\displaystyle\varphi(\textbf{v}(x),\textbf{w}(x))

\displaystyle=\sum_{e=0}^{\gamma-1}\big(\theta_{\frac{\xi}{\gamma}}(x^{\gamma})\sum_{j=0}^{\gamma-1}w_{j}v_{j+e}x^{\xi-1-e}\big)+\sum_{l=0}^{\delta-1}\big(\theta_{\frac{\xi}{\delta}}(x^{\delta})\sum_{d=0}^{\delta-1}w^{\prime}_{d}v_{d+l}x^{\xi-1-l}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\xi-1}S_{i}x^{\xi-1-i}

in
\frac{\mathscr{R}[x]}{\langle x^{\xi}-1\rangle}
. Thus,
\varphi(\textbf{v}(x),\textbf{w}(x))=0
if and only if
S_{i}=0
for all
0\leq i\leq\xi-1
.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
(F(x)|0)\text{ and }(l(x)|G(x))
be the elements in
\mathscr{R_{\gamma,\delta}}
such that
\varphi((F(x)|0),(l(x)|G(x)))=0
, then
F(x)l^{*}(x)=0
in
\frac{\mathscr{R}[x]}{\langle x^{\gamma}-1\rangle}
. If
(0|D_{1}(x))\text{ and }(m(x)|D_{2}(x))
be the elements in
\mathscr{R_{\gamma,\delta}}
such that
\varphi((0|D_{1}(x)),(m(x)|D_{2}(x)))=0
, then
D_{1}(x)D_{2}^{*}(x)=0
in
\frac{\mathscr{R}[x]}{\langle x^{\delta}-1\rangle}
.

Proof.

Since

\displaystyle\varphi((F(x)|0),(l(x)|G(x)))

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle F(x)\theta_{\frac{\xi}{\gamma}}(x^{\gamma})x^{\xi-1-deg(l(x))}l^{*}(x)

\displaystyle=0,

in
\frac{\mathscr{R}[x]}{\langle x^{\xi}-1\rangle}
. This means that
F(x)\theta_{\frac{\xi}{\gamma}}(x^{\gamma})x^{\xi-1-deg(l(x))}l^{*}(x)=f(x)(x^{\xi}-1)
for some
f(x)\in\mathscr{R}[x]
. Suppose that
g(x)=f(x)x^{deg(l(x))+1}
, then we have

F(x)\theta_{\frac{\xi}{\gamma}}(x^{\gamma})x^{\xi}l^{*}(x)=g(x)(x^{\xi}-1),

from Lemma
5.1
we get,

F(x)x^{\xi}l^{*}(x)=g(x)(x^{\gamma}-1).

Since
x
and
x^{\gamma}-1
are co-prime, we have
F(x)l^{*}(x)=0
in
\frac{\mathscr{R}[x]}{\langle x^{\gamma}-1\rangle}
.

The other case can be proved by the similar argument.
∎

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
\mathscr{C}=\langle(g_{1}(x)+ua_{1}(x)|0),(0|g_{2}(x)+ua_{2}(x))\rangle
be a double cyclic code of length (
\gamma
,
\delta
) over
\mathscr{R}
, where
g_{1}(x),a_{1}(x),g_{2}(x)
and
a_{2}(x)
satisfy the conditions of Theorem
3.1
then,
\mathscr{C}^{\perp}=\big\langle\big(\big(\frac{x^{\gamma}-1}{a_{1}(x)}\big)^{*}+u\big(\frac{x^{\gamma}-1}{g_{1}(x)}\big)^{*}|0\big),\big(0|\big(\frac{x^{\delta}-1}{a_{2}(x)}\big)^{*}+u\big(\frac{x^{\delta}-1}{g_{2}(x)}\big)^{*}\big)\big\rangle
.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a separable double cyclic code of length
(\gamma,\delta)
over
\mathscr{R}
then,
\mathscr{C}=\mathscr{C_{\gamma}}\times\mathscr{C_{\delta}}
, where
\mathscr{C_{\gamma}}=\langle g_{1}(x)+ua_{1}(x)\rangle=\langle g_{1}(x),ua_{1}(x)\rangle
and
\mathscr{C_{\delta}}=\langle g_{2}(x)+ua_{2}(x)\rangle=\langle g_{2}(x),ua_{2}(x)\rangle
. Then it is easy to see that
\mathscr{C}^{\perp}=\mathscr{C_{\gamma}}^{\perp}\times\mathscr{C_{\delta}}^{\perp}
. By
[
21
]
we have
\mathscr{C_{\gamma}}^{\perp}=\big\langle\big(\frac{x^{\gamma}-1}{a_{1}(x)}\big)^{*},u\big(\frac{x^{\gamma}-1}{g_{1}(x)}\big)^{*}\big\rangle
and
\mathscr{C_{\delta}}^{\perp}=\big\langle\big(\frac{x^{\delta}-1}{a_{2}(x)}\big)^{*},u\big(\frac{x^{\delta}-1}{g_{2}(x)}\big)^{*}\big\rangle
. Hence the result follows.
∎

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
\mathscr{C}=\langle(F_{1}(x)|0),(t(x)|G_{1}(x))\rangle
be a double cyclic code of length
(\gamma,\delta)
over
\mathscr{R}
. Let
\mathscr{C}^{\perp}=\langle(\widehat{F_{1}}(x)|0),(\widehat{t}(x)|\widehat{G_{1}}(x))\rangle
be its dual code. Then
\widehat{F_{1}}^{*}(x)gcd(F_{1}(x),t(x))=\pi(x)(x^{\gamma}-1)
, for some
\pi(x)\in\mathscr{R}[x]
.

Proof.

Since
(\widehat{F_{1}}(x)|0)\in\mathscr{C}^{\perp}
, then
\varphi((F_{1}(x)|0),(\widehat{F_{1}}(x)|0))=0
and
\varphi((t(x)|G_{1}(x)),(\widehat{F_{1}}(x)|0))=0
in
\frac{\mathscr{R}[x]}{\langle x^{\xi}-1\rangle}
. Hence from Lemma
5.3
, we have
F_{1}(x)\widehat{F_{1}}^{*}(x)=0
and
t(x)\widehat{F_{1}}^{*}(x)=0
in
\frac{\mathscr{R}[x]}{\langle x^{\gamma}-1\rangle}
. This means
gcd(F_{1}(x),t(x))\widehat{F_{1}}^{*}(x)=0
in
\frac{\mathscr{R}[x]}{\langle x^{\gamma}-1\rangle}
. Thus, there exists some
\pi(x)\in\mathscr{R}[x]
such that
\widehat{F_{1}}^{*}(x)gcd(F_{1}(x),t(x))=\pi(x)(x^{\gamma}-1)
.
∎

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
\mathscr{C}=\langle(F_{1}(x)|0),(t(x)|G_{1}(x))\rangle
be a double cyclic code of length
(\gamma,\delta)
over
\mathscr{R}
. Let
\mathscr{C}^{\perp}=\langle(\widehat{F_{1}}(x)|0),(\widehat{t}(x)|\widehat{G_{1}}(x))\rangle
be its dual code. Then
\widehat{G_{1}}^{*}(x)F_{1}(x)G_{1}(x)=\mu(x)(x^{\delta}-1)gcd(F_{1}(x),t(x))
, for some
\mu(x)\in\mathscr{R}[x]
.

Proof.

Since
(\widehat{t}(x)|\widehat{G_{1}}(x))\in\mathscr{C}^{\perp}
. Consider the codeword
c(x)=\frac{F_{1}(x)}{gcd(F_{1}(x),t(x))}\star(t(x)|G_{1}(x))-\frac{t(x)}{gcd(F_{1}(x),t(x))}\star(F_{1}(x)|0)=(0|G_{1}(x)\frac{F_{1}(x)}{gcd(F_{1}(x),t(x))})
then,
\varphi((0|G_{1}(x)\frac{F_{1}(x)}{gcd(F_{1}(x),t(x))}),(\widehat{t}(x)|\widehat{G_{1}}(x)))=0
in
\frac{\mathscr{R}[x]}{\langle x^{\xi}-1\rangle}
. Hence from Lemma
5.3
, we have
G_{1}(x)\frac{F_{1}(x)}{gcd(F_{1}(x),t(x))}\widehat{G_{1}}^{*}(x)=0
in
\frac{\mathscr{R}[x]}{\langle x^{\delta}-1\rangle}
. Hence there exists
\mu(x)\in\mathscr{R}[x]
such that
G_{1}(x)F_{1}(x)\widehat{G_{1}}^{*}(x)=\mu(x)(x^{\delta}-1)gcd(F_{1}(x),t(x))
.
∎

6.
Reversible double cyclic codes

In this section we mainly focus on the reversibility of double cyclic code over
\mathscr{R}
. For any vector
c=(b|d)=(b_{0},b_{1},\ldots,b_{\gamma-1}|d_{0},d_{1},\ldots,d_{\delta-1})
in
\mathscr{R^{\gamma}}\times\mathscr{R^{\delta}}
, the reverse
c^{r}
of
c
is defined as
c^{r}=(b_{\gamma-1},b_{\gamma-2},\ldots,b_{0}|d_{\delta-1},d_{\delta-2},\ldots,d_{0})=(b^{r}|d^{r})
.

Definition 6.1
.

A double cyclic code
\mathscr{C}
of length
(\gamma,\delta)
over
\mathscr{R}
is said to be reversible double cyclic code if for all
c\in\mathscr{C}
,
c^{r}\in\mathscr{C}
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
f(x),g(x)
be two polynomials in
\mathscr{R}[x]
with
deg(g(x))\leq deg(f(x))
. Then,

Deoxyribonucleic acid or DNA is an acid found in almost every living organism mostly found in the nucleus of the cell (Eukaryotic cell) that contains the genetic information of the living organism. It is a sequence of two long polymers, called strands which are composed of four nucleotide bases namely Adenine(A), Guanine(G), Thymine(T) and Cytosine(C). Two strands are so twisted, forming a double helix, running in opposite directions to each other and joined together by hydrogen bonds between nucleotide bases. This attachment follows the Watson-Crick Complement rule.
A
pairs with
T
and
G
pairs with
C
, as per the Watson-Crick Complement rule.
A
and
G

\bar{X}
.
\bar{G}=C
, for instance, is the complement of
G

would be its complement. According to the Watson-Crick Complement rule, a DNA strand

will pair up with

. For instance, a DNA strand

will pair up with
3^{\prime}-ACTTAGA-5^{\prime}
. A DNA code

(a)

, where

.

(b)

including

, where
s_{1},s_{2}\in\mathscr{C}
and
s_{2}^{r}
is the reverse of
s_{2}
.

including

, where

(d)

The

8.
Conclusion

In this paper, we have studied the structure of double cyclic codes of length
(\gamma,\delta)
over the ring
\mathbb{F}_{q}+u\mathbb{F}_{q},u^{2}=0
when both
\gamma
and
\delta
are coprime with
q
. We also discussed the duality of these codes. We have studied the reversibility of double cyclic codes of length
(\gamma,\delta=(kp+1)\gamma)
, where both
\gamma
and
\delta
are coprime with
q
. Moreover we have also studied the reversible-complement codes over the ring
\mathbb{F}_{4}+u\mathbb{F}_{4},u^{2}=0
that are suitable for DNA code construction. Further we have provided some examples of these codes. For future studies, it will be interesting to study double cyclic codes over different rings with different lengths and extending these studies to DNA codes construction and DNA computing.
