Greedy Vector Balancing
Wojciech Czerwiński, Daniel Dadush, Ekin Ergen, Arka Ghosh, Sławomir Lasota, Łukasz Orlikowski

Abstract
In online vector balancing, vectors 𝐭 1 , … , 𝐭 n \mathbf{t}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{t}_{n} arrive one by one from a given set T T and the goal is to assign signs s 1 , … , s n ∈ { ± 1 } s_{1},\dots,s_{n}\in\{\pm 1\} in an online manner so as to minimize the largest norm of any signed prefix sum ∑ i = 1 k s i ​ 𝐭 i \sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}\mathbf{t}_{i} , k ∈ [ n ] k\in[n] . In this paper, we analyze the natural Euclidean greedy vector balancing algorithm for this problem: at each step k k , the sign s k ∈ { ± 1 } s_{k}\in\{\pm 1\} is chosen so that s k ​ 𝐭 k s_{k}\mathbf{t}_{k} has non-positive inner product with ∑ i = 1 k − 1 s i ​ 𝐭 i \sum_{i=1}^{k-1}s_{i}\mathbf{t}_{i} . Our main result is the first finite bound, independent of the sequence length n n , on the performance of greedy whenever T T is finite. When T ⊂ ℝ d T\subset\mathbb{R}^{d} consists of unit vectors, we prove that the signed sums produced by greedy have Euclidean norm at most ( 2 / δ T ) d − 1 (2/\delta_{T})^{d-1} , where δ T \delta_{T} is the minimum non-zero distance between vectors in T T and subspaces spanned by vectors in T T . The same upper bound holds when the sequences are composed of scaled down vectors in T T . We also provide a simple set T T for which Ω ⁡ ( d / δ T ) \Omega(\sqrt{d}/\delta_{T}) is a lower bound. We analyze the greedy algorithm by proving the existence of a bounded convex K T K_{T} that is T T -absorbing : ∀ 𝐱 ∈ K T \forall\mathbf{x}\in K_{T} and 𝐭 ∈ ± T \mathbf{t}\in\pm T , ⟨ 𝐱 , 𝐭 ⟩ ≤ 0 ⇒ 𝐱 + 𝐭 ∈ K T \langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{t}\rangle\leq 0\Rightarrow\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{t}\in K_{T} . We give an explicit construction of a set K T K_{T} contained in a ball of radius ( 2 / δ T ) d − 1 (2/\delta_{T})^{d-1} , based on chains of subspaces spanned by vectors in T T , which may be of independent interest. We further generalize our greedy vector balancing bound to the setting of online vector partitioning, where the sequence 𝐭 1 , … , 𝐭 n \mathbf{t}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{t}_{n} must be partitioned in an online manner into p p subsequences of nearly equal sum. As an application, we prove a special case of a conjecture of Bosman et al. (Theory of Computing Systems, 2025), which implies that a lexicographic version of total completion time scheduling under scenarios is polynomial time solvable when the number of scenarios is fixed.

Introduction

In online vector balancing, the task is to assign signs
s_{1},s_{2},\dots\in\{\pm 1\}
to an online sequence of vectors
\mathbf{t}_{1},\mathbf{t}_{2},\dots
from a
universe
T\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, such that the signed combinations
\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}\mathbf{t}_{i}
,
k\geq 1
, have as small norm (also called discrepancy) as
possible in a given target norm. The online constraint is that the sign of a
vector must be chosen immediately after it arrives without knowledge of the
future. This problem has been studied extensively in the literature, across
adaptive adversary
models
[
27
,
24
,
9
,
28
,
19
,
8
]
,
oblivious adversary
models
[
5
,
2
,
25
,
23
]
,
and stochastic models
[
3
,
4
]
both in terms
existential bounds as well as algorithms. Algorithmic applications have been
given to provide improved SGD convergence rates
[
26
]
, improved
algorithms for numerical integration
[
6
,
20
]
,
as well as online item allocation
[
11
]
.

The online vector balancing model was first proposed by
Spencer
[
27
,
28
]
under the framework of
a two player game, where the “Pusher” player chooses the sequence of vectors
in
T
and the “Chooser” picks the signs. One of the simplest and most
natural online strategies for the Chooser, which will be the focus of this
work, is the Euclidean
greedy vector balancing algorithm
: at each
iteration, it picks a sign that (locally) minimizes the Euclidean norm of the
next iterate. The algorithm is extremely simple: at iteration
k
, greedy picks
a sign
s_{k}\in\{\pm 1\}
such that
s_{k}\mathbf{t}_{k}
has non-positive inner
product with the previous iterate
\mathbf{x}_{k-1}:=\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}s_{i}\mathbf{t}_{i}
, where
\pm 1
are both valid
when the inner product is zero. The
k
th iterate is then given by
\mathbf{x}_{k}=\mathbf{x}_{k-1}+s_{k}\mathbf{t}_{k}
. We shall say that a sequence
0:=\mathbf{x}_{0},\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots
is a
greedy sequence for
T
(or greedy
T
-sequence), if it
is consistent with the iterates produced by the greedy algorithm on some input
sequence from
T
. We denote the norm of a greedy
T
-sequence

0:=\mathbf{x}_{0},\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{n}
by
\max_{k\in[n]}\|\mathbf{x}_{i}\|
.

As observed by Spencer
[
27
]
, the
k
th iterate
\mathbf{x}_{k}

of a greedy sequence for the unit Euclidean sphere
{\cal S}^{d-1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

has norm at most
\sqrt{k}
, as the squared norm increases by at most
1
at
each iteration. This bound is also optimal for
any online algorithm
, as
one may pick the input sequence such that each next vector is a unit vector
orthogonal to the previous iterate. We note that much better bounds are known
when the Pusher is
oblivious
to choices made by Chooser instead of
adaptive, that is, when the Pusher must commit to the input sequence in
advance. In this case, randomized strategies allow for norm bounds growing
polylogarithmically with the sequence length

n

[
5
,
2
,
25
,
23
]
. The focus of this work
will be an improved analysis of greedy sequence norms however, where there is
no difference between an adaptive and oblivious Pusher.

While the above paragraph provides a complete understanding when the universe

T
is the unit sphere, there are multiple contexts appearing in applications
in which the universe
T
is instead finite and where one may hope for stronger
greedy sequence norm estimates. Our primary application will be in the context of scenario
scheduling
[
13
]
, which we detail later, where
T
consists
of binary vectors indicating which scenarios each job participates in and where
the optimal solution induces a greedy
T
-sequence. We also mention an
application by Lu, Guo and De Sa
[
26
]
in the context of stochastic
gradient descent (SGD), where
T
consists of the possible sample gradients and
greedy is used by a sample reordering algorithm called Gradient Balancing
Algorithm (Grab).

Main Contribution.

Motivated by the above, we ask the following
basic question: do greedy
T
-sequences have uniformly bounded norm,
independent of the length of the sequence, when
T
is finite? In this context,
by choosing
T
to be an
\epsilon
-net of the sphere, it is certainly
conceivable that one might be able to construct a greedy sequence whose norm
tends to infinity. As our main contribution, we in fact show that this is not
possible, and thus answer the above question in the affirmative. Somewhat
surprisingly, we are not aware of any prior work in this direction, even for
natural special cases such as
T=\{0,1\}^{d}
.

Our results are quantitative and expose a connection to geometric parameter of
a point set on a sphere called the
\delta
-distance property. This parameter
has been extensively studied in the linear programming
literature
[
12
,
15
,
14
,
17
,
18
,
22
,
16
]
,
where it has been shown to give a strong notion of numerical complexity of the
(normalized) rows of a constraint matrix. We give the definition below for
point sets on the unit sphere.

Definition 1
.

A finite set of vectors
T\subset{\cal S}^{d-1}
satisfies the
\delta
-distance
property if

\min\{{\rm dist}({\textsc{span}(U),\mathbf{t}}):U\subset T,\mathbf{t}\in T\setminus\textsc{span}(U)\}\ \geq\ \delta.

where
\textsc{span}(\cdot)
is the linear span and
{\rm dist}({\cdot,\cdot})
is the minimum
Euclidean distance. We further define
\delta_{T}\geq 0
to be the largest
\delta\geq 0
for which
T
satisfies the
\delta
-distance property.

Note that
\delta_{T}
is strictly positive since each term in the minimum is
positive (since
\mathbf{t}\notin\textsc{span}(U)
). If
T
is an infinite subset of
the sphere we always have
\delta_{T}=0
however, since by compactness of

{\cal S}^{d-1}
there is an infinite sequence of distinct points
\mathbf{t}_{1},\mathbf{t}_{2},\dots\in T
satisfying
\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\lVert\mathbf{t}_{i}-\mathbf{t}_{i+1}\rVert=0
.

With this definition in hand, we may state our main result.

Theorem 2
.

Let
T\subseteq{\cal S}^{d-1}
be a finite set. Define
G(T)
to the maximum
norm of any greedy sequence for
[-1,1]T:=\{a\mathbf{t}:a\in[-1,1],\mathbf{t}\in T\}
. Then
G(T)\leq(2/\delta_{T})^{d-1}
.

Note that
G(T)
in fact controls the norm of greedy sequences induced by
[-1,1]
scalings of elements in
T
. Given this, we may use the above theorem
to obtain estimates for arbitrary sets by appropriate scaling. Letting

{\cal B}^{d}(R)
denote the ball of radius
R
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we obtain the following
direct corollary:

Corollary 3
.

For
T\subseteq{\cal B}^{d}(R)
, let
\hat{T}:=\{\frac{\mathbf{t}}{\lVert\mathbf{t}\rVert}:\mathbf{t}\in T-\{0\}\}
. Then, we have that
G(T)\leq RG({\hat{T}})\leq R(2/\delta_{\hat{T}})^{d-1}
.

For the special case of
T=\{0,1\}^{d}
, it was shown by Alon and
Vu
[
1

that the point to subspace distance
\min\{{\rm dist}({\textsc{span}(S),\mathbf{t}}):\mathbf{t}\notin\textsc{span}(S),\mathbf{t}\cup S\subseteq\{0,1\}^{d}\}=d^{-d/2+o(d)}
. Since every vector in
T=\{0,1\}^{d}
has Euclidean norm at
most
\sqrt{d}
, by scaling this implies that
\delta_{\hat{T}}=d^{-d/2+o(d)}
as well. Applying the above corollary, we conclude that

G({\{0,1\}^{d}})\leq d^{d^{2}/2+o(d^{2})}
. As stated previously, this is the first
finite estimate on the behavior of the greedy algorithm even for this special
case. We will see an application of the bound for
G(\{0,1\}^{d})

We also briefly mention that

is the sample space,
{\cal D}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a

f(\cdot,\mathbf{y}),\forall\mathbf{y}\in S

continuous piecewise linear.

It is instructive to compare the bounds we achieve for greedy sequences to the best
uniform bounds achievable by any online algorithm. An essentially optimal
algorithm and bound in this regard has been given by
Bárány
[
9
]
. For a finite set
T\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
of
non-collinear vectors (pairs of distinct vectors in
T

independent) and target norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert

discrepancy of the best online algorithm against sequences in
T
is at most

\max_{\mathbf{y}\in\{-1,1\}^{T}}\lVert\sum_{\mathbf{t}\in T}y_{\mathbf{t}}\mathbf{t}\rVert

least
1/2
this quantity. The upper bound, which in fact also holds for
sequences in
[-1,1]T
, is obtained by the following very simple online
algorithm: choose signs so as to maintain a representation of the iterates of
the form
\sum_{\mathbf{t}\in T}y_{\mathbf{t}}\mathbf{t}
with
\mathbf{y}\in[-1,1]^{T}
. By convexity, note that the worst-case norm is achieved when
y\in\{\pm 1\}^{T}
. Such a representation can be
maintained using the fact that if
y,\Delta y\in[-1,1]
then either
y-\Delta y
or
y+\Delta y
is also in
[-1,1]
. While simple and (perhaps
surprisingly) optimal, it does not diminish the relevance of
understanding greedy sequences and their applications. Furthermore, maintaining
the requisite representation may become prohibitive if the universe
T
is
large (i.e.,
T=\{0,1\}^{d}
).

Under the Euclidean norm, one can show that

Overview of the Greedy Sequence Bound.

We provide a detailed overview of our
approach to
Theorem

2
. The main idea is to a construct a bounded convex
set which contains all greedy
T
-sequences. To this end, we define a set
{\cal K}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
to be
T
-absorbing
if it contains the origin and if
\forall\mathbf{x}\in{\cal K}
and
\mathbf{t}\in T\cup-T
,
\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{t}\rangle\leq 0

implies that
\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{t}\in{\cal K}
. It is direct to see by induction that
any such
K
contains the iterates of all greedy
T
-sequences. If
K
is also
convex, then this last statement automatically extends to
[-1,1]T
-sequences
as well, since then
\mathbf{x}\in K
and
\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{t}\in K
implies that
\mathbf{x}+[0,1]\mathbf{t}\subseteq K
.

Our notion of
T
-absorbing above is in fact a specialization of
Bárány’s notion of
T
-closedness
[
9
]
:
K
is

T
-closed if
\forall\mathbf{x}\in K
,
\forall\mathbf{t}\in T
, either
\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{t}\in K
or
\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{t}\in K
. Bárány associates every
online algorithm to a
T
-closed set containing the origin, which our
definition specializes to the greedy algorithm (note
T
-absorbing

\Rightarrow

T
-closed). In the case that
T
consists of non-collinear
vectors, Bárány approximately characterized the smallest
T
-closed
sets by showing that the convex hull of any
T
-closed set contains a shift of
the “universal”
T
-closed set
\{\sum_{\mathbf{t}\in T}y_{t}\mathbf{t}:\mathbf{y}\in\{0,1\}^{T}\}
(shifting does not affect
T
-closedness). For the

T
-absorbing case, it is formally easier to define the smallest such set as
the intersection of all (convex)
T
-absorbing sets is again (convex)

T
-absorbing. Note that these intersections are non-empty since
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is
trivially
T
-absorbing and all
T
-absorbing sets contain the origin. Our
construction will crucially make use of some of the structural properties of
the smallest such sets, though they seem more difficult to
characterize (even approximately) than in the
T
-closed setting.

-absorbing set

is always origin symmetric, i.e.,
{\cal K}_{T}=-{\cal K}_{T}
. This follows since
-{\cal K}_{T}
is also
T
-absorbing and thus
{\cal K}_{T}={\cal K}_{T}\cap-{\cal K}_{T}

-absorbing

\mathbf{t}

is

T
-projective

\pi_{\mathbf{t}}(\mathbf{x})+[-\mathbf{t},\mathbf{t}]\subseteq{\cal K},\forall\mathbf{t}\in T,\mathbf{x}\in{\cal K},

with dimension precisely

\text{dim}(T)-1:=d-1

\textsc{span}(U)^{\perp}

restricted to

is again one dimensional, spanned a unit vector

.
The direct extension of the two dimensional construction would then be to
define
{\cal K}_{T}:=\textsc{conv-hull}\left(\bigcup_{U\in{\cal U}_{T},\text{dim}(U)=d-1}{\cal K}_{U}+r_{d}[-\mathbf{r}_{U},\mathbf{r}_{U}]\right)
for the appropriate
choice of

. Unfortunately, with this construction, it is unclear
why
{\cal K}_{T}
is

-projective for any choice of

. For a pair

\mathbf{t}\notin U

since the main thing we know about

is the bound of

To remedy this issue, we will need to orthogonally extend sets

of all

are extended in more directions. Specifically, to each set

restricted to

hull of all these sets to construct

we at least resolve the last issue in the above paragraph since

can now be shown to work, however the proof is more complicated. Interestingly,

Section

1.1
Generalization to Vector Partitioning

We now consider the following
partition generalization of the online vector balancing game, which our results naturally extend to. Let a finite
vector set
T\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be given together with a number
p\geq 2

of desired partition pieces. The game between Pusher and Chooser starts with
the trivial partition
J_{1}\dot{\cup}\ldots\dot{\cup}J_{p}
with
J_{i}=\emptyset

for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,p\}
. Pusher picks a vector
\mathbf{t}_{k}\in T
at
time
k
, and Chooser has to assign the vector
\mathbf{t}_{k}
to one of the
partition pieces by adding the index
k
to one of the sets
J_{i}
, that is, the
set
J_{i}
is replaced by the set
J_{i}\dot{\cup}\{k\}
. Pusher seeks to maximize
and the Chooser minimize the largest difference between the sums of vectors
indexed by pieces of the partition. Letting
[p]:=\{1,\dots,p\}
, the value of the partition
J_{1}\dot{\cup}\dots\dot{\cup}J_{p}
is then defined as

\mathrm{val}(J_{1},\ldots,J_{p}):=\max_{n\geq 1}\max_{i_{1}\in[p]}\max_{i_{2}\in[p]}\left\|\sum_{j\in J_{i_{1}}\cap[n]}\mathbf{t}_{j}-\sum_{j\in J_{i_{2}}\cap[n]}\mathbf{t}_{j}\right\|.

It is straightforward to observe that the case
p=2
is equivalent to the original vector balancing game. Indeed, if
J_{1}
resp.
J_{2}
are the set of vectors to which Chooser assigns a plus sign
s_{j}=1
resp. a minus sign
s_{j}=-1
, then the value of the game after the
n
-th vector is
equal to
\left\|\sum_{j\in J_{1}}\mathbf{t}_{j}-\sum_{j\in J_{2}}\mathbf{t}_{j}\right\|=\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}s_{j}\mathbf{t}_{j}\right\|
.

The vector partitioning problem (in the offline setting) was first introduced by Bárány and
Doerr
[
7
,
10
]
. They proved that one
can always construct a partition
J_{1}\dot{\cup}\ldots\dot{\cup}J_{p}

such that
\mathrm{val}(J_{1},\ldots,J_{p})=O(d\max_{\mathbf{t}\in T}\lVert\mathbf{t}\rVert)
, for any norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert
. An extension of the greedy
algorithm to vector partitioning was analyzed by Aru, Narayanan, Scott, and
Venkatesan
[
3
]
in the stochastic setting, where they showed
that greedy achieves asymptotically near-optimal norm bounds when the vectors
\mathbf{t}_{1},\mathbf{t}_{2},\dots
are distributed iid from a “nice” distribution on the unit
ball. In this setting, the greedy algorithm, which they dubbed the
inner
product rule
, proceeds as follows: at step
k
assign
\mathbf{t}_{k}
to a partition
piece with which it has the smallest inner product, i.e., to any piece in
{\rm argmin}_{i\in[p]}\langle\sum_{j\in J_{i}}\mathbf{t}_{j},\mathbf{t}_{k}\rangle
.
We call any partition consistent with a run of the greedy algorithm a
greedy
partition
. One can easily verify that for a greedy partition
J_{1}\dot{\cup}J_{2}

when
p=2
, assigning
+1
to the elements
J_{1}
and
-1
to those
J_{2}

yields a signing consistent with a run of the greedy vector balancing
algorithm.

In the online context, our main result is that greedy’s value for partitioning
is at most its vector balancing value. That is, the
p=2
case is the hardest case
of the game.

Theorem 5
.

Let
p\geq 2
,
\mathbf{t}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{t}_{n}
be an input sequence from
T\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then, any greedy partition
J_{1}\dot{\cup}\dots\dot{\cup}J_{p}=[n]
for
\mathbf{t}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{t}_{n}
satisfies
\mathrm{val}(J_{1},\dots,J_{p})\leq G(T)
, where
G(T)
is the greedy
vector balancing bound as defined in
Theorem

2
.

The result follows from the simple but powerful observation that any two pieces

J_{k},J_{l}
of a greedy
p
-partition for
\mathbf{t}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{t}_{n}
also form a
greedy
2
-partition of the subsequence induced by
J_{k}\cup J_{l}
. That is, the
greedy partitioning algorithm is consistent with a run of the greedy vector
balancing on any two of its pieces. The bound therefore follows blackbox from
the vector balancing case. We note that Bárány’s online vector
balancing upper bound also extends to partitioning via a natural round-robin
extension of his balancing algorithm.

1.2
Application to Scenario Scheduling

We now detail an application of the greedy vector partitioning bound to the
problem of total completion time scheduling under scenarios introduced by
Bosman, van Ee, Ergen, Imreh, Marchetti-Spaccamela, Skutella and
Stougie
[
13
]
. In this problem, we are given
n
weighted jobs
with unit processing times to be scheduled on
p
identical machines, together
with
d
scenarios
S_{i}\subseteq[n]
,
i\in[d]
,
each of them represented by the subset
of jobs that are present in that scenario. A global assignment of jobs to
machines is adapted to any given scenario simply by restricting it to the jobs
present. The goal is to compute a global assignment of jobs to machines that
minimizes the average sum of weighted completion times over all jobs, where the
average is taken over scenarios. The formal description is given below:

MinAvgSTC(
d
)

Input:
Numbers
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
of jobs resp. machines, weights
w_{j}
for
j\in[n]
that are non-increasing in
j
, scenarios
S_{k}\subseteq[n]
for
k\in[d]
.

Task:
Find a partition
[n]=J_{1}\dot{\cup}\ldots\dot{\cup}J_{m}
of jobs so as to minimize

\displaystyle\mathrm{obj}(J_{1},\ldots,J_{m})\coloneqq\sum_{k=1}^{d}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j\in J_{i}\cap S_{k}}w_{j}\cdot|J_{i}\cap S_{k}\cap[j]\}|.

(2)

When there is only one scenario, it is classical that scheduling the jobs in a
round robin fashion in non-increasing order of weight is
optimal
[
21
]
.
[
13
]
showed that problem
is NP-hard when the number of scenarios is part of the input even when
m=2
.
However, they conjectured that the problem is polynomial for any fixed number
of scenarios. Furthermore, they provided a polynomial time dynamic programming
algorithm that solves the problem under the conjectured existence of a

balanced optimal
solution, which we state below:

Conjecture 6

(
[
13
]
)
.

MinAvgSTC
(d)
has an optimal solution
(J_{1},\ldots,J_{m})
such that for every scenario
k\in[d]
and each
j\in[n]
, the
j
largest jobs are assigned to the machines in such a way that the difference in number of jobs assigned to each pair of machines is bounded by a function
g(d)
of
d
only, or more formally

\max_{j\in[n],k\in[d]}\left\{\max_{i\in[m]}|J_{i}\cap S_{k}\cap[j]|-\min_{i\in[m]}|J_{i}\cap S_{k}\cap[j]|\right\}\leq g(d).

[
13
]
proved the conjecture for the case of unit weights

w_{1}=\cdots=w_{n}=1
. Using the greedy partitioning, we
prove the conjecture in the complementary regime of exponentially decreasing
weights:

Theorem 7
.

Conjecture
6
holds in the special case where
w_{j}\geq 2dw_{j+1}
for all
j\in[n-1]
.

The theorem implies that the dynamic program proposed by Bosman et
al.
[
13
]
can compute an optimal solution for the above choice of
weights with a running time of
O(nm^{O(g(d))})
, which is polynomial when the
number
d
of scenarios is fixed.

For the weights as above, we in fact show that
MinAvgSTC(
d
)
becomes
a lexicographic minimization problem. Namely, the optimal solution minimizes
the average completion time of the largest weight job, and subject to this,
minimizes the average completion time of the second largest weight job, etc. With this interpretation in mind, one can prove that the partition
J_{1}\cdot\cup\dots\cdot\cup J_{p}=[n]
produced by the optimal solution is a
greedy partition with respect to the vectors
\mathbf{t}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{t}_{n}
, where

\mathbf{t}_{i}\in\{0,1\}^{d}
is the indicator vector of the scenarios that job
j

participates in. The greedy vector balancing bound for
T=\{0,1\}^{d}
then
directly implies the conjecture in this case. We note that we still require the
dynamic program to compute the optimal solution in this setting as there can be
exponentially many non-equivalent greedy partitions.

which implies the analogous inclusion

R_{U}+\big({\cal B}^{m}(R_{d-1})\cap\textsc{span}(U)\big)\ \subseteq\ {\cal B}^{m}(R_{d})\cap\textsc{span}(T)
of balls,
and therefore

{\cal K}^{\delta}_{T}\ \subseteq\ {\cal B}^{m}(R_{d})\cap\textsc{span}(T).

Equivalent definition.

The inductive definition of
{\cal K}^{\delta}_{T}
can be equivalently presented as
the convex hull of multiple copies of just one-dimensional sets

{\cal K}^{\delta}_{\mathbf{t}}=[-\mathbf{t},\mathbf{t}]
,
for
\mathbf{t}\in T
,
shifted in multiple orthogonal directions along vectors
of norm
r_{i}
, for
i\in[d]
.
Going even further, one can take instead of each
{\cal K}^{\delta}_{\mathbf{t}}
only its endpoints

\{-\mathbf{t},\mathbf{t}\}
.
This intuition leads to the following non-inductive but equivalent definition of
{\cal K}^{\delta}_{T}
.

Define a
T
-chain
as any sequence

\{\mathbf{0}\}=V_{0}\subset V_{1}\subset\ldots\subset V_{\ell}=\textsc{span}(T)

of subspaces of
\textsc{span}(T)
where each
V_{i}
is spanned by some subset of
T
,
and
\text{dim}(V_{1})=1
.
There are only finitely many
T
-chains, since
T
is finite.
Given a
T
-chain
F=(V_{0}\subseteq V_{1}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq V_{\ell})

and a vector
\mathbf{x}\in\textsc{span}(T)
, we define the
F
-projection
of
\mathbf{x}

as the sequence

(\mathbf{x}^{F}_{1},\mathbf{x}^{F}_{2},\ldots,\mathbf{x}^{F}_{\ell})
where

\pi_{V_{i-1}}(\mathbf{x})+\mathbf{x}^{F}_{i}\ =\ \pi_{V_{i}}(\mathbf{x})

for every
i\in[\ell]
.
Thus
\mathbf{x}^{F}_{i}
is orthogonal to
V_{i-1}
and belongs to
V_{i}
.
We define the set

L_{F}\ :=\ \left\{\mathbf{x}\in\textsc{span}(T)\,:\,\lVert\mathbf{x}^{F}_{i}\rVert=r_{\text{dim}(V_{i})}\text{ for all }i\in[\ell]\right\}.

In particular, as
\text{dim}(V_{1})=1
and
\text{dim}(V_{\ell})=\text{dim}(\textsc{span}(T))=d
,
every vector
\mathbf{x}\in L_{F}
satisfies
\lVert\mathbf{x}^{F}_{1}\rVert=r_{1}=1
and

\lVert\mathbf{x}^{F}_{\ell}\rVert=r_{d}
.
The sets
L_{F}
allow us to give the following equivalent definition of
{\cal K}^{\delta}_{T}
:

Claim 12
.

{\cal K}^{\delta}_{T}=\textsc{conv-hull}\Big(\bigcup_{F}L_{F}\Big)
, where the union ranges
over all
T
-chains
F
.

{claimproof}

By induction on
d=\text{dim}(\textsc{span}(T))
.
If
d=1
, there is only one
T
-chain

F=(\{\mathbf{0}\}\subset\textsc{span}(\mathbf{t}))
,

L_{F}=\{-\mathbf{t},\mathbf{t}\}
,
and
{\cal K}^{\delta}_{T}=[-\mathbf{t},\mathbf{t}]=\textsc{conv-hull}(L_{F})
.

Suppose now that
d\geq 2
.
By induction assumption, for every
U\in{\cal U}_{T}
we have

{\cal K}^{\delta}_{U}=\textsc{conv-hull}\Big(\bigcup_{F^{\prime}}L_{F^{\prime}}\Big)
, where the union ranges
over all
U
-chains
F^{\prime}
.
Thus, by (
4
),

L_{U}\ =\ R_{U}+\textsc{conv-hull}\Big(\bigcup_{F^{\prime}}L_{F^{\prime}}\Big)\ =\ \textsc{conv-hull}\Big(\bigcup_{F^{\prime}}\big(R_{U}+L_{F^{\prime}}\big)\Big).

Any
U
-chain
F^{\prime}
can be extended to a
T
-chain
F
by adding
\textsc{span}(T)
at the end of
F^{\prime}
,
and
L_{F^{\prime}}
and
L_{F}
are related by
L_{F}=R_{U}+L_{F^{\prime}}
.
Conversely, every
T
-chain
F
extends an
U
-chain
F^{\prime}
in this way,
for some
U\in{\cal U}_{T}
, and therefore

{\cal K}^{\delta}_{T}=\textsc{conv-hull}\Big(\bigcup_{U\in{\cal U}_{T}}L_{U}\Big)=\textsc{conv-hull}\Big(\bigcup_{F}L_{F}\Big)
,
where the union ranges over all
T
-chains
F
.

Basic geometric properties.

For every choice of
U\in{\cal U}_{T}
and
\mathbf{r}\in R_{U}
, the set
{\cal K}^{\delta}_{T}
includes not only
the two shifted copies

\mathbf{r}+{\cal K}^{\delta}_{U}
and
-\mathbf{r}+{\cal K}^{\delta}_{U}
but, being the convex hull, includes also all the ‘shifted copies’
in-between these two ones, namely all

\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+{\cal K}^{\delta}_{U}
where
\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\in[-\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}]
:

Lemma 13
.

For every
U\in{\cal U}_{T}
and
\mathbf{r}\in R_{U}
, we have

[-\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}]+{\cal K}^{\delta}_{U}\subseteq{\cal K}^{\delta}_{T}
.

For every
U\in{\cal U}_{T}
and
\mathbf{r}\in R_{U}
, the projection of
\mathbf{r}+{\cal K}^{\delta}_{U}
on

\textsc{span}(U)
is exactly
{\cal K}^{\delta}_{U}
, since
\mathbf{r}
is orthogonal to
\textsc{span}(U)
.
Our next claim is a slight generalization of this observation,
relying on the fact that Minkowski sum commutes with projection:

Lemma 14
.

For every
U,U^{\prime}\in{\cal U}_{T}
with
U\subseteq U^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{r}\in R_{U}
,

\pi_{\textsc{span}(U^{\prime})}(\mathbf{r}+{\cal K}^{\delta}_{U})\ =\ \pi_{\textsc{span}(U^{\prime})}(\mathbf{r})+{\cal K}^{\delta}_{U}.

Lemma

15
reinterprets the value of
\bar{\delta}
in terms of distances
from a point to
\textsc{span}(U)
where
U\subset T
, and to
\textsc{span}(\mathbf{t})
, where

\mathbf{t}\in T\setminus\textsc{span}(U)
.
It follows by Def.
1
and Fig.
3
(left).
Its corollary,
Lemma

16
, obtained by
taking orthogonal complements of
\textsc{span}(U)
and
\textsc{span}(\mathbf{t})
,
is crucial for correctness of our construction.

Lemma 15
.

Let
U\subset T
,
\mathbf{t}\in T\setminus\textsc{span}(U)
, and
\mathbf{x}\in\textsc{span}(T)
.
If one of
{\rm dist}({\textsc{span}(U),\mathbf{x}})
,

{\rm dist}({\textsc{span}(\mathbf{t}),\mathbf{x}})
is smaller than
\bar{\delta}\lVert\mathbf{x}\rVert
then
the other one is larger than
\bar{\delta}\lVert\mathbf{x}\rVert
.

Lemma 16
.

Let
U\subseteq T
,

\mathbf{t}\in T\setminus\textsc{span}(U)
, and

\mathbf{x}\in\textsc{span}(T)
,
assuming
\text{dim}(\textsc{span}(U))+1=\text{dim}(\textsc{span}(T))
.
If one of
{\rm dist}({{\cal H}^{\bot}_{U}\cap\textsc{span}(T),\mathbf{x}})
,

{\rm dist}({{\cal H}^{\bot}_{\mathbf{t}}\cap\textsc{span}(T),\mathbf{x}})
is smaller than
\bar{\delta}\lVert\mathbf{x}\rVert
then
the other one is larger than
\bar{\delta}\lVert\mathbf{x}\rVert
.

Proof.

Since
\text{dim}(\textsc{span}(U))+1=\text{dim}(\textsc{span}(T))
, the subspace
{\cal H}^{\bot}_{U}\cap\textsc{span}(T)
is
a one-dimensional line.
According to
Lemma

15
,
for every
\mathbf{x}\in\textsc{span}(T)
,
if one of
{\rm dist}({\textsc{span}(U),\mathbf{x}})
,
{\rm dist}({\textsc{span}(\mathbf{t}),\mathbf{x}})
is
smaller than
\bar{\delta}\cdot\lVert\mathbf{x}\rVert
then
the other one is larger than
\bar{\delta}\cdot\lVert\mathbf{x}\rVert
.
We deduce
Lemma

16
using two orthogonality relations:
the hyperplane
\textsc{span}(U)
is orthogonal to the line
{\cal H}^{\bot}_{U}\cap\textsc{span}(T)
,
and the hyperplane
{\cal H}^{\bot}_{\mathbf{t}}\cap\textsc{span}(T)
is orthogonal to the line

\textsc{span}(\mathbf{t})
.
Therefore the angle between the line
\textsc{span}(\mathbf{t})
and
the hyperplane
\textsc{span}(U)
(the first pair) is exactly the same as the angle
between the line
{\cal H}^{\bot}_{U}\cap\textsc{span}(T)
and the
hyperplane
{\cal H}^{\bot}_{\mathbf{t}}\cap\textsc{span}(T)
(the second pair).
Therefore there is an isometry of
\textsc{span}(T)

that maps the first pair to the second one.
In consequence, the property of distances of
\mathbf{x}\in\textsc{span}(T)

to the first pair,
given by Lemma
15
, carries over to the second pair:
for every
\mathbf{x}\in\textsc{span}(T)
,
if one of
{\rm dist}({{\cal H}^{\bot}_{U}\cap\textsc{span}(T),\mathbf{x}})
,

{\rm dist}({{\cal H}^{\bot}_{\mathbf{t}}\cap\textsc{span}(T),\mathbf{x}})
is smaller than
\bar{\delta}\lVert\mathbf{x}\rVert
then
the other one is larger than
\bar{\delta}\lVert\mathbf{x}\rVert
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Lemmas

15
and
16
are stated for strict inequalities,
but reading them in contrapositive yields the versions with
non-strict inequalities holding true as well.

2.2
The sets
{\cal K}^{\delta}_{T}
are
T
-projective

We now show the correctness of our construction, that is, we prove
that
{\cal K}^{\delta}_{T}
is
T
-projective, by induction on
d=\text{dim}(\textsc{span}(T))
.

Induction base:
d=1
.

The line segment
{\cal K}^{\delta}_{\mathbf{t}}=[-\mathbf{t},\mathbf{t}]
is readily seen to be

\{-\mathbf{t},\mathbf{t}\}
-projective.

Warm-up:
d=2
.

We argue that
{\cal K}^{\delta}_{T}
is
T
-projective.
Consider an arbitrary
\mathbf{t}\in T
.
By Lemma
13
we get
[-\mathbf{r}_{\mathbf{t}},\mathbf{r}_{\mathbf{t}}]+[-\mathbf{t},\mathbf{t}]\subseteq{\cal K}^{\delta}_{T}
,
and therefore it is enough to prove

\pi_{\mathbf{t}}({\cal K}^{\delta}_{T})\ \subseteq\ [-\mathbf{r}_{\mathbf{t}},\mathbf{r}_{\mathbf{t}}].

As
{\cal K}^{\delta}_{T}
is a convex hull of line segments
K^{\prime}_{\mathbf{u}}
and
K^{\prime\prime}_{\mathbf{u}}
, for
\mathbf{u}\in T
, it is enough
to prove that the projection
\pi_{\mathbf{t}}
maps all the line segments into

[-\mathbf{r}_{\mathbf{t}},\mathbf{r}_{\mathbf{t}}]
.
This means that for every
\mathbf{u}\in T
and
\mathbf{x}\in{\cal K}^{\delta}_{\mathbf{u}}=[-\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u}]
we have to prove

Claim 17
.

\pi_{\mathbf{t}}(\mathbf{r}_{\mathbf{u}}+\mathbf{x})\in[-\mathbf{r}_{\mathbf{t}},\mathbf{r}_{\mathbf{t}}]
and
\pi_{\mathbf{t}}(-\mathbf{r}_{\mathbf{u}}+\mathbf{x})\in[-\mathbf{r}_{\mathbf{t}},\mathbf{r}_{\mathbf{t}}]
.

{claimproof}

We focus on the former claim, relying on the apparent symmetry.
Knowing that

{\rm dist}({\textsc{span}(\mathbf{r}_{\mathbf{u}}),\mathbf{r}_{\mathbf{u}}+\mathbf{x}})=\lVert\mathbf{x}\rVert=1=\bar{\delta}\lVert\mathbf{r}_{\mathbf{u}}+\mathbf{x}\rVert
,
we apply the non-strict version of Lemma
16

(applied to
d=2
,
U=\{\mathbf{u}\}
,
{\cal H}^{\bot}_{U}\cap\textsc{span}(T)=\textsc{span}(\mathbf{r}_{\mathbf{u}})
,

{\cal H}^{\bot}_{\mathbf{t}}\cap\textsc{span}(T)=\textsc{span}(\mathbf{r}_{\mathbf{t}})
)
to get
{\rm dist}({\textsc{span}(\mathbf{r}_{\mathbf{t}}),\mathbf{r}_{\mathbf{u}}+\mathbf{x}})\geq 1
,
which implies, by Fig.
3
(right) in case
d=2
, that

\pi_{\mathbf{t}}(\mathbf{r}_{\mathbf{u}}+\mathbf{x})\in[-\mathbf{r}_{\mathbf{t}},\mathbf{r}_{\mathbf{t}}]
.

Inductive step:
d\geq 2
.

Relying on the inductive assumption that the sets
{\cal K}^{\delta}_{U}
are
U
-projective for all
U\in{\cal U}_{T}
,
we argue that
{\cal K}^{\delta}_{T}
is
T
-projective.
We pick up an arbitrary
\mathbf{t}\in T
, and aim at proving
the inclusion
\pi_{\mathbf{t}}({\cal K}^{\delta}_{T})+[-\mathbf{t},\mathbf{t}]\subseteq{\cal K}^{\delta}_{T}
.
By convexity of
{\cal K}^{\delta}_{T}
it suffices to prove, for every
U\in{\cal U}_{T}
and
every vector
\mathbf{r}\in R_{U}
, the inclusion:

\displaystyle\pi_{\mathbf{t}}(\mathbf{r}+{\cal K}^{\delta}_{U})+[-\mathbf{t},\mathbf{t}]\ \subseteq\ {\cal K}^{\delta}_{T}.

(6)

We distinguish two cases, depending on whether
\mathbf{t}
belongs to
U
or not.

Case 1:
\mathbf{t}\in U
.

Since
\mathbf{t}
is orthogonal to
\mathbf{r}
, we have

\pi_{\mathbf{t}}(\mathbf{r}+{\cal K}^{\delta}_{U})+[-\mathbf{t},\mathbf{t}]\ =\ \mathbf{r}+\pi_{\mathbf{t}}({\cal K}^{\delta}_{U})+[-\mathbf{t},\mathbf{t}],

and since the set
{\cal K}^{\delta}_{U}
is
U
-projective by inductive assumption, the right-hand side is included in

\mathbf{r}+{\cal K}^{\delta}_{U}\ \subseteq\ {\cal K}^{\delta}_{T},

which implies (
6
).

Case 2:
\mathbf{t}\notin U
.

Let
B_{U}:={\cal B}^{m}(R_{e})\cap\textsc{span}(U)
be the ball of radius
R_{e}
inside
\textsc{span}(U)
,
where
e=\text{dim}(\textsc{span}(U))
.
Due to Lemma
11
we have
{\cal K}^{\delta}_{U}\subseteq B_{U}
, and therefore

\mathbf{r}+{\cal K}^{\delta}_{U}\subseteq\mathbf{r}+B_{U}
.
We distinguish two subcases, depending on whether the distance

{\rm dist}({{\cal H}^{\bot}_{\mathbf{t}},\mathbf{r}+B_{U}})
is smaller or larger than
R_{e}
.
Note that

{\rm dist}({{\cal H}^{\bot}_{\mathbf{t}},\mathbf{x}})\ =\ {\rm dist}({{\cal H}^{\bot}_{\mathbf{t}}\cap\textsc{span}(T),\mathbf{x}})
for every
\mathbf{x}\in\textsc{span}(T)
.

Case 2a:
{\rm dist}({{\cal H}^{\bot}_{\mathbf{t}},\mathbf{r}+B_{U}})\geq R_{e}
.

We prove the following inclusion:

\displaystyle\pi_{\mathbf{t}}(\mathbf{r}+{\cal K}^{\delta}_{U})\ \subseteq\ Y\ :=\ {\cal H}^{\bot}_{\mathbf{t}}\cap\textsc{span}(T)\cap{\cal B}^{m}(r_{d}).

(7)

Pick up an arbitrary point
\mathbf{u}\in{\cal K}^{\delta}_{U}\subseteq B_{U}
.
Let
\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\ :=\ \pi_{\mathbf{t}}(\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{u})
.
If
\mathbf{r}^{\prime}=\mathbf{0}
, we immediately deduce
\pi_{\mathbf{t}}(\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{u})\in Y

since
\mathbf{0}\in Y
.
Otherwise,
consider two lines:
\textsc{span}(\mathbf{r})
and
\textsc{span}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})
.
Relying on
\mathbf{u}\in B_{U}
,
R_{e}\leq{\rm dist}({{\cal H}^{\bot}_{\mathbf{t}},\mathbf{r}+B_{U}})

and
\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\in{\cal H}^{\bot}_{\mathbf{t}}
, respectively,
we get the chain of three inequalities:

{\rm dist}({\textsc{span}(\mathbf{r}),\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{u}})\ =\ \lVert\mathbf{u}\rVert\ \leq\ R_{e}\ \leq\ {\rm dist}({{\cal H}^{\bot}_{\mathbf{t}},\mathbf{r}+B_{U}})\ \leq\ {\rm dist}({\textsc{span}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}),\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{u}}).

Therefore the projection of

\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{u}
on
\textsc{span}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})
has norm smaller or equal to the norm
of the projection of
\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{u}
on
\textsc{span}(\mathbf{r})
.
The former projection is
\mathbf{r}^{\prime}
while the latter one is
\mathbf{r}
,
of norm
\lVert\mathbf{r}\rVert=r_{d}
,
which implies
\lVert\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\rVert\leq r_{d}
and hence

\pi_{\mathbf{t}}(\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{u})\in Y
.
This proves (
7
).

Using (
7
), we deduce (
6
):

\pi_{\mathbf{t}}(\mathbf{r}+{\cal K}^{\delta}_{U})+[-\mathbf{t},\mathbf{t}]\ \subseteq\ \big({\cal H}^{\bot}_{\mathbf{t}}\cap\textsc{span}(T)\cap{\cal B}^{m}(r_{d})\big)+[-\mathbf{t},\mathbf{t}]\ =\ \textsc{conv-hull}(L_{\mathbf{t}})\ \subseteq\ {\cal K}^{\delta}_{T}.

Case 2b:
{\rm dist}({{\cal H}^{\bot}_{\mathbf{t}},\mathbf{r}+B_{U}})<R_{e}
.

Let
U^{\prime}
be the inclusion-maximal subset of
T
with
\textsc{span}(U^{\prime})=\textsc{span}(U\cup\{\mathbf{t}\})
.
We start by observing that
e\leq d-2
.
Towards contradiction, suppose
e=d-1
, and take any
\mathbf{u}\in B_{U}
such that

{\rm dist}({{\cal H}^{\bot}_{\mathbf{t}},\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{u}})={\rm dist}({{\cal H}^{\bot}_{\mathbf{t}}\cap\textsc{span}(T),\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{u}})<R_{d-1}=\bar{\delta}\lVert\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{u}\rVert
.
By Lemma
16

(since
\text{dim}(\textsc{span}(U))=e=d-1
, we have
{\cal H}^{\bot}_{U}\cap\textsc{span}(T)=\textsc{span}(\mathbf{r})
)
we deduce that

{\rm dist}({\textsc{span}(\mathbf{r}),\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{u}})=\lVert\mathbf{u}\rVert>R_{d-1}
, a contradiction.
Thus

\text{dim}(\textsc{span}(U^{\prime}))=e+1\leq d-1
.
In consequence,
U^{\prime}\in{\cal U}_{T}
.

Claim 18
.

\mathbf{r}+{\cal K}^{\delta}_{U}\subseteq\textsc{conv-hull}(L_{U^{\prime}})
.

We apply Case 1 to
U^{\prime}
(as
\mathbf{t}\in U^{\prime}\in{\cal U}_{T}
), and obtain

\pi_{\mathbf{t}}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+{\cal K}^{\delta}_{U^{\prime}})+[-\mathbf{t},\mathbf{t}]\ \subseteq\ {\cal K}^{\delta}_{T},

for every
\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\in R_{U^{\prime}}
, which implies

\pi_{\mathbf{t}}(\textsc{conv-hull}(L_{U^{\prime}}))+[-\mathbf{t},\mathbf{t}]\ \subseteq\ {\cal K}^{\delta}_{T}.

By
18
we get

\pi_{\mathbf{t}}(\mathbf{r}+{\cal K}^{\delta}_{U})+[-\mathbf{t},\mathbf{t}]\ \subseteq\ \pi_{\mathbf{t}}(\textsc{conv-hull}(L_{U^{\prime}}))+[-\mathbf{t},\mathbf{t}].

Composing the two last inclusions yields (
6
), namely:

\pi_{\mathbf{t}}(\mathbf{r}+{\cal K}^{\delta}_{U})+[-\mathbf{t},\mathbf{t}]\ \subseteq\ \pi_{\mathbf{t}}(\textsc{conv-hull}(L_{U^{\prime}}))+[-\mathbf{t},\mathbf{t}]\ \subseteq\ {\cal K}^{\delta}_{T}
.

Proof of
18
.

Consider the projection of
\textsc{span}(T)
onto the subspace
\textsc{span}(U^{\prime})
, and
let
\mathbf{r}^{\prime}=\pi_{\textsc{span}(U^{\prime})}(\mathbf{r})
.
By Lemma
14
we get

\displaystyle\pi_{\textsc{span}(U^{\prime})}(\mathbf{r}+{\cal K}^{\delta}_{U})\ =\ \mathbf{r}^{\prime}+{\cal K}^{\delta}_{U}.

(8)

Furthemore, as
\mathbf{r}
is orthogonal to
\textsc{span}(U)
, its projection
\mathbf{r}^{\prime}
is also so.

By assumption,
{\rm dist}({{\cal H}^{\bot}_{\mathbf{t}},\mathbf{r}+B_{U}})<R_{e}
, which means that
there is a point
\mathbf{u}\in B_{U}
with
{\rm dist}({{\cal H}^{\bot}_{\mathbf{t}},\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{u}})<R_{e}
.
W.l.o.g. we may assume that
\mathbf{u}
belongs to the sphere of
B_{U}
.
Indeed, if
\mathbf{u}
is in the interior of
B_{U}
,
perturb it slightly, if necessary, to get
\mathbf{u}\neq\mathbf{0}
and not colinear with
\mathbf{t}
.
Then scale it positively
and negatively to get two colinear radiuses of
B_{U}
forming a line segment of length

2R_{e}
. As the distance of the line segment from
{\cal H}^{\bot}_{\mathbf{t}}
is smaller than
R_{e}
,
and the line segment is not orthogonal to
{\cal H}^{\bot}_{\mathbf{t}}
,
one of its ends is necessarily at distance smaller than
R_{e}
from
{\cal H}^{\bot}_{\mathbf{t}}
.
Thus we may replace
\mathbf{u}
by that endpoint.
In particular,
\lVert\mathbf{u}\rVert=R_{e}
.

Consider the distance of
\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\mathbf{u}\in\textsc{span}(U^{\prime})
to

\textsc{span}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})={\cal H}^{\bot}_{U}\cap\textsc{span}(U^{\prime})
and to
{\cal H}^{\bot}_{\mathbf{t}}\cap\textsc{span}(U^{\prime})
.
We claim:

\displaystyle{\rm dist}({{\cal H}^{\bot}_{U}\cap\textsc{span}(U^{\prime}),\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\mathbf{u}})\ =\ R_{e}\qquad\qquad{\rm dist}({{\cal H}^{\bot}_{\mathbf{t}}\cap\textsc{span}(U^{\prime}),\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\mathbf{u}})\ <\ R_{e}.

(9)

The left equality follows since
\mathbf{r}^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{u}
are orthogonal, and therefore

{\rm dist}({\textsc{span}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}),\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\mathbf{u}})\ =\ \lVert\mathbf{u}\rVert
.
To prove the right inequality, we notice that
\mathbf{t}\in U^{\prime}
and therefore the projection on
\textsc{span}(U^{\prime})

does not change the distance to
{\cal H}^{\bot}_{\mathbf{t}}
:

{\rm dist}({{\cal H}^{\bot}_{\mathbf{t}}\cap\textsc{span}(U^{\prime}),\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\mathbf{u}})\ =\ {\rm dist}({{\cal H}^{\bot}_{\mathbf{t}},\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{u}})

while,
by assumption, we have
{\rm dist}({{\cal H}^{\bot}_{\mathbf{t}},\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{u}})<R_{e}
.
Having (
9
), we apply Lemma
16
to get

R_{e}\geq\bar{\delta}\lVert\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\mathbf{u}\rVert
, which rewrites to

\lVert\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\mathbf{u}\rVert\leq R_{e}/\bar{\delta}=R_{e+1}
.
As
\lVert\mathbf{u}\rVert=R_{e}
, relying on
Pythagoras (cf. Fig.
3
, right) we obtain

\lVert\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\rVert\leq r_{e+1}
, and therefore using Lemma
13
we get

\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+{\cal K}^{\delta}_{U}\ \subseteq\ {\cal K}^{\delta}_{U^{\prime}}.

Decompose
\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime}
, where
\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime}
is orthogonal to
\textsc{span}(U^{\prime})
, and rewrite
add
\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime}
to both sides of
the above inclusion:

\mathbf{r}+{\cal K}^{\delta}_{U}\ =\ \mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime}+\big(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+{\cal K}^{\delta}_{U}\big)\ \subseteq\ \mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime}+{\cal K}^{\delta}_{U^{\prime}}.

Since
\lVert\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime}\rVert\leq\lVert\mathbf{r}\rVert=r_{d}
, and
\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime}

is orthogonal to
{\cal K}^{\delta}_{U^{\prime}}
,
we have

\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime}+{\cal K}^{\delta}_{U^{\prime}}\subseteq\textsc{conv-hull}(L_{U^{\prime}})
,
and in consequence

\mathbf{r}+{\cal K}^{\delta}_{U}\ \subseteq\ \textsc{conv-hull}(L_{U^{\prime}})
, as required.
∎

3
Generalization to Vector Partitioning

In this section, we consider the online vector balancing game from the introduction. We prove the same bounds on Chooser’s greedy algorithm for this game as in the previous variant. Similarly to the 2-partition balancing game, Chooser should minimize the value

\mathrm{val}(J_{1},\ldots,J_{p})
with the assignment of the vector
\mathbf{t}_{n}
, given the assignment of
\mathbf{t}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{t}_{n-1}
.
To this end, define
\mathbf{a}_{i}^{(n)}:=\sum_{j\in J_{i}\cap[n]}\mathbf{t}_{i}

as the partial sum of the vectors indexed by
j\in J_{i},j\leq n
.
We further define the matrix
A^{(n)}=\left(\mathbf{a}_{1}^{(n)}\ldots\ \mathbf{a}_{p}^{(n)}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times p}
with the
i
-th column
\mathbf{a}_{i}^{(j)}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,p\}
. Note that
\mathrm{val}(J_{1},\ldots,J_{p})=\displaystyle\max_{j\in[n]}\displaystyle\max_{i_{1}\in[p]}\displaystyle\max_{i_{2}\in[p]}\|\mathbf{a}_{i_{1}}^{(n)}-\mathbf{a}_{i_{2}}^{(n)}\|
.

Definition 19
.

Let

In this section, we show that Conjecture

Throughout the discussion, we will consider partial assignments of jobs to machines, or equivalently, partitions of
[j]
for some
j\in[n]

(J_{1}\cap[j],\ldots,J_{m}\cap[j])

Definition 22

\mathbf{a}_{i}^{(j)}(J_{1},\ldots,J_{m})=(a^{ij}_{k})_{\{k\in[d]\}}
with

For the defined vectors, we observe that optimizing the objective function (
2
) while assigning a single job corresponds to the greedy algorithm of the vector balancing game on

partitions.
Here, the vector that arrives is the incidence vector of the job
j

\mathbf{a}_{i}^{(j)}

Lemma 23
.

Let
(J_{1}\cap[j-1],\ldots,J_{m}\cap[j-1])
be a partial schedule. Any extension
(J_{1}\cap[j],\ldots,J_{m}\cap[j])
of this schedule that minimizes
\mathrm{obj}(J_{1}\cap[j],\ldots,J_{m}\cap[j])

j\in J_{i^{\prime}}
for some

i^{\prime}\in\mathrm{argmin}_{i\in[m]}\langle\mathbf{a}_{i}^{j-1},\mathbf{t}^{j}\rangle\text{ with }\ \mathbf{t}^{j}_{k}=\begin{cases}1,&j\in S_{k}\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
i^{\prime}
be the machine that the job
j
is assigned to. The objective values
\mathrm{obj}(J_{1}\cap[j],\ldots,J_{m}\cap[j])
and
\mathrm{obj}(J_{1}\cap[j-1],\ldots,J_{m}\cap[j-1])

\sum_{k:j\in S_{k}}w_{j}\cdot|J_{i^{\prime}}\cap S_{k}\cap[j]]|=w_{j}\cdot\langle\mathbf{a}_{i^{\prime}}^{(j)},\mathbf{t}^{j}\rangle=w_{j}\cdot\langle\mathbf{a}_{i^{\prime}}^{(j-1)}+\mathbf{t}^{j},\mathbf{t}^{j}\rangle=w_{j}(\langle\mathbf{a}_{i^{\prime}}^{(j-1)},\mathbf{t}^{j}\rangle+\|\mathbf{t}^{j}\|^{2}).

Here, the terms
w_{j}
and
\|\mathbf{t}^{j}\|^{2}
do not depend on the choice of the assignment, so the objective function is minimized if and only if
\langle\mathbf{a}_{i^{\prime}}^{(j-1)},\mathbf{t}^{j}\rangle
is minimized.
∎

Next, we argue that if
w_{j}\geq 2dw_{j+1}
for all

\mathrm{obj}(J_{1},\ldots,J_{m})
, the

-th job is placed so that

is as small as possible given the fixed partitioning of the first
j-1

Lemma 24
.

Let
n
jobs be given such that
w_{j}\geq 2dw_{j+1}
for all
j\in[n-1]
and let

be an optimal partition of the jobs with respect to
\mathrm{obj}
. Then for every

, we have

j\in J_{i}
with
i\in\mathrm{argmin}_{i\in[m]}\{\mathrm{obj}(J_{1}\cap[j],\ldots J_{m}\cap[j]\},

given
(J_{1}\cap[j-1],\ldots J_{m}\cap[j-1])
.

Proof.

Assume that this is not the case. Fix a partition
(J_{1},\ldots,J_{m})
that minimizes
\mathrm{obj}
and let
j
be the smallest index such that
(J_{1}\cap[j],\ldots,J_{m}\cap[j])
is not optimal with respect to
\mathrm{obj}
. Then, by the minimality of
j
,
(J_{1}\cap[j-1],\ldots,J_{m}\cap[j-1])
is optimal and there is a machine
i^{\prime}\in[m]
such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{obj}\left(J_{1}\cap[j-1],\ldots,(J_{i^{\prime}}\cap[j-1])\cup\{j\},\ldots,J_{m}\cap[j-1]\right)<\mathrm{obj}(J_{1}\cap[j],\ldots,J_{m}\cap[j]).

(10)

We fix the assignment corresponding to the left hand side, that is, for the index
i^{\prime}
as above, we define
(J_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,J_{m}^{\prime})=(J_{1}\setminus\{j\},\ldots,J_{i^{\prime}}\cup\{j\},\ldots,J_{m}\setminus\{j\})

To this end, we consider the contribution of each job to the objective value. The first
j-1

j\in J^{\prime}_{i^{\prime}}
and assume further

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in[d]:j\in S_{k}}\hskip-7.11317ptw_{j}\cdot|J^{\prime}_{i}\cap S_{k}\cap[j]|

\displaystyle=w_{j}\cdot\sum_{k\in[d]:j\in S_{k}}\hskip-7.11317pt|J^{\prime}_{i}\cap S_{k}\cap[j]|

(11)

\displaystyle\leq w_{j}\cdot\left(\sum_{k\in[d]:j\in S_{k}}\hskip-7.11317pt|J_{i}\cap S_{k}\cap[j]|-1\right)

Here, the inequality follows by (

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,1,1}\leq}\sum_{k\in[d]:j^{\prime}\in S_{k}}w_{j^{\prime}}|J^{\prime}_{i}\cap S_{k}\cap[j^{\prime}]|=w_{j^{\prime}}\sum_{k\in[d]:j^{\prime}\in S_{k}}|J^{\prime}_{i}\cap S_{k}\cap[j^{\prime}]|

In this section, we show a lower bound on

for

The lower bound depends on the dimension

We fix

and
\delta\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and construct set

with
\delta_{T}=\delta

Let
\mathbf{e}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}

For

\mathbf{u}_{i}=\mathbf{e}_{2i}\quad\mathbf{v}_{i}=-\sqrt{1-\delta^{2}}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{2i}+\delta\cdot\mathbf{e}_{2i-1}

The intuition behind this construction is as follows. By using
d

\mathbf{u}_{i}
and
\mathbf{v}_{i}

G(T)\geq\sqrt{d}/\delta
Each such pair of vectors allows us to increment the square of the norm of point reachable by a
[-1,1]T
-greedy sequence by
\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}
, which accumulates over all
d

as follows:

T=\bigcup_{i\in[d]}\{\mathbf{u}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i}\}

The above idea follows from observing the behaviour of a two-dimensional greedy sequence

of vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that

\mathbf{x}_{i+1}-\mathbf{x}_{i}\in\left\{\sqrt{1-\delta^{2}}\,\mathbf{u}_{1},\;\mathbf{v}_{1},\;-\sqrt{1-\delta^{2}}\,\mathbf{u}_{1},\;-\mathbf{v}_{1}\right\},

and extending this construction to

The set

and

Proof.

Observe, that for every

\min\{{\rm dist}({\textsc{span}(U),\mathbf{t}}):U\subset T,\mathbf{t}\in T\setminus\textsc{span}(U)\}=\delta.

Notice that the minimum on the left-hand side is always attained when
\text{dim}(\textsc{span}(U))=\text{dim}(\textsc{span}(T))-1

6
Concluding Remarks

In this paper, we have shown that iterates of the Euclidean greedy vector
balancing algorithm remain universally bounded against sequences taken from any
finite set
T\subset{\cal S}^{d-1}
. Our upper bound
(2/\delta_{T})^{d-1}
and
lower bound
\sqrt{d/2}/\delta_{T}
are unfortunately very far apart. A natural
question is thus to close this gap. We have also provided an application of our
greedy bound to scenario scheduling and SGD sample reordering. We however expect
there be many more interesting settings where greedy vector balancing bounds
can be applied, and we hope this will lead to a fertile area of future
research.
