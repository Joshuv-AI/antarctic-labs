ARTICLE

An initial value approach to the excitation of atmospheric Lamb and
Pekeris waves
Keiichi Ishioka1
Received: 25 March 2026 / Accepted: 24 August 2026
© The Author(s) 2026

Abstract
This paper aims to analytically elucidate the excitation mechanisms of atmospheric Lamb and Pekeris waves by formulating them as an initial value problem based on linear models. Previous studies on the excitation of atmospheric waves
by eruptions or explosions have primarily relied on numerical simulations; no study has analytically derived the excitation amplitudes of these waves from a given initial disturbance. In this study, we first formulate the excitation process
of Lamb waves analytically in both hydrostatic and non-hydrostatic models with an isothermal background atmosphere,
utilizing Fourier transforms and residue integration in the complex frequency plane. Furthermore, we extend this method
to present a novel residue-analytical method for determining the amplitudes of Lamb and Pekeris waves excited from an
initial disturbance in an atmosphere with a realistic vertical temperature structure. The validity of the analytically derived
amplitudes is verified by numerically calculating the time evolution of the initial value problem. In addition, we discuss the
attenuation characteristics of these waves when the temperature structure up to the thermosphere is taken into account. The
analytical approach presented in this study not only deepens the theoretical understanding of the excitation processes of
atmospheric waves from initial disturbances, but also contributes to the validation of complex nonlinear numerical models.
Keywords Lamb wave · Pekeris wave · Initial value problem · Hunga Tonga-Hunga Ha’apai eruption

1 Introduction
As the largest volcanic event since the establishment of
modern atmospheric observation networks, the 2022 Hunga
Tonga-Hunga Ha’apai eruption provided unprecedented
observational data. While Lamb waves (Lamb 1911) had
previously been detected in the Krakatoa eruption data by
Taylor (1929), the 2022 event led to the first observational
detection of Pekeris waves (Pekeris 1937) by Watanabe et al.
(2022), proving their existence, which had long been theoretically predicted. Regarding the excitation of these waves,
Watanabe et al. (2022) also conducted numerical simulations
using a General Circulation Model (GCM), yielding results
reasonably consistent with observations. Furthermore,
numerical simulations utilizing non-hydrostatic models

have also been performed (Suzuki et al. 2023). In relation
to the phase velocities and vertical structures of the excited
Lamb and Pekeris waves, Ishioka (2023) and Ishizaki et al.
(2023) have attempted to calculate the equivalent depths of
these waves with high precision from the vertical temperature structure of the atmosphere. In addition, Inchin et al.
(2023) conducted a comprehensive observational and raytracing analysis across multiple atmospheric layers, successfully accounting for non-concentric propagation and
spatial variations in signal amplitude influenced by background winds. While their work offers valuable insights into
acoustic-gravity waves, their analysis regarding Pekeris
modes was mainly linked to traveling ionospheric disturbances (TIDs) rather than lower-atmospheric observations
in the troposphere and stratosphere, partly due to the satellite channels used having larger weights in the lower troposphere. Consequently, the behavior and excitation of the
Pekeris mode itself were not fully addressed, highlighting
the need for further theoretical and analytical investigation.
As mentioned above, nonlinear numerical simulations for
the initial value problem concerning the excitation of such
Lamb and Pekeris waves have been conducted by Watanabe

et al. (2022) and others. Historically, however, numerical
computations of wave propagation using linear atmospheric
models with a stationary background atmosphere have
been carried out since Press and Harkrider (1962), primarily in connection with the monitoring of microbarometric
variations caused not only by volcanic eruptions but also by
nuclear explosions.
Although the excitation of Lamb waves and other atmospheric waves associated with eruptions and nuclear explosions has been addressed as an initial value problem in
numerous studies, to the best of the author’s knowledge, no
study has analytically determined the exact amplitudes of
Lamb and Pekeris waves excited from a given initial disturbance under a simplified setting. Moreover, while linear
numerical calculations, such as those by Press and Harkrider
(1962) and Mizutani and Yomogida (2023), utilize the governing equations of a non-hydrostatic system, there seems to
be no literature addressing the initial value problem of the
linearized excitation process in a hydrostatic system, which
was employed by Watanabe et al. (2022) and others.
Therefore, in this paper, we first analytically treat the
excitation process of Lamb waves assuming an isothermal
background atmosphere as an initial value problem in both
linearized hydrostatic and non-hydrostatic models, thereby
clarifying how the amplitudes of Lamb waves excited from
an initial disturbance are determined. Furthermore, by applying this treatment of the Lamb wave excitation problem in
an isothermal background within the hydrostatic model,
we present a novel method to determine the amplitudes of
Lamb and Pekeris waves excited from a given initial condition under a realistic vertical temperature distribution.
We then verify the validity of these analytically obtained
amplitude values by numerically computing the time evolution of the initial value problem. Additionally, as pointed
out by Ishioka (2023), when a realistic vertical temperature
distribution is considered, Lamb and Pekeris waves can no
longer exist as perfectly non-attenuating mode solutions; we
also provide a discussion on this aspect. To contextualize
our theoretical framework, our analysis primarily focuses
on horizontal wavelengths of ∼ 300 km (corresponding to
a Lamb wave period of ∼ 1000 s) motivated by the 2022
Tonga event (Watanabe et al. 2022), within a temporal scope
of a few hours where non-rotating linear dynamics remain
valid. Detailed quantitative estimates regarding vertical
propagation, attenuation scales, and applicability limits are
provided in the subsequent discussion.
The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 addresses the Lamb wave excitation problem in an
isothermal atmosphere within a hydrostatic model. Section 3 applies a finite difference model to compute the time
evolution in the hydrostatic case and compares it with the
analytical solution evaluated using the saddle point method.

7 Summary and discussion
In this study, although under a highly simplified setting, we
have provided a detailed analytical treatment of the initial
value problem concerning the excitation of Lamb waves in
an isothermal atmosphere within a hydrostatic framework,
with its non-hydrostatic extension presented in Appendix
C. To systematically highlight the unity of our theoretical
framework, this methodology is also applied to classic wave
excitation problems (geostrophic adjustment and coastal
Kelvin waves) as pedagogical reviews in Appendices A
and B. As a result, we demonstrated that the amplitude of
the Lamb wave excited from a given initial condition can
be determined by evaluating residues. Furthermore, at
least in the hydrostatic model, we showed that the gravity
wave components propagating alongside the excited Lamb
wave can be calculated quantitatively and with high precision using the saddle point approximation. By leveraging
these findings, we believe we have established a numerical
method to compute the extent to which Lamb and Pekeris
waves are excited under a realistic vertical atmospheric temperature distribution.
Although this paper considered standard Cartesian coordinates in the horizontal direction, by replacing 𝑘𝑘2 in the formulation with 𝑛𝑛(𝑛𝑛 + 1𝑘/𝑛𝑛2 (where a is the Earth’s radius and
n is the total wavenumber of spherical harmonics), the horizontal propagation of Lamb and Pekeris waves on a sphere
could be calculated using a spherical harmonic expansion.
While the effect of Earth’s rotation is not accounted for in
our formulation, this simplification poses no significant

13

Journal of the Meteorological Society of Japan

(2026) 104:33

issue when considering the short-term excitation processes
of Lamb and Pekeris waves as an initial value problem.
Furthermore, this study addressed the case where a realistic Earth atmospheric temperature distribution up to the
high-temperature region of the thermosphere is taken into
account. In such cases, the vertical structures of the Lamb
and Pekeris waves cannot be perfectly evanescent in the
thermosphere and must inevitably involve vertical energy
propagation. Consequently, perfectly modal free-oscillation
solutions cannot be obtained as long as the phase velocities
determined as eigenvalues remain strictly real numbers. To
resolve this, we showed that by extending the phase velocities to the complex plane and solving the eigenvalue problem, solutions for Lamb and Pekeris waves accompanied by
weak attenuation can be obtained.
Such attenuation has been discussed in previous studies, such as Lindzen and Blake (1972) and Salby (1979). In
those studies, the attenuation time constants were estimated
by considering energy leakage from the upper boundary for
approximate solutions that did not strictly satisfy the lower
boundary condition. The method presented in this study
thus enables the accurate determination of these attenuating solutions without resorting to such approximations. The
validity of the attenuating solutions obtained in this manner was also confirmed through comparison with numerical
time evolution calculations. Of course, because the system
treated in this paper does not include physical dissipation
effects such as radiation or turbulent viscosity, the practical significance of precisely treating the attenuation effect
due to upward energy leakage might not be overwhelmingly
large. Nonetheless, establishing the extent to which Lamb
and Pekeris waves can attenuate in a non-dissipative system is fundamentally important, at least from a theoretical
perspective.
Applying our theoretical results to the realistic physical
scales of the 2022 Tonga eruption (horizontal wavelength
∼ 300 km, period ∼ 1000 s) yields several practical insights:
First, regarding vertical structure, Lamb and Pekeris waves
are trapped modal solutions characterized by decay scales
(e.g., 𝑒𝑒−𝜅𝜅𝑧𝑧 with a decay scale of a few scale heights) or
nodal structures spanning several tens of kilometers. In contrast, the associated transient internal gravity waves propagate vertically fast enough to reach detectable amplitudes at
the mesopause (∼ 85 km) within roughly 45 min. Second,
concerning horizontal propagation, the attenuation rates
derived in Sect. 6 yield a horizontal 1/e attenuation distance
of ∼ 1𝑧5 × 106 km for the Lamb wave. This unrealistically
long distance arises because upward energy leakage—the
sole attenuation mechanism accounted for in our non-dissipative formulation—is extremely weak for this mode. In the
real atmosphere, thermal and mechanical dissipation (such
as radiative damping and turbulent dissipation) dominate

Lamb wave attenuation, which falls outside the present

Indeed, Watanabe et al. (2022) reported that the Pekeris
wave signal weakened considerably during its propagation
from Tonga to near Japan, which is highly consistent with
the enhanced attenuation rate of the Pekeris mode derived
here. Third, while our linear formulation naturally scales
with perturbation amplitude, the exponential decay of background density with altitude leads to a substantial growth
in perturbation wind speeds, eventually inducing nonlinear
breakdown in the upper atmosphere. Consequently, extending these quantitative evaluations into the thermosphere and
ionosphere (e.g., (Inchin et al. 2023)) falls outside the scope
of the present linear hydrostatic model.
Finally, the wave excitation problem addressed in this
study was strictly limited to cases with a stationary background state. When considering the actual wave excitation
and propagation from the Hunga Tonga-Hunga Ha’apai eruption, the effects of background winds are likely to be important. Although Inchin et al. (2023) investigated variations in
