Dual-version and dual-scale CSRR loaded sensor for mining-based material characterization

Madan Kumar Sharma, Abdullah Said Alkalbani, Satyanarayana Degala, Gopal Rathinam, Madhur Deo Upadhayay

ABSTRACT
In this work, dual-version and dual-scale sensor is proposed for mining-based material characterization. The sensor structure is configured using a circular-shaped patch loaded with a 3 × 3 CSRR array. The ground plane was fully loaded with a perfect electric conductor. A strong resonance for the (S21) parameter is achieved by four rectangular slots created surrounding the CSRR array. The first version of the sensor had an overall size of 100 mm × 60 mm and resonated at a frequency of 1.5 GHz. Furthermore, the sensor size was optimized, and the second version achieved an overall size of 30 mm × 20 mm. The second version provides a resonating peak at 4.88 GHz for the transmission coefficient. The effectiveness of the sensing area was analyzed using the electric field distribution and current density. Both sensors are fabricated and experimentally validated by comparison of simulated and measured results. In terms of sensing applications, mining-based materials (Copper, Chrome and Silica) were tested using both sensors. The dual-scale resonating capabilities of the sensors can effectively characterize the Material Under Test (MUT). In comparison, the proposed sensors have a compact size, dual scale of measurements, and capability of characterizing mining-based materials.

1. Introduction Accurate characterization of mining-based materials is critical in determining ore quality and ensuring efficient mining processes [ 1 ]. However, conventional characterization of mining-based materials is often labor-intensive, expensive, and not suitable for real-time applications [ 2 ]. To mitigate these challenges, resonator-based microwave sensor technology has been considered as a potential solution owing to its high sensitivity and non-destructive evaluation capabilities [ 3 ]. The microwave sensors provide material characterization by analyzing the interaction of electromagnetic waves with a Material Under Test (MUT) through absorption, reflection, and transmission, which are influenced by permittivity, conductivity, and permeability [ 4 , 5 ]. Their resonating behavior enhances the sensing accuracy, making them valuable across fields such as agriculture, biomedical, food, construction, and mining [ 6 – 8 ]. Among various designs, Split Ring Resonators (SRR) and Complementary SRR (CSRR) are widely used for their compact size, high sensitivity, and efficient electromagnetic control. CSRR- and DGS-based sensors have been applied for liquid and solid characterizations, demonstrating resonance shifts with edible oil, contaminated water, and dielectric solids [ 9 , 10 ]. Designs such as maze-shaped and curved-shaped CSRRs enhanced sensitivity and enabled dual testing, with resonances ranging from 2.45 to 4.05 GHz [ 11 , 12 ]. These studies highlight the versatility of CSRR sensors for diverse material-sensing applications. Recent studies have demonstrated various resonator-based humidity and material sensors with high sensitivity. An MXene-coated meander line sensor achieved single resonance at 4.1 GHz [ 13 ], whereas Zahra and Michal introduced an SRR-loaded transmission line for dual sensing and filtering [ 14 ]. A square SRR-based sensor was integrated into a three-node wireless network to detect ice conditions [ 15 ]. Interdigit capacitive sensors operating at 2.4 GHz were used for liquid and solid testing [ 16 ], and a two-port THZ-based sensor characterized solid materials effectively [ 17 ]. Enhanced performance has also been achieved using CSRR structures, including dumbbell-shaped [ 18 ] and interdigital electrode-integrated [ 19 ] designs. Overall, the SRR and CSRR architectures enable strong field confinement in the sensing area, significantly improving the sensor sensitivity to the materials under test (MUT). In order to provide dependable material characterization and monitoring, recent developments in sensor technology have shown the significance of multi-sensor dependency modeling, flexible sensing platforms, wireless sensor designs, and enhanced calibration techniques [ 20 – 22 ]. Multi-scale sensing approaches are becoming increasingly important, as evidenced by the development of high-precision cross-scale characterization techniques for complicated three-dimensional objects [ 23 ]. Additionally, sensor array accuracy and reliability have been enhanced by directionally regularized calibration techniques and physics-inspired error modeling [ 24 ]. The adaptability of modern sensing systems for high-frequency and condition-monitoring applications is further demonstrated by recent advancements in time-frequency integration architectures and planar magnetically linked current sensors [ 25 , 26 ]. In mining-related settings, image-processing-based techniques have also been effectively used for material identification and classification [ 27 ]. Furthermore, guided-surface lattice resonances and high-Q resonant structures have garnered significant interest because of their acute resonance features and increased sensitivity [ 28 ]. For cutting-edge industrial applications, sustainable material-based sensing and prediction techniques are also developed in [ 29 ]. The creation of the suggested dual-version and dual-scale CSRR-loaded sensor for mining-based material characterization is supported by the collective findings of this research, which demonstrate the increasing demand for small, precise, high-Q, and multi-scale sensing platforms. Despite a comprehensive search of existing literature, no prior work has been reported in the specific domain of mining‑based material characterization employing CSRR sensors. Indeed, all the existing works on CSRR sensors have been carried out on industrial, chemical, and biological materials. Most reported sensors in [ 9 , 10 , 12 , 13 , 15 – 17 , 19 ]operate with a single resonance, whereas only a few achieve dual [ 11 ] or triple-band performance [ 18 ]. Similarly, several designs [ 9 , 10 , 12 , 16 , 18 , 19 ]have been validated with liquid samples, whereas others [ 11 , 12 , 16 , 17 ] have focused on solids. Only a few studies [ 30 , 31 ] have addressed the characterization of mining-based substances. This highlights a clear research gap: the need for a versatile sensor capable of dual-scale, dual-version operation, with specific applicability to mining material characterization. In this work, a dual‑version and dual‑scale CSRR‑loaded sensor is presented. The sensor is specifically engineered to enhance the detection and differentiation of material properties commonly encountered in mining environments. The proposed sensor architecture leverages with CSRR array and slot stubs to achieve improved electromagnetic interaction with heterogeneous mining samples, thereby enabling precise and reliable material characterization. The major contributions of this study are outlined as follows: To evolve a new composite-shaped CSRR-loaded sensor. An optimized version of the sensor and different operating frequency scales are explored. Characterize mining-based materials (Cu, Chrome, Quartz) using dual-version and dual-scale sensors. Proposed sensor design principle The proposed sensor is fabricated on an FR4 substrate with a dielectric constant of 4.3, a loss tangent of 0.025, and a thickness of 1.5 mm. The design layout, and dimensional specifications, is depicted in Fig 1 . The sensor employs a two-port configuration with a full ground plane, and the sensing area is realized using a circular disc loaded with a composite-shaped complementary split-ring resonator (CSRR) array. A square slot surrounding the circular CSRR array enhances the resonance characteristics, yielding a strong response at approximately 1.5 GHz frequency. To facilitate measurements with powdered samples, a circular sample holder made of polyethylene terephthalate (PET) was designed, with a diameter of 56 mm and a height of 10 mm. The PET material had a dielectric constant of 2 and a loss tangent of 0.002. The simulated and measured transmission coefficient (S21) responses, with and without the sample holder, are presented in Fig 2 . The results demonstrate that the inclusion of the sample holder introduces negligible variations in the S21 response, indicating minimal interference with the sensor performance. Conclusion In this work, a dual‑version, dual‑scale CSRR‑loaded microwave sensors were designed, fabricated, and experimentally verified. Both sensor prototypes exhibited distinct and sharply defined resonant frequencies at 1.5 GHz and 4.88 GHz, respectively, enabling multi‑scale characterization within a compact structure. Three mineral samples Copper, Chrome, and Silica were analyzed using each fabricated sensor, demonstrating the sensor’s capability to differentiate materials based on their electromagnetic dielectric contrast. The second version of the sensor for chrome sample analysis is offered high Q-factor (1214) and best Figure of Merit (10). The combined advantages of compactness, low cost, and dual‑scale operation make the proposed sensor a strong candidate for non‑destructive material characterization. Additionally, the methodology presented in this study provides a foundation for developing portable, field‑deployable sensing devices tailored for rapid, on‑site evaluation of mining and mineral‑based materials.
