 ABSTRACT
The NB2 Guide Design is used to provide a defined neutron beam flux to at least one monochromator and
an end station. The known monochromator configuration will support a sample alignment station and the
end station will be able to accommodate either a neutron polarization development instrument or a spin
echo neutron spectrometer, as well as the potential for additional monochromators along the guide path.
The design proposed will meet the needs specified for those instruments defined by science requirements
documents, and will also provide insight into potential opportunities for future instrument developments.

 1. PURPOSE AND REQUIREMENTS
The NB2 Guide is designed to provide optimal beam transport given the requirements to meet future in-
strument needs within the scope of the High Flux Isotope Reactor Beryllium Reflector Replacement (HBRR)
maintenance activity. These are specified in science requirements documents intended to define a specific
measurement capability using the the thermal and cold neutron energy flux provided by the HFIR cold
source located with in the HFIR Beamtube 4 (HB4). In particular, the NB2 guide is anticipated to provide
neutron flux to a sample alignment station and a polarization development end station as specified in Crow
2025 and PILOT Instrument Development Team 2023. While these documents provide significant infor-
mation regarding the expected capability of these instruments, the guide design will only need to address
a subset of those capabilities. Those capabilities are seen in Table 1 and provide a set of metrics for a suc-
cessful design.

 2. NEUTRON BEAM DESIGN
The simulation software utilized to understand the proposed geometry and its alignment requirements is
the McStas ray-tracing toolkit [Willendrup and Lefmann 2021]. McStas provides a straightforward means
by which to replicate the anticipated performance of the HFIR cold source and the instruments that uti-
lize its neutron flux. This analysis comprises four distinct assemblies: the Cold Source, the Beam Tube,
the Main Shutter and the NB2 Guide System. The first three assemblies have been simulated and perfor-
mance analysed with significant detail in Frost et al. 2021 and Frost, Granroth, and Huegle 2025. The NB2
Guide will start immediately downstream of the HB4 Main Shutter, with an interface that is 40mm wide by
50mm tall. The guide will curve to lose line-of-sight of the cold moderator and to reach sufficient separa-
tion from the neighboring beam lines. Channeled guides are needed to accommodate the curvature of the
guide while also maintaining a short-wavelength cut off required by the alignment station. An overview of
the beam line layout can be seen in Figure1
2.1 STRAIGHT SECTION A (GUIDE 1)
The First Guide Section is used to transport the emitted beam from Channel 1 of the Main Shutter Guide
Insert and preserve the phase space density in the useful spectral range needed by the alignment station
and end station located along NB2. The short wavelength cut off for this instrument is 2.0Å with beam
trajectories as wide as 0.8◦ from the nominal beam direction. This suggests that m=4.0 coatings on all sur-
faces along this guide section would be appropriate, however this would only make a difference for a small
fraction of the total beam intensity. These high-angle features of the beam phase space would also be re-
flected multiple times, creating a situation where the value added by the higher reflectivity cutoff would be
very low. Optimal transport efficiency along this section of the guide is determined by the illumination re-
quirements at the monochromator installed after Guide 2, in particular its mosaic, orientation and focusing
performance to meet the requirements of the Sample Alignment Station. Furthermore, as this section is not
curved, channels will likely not be required.
2.2 CURVED SECTION A (GUIDE 2)
The second guide section ensures the short wavelength cutoff that will help with higher energy background
effects is not too aggressive and does not eliminate flux needed by the the alignment station. Thus, there
is a balance between the amount of curvature that can be tolerated by the neutron beam and the reflectiv-
ity requirements of that geometry. This is dictated by the short wavelength spectrum cut off requirements
downstream and the overall cost of the guide itself. Super-mirror coatings as high as m=6 could be needed
here, but can be optimized by utilizing the multi-channel geometry and not transporting neutrons of un-
necessarily short wavelength or high divergence from the nominal beam trajectory. These concepts are
described well in Figures 6 and 7 of Frost, Granroth, and Huegle 2025 .
2.3 MONOCHROMATOR INTERFACE
The monochromator material used to extract a specific wavelength (and associated higher orders) for the
alignment station will be Highly Ordered Pyrolytic Graphite (HOPG) crystals in a stack aligned within
the guide itself. The crystals will be installed during fabrication of the guide component, which will re-
quire single-crystalline silicon or sapphire windows with the proper reflecting coating on the diffracted
side of the guide. This is needed in order to maintain transport efficiency for all neutrons near the HOPG,
but also transmit those that are diffracted from the guided beam path with as little attenuation as possible.
The shape of this segment of the guide will look like a continuation of the curvature by the segments pre-
ceding it, with the exception of the vanes that define the three channel geometry. The vane feature will not
be included near the HOPG, but will resume downstream into the next section of guide; a continuation of
the curvature by Guide 2.

2.4 CURVED SECTION B (GUIDE 3)
The curved guide section beyond the HOPG monochromator will continue with curvature, but will begin
to straighten out in order to meet the location of the proposed velocity selector for the instrument at the
guide end station. The less aggressive curvature at this part of the guide will also permit a lower reflectiv-
ity cutoff as the angle of interaction with the guide surfaces will be shallower. Optimization of the reflec-
tivity needed to meet the science requirements for the end station instrument will be critical to achieving
substantial value in this part of the design.

2.5 STRAIGHT SECTION B (GUIDE 4)
The straight guide section will be used to transport the neutrons that make it past the monochromator and
curved guide sections towards the instrument end station for NB2. This distance will also allow for the im-
plementation of a Velocity Selector (VS) and any guide tapering to achieve a certain on-sample flux value,
though it should be noted that beam divergence and losses will increase if these techniques are utilized.
More importantly, that distance will provide opportunity for any structure in the beam phase-space from
the channeled and curved guide sections to “smooth out” for use as a neutron radiography or small-angle
neutron scattering test setup. The final shape of the beam phase space across a range of relevant wave-
lengths will be included in the final result for appreciation by any potential future technical developments
that may arise.

 3. PERFORMANCE OF THE PROPOSED DESIGN
As stated above, simulations of the incident neutron beam were performed using the McStas toolkit. The
exit end of each guide section will have a set of monitors dedicated to the spatial distribution, horizontal
and vertical phase-space distribution, and wavelength spectrum to indicate if the guide section will meet
the goals laid out by the science requirements document. One then uses a figure-of-merit to resolve a cer-
tain feature of the incident beam at the sample, to determine whether this lies within the science require-
ments or adds little value to improve the instrument performance.

3.1 NUMBER OF CHANNELS IN THE GUIDE SYSTEM
Varying the number of channels in the curved sections of a guide system can help prevent a "lopsided"
phase space and increase the performance of the overall guide system. Since the number of guide sections
is limited (4 sections), and the number of possible channels is equally low (1, 3 or 5 channels), it was fea-
sible to simulate all possible permutations. The flux was recorded over the region of interest (2.5-20 Å) at
the Sample Position of the PILOT instrument (4m downstream of the guide exit) over a 40×40 mm2 area
(Figure 2). All m-Values were set to m = 6. As one would expect, the guide system performance hinges
the most on the number of channels in its curved sections (Sections 2 and 3). The overall intensity gain of
the 1-5-5-1 configuration compared to the 1-3-3-1 configuration is minimal and does not justify the cost
associated with this upgrade. Additionally, the 1-3-3-1 configuration performs slightly better at long wave-
lengths (Figure 2, insert), where spectrum is starved of intensity. The 1-3-3-1 configuration of channels is
therefore preferable. See also Table 2.

3.2 STRAIGHT SECTION A (GUIDE 1)
Parameter scans of the reflectivity critical cutoff that characterize the impact to the performance of the
Sample Alignment Station will be the focus of the first two guide sections. The transport efficiency pro-
vided by the super mirror cutoff of this section and the next one are optimized based on the needs of the
Sample Alignment Station. The spectral intensity around 2.4Å neutron wavelength, beam flux distribution
and divergence at the sample position of the SAS Diffractometer will be the figure of merit for this case.
Guide 1’s m-Values were scanned from m = 1.0 to m = 6.0 and the intensity over the region of interest
(ROI, 2.3Å-2.7Å) was recorded at the Guide exit. A plot showing the increase in performance with m-
Value is seen in Figure 3. All m-Values not scanned were set to 6.0. The ideal m-Values of Guide 1 de-
termined by this method are m = 2.5 (left/right) and m = 3 (top/bottom). Plots of results showing the flux
distribution and divergence on sample for these values can be seen in Figure 4.

3.3 CURVED SECTION (GUIDE 2)
The curvature of this guide section was designed to provide a smooth and not-so-sharp redirection of the
incident beam away from the HB4 beamtube centerline, towards the best location identified in the com-
mon guide system for the SAS monochromator location. Much like the prior section, Guide 2’s m-Values
were scanned from m = 1.0 to m = 6.0 and the intensity over the region of interest (ROI, 2.3Å-2.7Å) was
recorded at the Guide exit. A plot showing the increase in performance with m-Value is seen in Figure 5.
All m-Values not scanned were set to 6.0. The ideal m-Values of Guide 1 determined by this method are
m = 5.0 (left/right) and m = 3.0 (top/bottom). Plots of results showing the flux distribution on sample and
divergence on sample for these values can be seen in Figure 6.

3.4 SAMPLE ALIGNMENT STATION MONOCHROMATORS
The compound monochromator design used to realize the Sample Alignment Station begins with a fixed
orientation crystal set that will select a distinct wavelength for use at the SAS. This is made up of a set of
crystals with a length of 11cm to span the width of the guide at 22◦ to provide a 2.4Å neutron wavelength
flux from the 002 reflection of the HOPG crystals.

3.5 CURVED SECTION B (GUIDE 3)

4.1 SAMPLE ALIGNMENT STATION PERFORMANCE
The sample Alignment Station receives a quasi-monochromatic beam of 2.4 Å. The FWHM of the hor-
izontal divergence is 0.7◦ within the 5x5 cm2 sample area. The total flux in the 5x5 cm2 sample area is
4.51×107 n/s (1.8×106 n/s/cm2 ). This meets the requirement of 1×106 n/s/cm2 (Table 1).

4.2 USANS PERFORMANCE
The USANS option is not explored further within this document since neither instrument design nor sci-
entific requirements case is well established at this point. Considering its positioning between the end of
Guide 3 (Figure 7) and the end of Guide 4 (Figure 9), the main concern might be the residual structure in
the vertical divergence.

4.3 PILOT POLARIZATION TEST STATION PERFORMANCE
The PILOT instrument receives a smooth beam with only minor structure in the horizontal divergence
[Figure 12]. The beam footprint will be slightly larger than the 4x4 cm2 FWHM laid out in the Scientific
requirements document. When comparing the optimized guide system’s performance to a hypothetical "all
m = 6" version, only very marginal differences can be observed above 2.5 Å. The total integrated inten-
sity over the sample area (4x4 cm2 ) amounts to 1.03×1010 n/s (6.43×108 n/s/cm2 ). When counting only
neutrons with a wavelength above 2.5 Å, this is reduced to 9.42×109 n/s (5.89×108 n/s/cm2 ). This is well
above the values requested in the SRD (1×108 n/s/cm2 ).
