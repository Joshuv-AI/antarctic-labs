The height of Dyck paths and checkerboard labellings
Helmut Prodinger

Abstract
. Dyck paths and certain black/white labelling of nodes leads to the white-height . Using generating functions and tricks of the trade, we establish that the average white-height among Dyck paths of half length n n is asymptotic to 1 2 ​ π ​ n \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\pi n} for two different models. These are appealing results that could be presented to students to learn the trade. A last section links walks with double steps and 2-Motzkin paths. For them, the white-height is the natural concept. Folks who might be offended by the notion of white-height might choose their own colours that they like.

2.
Labels based on parity of ordinates

In Figure
1
we see a Dyck path where the nodes between the path and the
x
-axis are label black/white as indicated. To get more
of a ‘checkerboard’ feeling is was redrawn after a rotation by 45 degrees.

Figure 1.
A Dyck path with black and white nodes. The same, but rotated by 45 degrees, so that it looks more ‘checkerboard.’

The
white-height
of an
x
-coordinate is the number of white nodes on that coordinate. The
white-height
of the whole Dyck path is the maximum of all these values. Since the white-height is more or less half of the ordinary height, we expect an asymptotic value
\sim\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\pi n}
when all Dyck paths of half-length
n
are equally likely, but we will provide more precise calculations.

A typical decomposition of a Dyck path is to consider the
first return
to the
x
-axis. Then the first and the last step of the first sojurn can be chopped off, leading to a recursion. But careful!
This leads then to a Dyck path where the labels are black-white–…instead of the original white-black–… . So we introduce two sequences of generating functions:

A_{h}(z)
counts paths white-black–…paths with
white-height

\leq h
and

B_{h}(z)
counts paths black-white–…paths with
white-height

\leq h
. These functions call each other recursively:

\displaystyle A_{h}

\displaystyle=1+zB_{h-1}A_{h},\quad A_{0}=0,

\displaystyle B_{h}

\displaystyle=1+zA_{h}B_{h},\quad B_{0}=1

or

\displaystyle A_{h}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-zB_{h-1}},\quad A_{0}=0,

\displaystyle B_{h}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-zA_{h}},\quad B_{0}=1.

One could now eliminate the
B_{h}
sequence to get

A_{h}=\cfrac{1}{1-\cfrac{z}{1-zA_{h-1}}},

which has the pleasant continued fraction appeal.

After doing a few calculations, one sees the quantity
\sqrt{1-4z}
popping up, and so it is time to introduce the substitution

z=\frac{u}{(1+u)^{2}}

that is successfully known since 1972
[
2
]
(and sometimes rediscovered). Then we find

\displaystyle A_{h}

\displaystyle=(1+u)\frac{1-u^{2h}}{1-u^{2h+1}},

\displaystyle B_{h}

\displaystyle=(1+u)\frac{1-u^{2h+1}}{1-u^{2h+2}}.

People might be surprised how these were found. Numerators and denominators satisfy second order recursions in all instances and can be guessed/solved. For such guessing approaches we use the program
gfun
.
Having the final forms, they may be proved by induction in a routinely fashion.

We are only interested in
A_{h}(z)
and consider
B_{h}(z)
just as an auxiliary variable.
Pushing
h
to infinity means that there is no restriction w.r.t. the white-height, and so all Dyck paths should be counted. Indeed,

A_{\infty}=1+u=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4z}}{2z}
. The difference
A_{\infty}-A_{h}
refers to all Dyck paths with white-height
>h
, a quantity that comes handy
when one wants to compute the average white-height.

A_{\infty}-A_{h}=(1+u)\frac{1-u^{2h+1}-1+u^{2h}}{1-u^{2h+1}}=(1+u)\frac{u^{2h}(1-u)}{1-u^{2h+1}}=\frac{1-u^{2}}{u}\frac{u^{2h+1}}{1-u^{2h+1}}.

Figure 2.
A path related to the functions
B_{h}(z)
.

We will show how to extract the coefficients of
A_{\infty}-A_{h}
. This follows the method that was used in
[
2
]
.

\displaystyle[z^{n}](A_{\infty}-A_{h})

\displaystyle=[z^{n}](A_{\infty}-A_{h})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint\frac{dz}{z^{n+1}}\frac{1-u^{2}}{u}\frac{u^{2h+1}}{1-u^{2h+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint\frac{du(1-u)(1+u)^{2n+2}}{(1+u)^{3}u^{n+1}}\frac{1-u^{2}}{u}\sum_{k\geq 1}u^{k(2h+1)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint\frac{du(1-u)^{2}(1+u)^{2n}}{u^{n+2-k(2h+1)}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 1}[u^{n+1-k(2h+1)}](1-2u+u^{2})(1+u)^{2n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 1}\biggl[\binom{2n}{n+1-k(2h+1)}-2\binom{2n}{n-k(2h+1)}+\binom{2n}{n-1-k(2h+1)}\biggr].

For the average, we must sum:

\sum_{h\geq 1}\sum_{k\geq 1}\biggl[\binom{2n}{n+1-k(2h+1)}-2\binom{2n}{n-k(2h+1)}+\binom{2n}{n-1-k(2h+1)}\biggr].

To do asymptotics, it is more convenient to start from

\sum_{h\geq 1}\frac{1-u^{2}}{u}\frac{u^{2h+1}}{1-u^{2h+1}}=\frac{1-u^{2}}{u}\sum_{h\geq 0}\frac{u^{2h+1}}{1-u^{2h+1}}-(1+u).

One sets
u=e^{-t}
and studies

\sum_{h\geq 0}\frac{u^{2h+1}}{1-u^{2h+1}}=\sum_{h\geq 0}\sum_{k\geq 1}e^{-k(2h+1)t}.

The next step is to consider/compute the celebrated Mellin transform. A general reference for Combinatorics with an analytic flavour is

[
6
]
. The reader might again follow
[
7
]
;

\mathscr{M}\sum_{h\geq 0}\sum_{k\geq 1}e^{-k(2h+1)t}=\Gamma(s)\sum_{h\geq 0}\sum_{k\geq 1}k^{-s}(2h+1)^{-s}=\Gamma(s)\zeta^{2}(s)(1-2^{-s}).

By the inverse Mellin transform we get the function back:

\mathscr{S}:=\sum_{h\geq 0}\sum_{k\geq 1}e^{-k(2h+1)t}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{2-i\infty}^{2+i\infty}\Gamma(s)\zeta^{2}(s)(1-2^{-s})t^{-s}ds

which is approximated by shifting the line of integration to the left and collecting residues. We consider
s=1,0,-1
and use a computer;

\mathscr{S}\sim\frac{-\log(t)+\log(2)+\gamma}{2t}+\frac{t}{144}.

Since

u=-\log(t)=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4z}}{2z}-1,

we may at the end of the day express our expansions in terms of
\sqrt{1-4z}\,
;

-\log(t)+\log(2)+\gamma-2+t+\Bigl(\frac{-\log(t)}{6}-\frac{35}{72}+\frac{\log(2)+\gamma}{6}\Bigr){t}^{2}

or, in the
z
-world,

-\frac{1}{2}\log(1-4z)-2\sqrt{1-4z}-2+\textsf{further terms}.

Singularity analysis of generating functions
[
5
]
allows to translate the expansion into an asymptotic expansion of the coefficients. This leads to

\frac{4^{n}}{n}+\frac{4^{n}}{\sqrt{\pi}n^{3/2}}+\textsf{smaller order terms}.

However, this must be divided by Catalan numbers

\frac{1}{n+1}\binom{2n}{n}=\frac{4^{n}}{\sqrt{\pi}n^{3/2}}+\textsf{smaller order terms}

After this division one finds the average white-height among all Dyck-paths of semilength
n
to be asymptotic to

\textsf{Average white-height}\sim\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\pi n}+1+\textsf{smaller order terms}.

3.
The checkerboard model

Now we move to the model where no rotation is necessary to see the checkerboard pattern.

Figure 3.
A Dyck path with black and white nodes in ‘checkerboard’ mode.

The generating functions are a bit more difficult than in the previous instance. We do not need an auxiliary sequence, and only consider
A_{\ell}(z)
related to path with
white-height
\leq\ell
. It is not obvious to link it to
A_{\ell-1}(z)
. We must also consider the ordinary height (at an
x
-coordinate (=abscissa)), which is just the corresponding

y
-value (=ordinate) of the path. A path counted by
A_{\ell}(z)
will be decomposed into sojourns, namely the pieces between returns to the
x
-axis. The following drawings might clarify things a bit:

Figure 4.
A sojourn of height 4 and white-height 3. After chopping off first and last step, the height is 3 and the white-height is 2

Figure 5.
A sojourn of height 3 and white-height 3. After chopping off first and last step, the height is 2 and the white-height is still 2.

In general if the white-height of a sojourn is
=\ell
, the height of this sojourn is
=2\ell-1
or
=2\ell-2
. By summing, if the
white-height of a sojourn is
\leq\ell
, the height of this sojourn is
\leq 2\ell-1
. The quantity
H_{i}
corresponding to paths of height
\leq i
is well-known.
It follows from the recursion

H_{i}=\frac{1}{1-zH_{i-1}},\ H_{0}=1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad H_{i}=(1+u)\frac{1-u^{i+1}}{1-u^{i+2}}.

And finally

\displaystyle A_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-zH_{2\ell-1}}=\frac{1}{1-\dfrac{u}{(1+u)^{2}}(1+u)\dfrac{1-u^{2\ell}}{1-u^{2\ell+1}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+u)(1-u^{2\ell+1})}{(1+u)(1-u^{2\ell+1})-u(1-u^{2\ell})}=(1+u)\frac{1-u^{2\ell+1}}{1-u^{2\ell+2}}.

Further,

A_{\infty}-A_{\ell}=(1+u)-(1+u)\frac{1-u^{2\ell+1}}{1-u^{2\ell+2}}=\frac{1-u^{2}}{u}\frac{u^{2\ell+2}}{1-u^{2\ell+2}}.

The generating function

\frac{1-u^{2}}{u}\frac{u^{2\ell}}{1-u^{2\ell}}

refers to paths with white-height
\geq\ell
in the checkerboard model.

Pulling out coefficients can be done as before;

\displaystyle[z^{n}]\frac{1-u^{2}}{u}\frac{u^{2\ell}}{1-u^{2\ell}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint\frac{dz}{z^{n+1}}\frac{1-u^{2}}{u}\frac{u^{2\ell}}{1-u^{2\ell}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint\frac{du(1-u)(1+u)^{2n+2}}{(1+u)^{3}u^{n+1}}\frac{1-u^{2}}{u}\sum_{k\geq 1}u^{2k\ell}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint\frac{du(1-u)^{2}(1+u)^{2n}}{u^{n+2-2k\ell}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 1}[u^{n+1-2k\ell}](1-2u+u^{2})(1+u)^{2n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 1}\biggl[\binom{2n}{n+1-2k\ell}-2\binom{2n}{n-2k\ell}+\binom{2n}{n-1-2k\ell}\biggr].

And

\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\frac{e^{-2\ell t}}{1-e^{-2\ell t}}=\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\sum_{k\geq 1}e^{-2k\ell t};

the Mellin transform is

\mathscr{M}\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\sum_{k\geq 1}e^{-2k\ell t}=\Gamma(s)2^{-s}\zeta^{2}(s).

Once again we must collect residues of

\Gamma(s)2^{-s}\zeta^{2}(s)t^{-s}.

Applying the inverse Mellin transform, multiplying with the factor
\frac{1-u^{2}}{u}
and rewrite the expansion in the
z
-world leads to

-\frac{1}{2}\log(1-4z)+\gamma-2\log(2)+\sqrt{1-4z}+\textsf{further terms}.

And after translating the expansion around
z\sim\frac{1}{4}
to an expansion for
n\to\infty
(singularity analysis, transfer theorems) and finally dividing by the Catalan numbers, we get

\textsf{Average white-height}=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\pi n}-\frac{1}{2}+\textsf{further terms},

assuming that all Dyck paths of semi-length
n
are equally likely.

We use the variable
Z
for a step in the lattice
7
, which are double steps in the original lattice. First, we ignore the layer below the green line. The situation
has a Motzkin path flavour. The equation of interest is

F=1+2ZF+Z^{2}F^{2}=(1+ZF)^{2}=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4Z}}{2Z^{2}}-\frac{1}{Z}

Now, including the layer below the green line

G=1+ZG+Z^{2}FG=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4z}}{2z},

which is the generating function of the Dyck numbers.

This scheme can be seen in the context of the height.

c_{h}=1+2Zc_{h}+Z^{2}c_{h-1}c_{h}=\frac{1}{1-2Z-Z^{2}c_{h-1}},\quad c_{1}=\frac{1}{1-2Z-Z^{2}c_{0}},\ c_{0}=0.

Then we need

F_{h}=\frac{c_{h}}{Z^{2}}=(1+u)^{2}\frac{1-u^{2h}}{1-u^{2h+2}},

as can be seen be induction. Further

G_{h+1}=1+ZG_{h+1}+Z^{2}F_{h}G_{h+1}=\frac{1}{1-Z-Z^{2}F_{h}}=(1+u)\frac{1-u^{2h+2}}{1-u^{2h+3}},

again by induction. To say it again:

G_{h}=(1+u)\frac{1-u^{2h}}{1-u^{2h+1}},

which is generating function of paths with white-height
\leq h
.
Here is a special case:

G_{1}=1+ZG_{h+1}+Z^{2}F_{h}G_{h+1}=\frac{1}{1-Z}=(1+u)\frac{1-u^{2}}{1-u^{3}}

since only flat steps are allowed. Further, we see that

G_{\infty}=\lim_{h\to\infty}(1+u)\frac{1-u^{2h}}{1-u^{2h+1}}=1+u=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4z}}{2z}.

We obtained a continued fraction

\cfrac{1}{1-Z-\cfrac{Z^{2}}{1-2Z-\cfrac{Z^{2}}{1-2Z-\cfrac{Z^{2}}{\dots}}}}=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4Z}}{2Z},

which can be compared with

\cfrac{1}{1-\cfrac{z}{1-\cfrac{z}{1-\cfrac{z}{\dots}}}}=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4z}}{2z}.

The combinatorial meaning of
z
(previous section) and
Z
(this section) is slightly different;
z
refers to the half length, and
Z
to the number of double-steps.
That explains the slight diffence of

(1+u)\frac{1-u^{2h+1}}{1-u^{2h+2}}\ \text{ versus }\ (1+u)\frac{1-u^{2h}}{1-u^{2h+1}}.
