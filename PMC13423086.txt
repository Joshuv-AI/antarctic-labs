PregInPoxVac maternal protocol: a phase 3 trial evaluating maternal immunogenicity and safety of the MVA-BN vaccine in the Democratic Republic of the Congo
Authors: Paulina Morales Ruiz, Solange Milolo Tshilumba, Kirsten Maertens, Vivi Maketa, Primo Kimbulu, Felly Mitashi, Mahmoud Abu Azoum, Gustave Kasereka, Aurélie Bisumba, Maha Salloum, Freddy Bikoli-Bolombo, Marthe Le Prevost, Gwen Lemey, Geofrey Makenga, Pierre Van Damme, Jean-Pierre Van Geertruyden, Hypolite Muhindo-Mavoko, Patrick Mitashi, Trésor Zola Matuvanga, Ynke Larivière

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionMpox remains endemic in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC), with pregnant women at increased risk of severe disease and adverse outcomes. The WHO has called for data on mpox vaccination in high-risk groups, but no clinical trial has assessed the safety or immunogenicity of mpox vaccines in this population. The modified vaccinia Ankara–Bavarian Nordic (MVA-BN) vaccine, a third-generation, highly attenuated, non-replicating, live virus vaccine, has shown an excellent safety profile in adolescents and adults, including immunocompromised individuals, and is expected to be safe during pregnancy.Methods and analysisPregnancy & Infancy mPox Vaccine study is a phase 3, open-label trial that aims to evaluate the safety, reactogenicity and immunogenicity of the MVA-BN vaccine in pregnant and postpartum women in Boende, the DRC, compared with adults of the POX-MVA-045 study (NCT06549530) in DRC and Uganda. A total of 359 pregnant women (16–35 years old, in their second or third trimester of pregnancy) are being enrolled and randomised to receive the homologous MVA-BN vaccine regimen at a 28-day interval, with the first dose administered either before 32 weeks of gestation during pregnancy or within 72 hours postpartum (3:2 ratio randomisation). An additional, non-randomised group will receive post-exposure prophylaxis following confirmed mpox exposure. Primary immunogenicity analyses will assess the non-inferiority of neutralising antibody titres in pregnant women compared with those in non-pregnant adults. Primary safety analyses will compare reactogenicity in pregnant women to that observed in adults and safety in pregnant women to safety observed in adults and women vaccinated in the immediate postpartum period. Secondary analysis will evaluate neutralising antibody titres and total binding antibody concentrations over time for pregnant and postpartum women compared with adults and each other.Ethics and disseminationThe trial integrates ancillary care pathways, local capacity building and formative workshops that co-developed research tools. Ethical approvals were obtained from institutional and national review boards in the DRC and Belgium. Results will be shared with local communities, national stakeholders and global health agencies.Trial registration numberNCT06844500 & PACTR202511506487215.

Introduction

Mpox (formerly known as monkeypox) is a zoonotic disease caused by an Orthopoxvirus closely related to the variola (smallpox) virus.1–3 In the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC), where mpox is endemic, historical and recent evidence have documented severe adverse pregnancy outcomes, including foetal loss, miscarriage, stillbirth and congenital mpox infection, highlighting pregnant women and neonates as a particularly vulnerable population.2 4–10

Recognising vaccination as a primary strategy for mpox prevention, the WHO has called for clinical data on vaccine use in vulnerable groups and in pregnant women.11 Despite this call, no clinical trial data on the safety or immunogenicity of a third-generation mpox vaccine, considered suitable for use during pregnancy, are available. To address this evidence gap, the Pregnancy & Infancy mPox Vaccine study (PregInPoxVac) consortium is conducting a phase 3, open-label clinical trial evaluating the immunogenicity, safety and reactogenicity of the modified vaccinia Ankara–Bavarian Nordic (MVA-BN) vaccine administered during pregnancy and the immediate postpartum period, in the DRC.

MVA-BN, marketed as JYNNEOS, IMVAMUNE or IMVANEX, is a third-generation, highly attenuated, non-replicating, live virus vaccine developed for the prevention of smallpox and mpox. It has demonstrated a strong safety and immunogenicity profile in over 23 clinical trials involving nearly 9000 participants, including children and immunocompromised individuals.12–17 Moreover, the MVA-BN vaccine backbone has already been used as MVA-BN-Filo vaccination against Ebola in both pregnant women and infants, without identified safety concerns.18–20 In broader populations, vaccine effectiveness during the 2022 global mpox outbreak (clade II) ranged from 66% to 90% after two prophylactic doses, and from 78% to 89% following post-exposure administration.21 Adverse events were generally mild, and serious events were extremely rare and deemed unrelated to the vaccine.14 17 21–27 Although vaccine effectiveness against Clade I Monkeypox virus has not been directly established, vaccinia-based vaccines are understood to offer cross-protection against orthopoxviruses, consistent with historical estimates of 82–85% effectiveness of first-generation smallpox vaccines against mpox.28

The study will be conducted in Boende, a remote rural area of the DRC with a known mpox burden and substantial structural health barriers, including limited access to essential healthcare, high burden of sexually transmitted infections (STIs), malnutrition and endemic malaria.29–32 However, the location chosen builds on an existing clinical trial environment established by the EBL2007 vaccine trial33 34 and integrates an ancillary care policy previously implemented and evaluated to ensure access to basic healthcare for study participants.35

The primary objective of this trial is to evaluate the immunogenicity and safety of a two-dose MVA-BN regimen administered during pregnancy and the immediate postpartum period. Immunogenicity and safety outcomes will be benchmarked against adult data from the POX-MVA-045 trial conducted in the DRC and Uganda (NCT06549530),36 which applied the same vaccination regimen.37 To complement the clinical objectives, this study integrates contextually appropriate, participant-centred strategies, including ancillary care for participants’ medical events, local capacity building and co-creation of study tools with local stakeholders. These embedded strategies align with the 2025 Joint Statement on Strengthening Clinical Trials38 and aim to contribute to more equitable, ethical and sustainable clinical trial models in underserved settings.

Methodology

Trial design

This open-label, phase 3 clinical trial (NCT06844500)39 evaluates the immunogenicity, reactogenicity and safety of the standard MVA-BN vaccine regimen in three groups of pregnant and postpartum women: women vaccinated during pregnancy (Maternal Group 1, MG1), women vaccinated within 72 hours after delivery (Maternal Group 2, MG2) and pregnant women receiving MVA-BN as post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) following confirmed mpox exposure (Maternal Group 3, MG3). This design reflects three complementary vaccination scenarios: MG1 establishes direct evidence on maternal and foetal safety and immunogenicity when MVA-BN is administered during gestation; MG2 serves as a safety benchmark, allowing general adult vaccine-related effects to be distinguished from outcomes specific to vaccination during pregnancy; and MG3 evaluates the use of MVA-BN as PEP during pregnancy. A historical adult arm of the POX-MVA-045 study, conducted in non-pregnant adults in the DRC and Uganda (NCT06549530), serves as the primary comparator for immunogenicity non-inferiority and reactogenicity assessments across these groups (figure 1). This cohort represents the population in which MVA-BN efficacy has been established, unconfounded by the immunological changes associated with pregnancy.

Trial design. SC, subcutaneous.

Investigational product

This trial administers the MVA-BN vaccine, a third-generation, highly attenuated, live, non-replicating vaccine containing the active ingredient modified vaccinia Ankara (MVA) from Bavarian Nordic. Participants receive two 0.5 mL standard doses, each containing 0.5×108 to 3.95×108 Inf.U/0.5 mL. The doses will be administered 28 days apart (±3 days) as subcutaneous injections into the deltoid region, preferably in the non-dominant arm.

Setting

The study will be conducted at the Boende General Reference Hospital (GRH), Tshuapa Province, DRC (figure 2). Two satellite sites (Motema Mosantu and Marie-Louise Health Centres) will support maternal recruitment, delivery and immediate postpartum procedures, where applicable. Participants may also be recruited from surrounding health zones. The site workforce includes staff nurses, doctors, specialists (gynaecologists and paediatricians), laboratory technicians and social scientists from Boende and Kinshasa, most of whom have prior clinical trial experience at the site34 40; with additional staff receiving protocol-specific training before study initiation.

Sample size calculation

Sample size calculation is performed for the primary immunogenicity endpoint: serum neutralising antibody titres, measured by plaque reduction neutralisation test (PRNT), 2 weeks after the second dose in pregnant women (MG1), compared with adults from the POX-MVA-045 study.

Assuming an SD of 0.6 for log₁₀-transformed titres, a non-inferiority margin of 0.667 for the geometric mean titre (GMT) ratio, a two-sided 5% significance level, a 15% dropout rate and a power of 80%, a sample of 215 evaluable pregnant women (MG1) is required. For safety evaluation, participants vaccinated after pregnancy (MG2) are recruited in a 3:2 ratio (n=144), allowing comparison of safety outcomes between vaccination during and after pregnancy. Additionally, maternal and neonatal morbidity and mortality surveillance data being collected in parallel at Boende’s health centres will provide an estimate of the background incidence of maternal and neonatal outcomes among non-study deliveries at these sites.

For MG3, enrolment is driven by the occurrence of confirmed exposures among pregnant women during the trial enrolment period, and no formal sample size is therefore pre-specified for this group. Immunogenicity, safety and reactogenicity outcomes in MG3 will be summarised descriptively on an exploratory basis, without formal power calculations. This group was included primarily on ethical grounds, to ensure access to post-exposure prophylaxis.

The adult group of the POX-MVA-045 study includes 230 participants, as determined by that study’s sample size calculation (assuming an SD of 0.6 for log₁₀-transformed titres, a non-inferiority margin of 0.667 for the GMT ratio, a two-sided 5% significance level, ≥90% power and allowing for approximately 14% dropout). Data from all adult participants will be used for comparative analyses of immunogenicity and safety. Although a smaller reference sample of 215 adults would be sufficient for a non-inferiority comparison, using the full dataset maximises the precision of the comparative estimates without affecting the prespecified power for the maternal groups.

This study is not powered to detect statistically significant differences in safety outcomes; however, the detectability of safety events is described probabilistically. With the established sample size, an event with an underlying frequency of approximately 1 in 75 participants would be expected to be observed at least once with >95% probability.

Blinding

This is an open-label trial; therefore, participants, site staff and laboratory personnel involved in endpoint evaluation are aware of the maternal group assignments.

Patient and public involvement

Seven months before trial initiation, participatory workshops were conducted with local stakeholders to explore local knowledge, attitudes and perceptions related to mpox, vaccination, ancillary care and clinical research. Insights from these sessions informed trial protocol adaptations and supported the co-creation of infographic materials with a local artist to enhance community understanding and engagement. These findings will be published separately. During subsequent workshops, the study was presented using these infographic materials, and pregnant women who expressed interest were invited to schedule a screening appointment at the study site.

Population

Participant enrolment

Pregnant women who meet the eligibility criteria are allocated to receive the MVA-BN vaccine regimen either during pregnancy (MG1; n=215) or within 72 hours postpartum (MG2; n=144). An additional PEP group (MG3), including pregnant women in any trimester with recent exposure to a laboratory-confirmed or clinically confirmed mpox case, may be enrolled. Enrolment into this group, via convenience sampling, is possible within 14 days of exposure (ideally within 4 days), provided the woman remains asymptomatic, as per WHO guidelines.38 Women initially enrolled in MG2 who report exposure to a confirmed mpox case during pregnancy will be reallocated to MG3 and vaccinated during pregnancy with PEP. A historical control group (n=230) of non-pregnant adults aged 18–<50 years from the POX-MVA-045 trial (NCT06549530), conducted in the DRC and Uganda, will serve as a reference group for comparison of maternal immune responses. This cohort includes both male and female adults, enabling us to isolate pregnancy-specific immunological effects.

Informed consent procedures

Separate consent documentation is used for pregnant women aged 18–35 years (consent), minors aged 16–17 years (assent), and the parents or legal guardians of minors (consent).

Information about the study (including its purpose, procedures, risks, benefits, rights and the use of biological samples) is explained in the participant’s preferred language (French or Lingala), using verbal communication and culturally tailored infographics. Participants or parents/guardians who are illiterate are supported by an impartial witness throughout the consent process, who co-signs the informed consent/assent form(s). Voluntary participation is emphasised, with clear assurance that refusal or withdrawal does not affect access to routine medical care or other health services. The consent/assent process is documented in the electronic case report form (eCRF), and a signed copy of the informed assent/consent is provided to the participant and their parent/legal guardian, if applicable.

Eligibility

Participants are eligible for enrolment if they meet all inclusion criteria and none of the exclusion criteria (see table 1). Screen failures may occur if a participant does not meet eligibility criteria at the time of screening. However, rescreening and consequent enrolment are permitted if the ineligibility is due to a transient condition or illness that has resolved by the time of rescreening. Blood specimens collected for screening assessments are repeated if the original blood draw was performed more than 28 days prior.

Objectives and endpoints

Objectives and endpoints of both study components are shown in online supplemental appendix 2.

Randomisation

Block randomisation is used to allocate participants to either MG1 or MG2 in a 3:2 ratio. A pre-established randomisation list is generated using sealedenvelope.com with a unique seed and variable block sizes of 4, 6 and 8. No randomisation will be conducted for MG3, as this group is enrolled through convenience sampling.

The sponsor is responsible for preparing the randomisation booklets, each containing 30 sequentially numbered, sealed envelopes indicating the assigned group (MG1 or MG2). These booklets are stored securely at the site, accessible only to authorised staff.

Study visits

A detailed description of study visits and procedures is provided in online supplemental appendix 3. To promote retention and protocol adherence, study staff schedule follow-up visits in advance and provide reminder phone calls or perform house visits when the participant lives outside the mobile phone network range, whenever possible. Adherence to the study protocol is monitored primarily through participant attendance at scheduled study visits.

Intervention discontinuation and participant withdrawal

Vaccination may be delayed in the presence of temporary clinical conditions identified at screening or prior to dosing, including acute illness or fever (≥38.0°C), in which case vaccination is postponed until resolution of symptoms. Participants receiving treatment for acute malaria are rescheduled once treatment is completed and after a minimum 3-day post-treatment interval, while screening specimens collected more than 28 days prior to enrolment must be repeated before vaccination.

Vaccination may be withheld or discontinued in the event of a serious adverse event (SAE), a Grade 3 or higher adverse event considered related to the investigational product, or any new medical condition that in the judgement of the principal investigator (PI) renders continued vaccination unsafe. Permanent discontinuation will occur in cases of anaphylaxis, severe hypersensitivity reactions, vaccine-related SAEs, participant withdrawal of consent, non-compliance, loss to follow-up, or ineligibility discovered post-enrolment. The PI is encouraged to consult the sponsor prior to withdrawal, except in emergencies.

Participants may be withdrawn from vaccination or trial participation as determined by the PI or the sponsor, particularly for medical reasons, safety concerns or non-compliance. Reasons for withdrawal will be documented in source documents and eCRFs.

Data collection

Each participant receives a unique ID at screening, which is used for all data and sample tracking to ensure the de-identification of personal information.

Socio-demographics

At screening and on day 0, site staff collect participants’ socio-demographic characteristics, including age, sex, education level, occupation and lifestyle factors (eg, alcohol, tobacco and drug use).

Medical history

Medical history is recorded, including major past conditions (surgeries, diabetes, hypertension, heart disease, congenital abnormalities, neurological conditions) and current illnesses (if any at screening) for which participants require medication or that could interfere with the study findings. Moreover, nutritional status, immunisation history, concomitant medication use, comorbidities and previous pregnancy outcomes are also collected. Pregnancy development is documented at every visit, and foetal development is assessed by obstetric ultrasound.

At birth, the following neonatal outcomes are recorded: gestational age at delivery (assessed through the Finnström score), birth weight, length and head circumference, APGAR scores (at 1, 5 and 10 min), comorbidities (eg, respiratory distress, neonatal infections) and neonatal mortality (when applicable, early or late). The following long-term infant outcomes are evaluated: at 6 and 12 weeks, 6 months, and 13 months of age: neurodevelopmental milestones, the presence of chronic conditions, and immune responsiveness to the vaccine, adverse events and reactogenicity

Adverse events

Participants are closely monitored for adverse events (AEs) throughout the study period. Solicited local and systemic AEs (see table 2) will be recorded in the participant journal for 7 days following each vaccine dose (ie, days 0–7 and days 28–35). To facilitate understanding across literacy or education levels, the journal uses emojis, illustrations and practical examples to help participants recognise and document adverse events and common vaccine reactions. Extra pages are provided to allow continued documentation of any solicited symptoms persisting beyond day 7 or day 35 (online supplemental appendix 4).

All unsolicited adverse events (UAEs) will be captured for 28 days following each vaccine dose, or until they are resolved or stabilised. UAEs occurring within the first 7 days will be recorded in the participant journal and transcribed into the eCRF. After this initial period, events will be actively inquired about during the day 28 (4 weeks after the first dose) and day 56 (4 weeks after the second dose) study visits. Participants may also attend unscheduled study visits at any time to report adverse events; these reports will be documented in the eCRF. Medically attended (U)AEs (MAAEs) will be collected from the time of informed consent signing through the end of study participation. Other AE categories include: suspected unexpected serious adverse reactions (SUSARs), and adverse events of special interest (AESIs). AESIs are specific SAEs identified as particularly relevant to the vaccine or study population and will be closely monitored throughout the trial (see table 3). Statistical considerations

Analysis

All statistical analyses are performed using validated software, RStudio or Statistical Analysis System software (SAS). Statistical significance is defined as p<0.05, unless otherwise specified.

No imputation is applied to endpoints involving multiple time points; only observed data are analysed to avoid bias. Sensitivity analyses may be conducted to assess the impact of missing data on persistence outcomes but will not involve imputation.

Robustness of GMT ratios is explored through subgroup analyses, adjusting for baseline immunity and excluding participants not meeting per-protocol criteria.

Analysis sets

The safety analysis set includes all participants who received at least one dose of the study vaccine and serves as the primary population for safety evaluations. A full analysis set (FAS) includes all vaccinated participants who received at least one dose and have a baseline neutralising antibody result, regardless of protocol deviations. For immunogenicity analyses, a per protocol set (PPS) serves as the primary analysis population, comprising participants who receive both vaccine doses, have a baseline and at least one post-vaccination immunogenicity result, and have no major protocol deviations that affect immune response. A modified intent-to-treat (mITT) population includes all participants who are seronegative at baseline and received at least one vaccine dose.

Primary analyses

Main study

The primary immunogenicity endpoint compares serum-neutralising antibody titres against vaccinia virus between women vaccinated during pregnancy (MG1) and historical adult controls from the POX-MVA-045 study. Titres will be log10-transformed, and GMTs and their ratios will be derived by back-transforming mean differences and presented with 95% CIs. Non-inferiority is concluded if the lower bound of the CI exceeds the pre-specified margin of 0.667, consistent with the margin applied in the POX-MVA-045 comparator study and with the margin most commonly applied in vaccine immunogenicity non-inferiority trials.41 For assay results, values below the Lower Limit of Quantification (LLOQ) are replaced with half the LLOQ; values above Upper Limit of Quantification (ULOQ) are capped at the ULOQ. Data normality is assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test, and appropriate parametric or non-parametric tests will be selected accordingly.

Primary safety and reactogenicity endpoints are analysed descriptively, with the frequency and proportion of participants experiencing each adverse event reported by maternal group, alongside 95% CIs.

Secondary analyses

Main study

Secondary endpoints include analyses of neutralising antibody titres and total binding antibody concentrations over time. GMTs and GMCs, along with their respective ratios, are reported with 95% CIs and visualised graphically across all time points. Seroconversion rates are calculated based on a ≥twofold increase in neutralising antibody seroconversion rate or a ≥fourfold increase in total binding antibody seroconversion rate, depending on baseline titres, following the same seroconversion criteria applied in prior phase 3 MVA-BN studies.37 42 Differences between groups will be assessed using the Miettinen-Nurminen method or a similar approach.

Handling of intercurrent events

Intercurrent events (ICEs) are defined as events occurring after the first dose of study vaccine that may affect the measurement or interpretation of the primary immunogenicity endpoint—neutralising antibody titres measured by PRNT 2 weeks after the second MVA-BN vaccination (day 42). ICEs include missing or incorrect dosing, early trial discontinuation before the primary endpoint visit, compromised sample viability or out-of-window sample collection, use of immune-modifying medications or vaccines, intercurrent mpox infection, and major protocol deviations affecting immune status. Participants experiencing ICEs are excluded from the immunogenicity evaluable population.

Data monitoring

Medical review and monitoring

Independent sponsor medical doctors will review clinical and safety data. Moreover, an independent Data Safety and Monitoring Board (DSMB) comprising one infectious disease specialist, a vaccinologist, a paediatrician, a maternal health expert, a biostatistician, an epidemiologist and an ethics expert, reviews safety data at predefined time points. Based on its assessments, the DSMB may recommend continuation, modification or termination of the trial to safeguard participants and ensure proper conduct.

Data management and quality assurance

Study data are entered directly into RedCap, a secure electronic data capture system using eCRFs. Built-in validation checks, including range checks and consistency checks, are applied at the point of data entry to promote data quality. Source data verification and routine data quality reviews are conducted by the Contract Research Organization (CRO) monitors in accordance with the monitoring plan. Access to the system is role-based and restricted to authorised study personnel. All data are stored on secure servers with regular backups and are de-identified prior to analysis. Detailed data management procedures, including data handling, coding and archiving processes, are described in the study-specific Data Management Plan and Statistical Analysis Plan, which will be made available from the corresponding author on reasonable request once finalised.

Ancillary care

Ancillary care (AC), care provided to participants, not related to the clinical trial procedures, but necessary to support the health and well-being of participants, is foreseen for any AEs occurring during the study period, including those arising outside scheduled trial assessments.43–45 Based on individual needs, AC include counselling, direct medical care or referral to external healthcare facilities when reasonably accessible. Additionally, medical expenses incurred outside the study site may be covered or reimbursed. All AC measures are documented for monitoring and evaluation.46 A dedicated ancillary care algorithm and policy, developed specifically for this trial, will be published elsewhere. To support AC, the leading site’s pharmacy is equipped with essential supplies, which are regularly monitored to ensure the availability of medicines, materials and treatments aligned with participants’ needs. Satellite sites are also supplied with hospitalisation, surgical and medical equipment to provide care for both mothers and infants, regardless of trial participation.

Research ethics approval

This study is conducted in compliance with the International Council for Harmonisation – Good Clinical Practice (ICH-GCP), the Declaration of Helsinki and applicable national regulations. Ethical and regulatory approvals were obtained from the Comité National d'Éthique de la Santé (CNES) (DRC; Ref. 641/CNES/BN/PMMF/2025), the Comité d'éthique de l'École de santé publique (Ref. Confidentiality

All data and study findings are confidential and will not be shared with anyone not directly involved in the study or divulged outside the authorised research team. The PI and site staff are responsible for ensuring that any documents linking ID numbers to personal identifiers are securely stored and not shared with the sponsor or third parties. Access to the electronic data capture (EDC) system is restricted to authorised personnel via controlled access. Data transmitted to the sponsor or external individuals (eg, laboratory samples for immunogenicity analysis or statistical analysis for DSMB review) are fully de-identified. Study vaccines are labelled for research use only and administered in accordance with the protocol. Any leftover samples collected for immunogenicity will be retained for future use, subject to participant consent and additional ethical approval.

Community level dissemination

Insights from the pre-trial community workshops informed the co-development of culturally adapted infographics that explain the trial’s purpose and procedures to participants. Recognising the influence of healing churches and traditional practices in the Boende region, the study team engages appropriate leaders aiming to bridge cultural gaps, foster transparency and improve maternal health. In addition, regular discussions with site staff are held to identify emerging community rumours and misconceptions that may affect trial participation or trust, and to jointly discuss mitigation strategies that actively engage the community.

Scientific and policy dissemination

Study findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications in recognised scientific journals, presentations at national, regional and international conferences, and website updates targeting researchers and practitioners (see: https://www.uantwerpen.be/en/projects/preginpoxvac/).47

Where applicable, evidence generated from the trial will be incorporated into technical and policy guidance, including the Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) for MVA-BN regarding use in pregnant women, WHO Strategic Advisory Group of Experts (SAGE) recommendations and, where relevant, national policy guidance in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, thereby contributing to improved mpox vaccine strategies and broader maternal–child health interventions.

Discussion

The DRC continues to experience sustained mpox transmission across multiple provinces,3–6 48–50 and reported cases in pregnant women raise serious concerns, including severe maternal disease, miscarriage, stillbirth and congenital infection (5,6). Although extensive evidence supports the safety and immunogenicity of the MVA-BN vaccine in adults and adolescents, and growing data exist for immunocompromised individuals and children (21–30), pregnant women continue to be systematically excluded from clinical trials.51 This exclusion delays access to potentially life-saving interventions for a population at increased risk. The PregInPoxVac study addresses this evidence gap by evaluating MVA-BN in a real-world mpox-endemic setting and generating the first prospective data on maternal immunogenicity, safety and reactogenicity. Its findings may inform future research in maternal immunisation and contribute to policy development for mpox and related orthopoxvirus infections.

When interpreting the findings of this study, it is important to consider both the strengths and limitations. From a methodological perspective, the sample size was powered for immunogenicity rather than formal safety comparisons; however, the 359 sample size allows probabilistic detection of AE with an underlying frequency of 1 in 75 with >95% probability. The absence of specific adverse events or statistically significant differences in safety outcomes should not be interpreted as evidence of safety equivalence, and safety findings should be interpreted with appropriate caution.

From an epidemiological perspective, the study is conducted in a malaria-endemic region, with a high burden of food insecurity and undernutrition. Pregnant women in this setting also experience high rates of adolescent pregnancies, anaemia, STIs and limited access to antenatal care.29 32 52 These overlapping challenges, seen in this setting, may influence immune responses and limit the direct extrapolation of findings to higher-income or lower-burden settings.

From a design and implementation viewpoint, sociocultural dynamics in Boende, including the influence of churches, reliance on traditional medicine and faith-based healing, and low literacy levels, required early and sustained engagement with the local community to understand their doubts and fears and to provide timely and accurate information. These efforts enabled the establishment of a trust network between the team and the community, and the co-development of participant-centred strategies. Concurrently, the limited health infrastructure posed logistical challenges. Yet, by building on the foundations of the earlier EBL2007 Ebola vaccine trial, conducted in Boende,31 33 34 40 the study leveraged existing partnerships, infrastructure and trained personnel, while further investing in hospital and laboratory capacity to support both the study participants and the broader community.

By generating the first prospective data on MVA-BN immunogenicity, safety and reactogenicity during pregnancy and the postpartum period, PregInPoxVac addresses a critical gap in maternal immunisation research. Beyond its clinical objectives, the trial strengthens local research capacity and partnerships in Boende, offering a model for conducting ethically inclusive and sustainable vaccine research in mpox-endemic, under-resourced settings.

Roles and responsibilities

The University of Antwerp, as the consortium lead and trial sponsor, is responsible for regulatory compliance, data management and reporting. The University of Kinshasa (UNIKIN) leads trial implementation at the DRC site and oversees day-to-day study conduct. The Penta Foundation is responsible for the social science component, including the design and analysis of the qualitative research. ACE Research provides independent monitoring, GCP quality assurance and manages the Trial Master File.

Bavarian Nordic (BN) supplies the investigational product (MVA-BN vaccine), coordinates shipment and cold-chain logistics of the vaccines, and leads central laboratory analyses for maternal and child immunogenicity endpoints. Additional immunogenicity analyses are conducted by the National Institute of Biomedical Research (INRB) in the DRC (10% of samples) and VisMederi in Italy (10% of samples).

Trial governance and oversight

Trial oversight is provided through the study governance structure comprising the sponsor, the principal investigator and study leadership team, and collaborating consortium partners. Collectively, these groups contribute expertise in vaccinology, maternal immunisation, infectious diseases, tropical medicine, epidemiology, biostatistics, ethics, social sciences and clinical trial conduct. Trial progress, protocol adherence, data quality and operational performance are reviewed through regular governance meetings, while participant safety is independently monitored by the DSMB.

Substantial protocol amendments are communicated to relevant stakeholders and implemented only after formal approval, with participants informed where applicable. The current protocol version is PregInPoxVac (Protocol identification number: PIPVac-001, Version 3), approved by ACOREP, with all protocol versions and amendments version-controlled and archived.
