Extraction of charmonium branching fractions from J / ψ → γ ​ η c J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c} radiative decays Preprint: TUM-EFT 215/26
Magnus C. Schaaf, Antonio Vairo

Abstract
We assess the tension between theoretical predictions and the values quoted by the Particle Data Group for the partial decay width and branching fraction associated with the radiative charmonium decay J / ψ → γ ​ η c J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c} . A profile scan over the most recent Particle Data Group data depending on the branching fraction ℬ ⁡ ( J / ψ → γ ​ η c ) \BF(J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c}) suggests that the correlation between measured branching fractions is compatible with lattice quantum chromodynamics determinations of the partial decay widths Γ ⁡ ( J / ψ → γ ​ η c ) \Gamma(J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c}) and Γ ⁡ ( η c → γ ​ γ ) \Gamma(\eta_{c}\to\gamma\gamma) . We propose a theoretically grounded photon line shape for the radiative decay spectrum and a prescription for the extraction of (product) branching fractions involving the magnetic dipole transition J / ψ → γ ​ η c J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c} . This approach obviates the need to modify the photon energy spectrum line shape using empirical damping functions, as done in the most recent experimental extractions of ℬ ⁡ ( J / ψ → γ ​ η c ) \BF(J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c}) from the photon line shape, thereby eliminating an inherent ambiguity in the determination of the derived observables.

The above contrasts point towards an inconsistency in the extraction of
\BF(J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c})
from data.
In the CLEO and KEDR analyses
33
;
5
;
6
, the branching fraction is determined by fitting a line shape for the photon energy spectrum to measured data of the radiative decay, from which the signal yield is extracted.
The line shape is modeled by a relativistic Breit–Wigner distribution, modified by an
E_{\gamma}^{3}
factor, with
E_{\gamma}
denoting the emitted photon’s energy.
This factor naturally arises in theoretical calculations of the transition width
15
and is necessary to reconcile the spectrum with Low’s theorem, equivalent to the modification of the Ore–Powell spectrum in quantum electrodynamics
30

Experimentally, it accounts for the observed asymmetry in the photon energy spectrum
33
;
16

If the
\eta_{c}

Such a cut-off is explicitly introduced in the CLEO
33

5
;
6

1
;
4

\BF(J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c})
through the use of
ad hoc

\eta_{c}
peak region of the photon energy spectrum.
Since there is no theoretical justification for any specific form of such damping functions, their introduction leads to unphysical, cut-off-dependent extractions of the branching fraction.
In a different theoretical study
36
, the power-like tail of the line shape is subtracted in dimensional regularization.

In this Letter, we examine the dependence of the partial decay width
\Gamma(\eta_{c}\to\gamma\gamma)
on the value of the branching fraction
\BF(J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c})
in a Particle Data Group-like multiparticle fit.
We find that the fit can yield results compatible with recent lattice quantum chromodynamics calculations for larger values of
\BF(J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c})
, demonstrating that a reliable extraction of
\BF(J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c})
from measured data is essential.
We revisit the theoretical description of the magnetic dipole transition and propose a different method to extract the
J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c}
signal yield from the photon energy spectrum line shape.
The method is cut-off independent and therefore eliminates the ambiguity affecting present determinations of
\BF(J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c})
from the line shape.
To assess the impact on the measured branching fractions, we reevaluate experimental data on the radiative decay with the proposed line shape.

III
Line shape

As previously recognized
16
, the reported values for the branching fraction corresponding to the
J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c}
transition in Refs.
33
;
5
actually refer to the process
J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c}\to\gamma X
, where the intermediate state can be off-shell, and
X
denotes its final decay modes.
The problem lies in the fact that the function that parametrizes the differential
J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c}\to\gamma X
width, which is a Breit–Wigner distribution times
E_{\gamma}^{3}
, contains both the contribution from the magnetic dipole transition
J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c}
around the
\eta_{c}
peak and the Ore–Powell spectrum at high energies
30
.

More specifically, for photon energies
E_{\gamma}
close to the
1S
hyperfine splitting
\Delta E_{\textnormal{{HFS}}}\equiv M_{J/\psi}-M_{\eta_{c}}
, the differential decay width for the inclusive decay
J/\psi\to\gamma X
can be obtained within potential nonrelativistic QCD as
30
;
16

\begin{split}\frac{\mathrm{d}\Gamma_{J/\psi\to\gamma X}}{\mathrm{d}E_{\gamma}}\mspace{16mu}&\underset{\mathclap{E_{\gamma}\lesssim$500\text{\,}\mathrm{MeV}$}}{\approx}\mspace{16mu}\frac{\mathrm{d}\Gamma_{J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c}\to\gamma X}^{\textnormal{({M1})}}}{\mathrm{d}E_{\gamma}}\\
&\underset{\mathclap{\textnormal{{LO}}}}{=}\mspace{3mu}A\frac{E_{\gamma}^{3}}{\pi}\frac{\Gamma_{\eta_{c}}/2}{(\Delta E_{\textnormal{{HFS}}}-E_{\gamma})^{2}+\Gamma_{\eta_{c}}^{2}/4}\,,\end{split}

(1)

where
\Gamma_{\eta_{c}}
represents the total width of the
\eta_{c}
, in the region of interest
E_{\gamma}\lesssim$500\text{\,}\mathrm{MeV}$
, which is about the
J/\psi
binding energy
16
.
Here and in the following, LO refers to the leading order in the nonrelativistic expansion, for which

A=\frac{4}{3}\frac{{c_{\mathrm{F}}^{\textnormal{em}}}^{2}\alpha e_{c}^{2}}{m_{c}^{2}}\,,

(2)

where
\alpha
is the fine structure constant,
e_{c}=2/3
is the fraction of electric charge carried by the charm quark,
m_{c}\approx M_{J/\psi}/2
is the charm quark mass, and
c_{\mathrm{F}}^{\textnormal{em}}=1+\kappa_{c}^{\textnormal{em}}
, with
\kappa_{c}^{\textnormal{em}}=2\alpha_{\mathrm{s}}/(3\pi)+\Order(\alpha_{\mathrm{s}}^{2})
the anomalous magnetic moment of the heavy quark
15
.
The differential decay width is the product of
E_{\gamma}^{3}
with a nonrelativistic Breit–Wigner distribution.
It vanishes as
\Order(E_{\gamma}^{3})
for
E_{\gamma}\to 0
in accordance with Low’s theorem and develops an
\Order(E_{\gamma})
Ore–Powell tail in the region
E_{\gamma}\gg\Delta E_{\textnormal{{HFS}}}
.
The latter includes high-energy events not associated with the production of an intermediate
\eta_{c}

30
.
The partial decay width of
J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c}
is recovered from
Eq.

1
by taking the on-shell limit
\Gamma_{\eta_{c}}\to 0
,

\begin{split}\frac{\mathrm{d}\Gamma_{J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c}}}{\mathrm{d}E_{\gamma}}&=\lim_{\Gamma_{\eta_{c}}\to 0}\frac{\mathrm{d}\Gamma_{J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c}\to\gamma X}^{\textnormal{({M1})}}}{\mathrm{d}E_{\gamma}}\\
&\underset{\mathclap{\textnormal{{LO}}}}{=}\mspace{3mu}AE_{\gamma}^{3}\delta(\Delta E_{\textnormal{{HFS}}}-E_{\gamma})\,,\end{split}

(3)

where the
\delta
-function fixes the energy of the emitted photon, yielding

\Gamma_{J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c}}\underset{\textnormal{{LO}}}{=}A{(\Delta E_{\textnormal{{HFS}}})}^{3}\,,

(4)

in agreement with well known results
13
;
15
.

The photon energy spectrum line shape near the
1S
hyperfine splitting is given by

\frac{\mathrm{d}N_{\gamma}}{\mathrm{d}E_{\gamma}}=N_{J/\psi}\frac{\mathrm{d}\BF(J/\psi\to\gamma X)}{\mathrm{d}E_{\gamma}}=\frac{N_{J/\psi}}{\Gamma_{J/\psi}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\Gamma_{J/\psi\to\gamma X}}{\mathrm{d}E_{\gamma}}\,,

(5)

where
N_{J/\psi}
and
\Gamma_{J/\psi}
are the total number and total width of
J/\psi
.
Integrating
Eq.

5
gives the total number of emitted photons
N_{\gamma}
.
Using
Eq.

1
yields a divergent result because the integration domain at high energies violates the conditions under which
Eq.

1
is valid.
We are, however, not interested in
N_{\gamma}
, but in the total number of
J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c}
transitions,
N_{\eta_{c}}
, which is given by

N_{\eta_{c}}=N_{J/\psi}\BF(J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c})\,.

(6)

The branching fraction for the magnetic dipole transition can be recovered from the differential decay width
\left.{\mathrm{d}\Gamma_{J/\psi\to\gamma X}}\middle/{\mathrm{d}E_{\gamma}}\right.
via

\BF(J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}E_{\gamma}}{\Gamma_{J/\psi}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\Gamma_{J/\psi\to\gamma X}}{\mathrm{d}E_{\gamma}}w(E_{\gamma})\,,

(7)

where choosing

w(E_{\gamma})\underset{\textnormal{{LO}}}{=}\pi\frac{\Gamma_{\eta_{c}}}{2}\delta(\Delta E_{\textnormal{{HFS}}}-E_{\gamma})

(8)

reproduces the branching fraction that follows from
4
, when replacing the differential decay width with
Eq.

1
.
This is because at leading order the combination

\frac{\mathrm{d}\Gamma_{J/\psi\to\gamma X}}{\mathrm{d}E_{\gamma}}\times\pi\frac{\Gamma_{\eta_{c}}}{2}\delta(\Delta E_{\textnormal{{HFS}}}-E_{\gamma})

coincides with the on-shell limit

\lim_{\Gamma_{\eta_{c}}\to 0}\frac{\mathrm{d}\Gamma_{J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c}\to\gamma X}^{\textnormal{({M1})}}}{\mathrm{d}E_{\gamma}}\,.

Hence,
N_{\eta_{c}}
can be obtained from the photon energy spectrum line shape as

\begin{split}N_{\eta_{c}}&=N_{J/\psi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}E_{\gamma}}{\Gamma_{J/\psi}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\Gamma_{J/\psi\to\gamma X}}{\mathrm{d}E_{\gamma}}w(E_{\gamma})\\
&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}E_{\gamma}\frac{\mathrm{d}N_{\gamma}}{\mathrm{d}E_{\gamma}}w(E_{\gamma})\\
&\underset{\textnormal{{LO}}}{=}\pi\frac{\Gamma_{\eta_{c}}}{2}\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}N_{\gamma}}{\mathrm{d}E_{\gamma}}\right\rvert_{E_{\gamma}=\Delta E_{\textnormal{{HFS}}}}\,.\end{split}

(9)

This equation provides the number of
J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c}
transitions as a function of parameters entering the line shape
5
only;
it does not depend on a cut-off since it does not involve integrating over the photon energy, and it gives a branching fraction consistent with perturbation theory at leading order.

After inserting the differential decay width
1
into
Eq.

5
, the photon energy spectrum line shape reads

\frac{\mathrm{d}N_{\gamma}}{\mathrm{d}E_{\gamma}}\underset{\textnormal{{LO}}}{=}NE_{\gamma}^{3}\frac{\Gamma_{\eta_{c}}/2}{(\Delta E_{\textnormal{{HFS}}}-E_{\gamma})^{2}+\Gamma_{\eta_{c}}^{2}/4}\,.

(10)

It depends upon three parameters,
\Delta E_{\textnormal{{HFS}}}
,
\Gamma_{\eta_{c}}
, and an overall normalization
N
that determines the strength of the signal at the peak,
.\left.{\mathrm{d}N_{\gamma}}\middle/{\mathrm{d}E_{\gamma}}\right.\rvert_{E_{\gamma}=\Delta E_{\textnormal{{HFS}}}}
.
Once they have been fitted to data, we obtain the number of signal events from
Eq.

9
, which is the key ingredient to the measured branching fraction.
In actual fits to experimental data,
Eq.

10
must be multiplied by an energy-dependent efficiency
\epsilon(E)
and convolved with a detector resolution function
D(E_{\gamma}\nonscript\>|\nonscript\>E)
,

\frac{\mathrm{d}N_{\gamma}^{\textnormal{(obs)}}(E_{\gamma})}{\mathrm{d}E_{\gamma}}=\int\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}E\frac{\mathrm{d}N_{\gamma}(E)}{\mathrm{d}E}\epsilon(E)D(E_{\gamma}\nonscript\>|\nonscript\>E)\,,

(11)

to describe the observed events.
The proposed line shape can also be applied to exclusive decays
\eta_{c}\to X_{i}
.
In this case, experiments typically measure the invariant mass
M_{X_{i}}
distribution of the
J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c}\to\gamma X_{i}
decay.
This distribution can then be related to the photon energy spectrum line shape by momentum conservation,

E_{\gamma}(M_{X_{i}})=\frac{M_{J/\psi}^{2}-M_{X_{i}}^{2}}{2M_{J/\psi}}\,.

(12)

Equations

9
and
10
, which we propose to extract the number of
J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c}
transitions from the photon energy spectrum line shape, should be contrasted with the ones used in experiments.
The CLEO collaboration uses in its analysis
w(E_{\gamma})=e^{-E_{\gamma}^{2}/(8\beta^{2})}
, with
\beta=$65.0(25)\text{\,}\mathrm{MeV}$

33
.
The KEDR collaboration uses
w(E_{\gamma})=\Delta E_{\textnormal{{HFS}}}^{2}/(E_{\gamma}\Delta E_{\textnormal{{HFS}}}+(E_{\gamma}-\Delta E_{\textnormal{{HFS}}})^{2})
in Ref.
5
, and a function that is unity for
\lvert E_{\gamma}-\Delta E_{\textnormal{{HFS}}}\rvert\leq 2\Gamma_{\eta_{c}}
, vanishes for
\lvert E_{\gamma}-\Delta E_{\textnormal{{HFS}}}\rvert\geq 4\Gamma_{\eta_{c}}
, and interpolates linearly in between in Ref.
6
.
The BESIII collaboration adopts the CLEO and the first KEDR damping functions in many analyses involving the magnetic dipole transition.
Their extractions rely on the damping functions.
The ensuing results fail to reproduce the leading order perturbative result once inserted in
Eq.

7
.
In contrast,
Eq.

9
is model independent and consistent with perturbation theory as long as the differential width has the form
1
.
Another difference between
Eq.

10
and the parameterizations of the photon energy spectrum used in experimental analyses is that
Eq.

10
incorporates a nonrelativistic Breit–Wigner distribution, consistently with a nonrelativistic effective field theory approach, while the latter incorporate a relativistic Breit–Wigner distribution.
The difference is of higher order in the nonrelativistic expansion.
The additional terms resummed by the relativistic Breit–Wigner distribution are spurious, as they capture only a subset of relativistic corrections
16
.
Nevertheless, the difference between nonrelativistic and relativistic Breit–Wigner may serve as an estimate of higher-order corrections.

IV
Branching fractions

J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c}

Three different observables are examined below.

\BF_{\textnormal{CLEO}}^{\textnormal{({M1})}}=$2.01(13)(27)\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}$\,.

37

is an increase of
\BF_{\textnormal{BESIII}}^{\textnormal{({M1})}}
and a decrease of
\BF_{\textnormal{BESIII}}^{\textnormal{($\gamma\gamma$)}}
compared to Ref.
4
.

V
Discussion and conclusions

Significant discrepancies between the experimental average and fit values provided by the Particle Data Group
37
and theoretical predictions of the
J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c}
width can be explained by an inconsistency in the current extraction of the
J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c}
transition width from data.
The Particle Data Group average is based on the two measurements of Crystal Ball
25
and CLEO
33
.
It is pulled down by the former, whose photon energy spectrum line shape is fitted by just a Breit–Wigner distribution, indicating that Crystal Ball data is insensitive to the modification induced by the
E_{\gamma}^{3}
factor.
In the multiparticle fit by the Particle Data Group, the results for
\Gamma(\eta_{c}\to\gamma\gamma)
and
\BF(J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c})
are strongly correlated, as shown by the profile scan in
Fig.

1
, and a larger value of
\BF(J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c})
would render
both

\Gamma(\eta_{c}\to\gamma\gamma)
and
\Gamma(J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c})
consistent with lattice quantum chromodynamics determinations of these quantities
10
;
26
;
31
;
19
;
32
.

In view of this observation, we have revised the determinations of
\BF(J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c})
from CLEO data
33
,
\BF(J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c})\times\BF(\eta_{c}\to\gamma\gamma)
from BESIII data
1
, and
\BF(J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c})\times\BF(\eta_{c}\to p\bar{p})
from BESIII data
4
by proposing a new way to extract the number of produced
\eta_{c}
from the signal.
So far, this number was obtained by convolving the signal with an arbitrary damping function.
In such an approach, however, the result depends on the chosen function and is inconsistent with perturbation theory.
We propose instead to extract the yield of
\eta_{c}
from the peak strength of the signal, as expressed in
Eq.

9
, which is the main theoretical finding of this Letter.
Following this approach, there is no need to introduce arbitrary damping functions, and the result is consistent with leading order perturbation theory.

We obtain from the CLEO data a value of
\BF(J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c})
, given in
Eq.

13
, that is in line with the value quoted by the CLEO collaboration
33
, and from the BESIII data a somewhat larger value, given in
Eq.

16
, than the one reported by the BESIII collaboration
4
.
Both values are larger than the fit value obtained by the Particle Data Group but lead to comparable values of
\BF(\eta_{c}\to\gamma\gamma)
extracted from the combined branching fractions of several exclusive processes of the type
J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c}\to\gamma X_{i}
entering the fit.
