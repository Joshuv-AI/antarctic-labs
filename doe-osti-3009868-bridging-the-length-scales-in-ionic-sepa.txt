 A. Executive Summary

We pursued a data science driven machine learning (ML) approach that blended molecular scale attributes
informed from molecular dynamics (MD) simulation and materials properties to the selectivity and
energy efficiency in targeted ionic separations using electric fields. The model mixtures investigated for
ionic separations are pH sensitive and include organic acids, silica and boron, transition metals, such as
copper and chromium. There were two major research thrusts of this project. Firstly, we investigated
surrogate models and deep learning that relate material chemistries and structures to selective transport of
ionic species under applied electric fields. Secondly we investigated how the bipolar junction interfacial
design and water dissociation catalyst in bipolar membranes affect reverse bias polarization behavior and
pH modulation in deionization platforms as a function of the platform operating parameters (e.g., cell
voltage, residence time, and salt feed concentration). As a result of this work, we also were able to start a
new direction, namely ML models for molecular design of surfactants, The results of our investigations
were published in peer reviewed journals and are listed below.

 1. Nnadili, M.; Okafor, A.; Olayiwola, T.; Akinpelu, D.; Kumar, R.; Romagnoli, J.: Surfactant-
 Specific AI-Driven Molecular Design: Integrating Generative Models, Predictive Modeling, and
 Reinforcement Learning for Tailored Surfactant Synthesis, Industrial and Engineering Chemistry
       Research 2024, 63, 6313. https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.iecr.4c00401

 This paper presents a three-part AI-driven framework for molecular design that integrates
 generative modeling, property prediction, and reinforcement learning. Using SELFIES
 representations, a VAE-based generative model, and graph neural networks, the approach
 generates valid molecules optimized for target properties. Validation through similarity analysis,
 saliency maps, and molecular dynamics simulations demonstrates that the framework can reliably
 produce stable molecules meeting desired property thresholds.

2. T. Olayiwola, L.A. Briceno-Mena, C.G. Arges, J.A. Romagnoli, Synergizing data-driven and
 knowledge-based hybrid models for ionic separations, ACS ES&T Engineering, 2024, 4,
   3032, https://doi.org/10.1021/acsestengg.4c00405

 A hybrid modeling framework was developed for electrodialysis (ED) and resin-wafer
 electrodeionization (EDI) in brackish water desalination. It combines a physics-based
 compositional model with machine learning to simulate system behavior. Synthetic data trains a
 multi-output surrogate model, which is then fine-tuned using limited experimental data. The
 model accurately predicts experimental outcomes and reveals that cell voltage is the most
 influential factor affecting separation efficiency and energy consumption. Using multiobjective
 optimization, conditions were identified that achieve 99% separation efficiency with energy use
 below 1 kWh/kg.

3. T. Kulkarni, A. Muhammad I Al Dhamen, X. Zhang, C.-W. Chiu, H. Zhang, F. Shi, R.
 Kumar, C.G. Arges, Bipolar membrane capacitive deionization for the selective capture of
 lithium ions from brines and conversion to lithium hydroxide, Journal of the Electrochemical
   Society, 2024, 171, 103502, https://doi.org/10.1149/1945-7111/ad7a25

 The research presents a novel bipolar membrane capacitive deionization (BPM-CDI) system for
 direct lithium extraction and LiOH production, addressing the inefficiencies of traditional solar
 evaporation methods. A key innovation is that there is no need for external acids or bases during
 lithium recovery.De-lithiated lithium iron phosphate (LFP) electrodes are used for selective
 lithium ion (Li⁺) capture. The system effectively separates Li⁺ from synthetic brines with high
 concentrations of competing ions (Mg²⁺ and Na⁺). After 8 recirculation cycles, it achieves a LiOH
 concentration of 124 ppm. Post-mortem analysis confirms the electrodes remain stable and intact.
 Molecular dynamics simulations and H-cell experiments reveal a pH inversion mechanism that
 enables LiOH formation during Li⁺ release. Overall, BPM-CDI with selective electrodes offers a
 promising, efficient, and sustainable electrochemical method for lithium recovery and LiOH
 production.

4. B. Yang, X. Zhang, B. Shrimant, T. Kulkarni, R. Kumar, C.G. Arges, Selective phosphate
 removal with manganese oxide composite anion exchange membranes in membrane capacitive
 deionization, Chemical Engineering Journal, 2024, 495,
   153468, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cej.2024.153468

 The research develops manganese oxide (Mn₂O₃) composite anion exchange membranes for
 membrane capacitive deionization (MCDI) to selectively remove phosphate ions from water,
 addressing eutrophication risks. The optimized system achieves ≥31.8% phosphate removal and
 1.1 selectivity over chloride, with stable performance over 30 cycles. Computational analysis
 reveals strong phosphate affinity to Mn₂O₃, offering a promising strategy for targeted ion removal
 in wastewater treatment.

5. T. Kulkarni, B. Yang, X. Zhang, R. Kumar, C.G. Arges, ACS Spotlight: Bipolar membranes for
 electrochemical energy conversion, chemical manufacturing, and separations, ACS Applied
 Energy Materials, 2024, 7, 11361, Invited for the Early Career
   Forum, https://doi.org/10.1021/acsaem.3c03260

 This review highlights the role of bipolar membranes (BPMs) in advancing electrochemical
 platforms for sustainable energy conversion, chemical manufacturing, and separations. BPMs
 enable in situ pH control, improving efficiency in systems like water electrolyzers, fuel cells,

 durability, and modeling efforts, while also addressing challenges compared to conventional
 monopolar membranes.

6. X. Zhang, C.G. Arges, R. Kumar, Computational investigations of the water structure at α-
 Al2O3(0001)-water interfaces, Journal of Physical Chemistry C, 2023, 127,
   15600, https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jpcc.3c03243

 The α-Al₂O₃(0001)–water interface was analyzed using ab initio molecular dynamics (AIMD)

 Differences in water behavior were observed between Al-terminated and O-terminated alumina

 of water–oxide interfaces through combined simulation and spectroscopic analysis.

8. T. Kulkarni, A. Muhammad I Al Dhamen, and C.G. Arges, Bipolar membrane capacitive
 deionization for pH-assisted ionic separations, ACS ES&T Engineering, 2023, 3,
   2171, https://doi.org/10.1021/acsestengg.3c00041

 This work demonstrates bipolar membrane capacitive deionization (BPM-CDI/MCDI) as an
 effective method for selective ionic separations relevant to critical minerals and circular economy
 applications. Guided by Pourbaix diagrams and acid–base equilibria, BPM-MCDI enables
 controlled generation of acidic or alkaline streams, with pH most sensitive to cell voltage. The
 system achieved highly selective separations, including over sixfold higher removal of Cu²⁺ than
 Na⁺, more than 40% greater copper removal than conventional MCDI, and fivefold higher
 recovery of itaconic acid from brine mixtures.

9. M.V. Ramos-Garcés, D.I. Senadheera, K. Arunagiri, P.P. Angelopoulou, G. Sakellariou, K. Li,
 B.D. Vogt, R. Kumar, and C.G. Arges, Ion transport on self-assembled block copolymer
 electrolytes with different side chain chemistries, Materials Advances, 2023, 4,
   965, https://doi.org/10.1039/D2MA00919F

 Ion-exchange membranes are critical to electrochemical systems, where their structure strongly
 affects ionic conductivity and efficiency. This study examines microphase-separated block
 copolymer electrolytes with different ionic-domain side chains (alkyl, alkoxy, and zwitterionic).
 Despite similar ion-exchange capacities and water uptake, membranes with hydrophobic alkyl
 side chains exhibit an order-of-magnitude higher ionic conductivity. Simulations show this arises
 from larger, better-connected water clusters that enhance ion transport, while more hydrophilic
 side chains bind water and reduce effective conductivity.

10. M.L. Jordan, G. Kokoszka, H.K. Gollage Dona, D.I. Senadheera, R. Kumar, Y.J. Lin, and C.G.
 Arges, Integrated ion-exchange membrane resin wafer assemblies for aromatic organic acid
 separations using electrodeionization, ACS Sustainable Chemistry & Engineering, 2023, 11,
    945, https://doi.org/10.1021/acssuschemeng.2c05255

 Aromatic acids like p-coumaric acid are important chemical intermediates, but their purification
 from fermentation broths is difficult using conventional electrodeionization (EDI) due to fouling
 and irreversible binding. This study introduces a new laminated membrane wafer assembly
 (MWA) for EDI that greatly improves p-coumaric acid recovery. Compared with state-of-the-art
 resin wafer EDI modules, the new MWA achieves a sevenfold increase in capture while reducing
 energy consumption by 70%. The improved performance is attributed to lower interfacial
 transport resistance and the use of imidazolium-based anion exchange membranes and ionomer
 binders, which enhance p-coumarate transport via π–π interactions. Overall, the MWA design
 reduces membrane usage, improves efficiency, and offers potential capital cost savings.

11. M.A. Alkhadra, M.L. Jordan, H.Tian, C.G. Arges, and M.Z. Bazant, Selective and chemical-free
 removal of toxic heavy metal cations from water using shock ion extraction, Environmental
    Science & Technology, 2022, 56, 14091, https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.2c05042

 Electrochemical methods offer promising capabilities for water purification. This study
 introduces shock ion extraction (shock IX), a hybrid, chemical-free process that combines shock
 electrodialysis with an ion exchange resin wafer to selectively remove toxic heavy metals from
 water. Compared to ion exchange alone, shock IX provides higher and longer-lasting ion removal,
 improved multivalent ion selectivity, lower energy use, and better hydrodynamics and scalability.

12. E.J. Park, C.G. Arges, H. Xu, and Y.S. Kim, Membrane Strategies for Water Electrolysis, ACS
    Energy Letters, 2022, 7, 3447–3457, https://doi.org/10.1021/acsenergylett.2c01609

 Hydrogen is a promising clean energy carrier, with green hydrogen from renewable-powered
 water electrolysis playing a key role in decarbonizing hard-to-abate sectors. Ion-exchange
 membranes are critical to efficient electrolysis, and while perfluorosulfonic acid membranes
 dominate today, alternative acidic, alkaline, and bipolar membranes are being explored to reduce
 cost and improve durability. This work reviews these membrane types and outlines future
 research directions for advancing membranes in green hydrogen production.
