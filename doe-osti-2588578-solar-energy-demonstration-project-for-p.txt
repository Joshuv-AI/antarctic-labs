 In 2022, Worthen Library adopted a 5-year Strategic Plan which includes a goal
to reduce reliance on fossil fuel and reduce operating expenses. This goal is aligned
with: the revised South Hero VT Town Plan which calls for reduction of reliance on
non-renewable energy; Vermont’s goals to reduce energy consumption overall and
increase use of renewable energy sources.
 Adding solar panels to the roof of the Library will help meet this goal and provide
a demonstration of how renewable energy can help public libraries achieve their
mission.This grant will be used to purchase and install solar panels with the goal of
covering 100% of the cost of annual electric usage. Panels were mounted on the library
roof as well as the roof of an adjacent building. Power generated by the library roof
panels is now tied directly to the library electric bill. Power generated on the neighboring
roof will be sent to the Vermont Electric Coop, which will assign them first to the library
with any overage credited to the building owners.
 The solar panels are projected to produce over 100% of the library’s kilowatt
usage in a typical year for electrical heating, lighting and other wiring. Using solar power
will save the Library several thousands of dollars in operating costs each year.
 A monitor installed in the library will provide real time information on power
generation to assist with patron education on solar power. The data collected through
programming will be available to demonstrate to the public, and other Vermont libraries,
the cost-effectiveness of solar power.
 The panels are located on the south-facing roof of the Library, which is easily
visible from US Rt 2, a scenic byway and the major thoroughfare through Grand Isle
County. As a result, they provide a passive demonstration of the value of renewable
energy to the thousands of people who pass by each day to and from work or play.
Property owners and businesses can then gain information through Library
programming about renewable energy, which is expected to spur installations as our
nation moves toward more sustainable energy sources.

Project Objectives:

A. Project Objectives

 In this project, the South Hero Library Foundation purchased and installed a 39.95 kW
DC roof-mounted solar array on the south-facing roof of the Worthen Library as well as on the
south-facing roof of the neighboring 54 Community Lane building. This 94 panel array has a
projected output of 39,950 kWh of power which will cover the Library’s annual power usage for
heat and lighting. Any power overage will be distributed to other local organizations as
designated by the Library. Library programming and added resources will be available to all
patrons.

The foundation’s goals are to:
 1.​ Reduce reliance on fossil fuels for heat and power
 2.​ Save taxpayer dollars earmarked for operations for the cost of heat and electric usage
 through the use of solar energy
 3.​ Serve as an example for other Vermont public libraries and the public of the value of
 renewable energy for the environment as well as budget savings.

 In addition to these goals, another outcome of this project will be programming and
resources available for patrons; savings on operating costs will be reinvested into these
resources to encourage more investment by homeowners and businesses in renewable energy.

 The Library will incorporate educational programming to complement the solar array
installation, with the goal of educating patrons of all ages about the benefits of renewable
resources, and solar energy in particular. A solar monitoring station inside the Library will allow
students and other users to calculate and chart energy savings. The data collected through
programming will be available to demonstrate the cost-effectiveness of solar power to the
public and other Vermont libraries.

Significant Accomplishments and Conclusions:

​ The solar project has successfully met all key milestones outlined in our plan. The Library
Trustees selected a vendor after a thorough evaluation process, ensuring that the chosen
company aligned with the project's goals and financial framework. This decision marked a
critical step in moving forward with the installation.

​​ Following vendor selection, we recruited a team of qualified volunteers to assist with
project planning. These individuals brought valuable expertise in engineering, solar energy, and
project management, allowing for an efficient and well-coordinated effort. Their contributions
helped streamline the fundraising process, ensure compliance with legal and technical
requirements, and maintain clear communication with stakeholders.

​ Our second set of solar panels was installed on the building next door at 54 Community
Way, which houses Wally’s Bagels, the South Hero Land Trust, and the Community Health
Center. Securing approval for the use of this roof space presented an initial challenge, as the
Community Health Center was hesitant to participate in the project. Their concerns primarily
revolved around potential impacts on their facility, operations, and patient care.

 Page 9 of 12

​ To address these concerns, we engaged in extensive discussions and negotiations with the
Community Health Center leadership. We provided detailed information on the benefits of the
solar installation, including its contribution to sustainability and long-term energy savings for
the community. Additionally, we reassured stakeholders that the installation process would be
carefully managed to minimize disruptions. After thorough deliberation, we successfully
reached an agreement that allowed the project to move forward.

​ Another significant hurdle involved scheduling the installation. Since the Community Health
Center operates on the top floor and provides essential healthcare services, they were
concerned that construction noise and activity could interfere with patient appointments and
overall well-being. Recognizing the importance of maintaining a quiet and stable environment,
we worked closely with both the solar vendor and the health center staff to develop a
construction timeline that would mitigate disruptions. By carefully coordinating work hours,
sequencing tasks efficiently, and maintaining clear communication throughout the process, we
ensured that the installation proceeded smoothly while respecting the needs of patients and
staff.

​ Through collaboration, patience, and proactive problem-solving, we successfully completed
the installation of solar panels on the 54 Community Way building, marking another important
step toward our renewable energy goals.

​​ The final phase of the project—installation, inspections, and interconnection—was
successfully completed. The solar panels were installed according to the proposed design,
connected to the electrical grid, and subjected to thorough testing to ensure optimal
functionality. Final inspections confirmed that all systems met regulatory standards, and the
interconnection with the Vermont Electric Cooperative was established without issue.

​ With all milestones achieved, the solar project stands as a fully operational and successful
initiative, supporting the Library’s commitment to sustainability and energy efficiency.
