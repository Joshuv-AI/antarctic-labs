Neoadjuvant chemotherapy and bevacizumab for colorectal peritoneal metastases: clinical and translational outcomes of the phase II BEV-IP trial
Authors: Daryl Kai Ann Chia, Jesse Demuytere, Anne Hoorens, Karen De Meuleneir, Sarah Cosyns, Kurt Van der Speeten, Gabriel Liberale, Franky Vansteenkiste, Wouter Willaert, Wim Ceelen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Bevacizumab-based neoadjuvant therapy is safe before CRS/HIPEC.•Median overall survival is 40.6 months after multimodal therapy.•High nuclear HIF1A expression predicts improved survival.•Peritoneal HbEGF levels independently predict poor survival.Keywords: Colorectal peritoneal metastases, Bevacizumab, Neoadjuvant therapy, Cytoreductive surgery, HIPEC

Introduction

Colorectal cancer peritoneal metastases (CRPM) occur as synchronous disease in ∼5% and as metachronous relapse in up to 19% of patients., [[1], [2], [3]] Of note, patients with CRPM have a significantly worse prognosis compared to other isolated sites of colorectal cancer metastasis., [4,5], Cytoreductive surgery (CRS) is the mainstay of treatment in selected patients, while the optimal regimen of hyperthermic intraperitoneal chemotherapy (HIPEC) remains debated following the PRODIGE 7 trial., [[6], [7], [8]] Neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NACT) has been reported to be feasible, safe and to improve overall survival (OS) in CRPM, but has not been evaluated in a phase III randomized controlled trial., [[9], [10], [11], [12]]

Vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) has been implicated in tumor-related angiogenesis and the formation of malignant ascites and may be a potential therapeutic target., [13] The combination of chemotherapy with anti-VEGF therapy Bevacizumab (BEV, Avastin®, Genentech/Roche), a humanized monoclonal antibody, has shown improved OS in multiple clinical trials – such as AVF 2107 and E3200 – evaluating metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC) and may augment NACT for CRPM prior to CRS/HIPEC., [[14], [15], [16]] Preclinical and translational studies have demonstrated that BEV inhibits VEGF-mediated angiogenesis, enhances intraperitoneal (IP) chemotherapy delivery by lowering tumor interstitial fluid pressure and decreases IP VEGF concentration., [[17], [18], [19], [20], [21], [22]] However, anti-angiogenic therapy also impedes wound healing and increases the risk of complications in patients undergoing major surgery., [23,24], Specific to CRS/HIPEC, there is limited and conflicting evidence regarding the safety of BEV with NACT, with Eveno et al. reporting that pre-operative BEV increased the likelihood of major morbidity (OR 2.28, 95% CI 1.05 – 4.95)., [25,26],

Hence, the primary aim of the prospective phase II BEV-IP trial was to evaluate the rate of major surgical morbidity when BEV was added to neoadjuvant systemic chemotherapy in a prospective cohort of CRPM patients planned for CRS/HIPEC. Secondary aims included safety and tolerability of perioperative BEV, survival outcomes and evaluation of biomarkers that could predict response to BEV.

Methods

Patient recruitment

BEV-IP is a single arm, open label, prospective investigator-initiated phase II trial registered with ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT02399410) with a published trial protocol approved by the Ethical Committee of Ghent University Hospital 2014/1042., [27] Briefly, eligible patients were >18 years old with good performance status, histologically proven adenocarcinoma of the colon or rectum with peritoneal metastases amenable to complete or near complete macroscopic cytoreduction (CC score 0-1) at the time of surgery, peritoneal carcinomatosis index (PCI) <25 and the absence of systemic disease (except small superficial liver metastases suitable for simple excision). Patients were recommended to receive at least 3 months of NACT with BEV (Avastin® 5mg/kg intravenous) added to FOLFOX (5-fluorouracil [5FU], folinic acid and oxaliplatin) or FOLFIRI (5FU, folinic acid, irinotecan) although the final regimen was determined by the primary oncologist. Recruitment during the COVID-19 pandemic was limited due to healthcare service disruptions.

Sample size calculation

Based on published literature, 30-day postoperative morbidity and mortality related to CRS/HIPEC following NACT (without BEV) was 23 – 31% and 3 – 4% respectively., [27]

We thus assumed an expected event rate of 27% for morbidity and 3.5% for mortality. Using binomial estimation methods, a sample size of 45 patients would provide 95% confidence intervals of ∼ 14 – 40% for morbidity and 0.8 – 14% for mortality. To accommodate an anticipated dropout/attrition rate of up to 33%, a total of 60 patients were enrolled.

Response assessment and surgical evaluation

Clinical assessment, serum carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) and cross-sectional imaging by computed tomography (CT) of the chest and abdomen or 18-fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography (PET) scans were performed at baseline, after 3 cycles (∼6 weeks) of treatment and within 6 weeks of completing NACT. An interval of 6 weeks from BEV to CRS/HIPEC was recommended and it was permissible to omit the final cycle of BEV if surgery was planned. Measurable peritoneal lesions were graded according to the RECIST 1.1. criteria to assess response. CRS/HIPEC was performed in patients with a PCI <25 and no extensive small bowel involvement. The standard components of CRS included omentectomy, peritonectomy of involved surfaces, resection of the primary tumor (if present) and additional visceral resection(s) as required to accomplish completeness of cytoreduction score 0 (CC-0). The preferred HIPEC regimen was oxaliplatin 200 – 460mg/m2 IP was delivered as per institution protocol as this study pre-dated the publication of the results of the PRODIGE-7 trial., [8] While majority of patients received 90 min of therapy, the duration was shortened to 30 min in frail patients or those with cardiac or renal impairment.

Surgical morbidity, treatment-related toxicity & reporting procedure

Surgical morbidity was graded using the Clavien-Dindo classification and major morbidity was defined as CD grade ≥3B [25,26,28],. Chemotherapy associated toxicity was assessed using the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE), Version 4.0. Severe adverse events (SAEs) defined as CTCAE grade 3 were reported as per study protocol.

Post-surgical treatment and follow-up

Adjuvant treatment with BEV was ideally started within 6-8 weeks after surgery. Clinical review was planned at 3, 6, 9, 12, 18, and 24 months after surgery. Patients with progressive disease were managed with second or third line systemic treatment, locoregional treatment, surgical treatment, or palliative treatment as clinically indicated and after multidisciplinary discussion. Patient-reported Quality of Life (QOL) outcomes were assessed in the same setting as clinical reviews by Short Form Health Survey (SF-36) and European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer (EORTC) Quality of Life questionnaire (QLQ-C30).

Translational endpoints

Routine histology hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) and immunohistochemical (IHC) analysis was performed on formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue blocks from resected specimens. Given the association of hypoxia-inducible factor 1A (HIF1A) overexpression with adverse outcomes in colorectal cancer, HIF1A expression was evaluated in all available PM tumor samples after performing appropriate positive controls (Supplemental Figure 1A)., [29,30], The anti-HIF1A mouse monoclonal antibody (NB100–296, Novus Biologicals: 1/100, incubation 24 min) was used; visualization was achieved with the Ultraview Universal DAB Detection Kit (Ventana Medical Systems, Roche Diagnostics). Heat-induced epitope retrieval was performed with Cell Conditioning 1 (Ventana Medical Systems, Roche Diagnostics), 52 min at 95 °C. HIF1A staining was performed on 3-µm-thick sections via an automatic immunostainer (BenchMark Ultra, Ventana Medical Systems). Given that HIF1A translocates to the nucleus under hypoxic conditions, nuclear localisation was considered for scoring of HIF1A, similar to published literature., [31,32], (Supplemental Figure 1B) Furthermore, evaluation of cytoplasmic HIF-1a staining was not feasible due to background staining in intracellular and extracellular mucin. (Supplemental Figure 1C) A semi-quantitative immune-reactive score (IRS; range 0-12) was used, taking the product of an intensity score (0: none, 1: weak, 2: medium, 3: strong) and proportion score (0: 0%, 1: 1-10%, 2: 11-50%, 3: 51-80%, 4: 81-100%) as shown in Supplemental Table 1., [[33], [34], [35]] Peritoneal lavage was performed with 100 ml of saline before CRS for cytokine profiling. The lavage was snap-frozen at −80 degrees Celsius and analyzed using a 17-plex human angiogenesis cytokine array (Eve technologies, Supplemental Table 2).

Statistical analysis & imputation methods

Postoperative morbidity and mortality were reported as proportions with 95% confidence intervals using Wilson score method. Rates of operable cases, NACT completion, and serious adverse events (SAEs) were summarized descriptively. Overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) were defined from the time of recruitment to death from any cause and to disease progression or death, respectively and estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method. Log-rank tests were used for group comparisons. Hazard ratios (HRs) were estimated with Cox proportional hazards models. Multivariable regression was performed via backward stepwise selection guided by Akaike Information Criterion (AIC), sequentially removing variables that contributed least to model fit, yielding the most parsimonious model with the lowest AIC value., [[36], [37], [38], [39]] Diagnostics were performed on the final multivariable Cox model, with emphasis on HIF1A score as the primary biomarker of interest. Proportional hazards were assessed using Schoenfeld residuals, functional form via Martingale residuals for functional form (with HIF1A score as continuous variable), outlier detection via deviance residuals and influential observations via DFBETAs). Paired nonparametric comparisons were performed using the Wilcoxon matched-pairs signed rank test. Missing clinical and cytokine data were imputed using the missRanger R package, which applies random forest–based multiple imputation with predictive mean matching (pmm.k = 3, maxiter = 10, seed = 123). This method preserves data structure/distribution and accounts for nonlinearities and interactions particularly in datasets with mixed variable types., [40] Variables with >40% missingness or suspected non-random patterns were excluded. Missing data structure was visualised using UpSet plots and heatmaps. (Supplemental figure 2A,B) [41,42], Cut-point analysis for continuous variables was performed using the survminer R package to identify optimal threshold via maximally selected standardized log-rank statistics, with minimum subgroup proportion threshold of 0.1 (minprop = 0.1) to avoid extreme splits. All analyses were conducted using SPSS Statistics v28.0 (IBM), GraphPad Prism v9.1.1 (GraphPad Software), and RStudio v2022.02.2 + 485.

Results

Trial recruitment and baseline characteristics

From 2015 – 2021, 60 patients who fulfilled inclusion criteria were recruited from 4 centers in Belgium; 90% (54/60) of recruited patients received at least 1 dose of BEV and 94% of these patients (51/54) underwent CRS/HIPEC (Fig. 1). Baseline characteristics of 54 patients receiving BEV are presented in Table 1. Of note, 81.5% (44/54) of patients received at least 4 cycles of BEV which constituted complete NACT, with 14 SAEs occurring in 17.3% (9/54) and 3.7% (2/54) suffering CTCAE grade 5 SAEs. (Supplemental Table 3) From 54 patients who received ≥ 1 cycle of NACT BEV, 1.9% (1/54) experienced progression of disease precluding CRS.

Treatment response to NACT BEV and clinical outcomes following CRS/HIPEC

Compared to baseline levels at diagnosis, there was a modest albeit significant decrease in serum CEA levels after NACT (5.2ug/l to 4.58ug/l, p = 0.0058). (Fig. 2A) After 6 cycles of NACT, objective response was seen in 55.0% on either PET or CT scans. (Fig. 2B) The 90-day major morbidity and mortality rates were respectively 29.4% (95% CI 18.7 – 43.0%) and 3.9% (95% CI 1.1 – 13.2%). (Table 2)[27] Anastomotic leaks were reported in 7 patients (5 gastrointestinal and 2 ureteric anastomoses) but did not appear to be related to number of cycles of NACT BEV, as median cycles of BEV received were similar in both leak and no-leak groups (leak vs. no leak, median cycles of BEV, 4(1-7) vs. 4(4-6), p = 0.883). Two patients developed enteric and urinary fistulae following anastomotic leaks (3.9%) and were managed conservatively. The detailed major surgical complications are reported in Supplemental Table 4.

After surgery, 70.6% (36/51) of patients received a median (IQR) of 5(3– 6) cycles of chemotherapy and 2 (0 – 6) cycles of BEV. A total of 12 SAEs occurred in 17% (8/47) of patients on follow up. By chemotherapy regimen, median overall survival was 56.4 months (95% CI, 29.2 to not reached) with FOLFIRI and 36.0 months (95% CI, 23.4 to not reached) with FOLFOX (log-rank p = 0.510). In sidedness-specific subgroup analyses, FOLFIRI was not associated with a statistically significant difference in overall survival compared with FOLFOX in either subgroup. The hazard ratio for overall survival with FOLFIRI versus FOLFOX was 1.98 (95% CI, 0.52 to 7.55; p = 0.32) in left-sided tumours and 0.38 (95% CI, 0.12 to 1.20; p = 0.10) in right-sided tumours.

QOL outcomes

Quality of life (QOL) outcomes, assessed using both SF-36 and EORTC QLQ-C30 instruments, demonstrated a predictable decline in multiple functional domains around the time of CRS/HIPEC. This was most pronounced in physical function, role limitation, social functioning, and pain (SF-36), as well as physical and role functioning (QLQ-C30) around the time of CRS (before adjuvant chemotherapy). Importantly, longitudinal assessment revealed that QOL scores across most domains gradually improved during adjuvant therapy, returning to baseline levels by its completion. Paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests comparing baseline and post-adjuvant timepoints showed no statistically significant differences in any domain, indicating that despite a transient and expected perioperative decline, overall QOL returned to baseline levels over time (Supplemental Figures 4-6).

Integrative Cox modelling of overall survival incorporating clinical variables and pathway-driven biomarkers

Univariate Cox regression was performed on cytokine data from blood and peritoneal lavage using both available-cases and imputed datasets after evaluating the degree of missingness. (Supplemental Figure 2A,B and Supplemental Tables 6,7). From the latter, elevated Angiopoietin-2, Endoglin, HbEGF and PLGF from peritoneal lavage were significantly associated with modest increase in mortality. (Supplemental Table 7).

Higher nuclear HIF1A expression, assessed using a semiquantitative IRS, was associated with improved overall survival (HR 0.833, 95% CI: 0.696–0.997, p = 0.046). Cut-point analysis identified an optimal IRS threshold of 4 to dichotomise patients with PM demonstrating high and low HIF1A staining (Fig. 4A,B). Patients with high HIF1A expression (>4) demonstrated a trend toward improved survival compared to those with low expression (≤4) on univariate analysis (HR 0.39, 95% CI: 0.15–1.06, p = 0.066).

An integrative multivariable Cox regression model was developed using stepwise backward elimination (AIC) to evaluate the prognostic relevance of HIF1A expression, elevated peritoneal cytokines, and key clinical variables including age, synchronous presentation, number of NACT–bevacizumab cycles, major morbidity, PCI score, change in CEA, BRAF mutation status, and completeness of cytoreduction. This composite approach identified PCI score, BRAF mutation, peritoneal HbEGF levels, and high HIF1A expression as independent predictors of overall survival (Table 3). Details of patients with BRAF mutation are presented in Supplemental Table 8. Notably, patients with peritoneal metastases exhibiting strong HIF1A staining demonstrated significantly improved OS after multivariable adjustment (HR 0.17, 95% CI: 0.04–0.65,p = 0.010; Fig. 4C). The median OS (95%) in patients with low vs. high HIF1A expression was 32.2 months (95% CI 18.4 – 72.7) and 65.7 months (95% CI 29.2 – NA) respectively. The final Cox model with HIF1A as a covariate satisfied all key assumptions (proportional hazards, functional form and influence), supporting the robustness of the survival inference. Discussion

CRPM is a biologically an d clinically distinct subset of mCRC associated with poor prognosis., [4,5], Despite aggressive treatment with CRS/HIPEC, the risk of recurrence and disease-related mortality remains substantial., [[6], [7], [8]] Angiogenesis plays a central role in mCRC, providing a compelling biological rationale for incorporating anti-angiogenic drugs like BEV to NACT for CRPM., [43,44], While retrospective studies have reported median OS ranging from 31.5 to 49.0 months with NACT and BEV, its safety, tolerability and efficacy have yet to be systematically evaluated in prospective trials., [16,26], In this phase II trial, we report the 90-day major morbidity and mortality rate to be 29.4% (95% CI 18.7 – 43.0%) and 3.9% (95% CI 1.1 – 13.2%), consistent with prior studies reporting major morbidity rates of 17.6 – 36.0% and morbidity rates of 0 – 6.2%., [11,16,25,26,45], Following a median (IQR) of 6(6 −7) cycles of systemic chemotherapy and 4(4 −6) cycles of BEV, 94% of patients proceeded to CRS/HIPEC. The median OS was 40.6 (32.4 – 48.8) months in all patients and 65.7 months (95% CI 38.7 – 65.7) in patients with complete macroscopic resection (CC-0). QOL indices notably returned to baseline after completion of treatment. These findings were consistent with results from the recent CAIRO6 trial which reported a median OS of 44 months (overall cohort) and 54 months (CC-0), respectively., [45] Taken together, results from this pragmatic phase II trial provide preliminary evidence that NACT with BEV is safe and well-tolerated in CRPM with promising oncologic outcomes and recovery of QOL. While there was possible differential treatment effect with a trend toward improved OS in patients with right-sided tumors receiving NACT FOLFIRI, the estimates were imprecise and did not reach statistical significance and should be interpreted with caution.

To investigate the possible mechanistic underpinnings of efficacy conferred by BEV, we conducted IHC analysis on HIF1A, a key regulator of oxygen homeostasis., [46] In the presence of hypoxia, HIF1A dimerizes with HIF1B to translocate into the nucleus, activating multiple target genes including VEGF., [47,48], Elevated HIF1A expression has been associated with aggressive tumor phenotype and poor prognosis in multiple cancer types including breast, lung, esophageal and hepatocellular carcinomas., [39,[49], [50], [51]], In CRC, a meta-analysis of 2984 CRC patients from 23 studies by Chen et al. reported HIF1A overexpression was significantly associated with increased mortality (HR 2.06, 95% CI 1.55 – 27.4)., [52] In contrast, results from the BEVIP study demonstrated that nuclear overexpression of HIF1A was an independent predictor for improved OS following NACT with BEV (HR 0.17, 95% CI 0.04 – 0.65) after adjusting for key clinical and translational variables. We postulate that nuclear HIF1A overexpression in CRPM patients treated with BEV may arise from disrupted VEGF-mediated negative feedback or hypoxia secondary to effective VEGF therapy in contrast to naïve tumors where HIF1A overexpression may reflect an aggressive intrinsic biology and increased downstream signaling. While data on HIF1A in BEV-treated populations is limited, findings by Mendes et al. suggest that the prognostic significance of HIF1A may be shaped by the immune microenvironment, with elevated HIF1A expression in peritumoral inflammatory cells associated with poor OS, and similarly elevated expression in intratumoral lymphoid cells linked to better OS – underscoring that the role of HIF1A in tumor progression is highly context-dependent., [53] Preclinical studies using human colorectal cancer cell lines have also reported HIF1A upregulation following exposure to BEV, supporting the hypothesis that VEGF inhibition may induce compensatory HIF1A overexpression., [54] To our knowledge, this is the first report that nuclear HIF1A may be a surrogate of target engagement and potential biomarker of response to BEV rather than tumor aggressiveness. Using maximally selected rank statistics (survminer package, R) identified an IRS threshold of 4 as the optimal discriminator for OS and warrants validation in larger studies.

Peritoneal lavage offers direct access to the tumor microenvironment and enables the characterization of locoregional cytokine levels indicating either therapeutic response or adaptive resistance to BEV. In this study, 17-plex cytokine profiling of peritoneal lavage identified elevated HbEGF as an independent predictor for poor OS after multivariable adjustment for key variables. HbEGF, a member of the epidermal growth factor superfamily of ligands, is capable of inducing VEGF-independent angiogenesis through activation of PI3K/MAPK/eNOS signaling pathways., [55] While HbEGF has been implicated in tumor progression in preclinical models of CRC, its clinical relevance has not been investigated., [56] The exploratory signals involving both HIF1A and HbEGF highlight mechanistically plausible pathways linked to BEV’s mode of action and represent promising candidate biomarkers deserving further validation in larger translational studies. The increasing use of diagnostic laparoscopic in assessing resectability prior to CRS/HIPEC may be an unprecedented opportunity to sample the peritoneal compartment in CRPM patients on BEV, potentially enabling biomarker-guided personalized therapy. Patients harboring molecular surrogates of poor response may benefit from treatment de-escalation or the addition of investigational agents targeting HbEGF or HIF1A within the context of a clinical trial., [[57], [58], [59]]

To date, this study represents one of the largest prospective series of CRPM patients receiving NACT and BEV prior to CRS/HIPEC. This cohort offers a realistic reflection of the regimen’s safety, tolerability and functional recovery regimen over a substantial follow-up period. Although the sample size was calculated based on the stipulated primary endpoint, exploratory translational analyses introduced high-dimensional variables, increasing the risk of overfitting and introducing limitations of multiple testing. To mitigate this, we applied backward stepwise regression guided by AIC, an objective and data-driven approach that penalizes and reduces model complexity in favor of parsimony without relying on arbitrary significance thresholds or subjective cherry-picking by investigators., [38,60], In addition, we acknowledge that the translational analysis reflect a single time point and future longitudinal validation through serial sampling would be essential. Moreover, the potential survival benefit from biomarker-guided patient selection warrants investigation in a randomized controlled setting. Despite these limitations, our modelling strategy was intentionally conservative, designed to integrate clinical and translational data robustly and concurrently minimizing bias in variable selection. While the median OS of the entire cohort was comparable to published studies evaluating NACT and CRS/HIPEC, the improved OS in the subgroup of patients overexpressing HIF1A supports a precision oncology approach for CRPM, where biomarkers may stratify therapeutic responses or even guide treatment options.

In conclusion, the BEV-IP study supports the real-world feasibility of incorporating BEV to NACT in patients with CRPM and highlights mechanistically aligned positive translational signals. The identification of HIF1A and HbEGF as prognostic markers is biologically coherent with BEV’s mode of action and provides a strong rationale for future precision oncology trials targeting angiogenesis in CRPM.
