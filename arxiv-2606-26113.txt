On triviality and scalar curvature estimation of gradient h h -almost Yamabe solitons
Absos Ali Shaikh ∗1 , Ananya Tripathi 2

Abstract
. In this study we have explored gradient h h -almost Yamabe solitons on both compact and complete non-compact Riemannian manifolds. We have established several sufficient conditions for the triviality of such solitons with respect to integral inequalities involving the scalar curvature and the soliton function. In this regard, we have acquired scalar curvature estimation under certain L 2 L^{2} -integrability conditions, and defined signal of the function h h . Our results extend and refine former works on almost and h h -almost Yamabe solitons, and characterize the geometric structures of generalized Yamabe solitons.

1.
Introduction

The concept of Yamabe flow was originated by Hamilton
[
7
,
8
]
for resolving the Yamabe conjecture. The evolution equation of Yamabe flow on a Riemannian manifold is as follows:

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial g(t)}{\partial t}=-R_{g(t)}g(t),\\
g(0)=g_{0},\end{cases}

(1)

where
g(t)
is a one-parameter family of metrics on the manifold and
R_{g(t)}
denotes the scalar curvature corresponding to
g(t)
.

A natural generalization of self-similar solutions to the Yamabe flow leads to the notion of Yamabe solitons. A complete connected Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
,
n\geq 2
, is termed as a Yamabe soliton if it possesses a smooth vector field
X
satisfying

\frac{1}{2}\pounds_{X}g=Rg-\lambda g,

(2)

for some constant
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. Here,
\pounds_{X}
is the Lie derivative in the direction of the vector field
X
and
R
is the scalar curvature corresponding to
g
.
The Yamabe soliton is called a gradient Yamabe soliton if
X=\nabla f
, where
f:M^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is some smooth scalar field, and in this case the equation (
2
) can be rewritten as

\nabla^{2}f=Rg-\lambda g,

(3)

where
\nabla^{2}f
is the Hessian of the potential function
f
.

Barbosa and Riberio
[
1
]
initiated the study of almost Yamabe soliton by replacing the soliton constant
\lambda
to a smooth scalar field on
M^{n}
. Later in 2021, Zeng
[
16
]
extended this conception to
h
-almost Yamabe soliton, providing a broader framework by incorporating a smooth scalar field
h
. An
h
-almost Yamabe soliton, denoted by
(M^{n},g,X,h,\lambda)
is a complete Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
, equipped with a smooth vector field
X
, and satisfying the equation

\frac{h}{2}\pounds_{X}g=Rg-\lambda g,

(4)

for some smooth scalar fields
h
and
\lambda
. The function
h:M^{n}\to R
is said to have defined signal if, either
h>0
, or
h<0
. If
\lambda
is constant then the soliton is termed as
h
-Yamabe soliton.
When
\pounds_{X}g=\pounds_{\nabla f}g
, for any smooth scalar field
f
on
M^{n}
, the fundamental equation (
4
) takes the form

h\nabla^{2}f=Rg-\lambda g,

(5)

and in this instance, the soliton is termed as the
h
-almost gradient Yamabe soliton with potential function
f
, and is denoted by
(M^{n},g,f,h,\lambda)
. In case the potential field
X
is Killing or the potential function
f
is constant, the soliton is considered to be trivial, and non-trivial otherwise. It should be mentioned that an
h
-almost Yamabe soliton is expanding, shrinking, or steady according as
\lambda<0
,
\lambda>0
, or
\lambda=0
on
M^{n}
, respectively, and that is undefined if
\lambda
has no specific sign.

In terms of local coordinates equation (
5
) can be expressed as

h\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}f=Rg_{ij}-\lambda g_{ij}.

(6)

We have derived some examples of
h
-almost gradient Yamabe soliton, including the case of expanding and shrinking.

Example 1.1
.

Let us consider the warped product manifold

M^{n}=I\times{}_{\cosh t}\mathbb{H}^{(n-1)}
with metric
g=dt^{2}+\cosh^{2}tg_{0}
, where
I=(0,\infty)
, and
(\mathbb{H}^{(n-1)},g_{0})
is the
(n-1)
-dimensional hyperbolic space. Then, we can show that
Ric_{g}=-(n-1)g
, and hence the scalar curvature of
(M^{n},g)
is given by,
R=-n(n-1)
.

Now, let us define a smooth scalar field
u:M^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
by
u(t,x)=\sinh t
. Then, we can compute

\nabla^{2}u=\sinh tg,\quad\text{i.e.,}\quad\csch t\nabla^{2}u=g

Therefore,
\csch t\nabla^{2}u=(R-\lambda))g
, where
\lambda=-(n^{2}-n+1)<0
. So,
(M^{n},g,\nabla u,\csch t,\lambda)
is a non-compact expanding
h
-almost gradient Yamabe soliton.

Similarly,
((0,\frac{\pi}{2})\times{}_{\sin t}\mathbb{S}^{(n-1)},dt^{2}+\sin^{2}tg_{0},\nabla\cos t,\frac{1}{\cos t},(n^{2}-n+1))
is an example of non-compact shrinking
h
-almost gradient Yamabe soliton. To be precise, here

\displaystyle Ric_{g}=(n-1)g\quad\text{i.e.,}\quad R=n(n-1),

and

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}\cos t=-\cos tg,\quad\text{i.e.,}\quad\frac{1}{\cos t}\nabla^{2}\cos t=-g.

Example 1.2
.

Let us consider
\mathbb{R}^{3}
equipped with standard flat metric
g=dx^{2}+dy^{2}+dz^{2}
, and let
u:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
be defined by

u(x,y,z)=a(x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2})+b(x+y+z)+c,

for some nonzero constants
a
,
b
and
c
. Then it is easy to check that
\nabla^{2}u=2ag
.

Hence,
(\mathbb{R}^{3},g,\nabla u,\frac{1}{a},-2)
is an
h
-almost gradient Yamabe soliton, where
h
is constant function.

There is a well-known result due to Chow, Lu and Ni
[
4
]
(see also
[
2
]
and
[
9
]
, for alternative proofs of this result) asserting that any compact gradient Yamabe soliton has constant scalar curvature
R=\lambda
, and hence it is trivial. For the complete non-compact case the soliton is trivial under certain additional conditions (See, for instance
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
).

Zeng
[
16
]
combined the quasi Yamabe soliton, initiated by Leandro and Pina
[
6
]
, with
h
-almost Yamabe soliton for the gradient case. As a matter of fact, any almost
m
-quasi gradient Yamabe soliton with potential function
u
is actually an
h
-almost gradient Yamabe soliton admitting potential function
e^{-\frac{u}{m}}
, and
h=-me^{\frac{u}{m}}
is of negative signal. In recent times, Cunha and Siddiqi
[
5
]
have also derived certain results on scalar curvature of such gradient (
-me^{\frac{u}{m}}
)-almost Yamabe solitons.

In this framework we have established certain conditions in respect to which triviality of the soliton occurs in both compact (without boundary) and complete non-compact cases, and have estimated the scalar curvature under suitable integral and geometric constraints. Henceforth, we have considered
h
to be of defined signal. In the context of
[
16
]
, for the case of
h<0
, these outcomes are also applicable for almost quasi gradient Yamabe soliton.

Our first outcome provides some sufficient integral conditions for the potential vector field
\nabla f
to be Killing. In the first case we have modified the integral condition imposed by Zeng
[
16
, Theorem 1.2.]
through omitting an extra term in the condition. In the remaining two cases, we have considered defined sign of the soliton function
\lambda
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any compact
h
-almost gradient Yamabe soliton possessing potential function
f
, if any one of the following integral conditions holds then the soliton is trivial:

(i)

\int_{M}Ric(\nabla f,\nabla f)d\mu\leq 0
;

(ii)

The soliton is expanding,
R\leq\lambda,
and
\int_{M}\langle\nabla f,\nabla\frac{1}{h}R\rangle d\mu\geq 0
;

(iii)

The soliton is shrinking,
R\geq\lambda,
and
\int_{M}\langle\nabla f,\nabla\frac{1}{h}R\rangle d\mu\geq 0
.

Next, we have prescribed some sufficient integral relations involving scalar curvature and soliton function to obtain triviality.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that
(M^{n},g,f,h,\lambda)
be a compact
h
-almost gradient Yamabe soliton of dimension
n(\geq 3)
. Then the soliton becomes trivial if any one of the following holds:

(i)

h>0,
and
\int_{M}\frac{1}{h}R^{2}d\mu\geq\int_{M}\frac{1}{h}\lambda^{2}d\mu
;

(ii)

h<0,
and
\int_{M}\frac{1}{h}\lambda^{2}d\mu\geq\int_{M}\frac{1}{h}R^{2}d\mu
.

Further, in the remaining two results we have observed the complete non-compact
h
-almost gradient Yamabe soliton under previous analogous conditions along with some extra
L^{2}
-regularity criteria. The following result is motivated by
[
11
]
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose
(M^{n},g,f,h,\lambda)
be a complete non-compact
h
-almost gradient Yamabe soliton such that

|\nabla f|,\frac{1}{h}R\in L^{2}(M),0\leq R\leq\lambda
, and
\langle\nabla f,\nabla\frac{1}{h}\lambda\rangle\leq 0
. Then the soliton is trivial.

Here it is needed to be mentioned that,
L^{2}(M)
stands for the space of all real valued functions defined on
M^{n}
whose square is integrable over
M^{n}
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Assume that
(M^{n},g,f,h,\lambda)
be a complete non-compact
h
-almost gradient Yamabe soliton of dimension
n

(\geq 3)
with
R,|\nabla f|,|\nabla^{2}f|\in L^{2}(M)
. Then the soliton is either scalar flat, or trivial provided any one of the following conditions holds:

(i)

h>0,\text{ and }R^{2}\geq R\lambda
;

(i)

h<0,\text{ and }R^{2}\leq R\lambda
.

Lastly, as a consequence of Theorem
1.4
we have acquired the following corollary regarding Euclidean isometry of the soliton.

Corollary 1.4.1
.

Let a non-trivial complete non-compact
n
(\geq 3)
-dimensional
h
-almost gradient Yamabe soliton, satisfying (
5
), admits non-negative scalar curvature
R
with the additional conditions
R,|\nabla f|,|\nabla^{2}f|\in L^{2}(M)
, and
\lambda=ch
, for some nonzero constant
c
. Then
R=0
, and the manifold
(M^{n},g)
is isometric with
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, if either
h>0
and the soliton is expanding, or
h<0
and
R<\lambda
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we have proved some general results on gradient
h
-almost Yamabe soliton that are required to prove our core results. Similar results had been proposed by Hwang and Yung
[
10
]
on conformal vector fields in a different approach. These results also generalize the outcomes of Zeng
[
16
]
, concerning gradient (
-me^{\frac{u}{m}}
)-almost Yamabe soliton.

First recall that the potential function
f
of an
h
-almost gradient Yamabe soliton is a concircular scalar field with characteristic function
\frac{1}{h}(R-\lambda)
. Thus, it follows from (
5
) that

\nabla_{Z}\nabla f=\frac{1}{h}(R-\lambda)Z,\text{ for any vector field }Z.

(7)

Now, taking the trace of equation
(\ref{E1})
we get

\Delta f=\frac{n}{h}(R-\lambda).

(8)

Also, since norm of Riemannian metric
g
is
n
, from (
5
) we have

|\nabla^{2}f|^{2}=\frac{n}{h^{2}}(R-\lambda)^{2}.

(9)

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f,h,\lambda)
be an
h
-almost gradient Yamabe soliton. Then the following relations hold:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Delta|\nabla f|^{2}=\frac{n}{h^{2}}(R-\lambda)^{2}+\langle\nabla f,\nabla\frac{1}{h}(R-\lambda)\rangle;

(10)

\displaystyle Ric(\nabla f)=-(n-1)\nabla\frac{1}{h}(R-\lambda);

(11)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\langle\nabla f,\nabla R\rangle+(n-1)\Delta\frac{1}{h}(R-\lambda)+\frac{1}{h}R(R-\lambda)=0.

(12)

Proof.

Using (
7
), we have

\langle\nabla|\nabla f|^{2},Z\rangle=Z|\nabla f|^{2}=Z\langle\nabla f,\nabla f\rangle=2\langle\nabla_{Z}f,\nabla f\rangle=2\langle\frac{1}{h}(R-\lambda)\nabla f,Z\rangle,

for any vector field Z, and this entails

\frac{1}{2}\nabla|\nabla f|^{2}=\frac{1}{h}(R-\lambda)\nabla f.

(13)

Now, for any vector field
U
and smooth scalar field
\phi
on
M
,

div(\phi U)=\phi div(U)+\langle\nabla\phi,U\rangle.

(14)

Therefore, taking the divergence on (
13
), and applying (
8
) we obtain (
10
).

Again, since
\nabla g=0
, taking the covariant derivative with respect to an arbitrary vector field
X
on both sides of (
5
), we get

\displaystyle X\langle\nabla_{Y}\nabla f,Z\rangle-\langle\nabla_{\nabla_{X}Y}\nabla f,Z\rangle-\langle\nabla_{Y}\nabla f,\nabla_{X}Z\rangle=(\nabla_{X}\frac{1}{h}(R-\lambda))\langle Y,Z\rangle,

and so,

\displaystyle\langle\nabla^{2}_{X,Y}\nabla f,Z\rangle=\langle\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}\nabla f,Z\rangle-\langle\nabla_{\nabla_{X}Y}\nabla f,Z\rangle=\langle(\nabla_{X}\frac{1}{h}(R-\lambda))Y,Z\rangle,

(15)

for any vector field
Y,Z
. As a consequence we have,

\nabla^{2}_{X,Y}\nabla f=(\nabla_{X}\frac{1}{h}(R-\lambda))Y.

(16)

Now, we calculate with a local frame
\{E_{i}\}
which is parallel at a point
p\in M
:

\displaystyle\langle Ric(\nabla f),X\rangle=\langle\mathcal{R}(E_{i},X)\nabla f,E_{i}\rangle=\langle(\nabla^{2}_{E_{i},X}-\nabla^{2}_{X,E_{i}})\nabla f,E_{i}\rangle

(17)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle(\nabla_{E_{i}}\frac{1}{h}(R-\lambda))X,E_{i}\rangle-\langle(\nabla_{X}\frac{1}{h}(R-\lambda))E_{i},E_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle(\nabla_{E_{i}}\frac{1}{h}(R-\lambda))E_{i},X\rangle-(\nabla_{X}\frac{1}{h}(R-\lambda))\langle E_{i},E_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\frac{1}{h}(R-\lambda),X\rangle-n\langle\nabla\frac{1}{h}(R-\lambda),X\rangle.

Therefore, we obtain (
11
).

Lastly, by second contracted Bianchi identity,
\frac{1}{2}\langle\nabla f,\nabla R\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\nabla f(R)=divRic(\nabla f)
.

Also, we know that
divRic(\nabla f)=div(Ric(\nabla f))-\langle\nabla^{2}f,Ric\rangle.

Using equation (
6
) we can write in tensorial notation that

\langle\nabla^{2}f,Ric\rangle=g^{ik}g^{jl}\frac{1}{h}(R-\lambda)g_{ij}Ric_{kl}=\frac{1}{h}R(R-\lambda)\delta^{j}_{k}\delta^{k}_{j}=\frac{1}{h}R(R-\lambda).

(18)

From the above relations and (
11
), we acquire (
12
).
∎

3.
Proof of the results

3.1.
Proof of the Theorem
1.1
.

Proof.

First, we integrate the equation (
10
) over
(M^{n},g)
so that

n\int_{M}\frac{1}{h^{2}}(R-\lambda)^{2}d\mu=-\int_{M}\langle\nabla f,\nabla\frac{1}{h}(R-\lambda)\rangle d\mu,

(19)

where
d\mu
is the volume element of
(M^{n},g)
.

Also, from (
11
) we can infer that

\int_{M}Ric(\nabla f,\nabla f)d\mu=-(n-1)\int_{M}\langle\nabla\frac{1}{h}(R-\lambda),\nabla f\rangle d\mu.

(20)

Therefore, using the preceding two equations we have

\int_{M}Ric(\nabla f,\nabla f)d\mu=n(n-1)\int_{M}\frac{1}{h^{2}}(R-\lambda)^{2}d\mu\geq 0.

(21)

In view of our assumption on Ricci curvature, we get
R=\lambda
, and so applying (
5
) we conclude that the soliton becomes trivial.

For the last two assertions, we will first apply the formula (
14
), and equation (
8
) to obtain

div(\frac{1}{h}\lambda\nabla f)=\frac{n}{h^{2}}\lambda(R-\lambda)+\langle\nabla f,\nabla\frac{1}{h}\lambda\rangle.

(22)

integrating the above equation over
M^{n}
we get

\int_{M}\langle\nabla f,\nabla\frac{1}{h}\lambda\rangle d\mu=-n\int_{M}\frac{1}{h^{2}}\lambda(R-\lambda)d\mu.

(23)

Furthermore, using (
19
) and (
23
) we can write

n\int_{M}\frac{1}{h^{2}}(R-\lambda)^{2}d\mu=-\int_{M}\langle\nabla f,\nabla\frac{1}{h}R\rangle d\mu-n\int_{M}\frac{1}{h^{2}}\lambda(R-\lambda)d\mu.

(24)

i.e.,

n\int_{M}\frac{1}{h^{2}}R(R-\lambda)d\mu=-\int_{M}\langle\nabla f,\nabla\frac{1}{h}R\rangle d\mu.\leq 0,

(25)

according to our hypothesis.

Also, in both of the cases
(ii)
and
(iii)
,
R(R-\lambda)\geq 0
, and
R\neq 0
.

Therefore, we must have
R=\lambda
.
∎

3.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Proof.

Integrating by parts over
M^{n}
, we get

\int_{M}R\Delta fd\mu=-\int_{M}\langle\nabla f,\nabla R\rangle d\mu.

(26)

Now, multiplying both sides of (
8
) by
R
we integrate it over
M^{n}
, and substitute it in the above equation. Therefore,

\int_{M}\frac{1}{h}R(R-\lambda)d\mu=-\frac{1}{n}\int_{M}\langle\nabla f,\nabla R\rangle d\mu.

(27)

On the other side, integrating (
12
) we have

\int_{M}\frac{1}{h}R(R-\lambda)d\mu=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\langle\nabla f,\nabla R\rangle d\mu.

(28)

Since
n>2
, we infer that

\int_{M}\langle\nabla f,\nabla R\rangle d\mu=\int_{M}\frac{1}{h}R(R-\lambda)d\mu=0.

(29)

Now, when
h>0
, applying (
29
) we get the following:

\displaystyle 0\leq\int_{M}\frac{1}{h}(R-\lambda)^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{M}\frac{1}{h}R(R-\lambda)d\mu-\int_{M}\frac{1}{h}R^{2}d\mu+\int_{M}\frac{1}{h}\lambda^{2}d\mu

(30)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\frac{1}{h}\lambda^{2}d\mu-\int_{M}\frac{1}{h}R^{2}d\mu.

(31)

Therefore,
R=\lambda
holds, provided the inequality of case
(i)
occurs. Hence, the soliton is trivial.

In a similar way, we can prove this for the case
(ii)
.
∎

3.3.
Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Proof.

Consider the cut-off function
\eta_{r}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{B}_{r}(p))
as in
[
3
]
satisfying,

\begin{cases}0\leq\eta_{r}\leq 1&\text{ everywhere}\\
\eta_{r}=1&\text{ in }\mathbb{B}_{\frac{r}{2}}(p)\\
supp(\eta_{r})\subset\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)\\
|\nabla\eta_{r}|^{2}\leq\frac{d}{r^{2}}\\
\Delta\eta_{r}\leq\frac{d}{r^{2}},\end{cases}

(32)

where
\mathbb{B}_{r}(p)
denotes the geodesic open ball of radius
r>0
centered at
p\in M^{n}
, and
d
is a positive constant.

Since,
\int_{M}div(\eta^{2}_{r}\frac{1}{h}(R-\lambda)\nabla f)=0
, using (
8
) we obtain

n\int_{M}\eta^{2}_{r}\frac{1}{h^{2}}(R-\lambda)^{2}d\mu+\int_{M}\eta^{2}_{r}\langle\nabla\frac{1}{h}(R-\lambda),\nabla f\rangle d\mu+2\int_{M}\eta_{r}\frac{1}{h}(R-\lambda)\langle\nabla\eta_{r},\nabla f\rangle d\mu=0.

(33)

This implies

n\int_{M}\eta^{2}_{r}\frac{1}{h^{2}}(R-\lambda)^{2}d\mu\leq-\int_{M}\eta^{2}_{r}\langle\nabla\frac{1}{h}R,\nabla f\rangle d\mu-2\int_{M}\eta_{r}\frac{1}{h}(R-\lambda)\langle\nabla\eta_{r},\nabla f\rangle d\mu,

(34)

because of our hypothesis.

Furthermore, in the same way as we get (
33
), we can have

-\int_{M}\eta^{2}_{r}\langle\nabla\frac{1}{h}R,\nabla f\rangle d\mu=n\int_{M}\eta^{2}_{r}\frac{1}{h^{2}}R(R-\lambda)d\mu+2\int_{M}\eta_{r}\frac{1}{h}R\langle\nabla\eta_{r},\nabla f\rangle d\mu.

(35)

Therefore, applying the Cauchy-Schwarz’s inequality, and using our assumptions
\frac{1}{h}R,|\nabla f|\in L^{2}(M),0\leq R\leq\lambda
, we acquire

\displaystyle-\int_{M}\eta^{2}_{r}\langle\nabla\frac{1}{h}R,\nabla f\rangle d\mu

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\int_{M}\eta_{r}\frac{1}{h}R\langle\nabla\eta_{r},\nabla f\rangle d\mu

(36)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2{(\int_{M}\eta_{r}^{2}\frac{1}{h^{2}}R^{2}d\mu)}^{\frac{1}{2}}{(\int_{M}|\nabla\eta_{r}|^{2}|\nabla f|^{2}d\mu)}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{d_{1}}{r},\quad\text{for some constant $d_{1}>0$}.

Again, applying the Cauchy-Schwarz’s and Young’s inequalities, we obtain

\displaystyle-2\int_{M}\eta_{r}\frac{1}{h}(R-\lambda)\langle\nabla\eta_{r},\nabla f\rangle d\mu

(37)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2{(\int_{M}\eta_{r}^{2}\frac{1}{h^{2}}(R-\lambda)^{2}d\mu)}^{\frac{1}{2}}{(\int_{M}|\nabla\eta_{r}|^{2}|\nabla f|^{2}d\mu)}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{M}\eta_{r}^{2}\frac{1}{h^{2}}(R-\lambda)^{2}d\mu+\int_{M}|\nabla\eta_{r}|^{2}|\nabla f|^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{M}\eta_{r}^{2}\frac{1}{h^{2}}(R-\lambda)^{2}d\mu+\frac{d_{2}}{r^{2}},\quad\text{for some constant $d_{2}>0$}.

Hence, from (
34
), (
36
), and (
37
) we can conclude that, as
r\to\infty
,
R=\lambda
holds, and so by (
5
) the soliton is trivial.
∎

3.4.
Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Proof.

As in the Theorem
1.3
we consider the same cut-off function
\eta_{r}\in C^{2}_{0}(\mathbb{B}_{r}(p))
that fulfills the conditions in (
32
). Then we have,
\Delta\eta^{2}_{r}\leq\frac{4d}{r^{2}}
.

Now, multiplying both sides of (
12
) by
\eta^{2}_{r}
, and then integrating it over
M^{n}
, we can write

\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\eta^{2}_{r}\langle\nabla f,\nabla R\rangle d\mu+(n-1)\int_{M}\eta^{2}_{r}\Delta\frac{1}{h}(R-\lambda)d\mu+\int_{M}\eta^{2}_{r}\frac{1}{h}R(R-\lambda)d\mu=0,

(38)

which implies that

\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\eta^{2}_{r}\langle\nabla f,\nabla R\rangle d\mu+(n-1)\int_{M}\frac{1}{h}(R-\lambda)\Delta\eta^{2}_{r}d\mu+\int_{M}\eta^{2}_{r}\frac{1}{h}R(R-\lambda)d\mu=0.

(39)

Again, integrating by parts, and using (
8
) we get

\int_{M}\eta^{2}_{r}\langle\nabla f,\nabla R\rangle d\mu+n\int_{M}\eta^{2}_{r}\frac{1}{h}R(R-\lambda)d\mu+2\int_{M}\eta_{r}R\langle\nabla\eta_{r},\nabla f\rangle d\mu=0.

(40)

Combining the above two equations with (
9
), and using Cauchy-Schwarz’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle(\frac{n}{2}-1)\int_{M}\eta^{2}_{r}\frac{1}{h}R(R-\lambda)d\mu

\displaystyle=
