3. Executive Summary:

 This project supported State Fish and Wildlife Agencies (SFWAs) in their
 participation in an industry-led process to develop nationwide utility scale solar
 energy wildlife guidelines intended to mitigate the impacts of large-scale solar
 development on wildlife and natural habitats. Led by the Association of Fish and
 Wildlife Agencies (AFWA), the project focused on documenting and communicating
 SFWA priorities, providing logistical and technical support for state engagement, and
 disseminating early outcomes to promote adoption and use.

 Over the course of the grant period, the project convened SFWAs, facilitated
 sustained engagement with industry partners, and supported the co-development of
 a Solar Communications Framework as a foundational component of the broader
 guidelines. The Communications Framework was formally adopted by AFWA
 membership and disseminated widely, and it now serves as the organizing structure
 for ongoing guideline development.

 By the end of the grant period, the guidelines were in late-stage development, with
 substantial progress made toward integrating state priorities and wildlife
 considerations into an industry-led guidance document. The project successfully met
 its objectives and established a durable foundation for continued collaboration,
 guideline completion, and future implementation.

4. Background:

 The rapid pace and scale of utility-scale solar development raises concerns related
 to wildlife habitat, land use, and regulatory uncertainty. Addressing these challenges
 requires early coordination, shared expectations, and science-based guidance
 developed collaboratively by regulators and industry.

 State Fish and Wildlife Agencies play a critical role in reviewing and permitting
 energy projects, yet historically have had limited opportunities to engage collectively
 in national-level guideline development. This project was designed to support
 SFWAs by enabling coordinated participation in an industry-led process to develop
 national solar wildlife guidelines, ensuring that state priorities and conservation
 considerations were meaningfully reflected.

5. Project Objectives:

 The objectives of the project were to:

 • Support SFWAs in participating in the development of nationwide solar
 wildlife guidelines.

 • Document, summarize, and communicate SFWA priorities related to solar
 development.

 • Provide financial, logistical, and technical support for SFWA engagement with
 industry partners.

 • Disseminate outcomes of the guideline development process and promote
 adoption and use.

6. Project Results and Discussion:

 Task 1: Coordinate SFWA Engagement and Participation in Solar Guideline
 Development

 Throughout the project period, AFWA convened SFWAs to identify, document, and
 communicate state concerns, needs, and priorities related to solar development.
 Across all reporting periods, a consistent priority emerged: the need for a flexible
 framework that emphasizes early and iterative communication between solar project
 proponents and state agencies.

 To support effective participation, the project provided logistical and financial support
 to states, enabling attendance at in-person and virtual meetings and sustained
 engagement in workgroups. Engagement activities included:

 • State-only coordination meetings to align priorities and strategies

• Joint state–industry working group meetings to review and revise draft guideline

• In-person meetings, including a multi-day meeting in Denver, Colorado, to
 advance late-stage drafting

These efforts ensured that SFWA perspectives were consistently incorporated into
the evolving guideline document.

Development of the Solar Communications Framework
Early in the project, SFWAs and industry partners jointly agreed to prioritize
development of a Solar Communications Framework as the first phase of the
broader solar guidelines. This decision reflected SFWA priorities and was fully
consistent with the Statement of Project Objectives.

The Communications Framework:
• Emphasizes early, transparent, and ongoing engagement between solar industry
 project proponents and SFWAs
• Establishes staged communication objectives and milestones
• Serves as a practical tool to reduce uncertainty and improve coordination

The framework was completed in May 2024, formally adopted by AFWA through a
member resolution, and disseminated to states, industry, and other stakeholders. It
became the structural foundation for subsequent guideline drafting. Below are links
to the framework as well as the AFWA Resolution in support of the framework.

Solar Guidelines Development with Industry
Building on the Communications Framework, the project supported sustained
engagement with industry partners to develop the broader guidelines.

Activities included:
• Reviewing and revising industry draft documents
• Aligning guideline structure with the stages defined in the Communications
 Framework
• Incorporating state priorities and specific wildlife considerations
• Conducting biweekly joint meetings and monthly state-only strategy sessions

By the end of the grant period, the guidelines were in late-stage development, with
substantial alignment between industry proposals and SFWA priorities.

8. Path Forward:

  While the grant concluded with the guidelines in late-stage development, the work
  completed under this award established a strong foundation for finalization,
  adoption, and future implementation. Remaining steps include completion of final
  guideline drafting, additional in-person engagement, and expanded dissemination.
  Although the grant has expired, AFWA continues to work the development of solar
  guidelines and plans to complete them in 2026. They would be available to DOE
  upon request once completed.
