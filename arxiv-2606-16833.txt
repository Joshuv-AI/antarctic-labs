Abstract
Context. Atomic oxygen (O i ), OH, H 2 ​ O \rm H_{2}O , and CO are the main carriers of oxygen in dense interstellar gas. The far-infrared lines of these species are considered important coolants of shock-heated dense gas associated with protostellar outflows. Their relative abundances depend on the nature of the shock and usually remain uncertain in observational studies. Aims. We determine the relative abundances of O i , OH, H 2 ​ O \rm H_{2}O , and CO in the warm inner parts of the protostellar clump OMC-2 FIR4 in Orion A. The clump contains several young stellar objects. Methods. The upGREAT receiver including the High Frequency Array (HFA, operating at 4.74 THz, λ = 63 ​ μ ​ m \lambda=63\,\mu{\rm m} ) onboard the Stratospheric Observatory for Far-Infrared Astronomy (SOFIA) was used to observe OMC-2 FIR4 in the lines of O i , OH, OD, HDO, and CO. Additional HDO lines were observed with the Atacama Pathfinder Experiment (APEX). Archival H 2 ​ O \rm H_{2}O and CO spectra observed by the Herschel satellite were included in the analysis. The observed lines were reasonably well reproduced by an expanding spherical shell model. Results. The O i spectrum at 63 ​ μ ​ m 63\,\mu{\rm m} towards OMC-2 FIR4 is dominated by a broad line component, on top of which medium-wide and narrow line components can be discerned. The same components are present in the OH, H 2 ​ O \rm H_{2}O , and high- J J CO spectra towards this source. We find that O i is more abundant than H 2 ​ O \rm H_{2}O in the shocked gas. In the broad line component, the following abundance ratios are derived: O i / H 2 ​ O ∼ 700 \rm H_{2}O\sim 700 , O i /OH ∼ 300 \sim 300 , O i /CO ∼ 4 \sim 4 . The high relative abundance of atomic oxygen there suggests an origin in dissociative J-shocks that are associated with strong ultraviolet radiation. The O i /CO ratio decreases below unity in the components with a smaller velocity dispersion, and these components also have higher abundances of H 2 ​ O \rm H_{2}O than the broad line component, although remaining below that of CO. The HDO/ H 2 ​ O \rm H_{2}O ratio in the low-velocity components corresponds to the average ratio in the icy mantles of dust grains, and the presence of water there could also be understood in terms of sublimation without invoking high-temperature chemistry.



1
Introduction

Winds and jets from embedded protostars give rise to collisionally heated and accelerated dense gas that can be traced by broad far-infrared (FIR) lines of
\rm H_{2}O
, CO, OH and atomic oxygen, O
i
(
Kristensen et al. 2010
;
Karska et al. 2014a
;
Mottram et al. 2014
;
Gusdorf et al. 2017
;
Kristensen et al. 2017b
;
van Dishoeck et al. 2021
and references therein). These lines are considered major coolants of shocked gas and, therefore, are important for the dynamical evolution of protostellar systems. The relative abundances of the mentioned species and their contributions to the cooling of FIR lines change from source to source
(
Karska et al. 2018
)
. These changes depend on the nature of the shocks associated with the outflows and, ultimately, on the luminosity and evolutionary stage of the central source and the physical conditions in the envelope. The two main types of shocks, dissociative J-shocks and smoother magnetohydrodynamic C-shocks
(
Draine 1980
)
, are predicted to leave their traces in the chemical composition of the gas during and immediately after the shock (e.g.
Neufeld & Dalgarno 1989
,
Hollenbach & McKee 1989
;
Kaufman & Neufeld 1996
;
Godard et al. 2019
;
Lehmann et al. 2020
). On the observational side, the abundance of water is typically found to be lower than predicted by chemical shock models according to which all volatile oxygen not locked up in CO goes to
\rm H_{2}O
. There are also indications that the cooling by O
i
is more important for Class I protostars compared to the earlier Class 0 stage
(
Karska et al. 2018
)
. Both observational results have been suggested to be traceable to ultraviolet (UV) radiation from the central star(s) or strong shocks impinging on non-dissociative C-shocks (
Karska et al. 2013
;
Kristensen et al. 2013
;
Karska et al. 2014b
;
Kristensen et al. 2017b
). On the other hand, the O
i
line at
63\,\mu{\rm m}
is predicted to be the main coolant of dense dissociative J-shocks
(
Hollenbach & McKee 1989
)
, and contributions from multiple shocks with different strengths are typically needed to explain the observed FIR line ratios. An obstacle to determining the oxygen budget in protostellar outflows is the scarcity of spectrally resolved O
i
observations.

Observationally, the analysis is complicated as bright O
i
emission also originates in photodissociation regions (PDRs) on the surfaces of dense clouds exposed to strong UV radiation from nearby hot stars. In regions forming stellar clusters it is possible to see O
i
fine structure lines arising from two separate components of warm gas: out-flowing gas near the protostars that is heated both mechanically and by radiation, and gas at cloud surfaces heated primarily by the photoelectric effect (
Herrmann et al. 1997
;
González-García et al. 2016
). An additional difficulty is that O
i
lines from massive-star-forming regions are often self-absorbed or absorbed by unrelated foreground clouds (
Leurini et al. 2015
;
Goldsmith et al. 2021
;
Guevara et al. 2024
).

Here, we present the O
i
, OH, and HDO spectra observed with the upGREAT receiver on the Stratospheric Observatory for Far-Infrared Astronomy (SOFIA) towards the strong FIR source OMC-2 FIR4 (
Mezger et al. 1990
;
Chini et al. 1997
;
Johnstone & Bally 1999
) in Orion Molecular Cloud 2 (OMC-2). OMC-2 is part of the integral-shaped filament in the Orion A molecular cloud complex. The distance of the cloud is approximately 390 pc
(
Kounkel et al. 2017
)
. In the analysis, we include several
\rm H_{2}O
and CO lines previously observed in the course of the
Herschel
key programme ”Chemical HErschel Surveys of Star-forming regions” (CHESS;
Ceccarelli et al. 2010
), and two HDO lines observed with the Atacama Pathfinder Experiment (APEX). The dense molecular clump OMC-2 FIR4 with a mass of approximately
30\,M_{\odot}
dominates the FIR continuum emission longward of
\lambda=160\,\mu{\rm m}
and is the strongest source of high-excitation FIR lines of
\rm H_{2}O
, OH, CO, and O
i
in OMC-2 (
Manoj et al. 2013
;
Kama et al. 2013
;
Furlan et al. 2014
;
González-García et al. 2016
). OMC-2 FIR4 contains several compact millimetre continuum sources (
Kainulainen et al. 2017
;
Shimajiri et al. 2008
;
Sato et al. 2023
) of which four are associated with known young stellar objects (YSOs). These are HOPS-108 and HOPS-64, detected in the course of the
Herschel
Orion Protostar Survey (HOPS;
Fischer et al. 2013
), and the compact radio continuum sources VLA 15 and VLA 16 detected by
Osorio et al. 2017
. The nature of these objects has also been discussed in
Adams et al. 2012
,
Furlan et al. 2016
, and
Tobin et al. 2019
.

The OMC-2 FIR4 clump is exposed to strong FUV radiation from the Orion Nebula Cluster (
Jørgensen et al. 2006
;
Favre et al. 2018
), and it is also claimed to be pervaded by an extremely high flux of cosmic-ray-like particles, possibly originating from an internal source (
Ceccarelli et al. 2014
;
Fontani et al. 2017
;
Favre et al. 2018
). In addition, several authors have presented evidence that a powerful outflow from the nearby intermediate-mass protostar HOPS-370 located in the OMC-2 FIR3 clump collides with the FIR4 clump, causing shocks that cannot be easily attributed to outflows from protostars inside the clump (
Shimajiri et al. 2008
;
González-García et al. 2016
;
Chahine et al. 2022
;
Sato et al. 2023
). According to
Sato et al. 2023
, the four embedded YSOs and two other
\lambda=1.3
mm sources are, however, associated with compact outflows detected in the CO
J=2-1
and SiO
J=5-4
lines (see Figure 10 of
Sato et al. 2023
). In addition, the Class 0 protostar HOPS-108 possibly drives a collimated molecular jet detected in SiO
(
Lattanzi et al. 2023
)
.

The purpose of the present work is to derive the relative abundances of O
i
, OH,
\rm H_{2}O
, and CO in the warm inner parts of the OMC-2 FIR4 clump and in the PDR at its surface. The abundance ratios have an immediate connection to the oxygen budget and the role of the FUV field in the two regimes. We employ an expanding spherical shell model for the clump. The expansion, implied by the observed line shapes, is assumed to be driven by winds from the embedded protostars. Although complex and disordered structures are seen in interferometric images of this object (
Chahine et al. 2022
;
Sato et al. 2023
), the simple model can produce spectral lines that resemble the single-dish spectra presented here.

2.1
SOFIA

The observations towards OMC-2 FIR4 were made during SOFIA Cycle 6 on December 11, 2018 on a flight starting from Palmdale, California. These were part of project 06_0179 (PI P. Caselli) employing the German REceiver for Astronomy at Terahertz Frequencies (GREAT;

The observations were made in the dual beam switching mode, where the secondary mirror was chopped between the source and two reference positions lying symmetrically to the equatorial east and west from the target. The switching amplitude was

2\times 125^{\prime\prime}

Despite the relatively large chop throw, post-flight data inspection revealed O
i
emission in both off-source chop phase signals; this manifested itself in pseudo-self-absorption in the on-source spectra after subtraction of the off-phase signal. However, thanks to the high quality of the deep pointed integration towards OMC-2 FIR4 we were able to recover the underlying spectra. By extracting data of the on- and off-source integrations for each pixel, for each chop phase separately, plus the calibration data on the hot and cold loads, it was possible to reference the off-source signal for each chop phase against the hot load (mimicking ”load switching”). This provided the astronomical off-source ”contamination” superimposed on the emission spectrum of the FIR sky. The good system stability of the GREAT receivers was beneficial to the process.

2.2
APEX

The APEX observations towards OMC-2 FIR4 were made in the course of the programme 0105.C-0281A-2020 (PI P. Caselli). These were single-point position switched observations with the reference chosen
120^{\prime\prime}
east of the on-position given above. The ground-state line of HDO at 465 GHz was observed in the LSB of the nFLASH460 receiver. These observations were obtained in October 2020. The HDO
2_{11}-2_{12}
line at 242 GHz was observed in April 2021 using the nFLASH230 receiver. The line was placed in the USB. The nFLASH receivers are dual polarisation, dual sideband (2SB) systems, meaning that both the upper and lower sidebands, separated by 12 GHz, are present in the output in two orthogonal polarizations. The widths of the sidebands are 8 GHz for nFLASH230 and 4 GHz for nFLASH460. The bands are recorded with several Fast Fourier Transform spectrometers at a spectral resolution of 61 kHz. The APEX spectra contain a large number of molecular lines, but here we concentrate on HDO which is the most relevant to the present study. Because the HDO transitions considered here require very high densities to be excited, we do not expect contamination from the off-signal despite the relatively short distance between the on- and off-positions.

4
Spherical shell model of OMC-2 FIR4

The spectral lines observed in the course of the present survey with SOFIA and APEX, as well as several other lines observed previously with
Herschel
/HIFI, were simulated using a 1-D spherical model. The goal was to estimate the relative abundances of the main oxygen bearing species, O
i
, OH,
\rm H_{2}O
, and CO in the warm (
\sim 300-500
K) and cool (30-40 K) components of the clump and in the PDR at its surface. In addition, we attempted to constrain the abundance of HDO in the shocked and cool gas components.

The envelope around the protostar(s) was assumed to extend from a distance of
1000
au (
2\aas@@fstack{\prime\prime}6
) to 8 000 au (
21^{\prime\prime}
) from the centre, and was assumed to be surrounded by a PDR extending to a radius of 12 000 au (
31^{\prime\prime}
). The outer radius of the envelope corresponds approximately to the apparent radius of the clump in the
250\,\mu{\rm m}
continuum, as observed with
Herschel
/SPIRE. We tested different inner radii from 100 au upwards. Values of the order of 1000 au give the best overall agreement with the observations that we modelled and were performed using beam sizes ranging from
\sim 6^{\prime\prime}
to
\sim 40^{\prime\prime}
. This size scale is consistent with the LVG analysis of the
\rm HCO^{+}
and
\rm N_{2}H^{+}
lines from OMC-2 FIR4 by
Ceccarelli et al. 2014
, who found the best fit with the line intensities and ratios assuming a warm (120 K) shell with a radius of
\sim 2000
au and a cool envelope (40 K) with a radius of
\sim 4000
au. In addition, the intensities of the lowest rotational lines of
\rm H_{2}O
observed by
Herschel
indicate that the extent of the warm component is a few 1000 au. This agrees with previous estimates for the sizes of ”cavity shocks” associated with outflows from intermediate-mass and massive protostars (
San José-García et al. 2016
;
van Dishoeck et al. 2021
and references therein).

The density distribution was assumed to follow a power law
n\propto r^{-2}
with a maximum
n(\rm H_{2})=3\times 10^{7}\,\rm cm^{-3}
at the inner boundary (1000 au). The density at the outer edge of the envelope (8000 au) was then
\sim 5\times 10^{5}\,\rm cm^{-3}
. The surrounding PDR was assumed to have a constant density of
3\times 10^{4}\,\rm cm^{-3}
. The dust temperature distribution was calculated taking into account the heating of the dust by both internal and external sources of radiation. The continuum radiative transfer program CRT
(
Juvela 2005
)
was used here. The external radiation field was assumed to be
10^{4}
times stronger than the interstellar radiation field in the solar neighbourhood, as estimated by
Mezger et al. 1982
. This corresponds to the estimate of
Jørgensen et al. 2006
for OMC-2 FIR4 obtained from radiative transfer modelling of the spectral lines and the dust continuum.

A 300 K blackbody with a bolometric luminosity of
30\,L_{\odot}
was placed at the centre of the spherical envelope. The central luminosity agrees with the estimates of
Adams et al. 2012
and
Furlan et al. 2014
. The present dust model assumes ”classical” grains with an MRN-like size distribution
(
Mathis et al. 1977
)
in radiative equilibrium. The gas temperature was assumed to be equal to the dust temperature inside the envelope but strongly elevated near its inner and outer edges. The temperature of the PDR shell needs to be near 100 K to give rise to the strong, narrow O
i
emission lines observed in off-centre pixels of the HFA. The warm outer layer is also consistent with the
Herschel
/PACS detection of extended O
i
emission around OMC-2 FIR4 reported in
González-García et al. 2016
. Similarly, high gas temperatures in the inner regions were needed to reproduce the observed intensities of the broad O
i
, OH,
\rm H_{2}O
, and CO lines. High gas temperatures can be envisioned to be caused by the photoelectric effect and gas-phase photo-processes, and, in the inner regions, also by mechanical shocks owing to protostellar outflows.

The non-thermal velocity distribution was assumed to be Gaussian, with the dispersion
\sigma_{v}
(FWHM/
\sqrt{8\ln 2}
) decreasing from
15\,\rm km\,s^{-1}
in the innermost shell to
0.5\,\rm km\,s^{-1}
in the outer layers. The blue-shifted self-absorption in the
\rm H_{2}O
spectra (Figs.
7
and
8
) suggests that the inner envelope is expanding. Expansion speeds in the range
2-3\,\rm km\,s^{-1}

1\,\rm km\,s^{-1}
.

The envelope model with a power-law distribution of the density was divided into five zones, where the gas temperature, non-thermal velocity dispersion, the expansion speed, and the abundances of the observed species were set by hand.

For simplicity, the fractional abundance of a species in a certain zone is non-zero only if this is necessary to explain the observed line profile. In the rest of the zones, the fractional abundance is set to zero either on chemical grounds or because the excitation conditions are such that the zone is likely to have little contribution to the observed line emission or absorption. 5
Discussion

5.1
PDR

The present model of OMC-2 FIR4 which consists of five shells was invoked in order to estimate the relative abundances of the major oxygen bearing species near the surface and in the inner, shocked parts of the clump. The outermost shell represents the PDR that has previously been found to sheathe the molecular ridge of OMC-2 (
Herrmann et al. 1997
;
González-García et al. 2016
). Assuming constant abundances in this PDR as we have done is a simplification. According to the models of
Hollenbach et al. 2009
, the relative abundances of O
i
, OH,
\rm H_{2}O
, and CO change rapidly in a PDR as functions of the visual extinction
A_{\rm V}
calculated from the cloud surface (see their Figure 3). This depends on photodissociation near the surface, accretion of atomic oxygen on the grain surfaces, and subsequent formation of water ice and its photo-desorption. In the models of
Hollenbach et al. 2009
the gas-phase abundances of
\rm H_{2}O
, OH, O
2
, and CO reach a maximum at depths
A_{\rm V}\sim
a few magnitudes into the cloud, depending on the external FUV field, and remain almost constant for another
\Delta A_{\rm V}\sim
a few magnitudes, before freezing out. The thickness of the outermost shell in our model corresponds to a hydrogen column density of
N_{\rm H}\sim 3.6\times 10^{21}\,\rm cm^{-2}
or a visual extinction of
A_{\rm V}\sim 3.6^{\rm mag}
. However, the clump is embedded in an ambient cloud, so the effective visual extinction at the bottom of this layer is probably higher than that. Using the analytical formula of
Hollenbach et al. 2009
for the plateau where these abundances peak (their Eqs. (20), (31), and (32)), the relative abundances of O, OH, and
\rm H_{2}O
estimated in the outer layer,
\sim 2200:1.7:1
(see Table
2
), correspond to a visual extinction of
A_{\rm V}\sim 4.2^{\rm mag}
(assuming
G_{0}=10^{4}
and
\sigma_{\rm H}=5\times 10^{-22}\,{\rm cm^{2}}
).

The bright elongated O
i
feature seen in the spectrally unresolved map from
Herschel
/PACS
(
González-García et al. 2016
)
coincides with the dense ridge connecting OMC-2 FIR3 and FIR4 (Fig.
1
) but is also aligned with a radio jet and a powerful CO outflow that seem to emanate from HOPS 370 in FIR3 (
Osorio et al. 2017
;
Sato et al. 2023
).
González-García et al. 2016
argued that the O
i
feature can be attributed to the jet from HOPS 370 and that the O
i
emission peak towards FIR4 represents a terminal shock of this jet. Based on the resolved spectra shown in Fig.
2
and recent interferometric observations discussed below, we think that the intensity maximum towards FIR4 can be more naturally explained by outflows that have their origins inside the FIR4 clump. In addition, we suggest that the dense PDR surrounding OMC-2 that produces the emission detected in the off-centre pixels of the HFA (Fig.
2
) can have a substantial contribution to the elongated O
i
feature between FIR3 and FIR4. This region can be relatively bright in O
i
owing to the high column densities. The fine-structure line of singly ionised carbon, C
ii
, at
158\,\mu{\rm m}
is another tracer of PDRs, although it probes their less dense outer parts (
Kaufman et al. 1999
; see also Figure 4 in
Hollenbach et al. 2009
). The OMC-2 cloud and the ridge between FIR3 and FIR4 do not stand out in the large-scale [C
ii
] mapping of Orion A with SOFIA presented in
Pabst et al. 2021
and
Higgins et al. 2021
. The integrated [C
ii
] line intensity map is dominated by limb-brightened shells associated with M42, M43, and NGC 1977 and an extended faint component. A comparison of the [C
ii
] and [O
i
] spectra towards the positions observed in the present survey is shown in Fig.
11
. The LSR velocity and width of the narrow [O
i
] component are similar to those of [C
i
]. The ratio of the integrated intensities [O
i
]/[C
ii
] ranges from
\sim 1.5
to
\sim 2.8
, expect for our (0,0) (
\sim 9.1
) and the offset (
+7\aas@@fstack{\prime\prime}9,+10\aas@@fstack{\prime\prime}8
) (
\sim 3.6
) where the [O
i
] line has a red-shifted wing. These ratios are in agreement with the predictions for a dense PDR with a strong incident FUV flux
(
Kaufman et al. 1999
)
. The [O
i
] line that originates in a jet would probably show velocity shifts and/or large line widths. Spectrally resolved O
I
mapping of the ridge is needed to confirm the presence of the putative O
i
jet.

5.2
Warm inner envelope

The emission of atomic oxygen associated with protostars can originate in wide-angle winds or collimated jets launched by protostar-disk systems, terminal or internal shocks within jets, or shocks occurring when the wide-angle winds collide with the molecular envelope surrounding the protostar (
Hollenbach 1985
;
van Kempen et al. 2010
;
Nisini et al. 2015
;
Kristensen et al. 2017a
;
Gusdorf et al. 2017
;
Sperling et al. 2021
). The different structural components have kinematic characteristics that may be recognized in resolved O
i
spectra. The present O
i
spectra with the detection range extending from
v_{\rm LSR}\sim-50
to
\sim+100\,\rm km\,s^{-1}
show no evidence of very high velocity jets or bullets. Similarly, the CO
5-4
spectrum observed with SOFIA is clean of line emission outside the velocity range shown in Fig.
9
up to
v_{\rm LSR}\sim\pm 200\,\rm km\,s^{-1}
. Judging from the size of the emission region and its physical conditions inferred from the modelling we assume that the observed broad line emissions of O
i
, OH and
\rm H_{2}O
come from outflow cavity shocks or jet terminal shocks in the inner parts of the FIR4 clump.

The O
i
spectrum towards OMC-2 FIR4 (excluding the PDR component) is made up of a broad (
\sigma_{v}=15\,\rm km\,s^{-1}
), nearly Gaussian component, and two narrower components (
\sigma_{v}=5\,\rm km\,s^{-1}
and
\sigma_{v}=2\,\rm km\,s^{-1}
), which are also seen in the OH,
\rm H_{2}O
and high-
J
CO spectra towards this source. The spectrum is measured towards the most prominent protostars, HOPS-108 and HOPS-64, embedded in the clump. The broadest line component corresponds to the innermost zone of our model (Table
2
), where the abundance of O
i
is approximately 700 times that of (ortho+para)
\rm H_{2}O
and 300 times that of OH. The high relative abundance of atomic oxygen suggests that the broad component originates in the cooling gas behind the fast molecular shocks that have been modelled by
Neufeld & Dalgarno 1989
,
Hollenbach & McKee 1989
, and, more recently, by
Lehmann et al. 2020
. According to these models, shocks moving at speeds exceeding the ion magnetosonic speed of the medium (typically above
\sim 30\,\rm km\,s^{-1}
) generate strong UV radiation that ionises and dissociates atoms and molecules in both the pre-shock and post-shock gas components, and heats these through the photoelectric effect. The component responsible for the broad O
i
emission may correspond to the second temperature plateau of a dissociative J-shock, where the re-formation of
\rm H_{2}
on the grains maintains the temperature at
\sim 400-500
K (see Fig. 14 of
Neufeld & Dalgarno 1989
). The plateau appears at high pre-shock densities (
n>10^{5}\,\rm cm^{-3}
), where freshly formed
\rm H_{2}
in excited vibrational states is collisionally de-excited by atomic hydrogen. This transforms the vibrational energy to the kinetic energy of the atoms and delays radiative cooling
(
Hollenbach & McKee 1989
)
.

In lower-velocity C-shocks, where neutrals and the ion-electron fluid have different flow velocities and temperatures, and the impact is mediated by ion-neutral collisions,
\rm H_{2}
is not significantly dissociated. In these conditions,
\rm H_{2}O
and CO are supposed to become the most important coolants (
Kaufman & Neufeld 1996
;
Godard et al. 2019
;
Kristensen et al. 2023
). Low-velocity shocks (with speeds around
\sim 5\,\rm km\,s^{-1}
) where the gas temperature does not rise high enough for efficient
\rm H_{2}O
formation are an exception, where the abundance of atomic O is predicted to be much higher than those of
\rm H_{2}O
and CO (see, for example, Table 2 in
Godard et al. 2019
).

The two model zones in the inner envelope with
\sigma_{v}=5\,\rm km\,s^{-1}
(zone 2) and
\sigma_{v}=2\,\rm km\,s^{-1}
(zone 3) have higher fractional abundances of
\rm H_{2}O
than zone 1, although remaining below those of O
i
and CO. The O
i
/CO ratio decreases from
\sim 4
in zone 1 to below 1 in zones 2 and 3. These tendencies indicate an increase in the proportion of molecular gas in zones 2 and 3. These zones may represent post-shock gas behind irradiated shocks or gas adjacent to shock fronts and heated by radiative or magnetic precursors, where water is produced in high-temperature gas-phase reactions or released from grains (and is also destroyed by photodissociation)
(
Jiménez-Serra et al. 2008
)
. The latter alternative is supported by the fact that the HDO/
\rm H_{2}O
ratio derived in these zones is
\sim 0.001-0.003
(Table
2
), which corresponds to values measured in massive hot cores, where the ices have been completely sublimated (
van Dishoeck et al. 2021
and references therein;
Slavicinska et al. 2024
). According to Table
2
, the
\rm H_{2}O/{\rm OH}
ratio increases strongly from zone 2 (
\sigma_{v}=5\,\rm km\,s^{-1}
) to zone 3 (
\sigma_{v}=2\,\rm km\,s^{-1}
), suggesting a weakening of the radiation field.

The physical conditions in an envelope are likely to change gradually with distance along and from the outflow axis (see, e.g., Fig. 8 in
San José-García et al. 2016
), so the geometry around a single outflow is cylindrical or conical rather than spherical. The OMC-2 FIR4 clump contains several jet-outflow systems with different orientations. The protostars HOPS-108 (Class 0) and HOPS-64 (Class I), and the millimetre source ”FraSCO 33”
(
Sato et al. 2023
)
are located within
3^{\prime\prime}
of the observed position and are therefore encompassed in the telescope beam for all the lines observed here. According to
Sato et al. 2023
, the three objects are associated with their own compact but rather disordered CO outflows: ”flow-6”, ”flow-4”, and ”flow-5” (see Figure 10 in
Sato et al. 2023
). The outflow associated with HOPS-108, ”flow-6”, has the largest velocity range, extending from
-5\,\rm km\,s^{-1}
to
\sim 50\,\rm km\,s^{-1}
. The collimated SiO jet discussed by
Lattanzi et al. 2023
extends to the south-east from the vicinity of HOPS-108 and may therefore be related to ”flow-6”. It is probably identifiable with the prominent SiO emission structure labeled ”shock 7” by
Sato et al. 2023
, which according to these authors is, however, caused by the energetic outflow from the intermediate-mass protostar HOPS-370 located in the FIR3 clump. The brightest and most extensive region of SiO emission (called ”shock-6” by
Sato et al. 2023
) is found on the western side of HOPS-108 and HOPS-64 in the same area as the compact outflows ”flow-4” and ”flow-5”. However,
Sato et al. 2023
favour the scenario presented previously by
Shimajiri et al. 2008
that this extensive shock is also caused by the HOPS-370 outflow.

Although evidence for a strong influence from the HOPS-370 outflow on the FIR4 clump seems solid, we consider it probable that the rather symmetric high-velocity wings seen in the O
i
, CO, and
\rm H_{2}O
lines towards the centre of the clump arise from shocks induced by internal sources. In accordance with the conclusions of
van Kempen et al. 2010
in the case of the low-mass protostar HH-46, we suggest that the broad O
i
line originates in high-velocity J-shocks near the embedded YSOs. Then it would be natural to assume that the wings of the
\rm H_{2}O
and CO lines come from the same gas. Judging from the low
\rm H_{2}O/{\rm{O\textsc{i}}}
and
\rm H_{2}O/
CO ratios, the narrower line components also come from dissociative J-shocks or externally irradiated C-shocks. In the former case, the narrow components could represent cooler gas further downstream of the broad component, which is the brightest in O
i
. As to the origin of the line wings, these ideas diverge from the concept presented in several previous studies of low-mass star-forming regions where the broad Gaussian component seen in the
\rm H_{2}O
and high-
J
CO spectra is attributed to non-dissociative shocks along the outflow cavities, whereas ”medium broad” (
\Delta v<10\,\rm km\,s^{-1}
) components are explained by jet-induced dissociative ”spot shocks” (
Kristensen et al. 2013
;
Mottram et al. 2014
;
van Dishoeck et al. 2021
).

6
Conclusions

Spectrally resolved observations with the High Frequency Array (HFA) of the upGREAT receiver onboard SOFIA show that the strong O
i
emission at 63
\mu
m towards the protostellar clump OMC-2 FIR4 is dominated by broad wings that are similar to those seen in OH,
\rm H_{2}O
and high-
J
CO lines towards this source. We assume that these lines originate in shocks caused by protostars embedded in the clump. Using an expanding spherical shell model that reproduces the observed lines reasonably well, we infer that oxygen is mainly atomic in the broad wing component. This component probably represents the post-shock cooling gas behind dissociative J-shocks that generate strong UV radiation as described in
Neufeld & Dalgarno 1989
,
Hollenbach & McKee 1989
, and
Lehmann et al. 2020
. According to our shell model, the components with medium-wide lines (
\sigma_{v}\leq 5\,\rm km\,s^{-1}
) still have more O
i
than
\rm H_{2}O
but the proportion of molecular gas is greater; the abundance ratio O
i
/CO is less than unity there. The HDO/
\rm H_{2}O
abundance ratio in this component is of the order of
10^{-3}
, which is supposed to represent the average deuterium fractionation ratio in the icy mantles of dust grains. Therefore, it is not necessary to invoke high-temperature chemistry in addition to ice sublimation to explain the presence of gaseous water in the component with medium-wide lines.

The six off-centre pixels of the upGREAT/HFA receiver show narrow
63\,\mu{\rm m}
O
i
lines that are likely to originate in the extended PDR at the surface of the OMC-2 cloud (
Herrmann et al. 1997
;
González-García et al. 2016
). We suggest that the elongated O
i
feature seen previously in the
Herschel
/PACS O
i
map between FIR3 and FIR4 probably has a contribution from the PDR in the direction of the dense ridge connecting the two clumps.
