Weather Representation
How is variable weather data represented in the model?

 Generating Multiple Years of Weather Data
The ReEDS-India model contains multiple years of weather data (2014-2024) that can be used to
create and stress-test different generation, transmission, and storage portfolios, thus supporting
 grid reliability solutions under a range of weather conditions.

Reserves Representation
How are policies on planning reserves incorporated into the model?

 Planning Reserve Margin Policy Options
In ReEDS-India, the planning reserve margin (PRM) can be set at the region-level (Northern,
Northeastern, Eastern, Southern, Western) or at the state-level (i.e., each region or each state
needs to have an X% PRM).
• Default: 7% PRM at the state-level based on latest Central Electricity Authority (CEA) requirements

 High-fidelity weather data across
 multiple weather years allows for a
 more accurate calculation of capacity
 credit and therefore the contribution of
 wind and solar PV to meeting the
 planning reserve margin.

 Technology Options for Operating Reserves
In ReEDS-India, the operating reserve (spinning reserves) requirement can be modified. The
contribution of different technologies to the operating reserve requirement is limited by the
ramping capability for that technology.
• Default: 5% of national electricity demand in each time slice (can adjust to cover sudden fluctuations in
 demand and variable renewable energy production)

 Technologies that can
 contribute to operating reserve
 requirement:

 Natural gas, diesel, hydropower,
 coal, nuclear, battery storage,
 biomass, and hydrogen turbines.

Key Takeaways
What are some overarching resource adequacy considerations for modelers?

  Key Takeaways to Support Resource Adequacy

Modeling with multiple years of historical weather data allows for a more robust
consideration of weather dependent resources.

Reliability inputs such as the planning reserve margin (PRM) and operating
reserves should be set in accordance with policy to ensure accurate
representation of constraints in the model.

Linking capacity expansion models (i.e., ReEDS) with resource models (i.e., reV)
and production cost models (i.e., PLEXOS) allows for iterative stress testing to
ensure system reliability under extreme conditions.
