Project Introduction
Group14 Technologies is leading a word-class team composed of Cabot Corporation,
Silatronix, Arkema, Pacific Northwest National Laboratories, and Farasis Energy to
research, fabricate, test and demonstrate lithium-ion batteries (LIB) implementing ≥30%
silicon-carbon (Si-C) composite anode material achieving aggressive next generation
automotive energy targets. The project’s centerpiece technology is Group14’s high
performing Si-C composite anode material already proven to provide 35% increased
energy density at 12% composite blended with graphite, >1000 cycle stability, and a
projected cell cost of <$125/kWh in conventional LIBs under a prior DOE VTO award (DE-
EE0001201). This project advances this baseline by taking a holistic approach to increase
silicon content for higher energy density and to improve stability during battery use (e.g.,
cycle life) through the optimization and incorporation of carbon additives, and binders for
the anode along with high performance co-solvent electrolyte additives. These anode
improvements are being paired with high-nickel, low-cobalt, commercially available
cathode to achieve the project’s performance targets.

Objectives
At project outset, the proposed battery performance targets were as follows: >350 Wh/kg,
>750 Wh/L, >1000 cycle stability, and >10-year calendar life. Over the course of the
project, the team has delivered to DOE a total of four battery builds comprising ≥0.4Ah
pouch cells. As presented to DOE, these batteries successfully achieved the project
targets of >350 Wh/kg, >750 Wh/L, and >1000 cycle stability. Regarding calendar life, as
agreed with DOE, the data were insufficient to make a final calendar life assessment, and
the project met the key performance objectives.

Approach
Our approach is to leverage Group14 Technologies’ carbon and Si-C composite expertise
in conjunction with our partnering organizations’ expertise to holistically improve the
performance of a LIB as measured by our yearly electrochemical targets. Within this
collaboration, each of our partners contribute a unique approach toward improving the
battery:

Group14 Technologies: Our core technology is to create a composite material that
accounts for and overcomes the failings of traditional silicon-based anode material
solutions. The basis for Group14 Si-C’s superior performance is its rationally designed
structure. At its foundation is an amorphous carbon scaffold, with its porosity fine-tuned
for promoting intra-particle growth of the desired type and size of silicon. The resulting Si-
C composite includes internal void space to buffer expansion, nano-silicon to prevent
particle fracture with cycling, and a carbon scaffold to enhance conductivity and particle
integrity while minimizing exposed silicon surface area.

Cabot Corporation: Global leader in nanoengineered conductive carbon additives for
LIBs. Their role in the project is: (1) to assist Group14 in characterization of carbon and
resulting Si-C and (2) to optimize the carbon additive type and loading to maximize
performance.

Silatronix: Producers of organosilicon co-solvent additives that improve LIB cycle and
calendar stability. Silatronix pioneered industrial relevant analytical methods for
investigating silicon-electrolyte interactions, including quantitative gassing analysis and
identification of degradation mechanisms. Leveraging their expertise, they will produce
an optimized electrolyte formulation that will synergize with Group14 Si-C to extend
battery life by controlling degradation mechanisms.

Arkema: A specialty chemicals and advanced materials developer and supplier that
successfully commercialized several electrode binders and separators coating resins,
especially for high-energy, high-voltage LIBs. Their role in the project is to develop an
optimized binder solution to maximize cohesion of Group14’s Si-C composite with other

anode components and to promote the long-term cycling and calendar performance of
the battery.

Farasis Energy: A leading U.S. developer and supplier of LIBs for EVs. Farasis has
designed and built its own Li-ion cell and battery system manufacturing plant and has
been in high volume production since 2010 with fully automated production capability of
4 GWh/y. By leveraging Farasis’ battery production research and experience the best
design and form factor of LIB will be utilized to promote high energy density and increase
cycle life.

Pacific Northwest National Lab (PNNL): World-renowned experts in energy storage
research. PNNL lead in mechanistic investigations regarding LIB materials and assure
the guidance to the project towards success. PNNL provides critical advanced materials
characterization support toward synergizing the team’s optimization efforts.

Results
Our multi-pronged approach brought together proven technology leaders for critical
LIB components – Si-C anode material (Group14), conductive carbon additives (Cabot),
binder (Arkema), and electrolyte (Silatronix) – as well as a partner well-versed in LIB
construction (Farasis), and advanced characterization expertise from PNNL. Our team
co-optimized these critical LIB components manufactured in USA and mitigated technical
risk by pursuing multiple, complementary technologies that address cycle and calendar
life challenges associated with LIBs comprising >30% silicon containing anodes. The
structure of Group14’s Si-C was specifically nano-engineered for high performance,
scalability, and low cost. The complementary technologies from our partners, similarly, to
the baseline Si-C, was chosen based on feasibility to boost performance of silicon-
containing LIBs, as well as scalability and cost effectiveness when scaled to automotive
production volumes.

During the first year of the project our primary focus was to establish the baseline
performance of LIBs containing Group14 Si-C to achieve baseline targets outlined in
Table I.1.1 and down-select the key anode components for an interim LIB build. The
baseline build was highly successful, demonstrating a projected >340 Wh/kg and cycle
life of 300 cycles. Furthermore, sixteen of these ~0.4Ah cells were delivered to and
tested at Argonne National Lab (ANL).
Over the course of FY21, Group14, Cabot, Farasis, Arkema, Silatronix and PNNL had
collaborated towards significant optimization of each of the functional materials that
comprise the anode. Group14 explored a combination of previously identified carbon
scaffold and Si-C modifications for the interim build. Over 13 different modified samples
were supplied by Group14 and screened by Farasis. In parallel to the work done at
Group14 to improve the Si-C composite performance, each of our partners optimized the
other anode materials to synergize with Group14 Si-C. The carbon additives, binders,
and electrolyte optimization were conducted at Cabot, Arkema and Silatronix respectively.
Finally, PNNL provided crucial support for this project by assessing the material

characteristics of the Si-C before and after various modifications using advanced
characterization techniques like XPS, HRTEM and electron diffraction (Figure I.1.A.1)

Farasis produced the interim cells in 2022 using a modified Si-C from Group14, electrolyte
with OS3 additive from Silatronix, commercial graphite, commercial binder, commercial
carbon additive, and commercial cathode. The interim build demonstrated a >600 cycle
life stability, achieved through the co-optimization of the anode active material with other
battery components. Sixteen of these 0.4Ah interim cells were delivered to ANL for
testing.

Screening of the other key battery components (binder, conductive additive, electrolyte)
was continued in 2022 towards the down-selection for the final cell build employed for
2023's final deliverable. For the conductive additive, Cabot demonstrated an
improvement in conductivity (Figure I.1.A.2) and electrochemical performance using a
new, optimized carbon nanostructure. For the electrolyte, Silatronix evaluated and
optimized different electrolyte compositions with organosilicon compounds to increase
the longevity of LIB devices. To this end, Silatronix screened various electrolyte
compositions for their ability to minimize gas generation in the presence of the anode at
high temperature (Figure I.1.A.3). Electrolyte composition containing OS3 additive that
was down selected using this study was shown to increase the cycle life of the cells.
Arkema optimized the latex binders to enable higher peel strength than standard
commercial binders. Group14 produced carbon scaffold and Si-C composite to support
the final cell build.

In 2023, we discussed the projected performance of the pre-final build and the decision
to extend the project, at no cost, through 2024 to improve performance of the final cell
build. During 2023-2024, 2 different binders were down selected for the final build through
a series of studies (Figure I.1.A.4). Two final builds (primary and secondary builds) were
proposed to minimize the risk associated with exploring new cell components. Primary
final build objective includes Binder 2 and was seen as the lowest risk based on the
available data. Secondary final build included a binder from Arkema with limited data set.
This build was expected to perform well and includes components from more project
partners.

Further notable technical accomplishment in 2024 was completion of the final build cells.
Fifteen cells each of the Primary (Commercial binder) & Secondary (Arkema binder) final
builds was delivered to Argonne National Lab along with 1 month of cycling data from
Farasis. The final deliverable 0.4 Ah pouch cells contain anode active material comprising
35% Si-C blended with graphite, cathode comprising high Ni NMC, proprietary electrolyte
comprising OS3.

Comparison of the calendar life for all the 3 cell builds throughout the project is shown in
Figure I.1.A.5. For the calendar life test, the cells were stored at 30°C and 45°C with 100%
state of charge (SoC) for 30 days increments. Battery performance was then measured
after each 30-day interval at a C/3 rate followed by an HPPC test at C/10 rate (1C pulse)
and charge-discharge at C/3 to calculate the recovered capacity. Calendar life of the
baseline and interim cells were collected by Farasis and the calendar life test for the final
cells was conducted by ANL. As can be seen, there was a relatively low degradation in
performance observed, for example, the capacity loss after 10 months for the cells stored
at 30°C was as low as 6%. Capacity loss after 10 months for the cells stored at 45°C was
as low as 12%. The performance of the interim build cells was comparable to the baseline

build with respect to calendar life. Capacity fade of the final cell build (tested at ANL
facility) was unexpectedly high, as high as 4% loss at 30°C in 2 months.

It is perplexing that the final cell builds showed faster degradation under calendar life
testing compared to the baseline or interim build cells. While there was a clear trend for
increased cycle life over the course of the project, this trend was not observed for the
calendar life. One possibility is that the cell format used for the current project, ~ 0.5 Ah
pouch, is suitable for demonstrating cycle life, but not calendar life. This topic can be
discussed between Group14 and DOE beyond the conclusion of the project. It is
important to note that Group14’s SCC55® has an extremely well received by battery
makers and OEMs and is currently employed in multiple batteries already in commercial
products1,2. Based on this commercial traction for the material, calendar life has thus far
not been an impediment for using the material in commercial-scale silicon batteries.

Conclusions
This project funded by the DOE provided a unique opportunity for the team of world-class
partners to come together with innovative LIB technologies and expertise to optimize and
enhance high Si-content anodes, particularly with economically scalable Group14 Si-C
material. Over the course of the project, we achieved the proposed targets through
combining Group14’s silicon-carbon anode material, novel carbon additives, and binders
for the anode along with high performance co-solvent electrolyte additives. The anode
configuration was designed to achieve a projected >340 Wh/kg and >750Wh/L energy
density when scaled to a fully optimized battery. The objective to demonstrate ≥1000
cycle stability was successfully achieved with actual cycle life above 80% retention for
well over 1500 cycles in the two final cell builds. Based on this information, in discussion
with the DOE we have agreement that we have met the project milestones and
deliverables.
