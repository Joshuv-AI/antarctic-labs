EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The project will require significant space over the lifetime of the project to successfully execute design,
technology maturation, component testing and verification, and final removal and installation. The types of
space required falls into three general categories: office, storage, and laboratory. Figure 1 below
summarizes the project space requirements by project lifecycle. The specific color coding indicates the
availability (and potential risk to the project) of the particular type of space: green indicates space that is
already allocated and in use by the LAMP project; yellow indicates space that is expected to be easily
transitioned from its current use to the project; red indicates a space that is use by multiple users or other
projects, and will most likely require high-level negotiations and planning to become available to the
project.
Figure 2 summarizes the LAMP Space Requirements by calendar year and type throughout the duration of
the project. As can be seen in Fig. 2, the maximum space needed by the project exceeds 40,000 square feet,
with an average space requirement over the lifetime of the project equal to approximately 25,000 square
feet.

1 Introduction
The goal of LAMP is to replace obsolete and near-end-of-life systems of the LANSCE linear accelerator
with modern systems. The project scope replaces the Cockcroft-Walton injectors with a modern radio-
frequency quadrupole (RFQ) injector and the drift-tube linac (DTL). It also includes a full test of the new
front-end from ion beam sources, through the new RFQ, and followed by limited testing of drift-tube linac
beam capture and acceleration. This modernization will ensure the long-term viability of LANSCE and
enhance its capability to respond to current and future NNSA mission needs through increased operational
reliability and performance.
Figure 3 shows a conceptual layout of the Front-End Upgrade. The DTL conceptual layout is not shown.
The preconceptual injector consists of two independent, nearly identical beamlines for H+ and H- beams,
merged into a common beamline upstream of the RFQ. The H- beamline consists of two H- ion sources,
required to provide optimal beam delivery to all H- experimental areas.
Each beamline contains a deflector, a chopper, a pre-buncher, a low-frequency buncher (in the H- beamline
only), and focusing solenoids and quadrupoles. Emittance measurement stations are included to match the
beam to key beamline points. The first solenoids provide transverse matching of the beam from ion source
to deflector, generating a waist in the middle of the deflector. The requisite time structure for each of the
120 Hz cycles is created by deflecting the beam between cycles. Quadrupoles are used to create the beam
waist in the low-frequency buncher and in the center of the chopper (H- only). The last set of quadrupoles
in each LEBT help to match the beam to the RFQ.
The Medium Energy Beam Transport (MEBT) follows the RFQ. It includes several sets of quadrupole
magnets and multiple RF re-buncher cavities to match the output beam from the RFQ into the DTL
structure. Several re-buncher cavities are required downstream of the bender that is used to combine both
the H+ and H- beams to maintain a sufficiently bunched beam for matching and injection into the DTL. The
additional choppers in the MEBT are used to reduce the side lobes (leading and trailing beam bunches) in
the compressed H- beam being sent to the Weapons Neutron Research (WNR) facility. Emittance
measurement stations are also included in the MEBT. Use of two choppers in the MEBT reduces the
intervene voltages required to deflect the beam.
The DTL consists of six individual tanks, each progressively accelerating the beams from 3 MeV to 100
MeV. The DTL uses permanent magnet quadrupole magnets for transverse beam focusing. There are
additional magnets for beam matching and beam diagnostics in the gaps between each DTL tank.

2 LAMP Project Schedule and Phases
LAMP hardware development and testing will be divided into two main phases:
 • Technology Maturation
           − From CD-0 through CD-2/3 (FY25-FY29)
 − Needs defined by the Technology Readiness Assessments (TRAs) and the Technology
 Maturation Plan (TMP).
 − The RFQ Test Stand will be used to demonstrate several Critical Technology Elements
 including WNR beam pulse structure and intensity through an RFQ, demonstration of
 simultaneous dual species (H+ and H-) acceleration through an RFQ, and acceleration of
 pre-bunched and chopped beam through an RFQ.
 − Conceptual physics and engineering design activities.

 • Design, Assembly, and Testing of the Final Design (Final Design FY27, Assembly & Testing
        FY28-FY31)
 − Long lead procurements will start at CD-3a sometime after CD-1 but before CD-2/3
 − The major procurements will begin at CD-2/3 and last up through installation and start of
 operations at CD-4.
 − Assembly, testing, and qualification of RFQ Injector and DTL subsystem components.
 − LAMP Accelerator Test (LAT) – An integrated system test of the 3-MeV RFQ Injector
 and the first DTL tank will be conducted.

Figures 4 and 5 show the LAMP and Scorpius project schedules, respectively. Eventually, integration of
these two schedules will be required to resolve any potential space use conflicts between the two projects.
LAMP expects to use some space currently being occupied by the Scorpius Project. There may also be
other space issues between other users that need to be resolved and planned for, for example, there are
several collocated users in the TA-53, Building 365 tunnel.

3 Project Scope of Work
The following technical resources and capabilities will support the execution of the LAMP project:
 - Physics and engineering design and simulations
 - Prototype development and testing
 - Ancillary equipment to operate the LAMP equipment.
 - Qualification and acceptance of major items (e.g. production accelerator modules, magnets, etc.)
 prior to installation at LANSCE.
 - Receipt inspection, storage, and inventory tracking
 - Operation and qualification of the RFQ Injector and DTL up to 10-MeV. The integrated test will
 comprise the following systems:
 o Production RFQ injector system comprised of dual ion sources, LEBT, and 3-MeV RFQ
 o MEBT to match the 3-MeV beam into the DTL accelerator.
 o A single DTL tank accelerating the beam from 3 MeV to approximately 10 MeV.
 o Post-acceleration beam optics, beam dump and associated diagnostics
 o A separate control center to remotely operate the integrated tests.
 - Development of a new SAD and ASE for both the 10-MeV front end configuration testing in the
 ADEF tunnel may be required. Updates to both documents will be required for commissioning
 and operation of the full upgrade.
 - Installation and commissioning including the new 100-MeV front end.
 - Removal and disposition of legacy accelerator equipment, including activated components.

4 Facility and Space Requirements
This section summarizes the facility and space requirements to execute all phases of LAMP agnostic to
facility or space location. Specific facility and space requirements for each phase of the project will be
described in separate sections below.
The following facility and space infrastructure required are:
 − Office and laboratory space
 − Assembly, testing, and storage space
 − Power
 − Cooling water
 − Radiation shielding and radiation safety interlock systems.
 − Overhead cranes
 − Gas systems
Table 1 summarizes the minimum facility infrastructure requirements to support LAMP. Table 2
identifies the gas systems that will be required of the facility, either as a source, or accommodated as a
stand-alone ancillary system (e.g., SF6 cart). Table 3 summarizes the estimated space needs by category
to support LAMP.
It should be noted that some aspects of LAMP technology maturation and integrated front-end testing will
have special facility requirements. For example, the integrated test accelerator will produce up to 20-mA
peak beam current proton and H- ion beams with energies up to 10 MeV during operation, with average
beam power up to 40 kW. Radiation generated during normal test operations and potential accident
scenarios will require radiation safety analysis to ensure appropriate shielding for personnel protection is
provided. This will also apply to the RFQ Test Stand operations during the technology maturation phase

Conclusions
The facility and space requirements for the LAMP project have been estimated and categorized by project
lifecycle including technology maturation, component testing and verification, and final removal and
installation. The types of space required falls into three general categories: office, storage, and laboratory.
Specific, preferred locations for each type of required space have been identified.
Some spaces have already been allocated to the project and are in use today, however, other spaces needed
to successfully complete the project at present are in use by multiple users and/or projects. These spaces
include the TA-53, Building 365 tunnel and Area A. It is recommended that negotiations and prioritization
of these spaces begin soon to minimize the impact to the project.
