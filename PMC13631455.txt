Postoperative patient experience following different minimally invasive surgical approaches for unruptured intracranial aneurysms: Superciliary approach versus trans-scalp lateral supraorbital approach
Authors: Myungsoo Kim, Jaechan Park, Wonsoo Son, Dong-Hun Kang, Im Hee Shin, Yeon Ran Hong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjective This prospective study compared postoperative patient experience between the superciliary approach (supra-eyebrow incision) and the trans-scalp lateral supraorbital approach (scalp incision) for unruptured anterior circulation aneurysms.Methods Patients (n=111) undergoing a superciliary or trans-scalp lateral supraorbital approach completed a six-item daily Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) symptom questionnaire (wound-related pain, generalized headache, depressed mood, fatigue, decreased appetite, and nausea) and underwent neurological examinations for 5 days postoperatively.Results Surgical outcomes, including clip placement adequacy and complications, did not differ between groups. On postoperative day (POD) 5, the superciliary group reported significantly less wound-related pain (3.34 vs. 4.70, p=0.003), headache (3.54 vs. 5.16, p=0.001), depressed mood (2.33 vs. 3.44, p=0.018), fatigue (3.11 vs. 4.46, p=0.009), and decreased appetite (3.61 vs. 5.00, p= 0.007) than the trans-scalp group. Stepwise regression identified the trans-scalp approach as a dominant predictor of wound-related pain, headache, depressed mood, and fatigue. Significant mood depression (VAS ≥5) occurred in 10 patients (16.4%) in the superciliary group versus 17 (34.0%) in the trans-scalp group on POD 5, and depressed mood correlated significantly with fatigue, decreased appetite, nausea, and headache.Conclusions The superciliary approach was associated with less postoperative pain and discomfort than the trans-scalp lateral supraorbital approach in the early period after successful aneurysm clipping. The trans-scalp approach was a dominant determinant of wound-related pain, headache, depressed mood, and fatigue, and depressed mood correlated with other postoperative symptoms in both groups.Keywords: Craniotomy, Intracranial aneurysm, Minimally invasive surgical procedures, Operative surgical procedure

INTRODUCTION

A variety of minimally invasive surgical approaches have been attempted for clipping intracranial aneurysms to reduce surgical traumatization while achieving surgical purposes. Reduced craniotomy size and tissue damage using a keyhole concept decreased intraoperative blood loss, occurrence of epidural hematoma, wound-related pain, scarring, risk of infection, hospital stay, and recovery time [4-6,10,12-14,16,17,19,23,27].

In comparison with a conventional pterional craniotomy (fronto-temporo-sphenoidal craniotomy), a superciliary approach, as described by Perneczky et al. [7,21,29] and a lateral supraorbital approach, described by Hernesniemi et al. [8] create a smaller lateral supraorbital craniotomy using a keyhole concept [31-33]. They use the same surgical corridor via a lateral supraorbital craniotomy, but the location and amount of surgical traumatization to the pericranial soft tissue is quite different. A superciliary approach uses a short supra-eyebrow skin incision with the incision of the underlying frontalis and corrugator supercilii, while a lateral supraorbital approach uses a fronto-temporal scalp incision behind the hairline with the incision of the underlying temporalis muscle [8,18,25]. To the authors’ knowledge, there are no comparative studies between the superciliary approach and the lateral supraorbital approach.

In recent years, patient experience beyond the surgeons’ viewpoint following minimally invasive surgical procedures has been highlighted in many surgical areas [2,3,9,24,30]. In addition to the surgeons’ experience and understanding of the surgical procedures, patient experience of the procedure is also important and should be deeply investigated.

Thus, the current authors investigated and compared not only the surgical outcomes but also the postoperative patient experience for the first postoperative week, often the most difficult period for patients, following the superciliary approach and trans-scalp lateral supraorbital approach for unruptured anterior circulation aneurysms. Patients’ sufferings, including surgical wound pain, headache, and general symptoms (depressed mood, fatigue, decreased appetite, and nausea), were investigated after the surgical procedures.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Patient population

From July 2022 to June 2023, 178 patients underwent surgical clipping for unruptured anterior circulation aneurysms using a superciliary approach or trans-scalp lateral supraorbital approach, among whom a total of 111 patients completed the daily symptom survey and underwent neurological examinations for 5 days postoperatively and were included in this study.

The inclusion criteria for this study were as follows: 1) age >20 years, 2) diagnosis of unruptured, intracranial aneurysms smaller than 10 mm in the anterior circulation, 3) treatment of the aneurysms with surgical clipping via a superciliary approach or trans-scalp lateral supraorbital approach, and 4) completion of the daily symptom survey, covering 6 common symptoms, from postoperative day 1 to day 5. The exclusion criteria included: 1) treatment of the aneurysms using another conventional surgical approach, such as a pterional approach or interhemispheric approach, 2) bilateral craniotomies for bilateral intracranial aneurysms, 3) history of a subarachnoid hemorrhage, 4) history of a craniotomy for any other reason, and 5) history of any psychiatric disorder, such as a mood or eating disorder. Following the approval of the research design by the Institutional Review Board (IRB No. 2021-03-020), a single-center prospective study was conducted.

Data collection

For the patients who underwent a superciliary approach or trans-scalp lateral supraorbital approach to clip unruptured aneurysms, the medical records were reviewed to obtain relevant clinical information, and all radiological data, including the preoperative digital subtraction angiography, postoperative computed tomography (CT), and postoperative computed tomography angiography (CTA), were reviewed.

The adequacy of the aneurysm clip placement was evaluated using 3D CTA on the first day after the operation. The Sindou 5-grade classification system was applied to any aneurysmal remnants after clipping [26]. The grade 1 aneurysmal remnant is a tiny remnant less than 50% of the aneurysm neck size.

The patients underwent medical and neurological examinations every day during admission. In addition, to evaluate postoperative pain and general symptoms, they were asked to complete a non-neurological daily symptom questionnaire postoperatively for 5 days (postoperative day 1 to 5). Postoperative day 0, the day of surgery, was not included in the daily symptom survey.

The postoperative symptom questionnaire included 6 items: surgical wound-related pain, generalized headache that is felt all over the head, depressed mood, fatigue, decreased appetite, and nausea (Table 1). Each day, the patients were asked to rate the severity of these symptoms on a Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) from 0 (no symptom at all) to 10 (worst symptom imaginable or unbearable symptom).

A standardized postoperative analgesic protocol was applied uniformly to both surgical approach groups throughout the study period.

Surgical procedures

For the superciliary approach, a very small, lateral supraorbital craniotomy with a diameter ≤2 cm is created using a supra-eyebrow skin incision that starts from the midpupillary line and extends laterally, measuring 3.5 cm in length (Fig. 1) [12,18,25]. Meanwhile, for the lateral supraorbital approach, a small, lateral supraorbital craniotomy with a diameter of 3-4 cm is created using a scalp incision behind the hairline in a gentle C shape and a one-layer myocutaneous flap (Fig. 2) [8]. The scalp incision extends from the midline to the level above the pinna and measures about 15 cm in length.

Illustration of a superciliary approach. The skin incision (dashed line) is located along the superciliary arch 1 cm from the supraorbital margin, starting from the midpupillary line and measuring 3.5 cm in length. A supraorbital mini-craniotomy with a diameter ≤2 cm is created. The temporalis muscle is slightly (less than 1 cm) cut to expose a site for a frontobasal lateral burrhole.

Illustration of a lateral supraorbital approach. After a long scalp incision (dashed line) behind the hairline, the scalp and temporalis muscle are elevated together as a myocutaneous flap and retracted forward. A lateral supraorbital craniotomy with a diameter of 3-4 cm is created.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analyses were performed with the aid of commercially available statistics software (IBM SPSS version 19.0; SPSS, Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). To explore and compare the daily postoperative symptom VAS scores between a superciliary approach and a lateral supraorbital approach, a two-sample t-test as a univariate analysis was performed for each symptom.

Pearson’s test was used to analyze the correlation between depressed mood and other postoperative symptoms. In addition, a stepwise multiple linear regression as a multivariate analysis was performed to identify the dominant factor among the postoperative symptoms. The significance level for the statistical analyses was set at 0.05.

RESULTS

Patient characteristics

A total of 111 patients were deemed eligible for this study. They underwent a superciliary (n=61) or lateral supraorbital approach (n=50) for one to three unruptured intracranial aneurysms, and then completed the postoperative daily symptom questionnaire. The clipped aneurysms (n=118) were located at the internal carotid artery (ICA; n=16, 13.6%), middle cerebral artery (MCA; n=73, 61.9%), and anterior cerebral artery (ACA; n=29, 24.6%), including an anterior communicating artery and A1 segment. The diameter of the clipped aneurysms ranged from 3 mm to 8.6 mm. The patient population consisted of 43 men (38.7%) and 68 women (61.3%) with a mean age of 61.9±8.8 years (range, 36-78 years).

The patient characteristics of the unruptured aneurysms are summarized according to the surgical approach, superciliary approach versus trans-scalp lateral supraorbital approach, in Table 1. The superciliary approach group (n=61) consisted of 24 men (39.3%) and 37 women (60.7%) with a mean age of 61.4±8.3 years (range 44-78 years), while the lateral supraorbital approach group (n=50) consisted of 19 men (38.0%) and 31 women (62.0%) with a mean age of 62.6±9.4 years (range, 36-78 years). Both treatment groups had comparable gender and age profiles. Meanwhile, the aneurysm location profiles were different between the two groups. Of note, although aneurysm location was included as a dummy variable in the stepwise multiple linear regression analysis, it was not selected as a significant predictor for any of the postoperative symptoms, indicating that this baseline discrepancy did not bias the outcomes.

Surgical outcomes

The surgical outcomes of a superciliary approach and trans-scalp lateral supraorbital approach are summarized in Table 1. There were no significant between-group differences with regard to the surgical results, i.e., the adequacy of clip placement to the aneurysm and associated surgical complications including symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage or infarct, symptomatic epidural hemorrhage or subdural hemorrhage, and procedure-related neurological deficits.

The state of clip placement to the aneurysm neck, assessed using a postoperative CTA, was complete without a significant residual sac in all the patients except a patient with an anterior choroidal artery (AChA) aneurysm, which was clipped via a superciliary approach. A branch of the AChA adhered to the posterior wall of the AChA aneurysm base, resulting in a grade 1 (less than 50% of the aneurysm neck size) aneurysm neck remnant after aneurysm clipping, according to the Sindou 5-grade classification system [26].

Postoperative CT scans demonstrated an epidural hematoma larger than 10 mL only in one patient in the trans-scalp lateral supraorbital approach group. This patient with an epidural hematoma of 20 mL was asymptomatic, and the hematoma did not require surgical removal. Meanwhile, there was no epidural hematoma larger than 10 mL in the superciliary approach group. In addition, there were no remarkable subdural hematomas in either approach group.

No postoperative neurological deficits were observed in either group. Postoperative CT scans of the brain revealed no symptomatic intracerebral hematoma or symptomatic cerebral infarct in either group.

Results of symptom assessment questionnaires following surgical procedures

Postoperative pain and general symptoms were assessed on postoperative day (POD) 1 to 5 and compared between the superciliary approach group and trans-scalp lateral supraorbital approach group, as presented by the mean VAS scores and standard deviations in Table 2. The patients who underwent the superciliary approach reported significantly less severe symptoms, including surgical wound-related pain, generalized headache, depressed mood, fatigue, and decreased appetite on POD 5.

The surgical wound-related pain was most severe on POD 1 and improved day by day. The wound-related pain was worse in the trans-scalp approach group compared to the superciliary approach group. On POD 5, the mean wound-related pain VAS scores were significantly different between the superciliary approach group and the trans-scalp approach group (3.34±2.26 vs. 4.70±2.49, p=0.003), with the superciliary approach group reporting less wound-related pain than the trans-scalp approach group. On POD 1 to 4, the difference in the severity of the wound-related pain was not statistically significant between the superciliary approach and trans-scalp approach groups, but there was still a clear trend toward significance (mean±SD: 5.57±2.65 vs. 6.58±2.87, p=0.058, POD 1; 4.57±2.56 vs. 5.54±2.77, p=0.059, POD 2; 4.49±2.48 vs. 5.34±2.88, p=0.099, POD 3; 3.89±2.48 vs. 4.72±2.69, p=0.092, POD 4).

Generalized headache was also most severe on POD 1, and decreased with each passing day for both groups. On POD 5, the mean generalized headache VAS scores were significantly different between the superciliary approach group and the trans-scalp approach group (3.54±2.23 in the superciliary approach group vs. 5.16±2.85 in the trans-scalp approach group, p=0.001), with the superciliary approach group reporting milder headaches than the trans-scalp approach group.

Some mood depression was reported postoperatively, and the between-group difference was statistically significant for most of the period. Significant mood depression with a VAS score ≥5 was reported in 10 patients (16.4%) in the superciliary approach group and 21 patients (42.0%) in the trans-scalp approach group on POD 1. The mood depression was sustained throughout the investigation period. On POD 5, mood depression with a VAS score ≥5 was reported in 10 patients (16.4%) in the superciliary approach group and 17 patients (34.0%) in the trans-scalp approach group. Overall, the superciliary approach group reported significantly less mood depression than the trans-scalp approach group. On POD 1, 2, 3, and 5, the mean mood depression VAS scores were significantly lower in the superciliary approach group than the trans-scalp approach group (2.48±2.33 vs. 3.74±2.75, p=0.011, POD 1; 2.36±2.11 vs. 3.58±2.79, p=0.012, POD 2; 2.46±2.19 vs. 3.50±2.72, p=0.031, POD 3; 2.33±1.95 vs. 3.44±2.73, p=0.018, POD 5).

Some fatigue was reported postoperatively. On POD 1, severe fatigue with a VAS score ≥7 was reported by 16 patients (26.2%) in the superciliary approach group and 17 patients (34.0%) in the trans-scalp approach group. Overall, the superciliary approach group reported less fatigue than the trans-scalp approach group. On POD 3 and 5, the mean fatigue VAS scores were significantly lower in the superciliary approach group than the trans-scalp approach group (3.95±2.87 vs. 5.08±2.69, p=0.036, POD 3; 3.11±2.17 vs. 4.46±2.98, p=0.009, POD 5).

Decreased appetite was reported postoperatively. On POD 1, severe appetite decrease with a VAS score ≥7 was reported by 13 patients (21.3%) in the superciliary approach group and 19 patients (38.0%) in the trans-scalp approach group. On POD 3, 4, and 5, the mean decreased appetite VAS scores were significantly lower in the superciliary approach group than the trans-scalp approach group (4.33±2.93 vs. 6.10±2.98, p=0.002, POD 3; 4.02±2.49 vs. 5.56±2.84, p=0.003, POD 4; 3.61±2.43 vs. 5.00±2.93, p=0.007, POD 5).

Some patients reported nausea postoperatively, yet there was no between-group difference. On POD 1, 10 (16.4%) patients reported severe nausea with a VAS score ≥7 in the superciliary approach group, while 13 (26.0%) patients reported severe nausea in the trans-scalp approach group. The postoperative nausea improved slowly over several days.

Correlation between depressed mood and other non-neurological symptoms

Some mood depression was reported postoperatively and positively correlated with other postoperative non-neurological symptoms. For the patients who experienced a trans-scalp lateral supraorbital approach, the correlations with depressed mood were all positively higher than 0.5 and statistically significant with p<0.01 (Table 3). The highest positive correlation was with fatigue (r=0.727), followed by nausea (r=0.626), generalized headaches (r=0.595), decreased appetite (r=0.566), and surgical wound-related pain (r=0.525).

For the patients who experienced a superciliary approach, the correlation between depressed mood and surgical wound-related pain was low (r=0.247) and not statistically significant. However, the other variable correlations with depression were all positive and statistically significant with p<0.01 (Table 3). Crucial determinants of postoperative non-neurological symptoms

For the stepwise multiple linear regression to identify the dominant variables determining the postoperative symptoms on POD 5, age, sex, aneurysm location (ICA, MCA, or ACA), and surgical technique (superciliary approach or trans-scalp lateral supraorbital approach) were all inserted as explanatory variables for each postoperative symptom. As the sex, aneurysm location, and surgical technique were categorical variables, they were regarded as dummy variables for defining the reference group (sex: male, surgical technique: superciliary approach, aneurysm location: ICA).

The surgical technique (trans-scalp lateral supraorbital approach) was selected as a dominant variable for surgical wound-related pain, generalized headache, depressed mood, and fatigue with a positive β coefficient (surgical wound-related pain: β=1.356, generalized headache: β=1.619, depressed mood: β=1.112, fatigue: β=1.264) and statistical significance of variable (surgical wound-related pain: t (p)=3.008 (0.003), generalized headache: t (p)=3.359 (0.001), depressed mood: t (p)=2.498 (0.014), fatigue: t (p)=2.632 (0.010)).

In addition, age was also selected as a dominant variable for decreased appetite and fatigue (decreased appetite: β=0.076, t (p)=2.751 (0.007), fatigue: β=0.064, t (p)=2.381 (0.019)).

Using the selected dominant variables, the results of the stepwise regression model were statistically significant (surgical wound-related pain: F (p)=9.050 (0.003), generalized headache: F (p)=11.282 (0.001), depressed mood: F (p)=6.239 (0.014), fatigue: F (p)=6.778 (0.002), decreased appetite: F (p)=7.506 (0.001)) (Table 4).

DISCUSSION

In the current study, the patients who underwent surgery in the superciliary approach reported fewer symptoms and discomfort postoperatively compared to those who underwent surgery in a trans-scalp lateral supraorbital approach. The trans-scalp lateral supraorbital approach was a dominant variable determining surgical wound-related pain, generalized headache, depressed mood, and fatigue.

Some mood depression has been reported by the patients following surgical treatment of unruptured intracranial aneurysms. In particular, the patients with a trans-scalp lateral supraorbital approach reported a significantly greater severity of mood depression than the patients with a superciliary approach in the current study. The trans-scalp lateral supraorbital approach produces a large operative wound in the fronto-temporal area, considerable swelling of the scalp and temporalis, and surgical wound-related pain, which may lead to a depressed mood and gloomy attitude. In addition, the current study showed that postoperative mood depression was positively and significantly correlated with other postoperative symptoms including generalized headache, fatigue, decreased appetite, and nausea. The superciliary approach is considered to be minimally invasive not only physically, but also in regard to the mood of patients.

The present study used the VAS score to monitor the severity of depressed mood. The VAS is a psychometric response scale used to measure subjective characteristics or attitudes in questionnaires [1,11]. Various depression rating scales or depression inventories can be used for persons with depression, which is typically characterized by a persistent depressed mood. However, the real-time monitoring of mood dynamics can be depicted efficiently using a VAS score [28].

The superciliary approach and lateral supraorbital approach have different advantages and shortcomings as surgical approaches. A minimally invasive surgical approach has been known to have the common advantages of smaller operative wounds, shorter operative duration, less intraoperative bleeding, less occurrence of postoperative epidural hemorrhage, lower risk of intracranial infection, and shorter length of hospital stay in comparison with a conventional approach [4-6,12-14,18,25,31,33]. Meanwhile, temporary palsy of the frontal branch of the facial nerve and temporary supraorbital hypesthesia are already known to be approach-related complications that can be encountered after a superciliary approach, whereas painful mastication is commonly reported after a trans-scalp lateral supraorbital approach with incision of the temporalis muscle [22].

In the superciliary approach, transient palsy of the frontalis muscle can occur due to stretch-induced nerve injuries of the frontal branch of the facial nerve during the retraction of the operative wound, resulting in neuropraxia or axonotmesis [15,20]. The occurrence and severity of temporary palsy of the frontalis muscle after the superciliary approach is dependent on the location of the frontal branch of the facial nerve from the supraorbital margin and can be predicted preoperatively. Percutaneous mapping of the frontal branch using a nerve conduction study was performed preoperatively to assess the risk of postoperative frontalis palsy after a superciliary approach in our previous study, in which the patients (11.5%) with a compound muscle action potential at the lowest location, 1.0 cm above the supraorbital margin, showed a high incidence (83.3%) of severe frontalis palsy lasting 2-5 months after surgery [15].

The cosmetic concerns and results are different between the superciliary approach and trans-scalp lateral supraorbital approach. The trans-scalp lateral supraorbital approach can produce an anterior temporal hollow and long operative scar at the front of the head just behind the hairline, with or without associated hair loss and disfigurement. The cosmetic results can be problematic for patients with baldness or a high forehead. Meanwhile, the superciliary approach leaves a very short, yet noticeable operative scar on the face, although the supra-eyebrow skin incision can heal well with a nearly invisible scar in younger patients. As the present study assessed only the first postoperative week, these long-term cosmetic differences were not directly measured; however, they are likely to meaningfully influence overall patient satisfaction beyond the early postoperative period, and should be considered together with the short-term symptom advantage of the superciliary approach demonstrated here.

The current study is limited because it is based on a self-report study, which can have a response bias. However, the symptom questionnaire is the only practical method to investigate patient experience, including subjective postoperative symptoms. The current prospective study was based on a well-designed questionnaire, allowing for a high level of confidence in the comparison of postoperative experience following surgical procedures. Although a standardized postoperative analgesic protocol was applied uniformly to both groups, the individual consumption of rescue analgesics was not prospectively tracked, and residual confounding from unmeasured variation in rescue medication use cannot be entirely excluded. The current investigation of postoperative patient experience was limited to the first postoperative week, the most difficult period for patients. Thus, future studies need to include long-term follow-ups regarding patient experience and satisfaction. To our knowledge, this is the first study to investigate and compare non-neurological postoperative symptoms following different minimally invasive surgical approaches for intracranial aneurysms. Despite such limitations, we consider our data a useful addition to the extremely limited literature available on this topic of patient experience following a craniotomy.

CONCLUSIONS

The patients who underwent the superciliary approach reported less symptoms and discomfort compared to those who underwent a trans-scalp lateral supraorbital approach in the early postoperative period following successful clip placement for unruptured intracranial aneurysms. The trans-scalp lateral supraorbital approach was a dominant variable determining surgical wound-related pain, generalized headache, depressed mood, and fatigue. Postoperative mood depression was correlated with other postoperative symptoms such as fatigue, decreased appetite, nausea, and generalized headache in both surgical groups. Nonetheless, the selection between the two approaches should not be based solely on early postoperative comfort; the superciliary approach carries a risk of frontalis muscle palsy and facial scarring, whereas the lateral supraorbital approach is associated with temporalis muscle atrophy and painful mastication, and these long-term cosmetic and functional trade-offs should also be weighed in the surgical decision-making process.
