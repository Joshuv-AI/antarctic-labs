Power series for roots of a trinomial and Kummer-like identities for higher order hypergeometric series Journal: (internal report CC26-4)
S. R. Mane

Abstract
We study the trinomial equation x n + p ​ x + q = 0 x^{n}+px+q=0 . Here p p and q q are both real and nonzero. For n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 , expressions for the roots have been published as hypergeometric series in powers of the parameter q n − 1 / p n q^{n-1}/p^{n} . For the special case of the cubic ( n = 3 n=3 ), we employ Kummer’s identities to derive alternative series solutions in powers of the discriminant D D , and also series in powers of 1 / D 1/D . We next derive new series, in powers of D D and also in powers of 1 / D 1/D , for all n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 . The resulting series suggest identities analogous to Kummer’s identities, for higher order hypergeometric series.

Introduction

We derive series solutions for the roots of the trinomial equation

x^{n}+px+q=0\,.

(1.1)

Here
p
and
q
are both real and nonzero.
The reason for this constraint is simple.
If
p=0
then
x^{n}+q=0
and the roots are the
n^{th}
roots of
-q
.
If
q=0
then
x^{n}+px=0
and the origin is a root and the remaining roots are the
(n-1)^{th}
roots of
-p
.
In principle, our series below (as well as those published in
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
)
are also valid for complex nonzero
p
and
q
, but branch cuts in the complex plane are required to obtained well-defined expressions.
We shall assume
p
and
q
are real in this note.
We note the following general symmetries of the roots of eq. (
1.1
).

1.

Suppose
n
is even. If we reverse the sign of
p
, eq. (
1.1
) is unchanged if we reverse the sign of
x
.

2.

Suppose
n
is odd. If we reverse the sign of
q
, eq. (
1.1
) is unchanged if we reverse the sign of
x
.

Birkeland
[
1
]
derived hypergeometric series solutions for the roots of the more general trinomial equation
x^{n}=gx^{s}+\beta
.
For the case
n=3
, Birkeland published the explicit series solutions; see
B
.
The more general trinomial equation has also been solved using Fuss-Catalan series, etc.
See for example, early work by Euler
[
2
]
and Lambert
[
3
,
4
]
.
A summary is given in
[
5
]
, which also contains additional references.

Here we derive alternative series solutions of eq. (
1.1
) in powers of the discriminant.
The discriminant
D
of the trinomial in eq. (
1.1
) is

D=(-1)^{n}(n-1)^{n-1}p^{n}-n^{n}q^{n-1}\,.

(1.2)

We treat the case
n=3
(the cubic trinomial) in detail in Section
2
.
In Sections
3
and
4
,
we treat the case
n>3
and derive series for the roots in powers of
D
and
1/D
.
Fuss-Catalan and hypergeometric series for the roots are presented in Section
5
.
The Appendices list relevant identities for
{}_{2}F_{1}
hypergeometric functions
and we employ Kummer’s identities to transform Birkeland’s series solutions for the trinomial cubic
[
1
]
to series in powers of
D
and
1/D
.
We also derive a different series, in powers of
D
, in
C
, for odd
n\geq 3
.

We dispense with the quadratic equation
x^{2}+px+q=0
here.
The discriminant is
D=p^{2}-4q
and the roots
r_{\pm}
are given by

r_{\pm}=\frac{-p\pm\sqrt{D}}{2}\,.

(1.3)

The roots are functions of
\sqrt{D}
, basically a terminating series.
The formalism in this note is unnecessary for a quadratic.
However, note the following. The roots
r_{\pm}
can also be written as

r_{\pm}=\frac{2q}{-p\mp\sqrt{D}}\,.

(1.4)

In this form, the expressions for
r_{\pm}
can be expanded as non-terminating series in powers of
\sqrt{D}/p
,
where the series converges absolutely if and only if
|D|<|p|^{2}
.
However, we do not pursue the matter further.

2
Cubic trinomial

We process Cardano’s solution for the depressed cubic equation
x^{3}+px+q=0
, which is a trinomial.
The discriminant is
D=-4p^{3}-27q^{2}
.
Define
u
and
v
as follows:

u=\biggl(-\frac{q}{2}+\frac{\sqrt{-D}}{2\sqrt{27}}\biggr)^{1/3}\,,\qquad v=\biggl(-\frac{q}{2}-\frac{\sqrt{-D}}{2\sqrt{27}}\biggr)^{1/3}\,.

(2.1)

Let
\omega=(-1+i\sqrt{3})/2
be a primitive third root of unity. Then the roots are

r_{0}=u+v\,,\qquad r_{1}=\omega u+\omega^{2}v\,,\qquad r_{2}=\omega^{2}u+\omega v\,.

(2.2)

We begin with
r_{0}
. Use eq. (
1.2a
) to obtain

\begin{split}r_{0}&=\Bigl(-\frac{q}{2}\Bigr)^{1/3}\biggl[\,\biggl(1-\frac{\sqrt{-D}}{\sqrt{27}q}\biggr)^{1/3}+\biggl(1+\frac{\sqrt{-D}}{\sqrt{27}q}\biggr)^{1/3}\,\biggr]\\
&=2\Bigl(-\frac{q}{2}\Bigr)^{1/3}\,F\Bigl(-\frac{1}{6},\frac{1}{3};\frac{1}{2};-\frac{D}{27q^{2}}\Bigr)\,.\end{split}

(2.3)

We next process the roots
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
.
For definiteness, let us assume
D<0
, so that
\sqrt{-D}
is real.
(The case
D>0
follows straightforwardly.)
Then use eqs. (
1.2a
) and (
1.2b
) to obtain

\begin{split}r_{1}&=\Bigl(-\frac{q}{2}\Bigr)^{1/3}\biggl\{-\frac{1}{2}\biggl[\biggl(1-\frac{\sqrt{-D}}{\sqrt{27}q}\biggr)^{1/3}+\biggl(1+\frac{\sqrt{-D}}{\sqrt{27}q}\biggr)^{1/3}\biggr]\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad-i\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\biggl[-\biggl(1-\frac{\sqrt{-D}}{\sqrt{27}q}\biggr)^{1/3}+\biggl(1+\frac{\sqrt{-D}}{\sqrt{27}q}\biggr)^{1/3}\biggr]\biggr\}\\
&=-\Bigl(-\frac{q}{2}\Bigr)^{1/3}\biggl\{F\Bigl(-\frac{1}{6},\frac{1}{3};\frac{1}{2};-\frac{D}{27q^{2}}\Bigr)-\frac{i}{\sqrt{3}}\sqrt{-\frac{D}{27q^{2}}}\,F\Bigl(\frac{1}{3},\frac{5}{6};\frac{3}{2};-\frac{D}{27q^{2}}\Bigr)\biggr\}\,.\end{split}

(2.4)

The expression for
r_{2}
is obtained from eq. (
2.4
) by reversing the sign of
i
:

\begin{split}r_{2}&=-\Bigl(-\frac{q}{2}\Bigr)^{1/3}\biggl\{F\Bigl(-\frac{1}{6},\frac{1}{3};\frac{1}{2};-\frac{D}{27q^{2}}\Bigr)+\frac{i}{\sqrt{3}}\sqrt{-\frac{D}{27q^{2}}}\,F\Bigl(\frac{1}{3},\frac{5}{6};\frac{3}{2};-\frac{D}{27q^{2}}\Bigr)\biggr\}\,.\end{split}

(2.5)

Next let us process eq. (
2.3
) using Kummer’s transformation in eq. (
1.1b
).
We attach a prime for reasons to be explained later.

\begin{split}r_{0}^{\prime}&=(-4q)^{1/3}\Bigl(1+\frac{D}{27q^{2}}\Bigr)^{-1/3}\,F\Bigl(\frac{2}{3},\frac{1}{3};\frac{1}{2};-\frac{D}{4p^{3}}\Bigr)\\
&=(-4q)^{1/3}\Bigl(-\frac{27q^{2}}{4p^{3}}\Bigr)^{1/3}\,F\Bigl(\frac{2}{3},\frac{1}{3};\frac{1}{2};-\frac{D}{4p^{3}}\Bigr)\\
&=\frac{3q}{p}\,F\Bigl(\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{3};\frac{1}{2};-\frac{D}{4p^{3}}\Bigr)\,.\end{split}

(2.6)

This is a real root and equals equals Zucker’s expression (
[
6
]
, eq. (2.7)).
Next let us process the second term in eq. (
2.4
), say
F_{s}
,
using Kummer’s transformation in eq. (
1.1a
)

\begin{split}F_{s}&\equiv\frac{i}{\sqrt{3}}\Bigl(-\frac{q}{2}\Bigr)^{1/3}\sqrt{-\frac{D}{27q^{2}}}\,F\Bigl(\frac{1}{3},\frac{5}{6};\frac{3}{2};-\frac{D}{27q^{2}}\Bigr)\\
&=\frac{i}{\sqrt{3}}\Bigl(-\frac{q}{2}\Bigr)^{1/3}\sqrt{-\frac{D}{27q^{2}}}\Bigl(1+\frac{D}{27q^{2}}\Bigr)^{-1/3}\,F\Bigl(\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{3};\frac{3}{2};-\frac{D}{4p^{3}}\Bigr)\\
&=\frac{i}{\sqrt{3}}\Bigl(-\frac{q}{2}\Bigr)^{1/3}\sqrt{-\frac{D}{27q^{2}}}\Bigl(-\frac{27q^{2}}{4p^{3}}\Bigr)^{1/3}\,F\Bigl(\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{3};\frac{3}{2};-\frac{D}{4p^{3}}\Bigr)\\
&=\frac{\sqrt{D}}{6p}\,F\Bigl(\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{3};\frac{3}{2};-\frac{D}{4p^{3}}\Bigr)\,.\end{split}

(2.7)

The roots
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
are given by

r_{1}^{\prime}=-\frac{r_{0}^{\prime}}{2}+F_{s}\,,\qquad r_{2}^{\prime}=-\frac{r_{0}^{\prime}}{2}-F_{s}\,.

(2.8)

Note the following.

1.

The series in eqs. (
2.3
), (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) converge unconditionally if and only if
|D|<|27q^{2}|
, whereas
the series in eqs. (
2.6
) and (

) converge unconditionally if and only if
|D|<|4p^{3}|
.

2.

Hence we attached primes to the second set of roots: the domains of convergence are different,
even though the two sets of roots are the same (when both sets of series converge).

3.

The constraint

is satisfied only if

and
-54q^{2}<4p^{3}<0
.
The constraint

is satisfied for all

Neither series converges if

.

4.

The root
r_{0}^{\prime}
is always real.

5.

If
D>0
, all three roots
r_{0}^{\prime}
,
r_{1}^{\prime}
and
r_{2}^{\prime}
are real.

6.

The presence of the square root
\sqrt{D}
in the expression for

is essential.
If
D>0
, then
r_{1}^{\prime}
and
r_{2}^{\prime}
are real and distinct.
If

r_{1}^{\prime}
and
r_{2}^{\prime}
form a complex conjugate pair.
If

and

Next we treat the domain
|D|>27|q|^{2}
.
To do so, let us write (setting

u=\biggl(\frac{\sqrt{-D}}{2\sqrt{27}}\biggr)^{1/3}\biggl(1-\frac{\sqrt{27}q}{\sqrt{-D}}\biggr)^{1/3}\,,\qquad v=-\biggl(\frac{\sqrt{-D}}{2\sqrt{27}}\biggr)^{1/3}\biggl(1+\frac{\sqrt{27}q}{\sqrt{-D}}\biggr)^{1/3}\,.

(2.9)

To avoid confusion with the series for the roots for
|D|<|27q^{2}|

let us denote the roots by
\rho_{0}
,
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
, with an obvious notation.
Then
\rho_{0}
is given by

\begin{split}\rho_{0}&=u+v\\
&=-\biggl(\frac{\sqrt{-D}}{2\sqrt{27}}\biggr)^{1/3}\biggl[\biggl(1+\frac{\sqrt{27}q}{\sqrt{-D}}\biggr)^{1/3}-\biggl(1-\frac{\sqrt{27}q}{\sqrt{-D}}\biggr)^{1/3}\biggr]\\
&=\frac{2q}{3}\biggl(\frac{\sqrt{-D}}{2\sqrt{27}}\biggr)^{1/3}\biggl(-\frac{27}{D}\biggr)^{1/2}\,F\Bigl(\frac{1}{3},\frac{5}{6};\frac{3}{2};-\frac{27q^{2}}{D}\Bigr)\\
&=-q\Bigl(-\frac{4}{D}\Bigr)^{1/3}\,F\Bigl(\frac{1}{3},\frac{5}{6};\frac{3}{2};-\frac{27q^{2}}{D}\Bigr)\,.\end{split}

(2.10)

Next process
\rho_{1}
as follows:

\begin{split}\rho_{1}&=\omega u+\omega^{2}v\\
&=\biggl(\frac{\sqrt{-D}}{2\sqrt{27}}\biggr)^{1/3}\biggl\{\frac{1}{2}\biggl[\biggl(1+\frac{\sqrt{27}q}{\sqrt{-D}}\biggr)^{1/3}-\biggl(1-\frac{\sqrt{27}q}{\sqrt{-D}}\biggr)^{1/3}\biggr]\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+i\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\biggl[\biggl(1+\frac{\sqrt{27}q}{\sqrt{-D}}\biggr)^{1/3}+\biggl(1-\frac{\sqrt{27}q}{\sqrt{-D}}\biggr)^{1/3}\biggr]\biggr\}\\
&=\biggl(\frac{\sqrt{-D}}{2\sqrt{27}}\biggr)^{1/3}\biggl\{\frac{q}{3}\sqrt{-\frac{27}{D}}\,F\Bigl(\frac{1}{3},\frac{5}{6};\frac{3}{2};-\frac{27q^{2}}{D}\Bigr)+i\sqrt{3}F\Bigl(-\frac{1}{6},\frac{1}{3};\frac{1}{2};-\frac{27q^{2}}{D}\Bigr)\biggr\}\,.\end{split}

(2.11)

The expression for
\rho_{2}
follows similarly, by reversing the sign of
i
:

\begin{split}\rho_{2}&=\omega^{2}u+\omega v\\
&=\biggl(\frac{\sqrt{-D}}{2\sqrt{27}}\biggr)^{1/3}\biggl\{\frac{q}{3}\sqrt{-\frac{27}{D}}\,F\Bigl(\frac{1}{3},\frac{5}{6};\frac{3}{2};-\frac{27q^{2}}{D}\Bigr)-i\sqrt{3}F\Bigl(-\frac{1}{6},\frac{1}{3};\frac{1}{2};-\frac{27q^{2}}{D}\Bigr)\biggr\}\,.\end{split}

(2.12)

Let us process the series in eq. (
2.10
) using Kummer’s transformation in eq. (
1.1d
)

\begin{split}F\Bigl(\frac{1}{3},\frac{5}{6};\frac{3}{2};-\frac{27q^{2}}{D}\Bigr)&=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{3}{2})\Gamma(\frac{1}{3})}{\Gamma(\frac{7}{6})\Gamma(\frac{2}{3})}F\Bigl(\frac{1}{3},\frac{5}{6};\frac{2}{3};1+\frac{27q^{2}}{D}\Bigr)\\
&\quad+\Bigl(1+\frac{27q^{2}}{D}\Bigr)^{1/3}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{3}{2})\Gamma(-\frac{1}{3})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{3})\Gamma(\frac{5}{6})}F\Bigl(\frac{7}{6},\frac{2}{3};\frac{4}{3};1+\frac{27q^{2}}{D}\Bigr)\\

(2.13)

Denote the processed root by
\rho_{0}^{\prime}
, then

\begin{split}\rho_{0}^{\prime}&=-\frac{3q}{2^{2/3}}\Bigl(-\frac{4}{D}\Bigr)^{1/3}\,F\Bigl(\frac{1}{3},\frac{5}{6};\frac{2}{3};-\frac{4p^{3}}{D}\Bigr)+\frac{3q}{2^{4/3}}\Bigl(-\frac{4}{D}\Bigr)^{1/3}\,\Bigl(-\frac{4p^{3}}{D}\Bigr)^{1/3}F\Bigl(\frac{7}{6},\frac{2}{3};\frac{4}{3};-\frac{4p^{3}}{D}\Bigr)\\
&=-3q\Bigl(-\frac{1}{D}\Bigr)^{1/3}\,F\Bigl(\frac{1}{3},\frac{5}{6};\frac{2}{3};-\frac{4p^{3}}{D}\Bigr)+3pq\Bigl(-\frac{1}{D}\Bigr)^{2/3}F\Bigl(\frac{7}{6},\frac{2}{3};\frac{4}{3};-\frac{4p^{3}}{D}\Bigr)\,.\end{split}

(2.14)

Next process the other hypergeometric function in the expression for

:

\begin{split}F\Bigl(-\frac{1}{6},\frac{1}{3};\frac{1}{2};-\frac{27q^{2}}{D}\Bigr)&=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(\frac{1}{3})}{\Gamma(\frac{2}{3})\Gamma(\frac{1}{6})}F\Bigl(-\frac{1}{6},\frac{1}{3};\frac{2}{3};1+\frac{27q^{2}}{D}\Bigr)\\
&\quad+\Bigl(1+\frac{27q^{2}}{D}\Bigr)^{1/3}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(-\frac{1}{3})}{\Gamma(-\frac{1}{6})\Gamma(\frac{1}{3})}F\Bigl(\frac{2}{3},\frac{1}{6};\frac{4}{3};1+\frac{27q^{2}}{D}\Bigr)\\
&=\frac{1}{2^{2/3}}F\Bigl(-\frac{1}{6},\frac{1}{3};\frac{2}{3};-\frac{4p^{3}}{D}\Bigr)+\frac{1}{2^{4/3}}\Bigl(-\frac{4p^{3}}{D}\Bigr)^{1/3}F\Bigl(\frac{2}{3},\frac{1}{6};\frac{4}{3};-\frac{4p^{3}}{D}\Bigr)\,.\end{split}

(2.15)

Let

\begin{split}F_{s}^{\prime}&=\sqrt{3}\biggl(\frac{\sqrt{-D}}{2\sqrt{27}}\biggr)^{1/3}F\Bigl(-\frac{1}{6},\frac{1}{3};\frac{1}{2};-\frac{27q^{2}}{D}\Bigr)\\
&=\sqrt{3}\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{-D}}{2\sqrt{27}}\Bigr)^{1/3}\biggl[\frac{1}{2^{2/3}}F\Bigl(-\frac{1}{6},\frac{1}{3};\frac{2}{3};-\frac{4p^{3}}{D}\Bigr)+\frac{1}{2^{4/3}}\Bigl(-\frac{4p^{3}}{D}\Bigr)^{1/3}F\Bigl(\frac{2}{3},\frac{1}{6};\frac{4}{3};-\frac{4p^{3}}{D}\Bigr)\biggr]\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\biggl[(-D)^{\frac{1}{6}}F\Bigl(-\frac{1}{6},\frac{1}{3};\frac{2}{3};-\frac{4p^{3}}{D}\Bigr)+\frac{p}{(-D)^{\frac{1}{6}}}F\Bigl(\frac{2}{3},\frac{1}{6};\frac{4}{3};-\frac{4p^{3}}{D}\Bigr)\biggr]\,.\end{split}

Then

3
Discriminant series I

3.1
Series in powers of the inverse discriminant

Let us derive a series in powers of
1/D
.
We assume
n
is odd, so
D=-(n-1)^{n-1}p^{n}-n^{n}q^{n-1}
.
Define
\eta=-n^{n}q^{n-1}/D
for use below.
We try a series of the following form, where
G_{1}
is a power series

r=-\frac{q}{p}\,G_{1}(\eta)\,.

(3.1)

Let

G_{1}=1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}\eta^{k}\,.

(3.2)

Here the
\alpha_{k}
are a set of coefficients to be determined.
Substitute into eq. (
1.1
) to obtain

-\frac{q^{n}}{p^{n}}G_{1}^{n}-qG_{1}+q=0\,.

(3.3)

Observe that

\begin{split}-\frac{q^{n}}{p^{n}}=-\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}q^{n}}{(n-1)^{n-1}p^{n}}&=\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}q^{n}}{D+n^{n}q^{n-1}}\\
&=q\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}q^{n-1}}{D}\,\frac{1}{1+n^{n}q^{n-1}/D}\\
&=-q\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}}{n^{n}}\frac{\eta}{1-\eta}\,.\end{split}

(3.4)

Also define a multinomial sum as follows.
Define
\tilde{M}_{0}=\tilde{M}_{1}=0
and for
k\geq 2
:

\tilde{M}_{k}=\sum_{j_{1}+2j_{2}+\dots+(k-1)j_{k-1}=k}\frac{n!}{j_{1}!j_{2}!\dots j_{k-1}!(n-j_{1}-j_{2}-\dots-j_{k-1})!}\alpha_{1}^{,j_{1}}\alpha_{2}^{j_{2}}\dots\alpha_{k-1}^{j_{k-1}}\,.

(3.5)

Then expand in powers of
\eta
to obtain

\begin{split}0&=-\frac{q^{n}}{p^{n}}\biggl(1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}\eta^{k}\biggr)^{n}-q\biggl(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}\eta^{k}\biggr)\\
&=-q\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}}{n^{n}}\eta(1+\eta+\eta^{2}+\dots)\biggl[1+n\alpha_{1}\eta+(n\alpha_{2}+\tilde{M}_{2})\eta^{2}+(n\alpha_{3}+\tilde{M}_{3})\eta^{3}+\dots\biggr]\\
&\qquad-q\biggl(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}\eta^{k}\biggr)\,.\end{split}

(3.6)

We equate terms in powers of
\eta
.
Then

\alpha_{1}=-\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}}{n^{n}}\,.

(3.7)

For
k>1
we obtain a recurrence for
\alpha_{k}
:

\begin{split}\alpha_{k}&=-\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}}{n^{n}}\biggl[1+n\alpha_{1}+(n\alpha_{2}+\tilde{M}_{2})+\dots+(n\alpha_{k-1}+\tilde{M}_{k-1})\biggr]\\
&=-\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}}{n^{n}}\Bigl[1+n(\alpha_{1}+\dots+\alpha_{k-1})+\tilde{M}_{2}+\dots+\tilde{M}_{k-1}\Bigr]\\
&=\alpha_{k-1}-\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}}{n^{n}}(n\alpha_{k-1}+\tilde{M}_{k-1})\\
&=\Bigl(1-\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}}{n^{n-1}}\Bigr)\alpha_{k-1}-\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}}{n^{n}}\tilde{M}_{k-1}\,.\end{split}

(3.8)

The above idea also works for even
n
.
For even
n
, the discriminant is

D=(n-1)^{n-1}p^{n}-n^{n}q^{n-1}
.
Recall
\eta=-(n^{n}q^{n-1})/D
.
Now try a series of the form, where
G_{2}
is a power series:

r=-\frac{q}{p}\,G_{2}(\chi)\,.

(3.9)

Substitute in eq. (
1.1
) to obtain

\frac{q^{n}}{p^{n}}G_{2}^{n}-pG_{2}+q=0\,.

(3.10)

Observe that

\begin{split}\frac{q^{n}}{p^{n}}=\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}q^{n}}{(n-1)^{n-1}p^{n}}&=\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}q^{n}}{D+n^{n}q^{n-1}}\\
&=q\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}q^{n-1}}{D}\,\frac{1}{1+n^{n}q^{n-1}/D}\\
&=-q\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}}{n^{n}}\frac{\eta}{1-\eta}\,.\end{split}

(3.11)

This is the same as for odd
n
in eq. (
3.4
).

All the rest of the formalism above follows. Nothing beyond this point in the previous derivation requires
n
to be odd.

Our series for the root
r
in eq. (
3.1
) satisfies the symmetries listed in the Introduction.
(i) If
n
is even, the discriminant is an even function of
p
. Hence
r
is an odd function of
p
.
(ii) If
n
is odd, the discriminant is an even function of
q
. Hence
r
is an odd function of
q
.

Series in powers of the discriminant

Next let us derive a series in powers of

(3.12)

Let

(3.13)

Substitute in eq. (
1.1

-\frac{q^{n}}{p^{n}}G_{3}^{n}-pG_{3}+q=0\,.

(3.14)

Leverage eq. (
3.4
) to deduce

-\frac{q^{n}}{p^{n}}=-q\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}}{n^{n}}\frac{1/\chi}{1-1/\chi}=q\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}}{n^{n}}\frac{1}{1-\chi}\,.

(3.15)

Also define a multinomial sum as follows.
Define
\tilde{M}_{0}^{\prime}=\tilde{M}_{1}^{\prime}=0
and for
k\geq 2
:

\tilde{M}_{k}^{\prime}=\sum_{j_{1}+2j_{2}+\dots+(k-1)j_{k-1}=k}\frac{n!}{j_{1}!j_{2}!\dots j_{k-1}!(n-j_{1}-j_{2}-\dots-j_{k-1})!}\beta_{1}^{,j_{1}}\beta_{2}^{j_{2}}\dots\beta_{k-1}^{j_{k-1}}\,.

(3.16)

Then expand in powers of
\chi
to obtain

\begin{split}0&=-\frac{q^{n}}{p^{n}}\biggl(1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\beta_{k}\chi^{k}\biggr)^{n}-q\biggl(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\beta_{k}\chi^{k}\biggr)\\
&=q\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}}{n^{n}}(1+\chi+\chi^{2}+\dots)\biggl[1+n\beta_{1}\chi+(n\beta_{2}+\tilde{M}_{2}^{\prime})\chi^{2}+(n\beta_{3}+\tilde{M}_{3}^{\prime})\chi^{3}+\dots\biggr]\\
&\qquad-q\biggl(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\beta_{k}\chi^{k}\biggr)\,.\end{split}

(3.17)

We equate terms in powers of
\chi
.
Then

\beta_{1}=\Bigl(1-\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}}{n^{n-1}}\Bigr)^{-1}\,.

(3.18)

For
k>1
we obtain a recurrence for
\beta_{k}
:

\begin{split}\beta_{k}&=\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}}{n^{n}}\biggl[1+n\beta_{1}+(n\beta_{2}+\tilde{M}_{2}^{\prime})+\dots+(n\beta_{k}+\tilde{M}_{k}^{\prime})\biggr]\\
&=\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}}{n^{n}}\Bigl[1+n(\beta_{1}+\dots+\beta_{k})+\tilde{M}_{2}^{\prime}+\dots+\tilde{M}_{k}^{\prime}\Bigr]\\
&=\beta_{k-1}+\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}}{n^{n}}(n\beta_{k}+\tilde{M}_{k}^{\prime})\\
\Rightarrow\qquad\Bigl(1-\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}}{n^{n-1}}\Bigr)\beta_{k}&=\beta_{k-1}+\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}}{n^{n}}\tilde{M}_{k}^{\prime}\\
\beta_{k}&=\Bigl(1-\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}}{n^{n-1}}\Bigr)^{-1}\biggl[\beta_{k-1}+\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}}{n^{n}}\tilde{M}_{k}^{\prime}\biggr]\,.\end{split}

(3.19)

However, the term
q(n-1)^{n-1}/n^{n}
in eq. (
3.17
) was not cancelled in the above procedure.

The root we derived in eq. (
3.12
) actually solves the equation

r^{n}+pr+q=\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}}{n^{n}}q\,.

(3.20)

However, all is not lost.

The equation we have solved is actually

r^{n}+pr+\Bigl(1-\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}}{n^{n}}\Bigr)q=0\,.

(3.21)

Let us define a modified value
q_{\rm eff,1}
such that

\begin{split}q&=q_{\rm eff,1}\Bigl(1-\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}}{n^{n}}\Bigr)\\
\Rightarrow\qquad q_{\rm eff,1}&=q\Bigl(1-\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}}{n^{n}}\Bigr)^{-1}\,.\end{split}

(3.22)

Then employ our series, replacing
q
by
q_{\rm eff,1}
in our formalism above.
Call the resulting root
r_{\rm eff,1}
, where

D_{\rm eff,1}=-(n-1)^{n-1}p^{n}-n^{n}q_{\rm eff,1}^{n-1}
and
\chi_{\rm eff,1}=-D_{\rm eff,1}/(n^{n}q_{\rm eff,1}^{n-1})

(3.23)

Then

satisfies the original trinomial equation

r_{\rm eff,1}^{n}+pr_{\rm eff,1}+q=0\,.

(3.24)

Admittedly the series for

is in powers of a modified discriminant, using

instead of
q
.

The above idea also works for even
n
.
For even
n
, the discriminant is

D=(n-1)^{n-1}p^{n}-n^{n}q^{n-1}

Now try a series of the form, where
G_{4}
is a power series:

(3.25)

Substitute in eq. (
1.1
) to obtain

\frac{q^{n}}{p^{n}}G_{4}^{n}-pG_{4}+q=0\,.

(3.26)

Leverage eq. (
3.11

\frac{q^{n}}{p^{n}}=-q\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}}{n^{n}}\frac{1/\chi}{1-1/\chi}=q\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}}{n^{n}}\frac{1}{1-\chi}\,.

in eq. (

All the rest of the formalism above follows. Nothing beyond this point in the previous derivation requires

(4.18)

(4.20)

Also define a multinomial sum as follows.
Define
\hat{M}_{0}=\hat{M}_{1}=0
and for
k\geq 2
:

\hat{M}_{k}=\sum_{j_{1}+2j_{2}+\dots+(k-1)j_{k-1}=k}\frac{n!}{j_{1}!j_{2}!\dots j_{k-1}!(n-j_{1}-j_{2}-\dots-j_{k-1})!}\{\hat{\alpha}_{1}^{,j_{1}}\hat{\alpha}_{2}^{j_{2}}\dots\hat{\alpha}_{k-1}^{j_{k-1}}\,.

\begin{split}0&=-\frac{q^{n}}{p^{n}}\biggl(1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\hat{\alpha}_{k}\hat{\eta}^{k}\biggr)^{n}-q\biggl(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\hat{\alpha}_{k}\hat{\eta}^{k}\biggr)\\

&\qquad-q\biggl(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\hat{\alpha}_{k}\hat{\eta}^{k}\biggr)\,.\end{split}

(4.23)

\begin{split}\hat{\alpha}_{k-1}&=\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}}{n^{n}}\biggl[n\hat{\alpha}_{k-1}+\hat{M}_{k-1}-(n\hat{\alpha}_{k}+\hat{M}_{k})\biggr]\\

\Rightarrow\qquad\hat{\alpha}_{k}&=\Bigl(1-\frac{n^{n-1}}{(n-1)^{n-1}}\Bigr)\hat{\alpha}_{k-1}+\frac{\hat{M}_{k-1}+\hat{M}_{k}}{n}\,.\end{split}

) solves the equation

\begin{split}r^{n}+pr+q&=-\frac{q}{\tilde{\eta}}\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}}{n^{n}}\,.\\
\Rightarrow\qquad r^{n}+pr+q\Bigl(1+\frac{1}{\tilde{\eta}}\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}}{n^{n}}\Bigr)&=0\,.\end{split}

\begin{split}1+\frac{1}{\tilde{\eta}}\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}}{n^{n}}&=1-\frac{D}{(n-1)^{n-1}p^{n}}\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}}{n^{n}}\\

Then

(4.28)

Then employ our series, replacing
q
by
q_{\rm eff,3}
in our formalism above.
Call the resulting root
r_{\rm eff,3}
, where

D_{\rm eff,3}=-(n-1)^{n-1}p^{n}-n^{n}q_{\rm eff,3}^{n-1}
and
\hat{\chi}_{\rm eff,3}=-D_{\rm eff,3}/((n-1)^{n-1}p^{n})

r_{\rm eff,3}=-\frac{q_{\rm eff,3}}{p}\,G_{6}(\chi_{\rm eff,3})\,.

(4.29)

Then
r_{\rm eff,3}
satisfies the original trinomial equation

r_{\rm eff,3}^{n}+pr_{\rm eff,3}+q=0\,.

(4.30)

The above idea also works for even
n
.
For even
n
, the discriminant is

D=(n-1)^{n-1}p^{n}-n^{n}q^{n-1}
and
\hat{\eta}=(n-1)^{n-1}p^{n}/D
.
Try a series of the form, where
G_{6}
is a power series:

r=-\frac{q}{p}\,G_{8}(\hat{\eta})\,.

(4.31)

Substitute in eq. (
1.1
) to obtain

6
Comments

We note the following general observation about series in powers of the discriminant or inverse discriminant.
Consider Kummer’s identity in eq. (
1.1d
).
It relates
F(a,b;c;z)
to
F(\dots;1-z)
.
We can interpret
F(\dots;1-z)
as a Taylor series in
z
expanded around
z=1
.
Here
z\propto q^{n-1}/p^{n}
(for example) and
1-z
is then proportional to the discriminant
D
.
Consider Kummer’s identity in eq. (
1.1a
) or eq. (
1.1b
).
It relates
F(a,b;c;z)
to
F(\dots;z/(z-1))=F(\dots;1+1/(z-1))
.
Then
1/(z-1)\propto 1/D
.
One can visualize a series in powers of the discriminant
D
(and also
1/D
)
as a Taylor series in
q^{n-1}/p^{n}
expanded around a base point which is not the origin.

Kummer derived identities for
{}_{2}F_{1}
hypergeometric functions, some of which are listed in
A
.
The use of those identities in Section
2
permitted us to transform a series in
-D/(27q^{2})
to one in
-D/(4p^{3})
, etc.
There appear to be no corresponding published identities for higher hypergeometric functions, e.g.
{}_{3}F_{2}
or
{}_{4}F_{3}
, etc.
Nevertheless, the series we derived in Sections
3
and
4
do exhibit such relationships, for all
n\geq 3
.
Recall
D=(-1)^{n}(n-1)^{n-1}p^{n}-n^{n}q^{n-1}
.
In Section
3
, we derived a series in
\chi=-D/(n^{n}q^{n-1})
and in
\eta=-(n^{n}q^{n-1})/D
.
In Section
4
, we derived a series in
\hat{\chi}=D/((-1)^{n}(n-1)^{n-1}p^{n})
and in
\hat{\eta}=((-1)^{n}(n-1)^{n-1}p^{n})/D
.
The series in Section
5
are in powers of
(-1)^{n}n^{n}q^{n-1}/((n-1)^{n-1}p^{n})
.
Let
z=(-1)^{n}n^{n}q^{n-1}/((n-1)^{n-1}p^{n})
.
Then

1-z=1-\frac{n^{n}q^{n-1}}{(-1)^{n}(n-1)^{n-1}p^{n}}=\frac{(-1)^{n}(n-1)^{n-1}p^{n}-n^{n}q^{n-1}}{(-1)^{n}(n-1)^{n-1}p^{n}}=\frac{D}{(-1)^{n}(n-1)^{n-1}p^{n}}=\hat{\chi}\,.

(6.1)

Also

\frac{z}{z-1}=\frac{\displaystyle\frac{n^{n}q^{n-1}}{(-1)^{n}(n-1)^{n-1}p^{n}}}{\displaystyle\frac{n^{n}q^{n-1}}{(-1)^{n}(n-1)^{n-1}p^{n}}-1}=\frac{n^{n}q^{n-1}}{n^{n}q^{n-1}-(-1)^{n}(n-1)^{n-1}p^{n}}=-\frac{n^{n}q^{n-1}}{D}=\eta\,.

(6.2)

Next let
z=\chi=-D/(n^{n}q^{n-1})
. Then

\frac{\chi}{\chi-1}=\frac{\displaystyle-\frac{D}{n^{n}q^{n-1}}}{\displaystyle-\frac{D}{n^{n}q^{n-1}}-1}=-\frac{D}{-D-n^{n}q^{n-1}}=\frac{D}{(-1)^{n}(n-1)^{n-1}p^{n}}=\hat{\chi}\,.

(6.3)

Next let
z=\eta=-(n^{n}q^{n-1})/D
. Then

1-\eta=1+\frac{n^{n}q^{n-1}}{D}=\frac{D+n^{n}q^{n-1}}{D}=\frac{(-1)^{n}(n-1)^{n-1}p^{n}}{D}=\hat{\eta}\,.

(6.4)

We have derived several series, with different arguments, which all satisfy the same trinomial equation
x^{n}+px+q=0
, for all
n\geq 3
.
However, possibly the most significant contribution in this note is not the series themselves but the relations between them.
The evidence suggests that identities analogous to Kummer’s do exist, purely in terms of the series themselves, without reference to a specific application.
It is a matter for future research.

Let

Kummer published the following identities:

\displaystyle F(a,b;c;z)

\displaystyle=(1-z)^{-a}F\Bigl(a,c-b;c;\frac{z}{z-1}\Bigr)\,,

(1.1a)

\displaystyle F(a,b;c;z)

\displaystyle=(1-z)^{-b}F\Bigl(c-a,b;c;\frac{z}{z-1}\Bigr)\,,

(1.1b)

\displaystyle F(a,b;c;z)

\displaystyle=(1-z)^{c-a-b}F(c-a,c-b;c;z)\,.

(1.1c)

\displaystyle F(a,b;c;z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(c)\Gamma(c-a-b)}{\Gamma(c-a)\Gamma(c-b)}F(a,b;a+b-c+1;1-z)

(1.1d)

\displaystyle\quad+(1-z)^{c-a-b}\frac{\Gamma(c)\Gamma(a+b-c)}{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)}F(c-a,c-b;c-a-b+1;1-z)\,.

\displaystyle(1+z)^{-2a}+(1-z)^{-2a}

\displaystyle=2\,F\Bigl(a,a+\frac{1}{2};\frac{1}{2};z^{2}\Bigr)\,,

(1.2a)

\displaystyle(1+z)^{-2a}-(1-z)^{-2a}

\displaystyle=-4az\,F\Bigl(a+\frac{1}{2},a+1;\frac{3}{2};z^{2}\Bigr)\,.

(2.1a)

(2.1b)

\displaystyle x_{3}

\displaystyle=-\frac{q}{p}\,F\Bigl(\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{3};\frac{3}{2};-\frac{27q^{2}}{4p^{3}}\Bigr)\,.

1.1c

(2.4)

.
This constraint requires

Next, suppose

The transformed expression for the root

Appendix C
Alternative discriminant series

We derive an alternative series for a root of the trinomial in eq. (
1.1
) in powers of the discriminant, for
n>3
.
We assume
n
is odd, so the discriminant has all negative coefficients, i.e.,
D=-(n-1)^{n-1}p^{n}-n^{n}q^{n-1}
.
For brevity define
\xi=-D/((n-1)^{n-1}p^{n})
.
Motivated by the series solution for the real root for a cubic,
we try a series of the following form, where
G_{0}
is a power series

r=\frac{sq}{p}\,G_{0}(\xi)\,.

(3.1)

Here
s
is a scale factor, to be determined below.
Let

G_{0}=1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{k}\xi^{k}\,.

(3.2)

Here
\gamma_{0}=1
and
\{\gamma_{k},k=1,2,\dots\}
are a set of coefficients to be determined.
Substitute into eq. (
1.1
) to obtain

\frac{s^{n}q^{n}}{p^{n}}G_{0}^{n}+sqG_{0}+q=0\,.

(3.3)

Retain terms in
G_{0}
to the zeroth order only.
Then we obtain zeroth order term, say
T_{0}
:

T_{0}=\frac{s^{n}q^{n}}{p^{n}}+(s+1)q=\frac{q}{p^{n}}(s^{n}q^{n-1}+(s+1)p^{n})\,.

(3.4)

We demand that
T_{0}\propto q\xi
, so that it can be absorbed/cancelled by the next-order term in
G_{0}
of
O(\xi)
.
Hence
s
must satisfy the constraint equation

\frac{s^{n}}{s+1}=\frac{n^{n}}{(n-1)^{n-1}}\,.

(3.5)

We know from the cubic trinomial that if
n=3
then
s=3
.
If
n=5
, then
s\simeq 2.0632865
.
Observe that the determination of the value of
s
itself requires the solving of a trinomial equation

(n-1)^{n-1}s^{n}-n^{n}(s+1)=0\,.

(3.6)

This is a weak point in the formalism, in that we must solve a specific version of eq. (
1.1
) to proceed.
Anyway, assume we know the value of
s
.
Then

\begin{split}T_{0}&=\frac{s^{n}q^{n}}{p^{n}}+(s+1)q\\
&=q(s+1)\biggl[\frac{n^{n}}{(n-1)^{n-1}}\frac{q^{n-1}}{p^{n}}+1\biggr]\\
&=\frac{q(s+1)}{(n-1)^{n-1}p^{n}}(n^{n}q^{n-1}+(n-1)^{n-1}p^{n})\\
&=q(s+1)\xi\,.\end{split}

(3.7)

Note also that

\begin{split}\frac{q^{n}}{p^{n}}=\frac{n^{n}q^{n}}{n^{n}p^{n}}=q\frac{-D-(n-1)^{n-1}p^{n}}{n^{n}p^{n}}=q(\xi-1)\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}}{n^{n}}\,.\end{split}

(3.8)

Hence

\frac{s^{n}q^{n}}{p^{n}}=q(s+1)(\xi-1)\,.

(3.9)

Also define a multinomial sum as follows.
Define
M_{0}=M_{1}=0
and for
k\geq 2
:

M_{k}=\sum_{j_{1}+2j_{2}+\dots+(k-1)j_{k-1}=k}\frac{n!}{j_{1}!j_{2}!\dots j_{k-1}!(n-j_{1}-j_{2}-\dots-j_{k-1})!}\gamma_{1}^{,j_{1}}\gamma_{2}^{j_{2}}\dots\gamma_{k-1}^{j_{k-1}}\,.

(3.10)

Then expand terms to obtain

\begin{split}0&=\frac{s^{n}q^{n}}{p^{n}}\biggl(1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{k}\xi^{k}\biggr)^{n}+sq\biggl(1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{k}\xi^{k}\biggr)+q\\
&=\frac{s^{n}q^{n}}{p^{n}}\biggl[1+n\gamma_{1}\xi+(n\gamma_{2}+M_{2})\xi^{2}+(n\gamma_{3}+M_{3})\xi^{3}+\dots\biggr]\\
&\qquad+sq(\gamma_{1}\xi+\gamma_{2}\xi^{2}+\dots)+q(s+1)\\
&=q(s+1)(\xi-1)\biggl[n\gamma_{1}\xi+(n\gamma_{2}+M_{2})\xi^{2}+(n\gamma_{3}+M_{3})\xi^{3}+\dots\biggr]\\
&\qquad+sq(\gamma_{1}\xi+\gamma_{2}\xi^{2}+\dots)+q(s+1)\xi\,.\end{split}

(3.11)

We equate powers of
\xi
. First

\begin{split}0&=-(s+1)n\gamma_{1}+s\gamma_{1}+(s+1)\\
\Rightarrow\qquad(ns+n-s)\gamma_{1}&=s+1\\
\Rightarrow\qquad\gamma_{1}&=\frac{s+1}{(n-1)(s+1)+1}\,.\end{split}

(3.12)

For higher powers of
\xi
, we obtain a recurrence for
\gamma_{k}
:

\begin{split}0&=(s+1)(n\gamma_{k-1}+M_{k-1})-(s+1)(n\gamma_{k}+M_{k})+s\gamma_{k}\\
\Rightarrow\qquad(ns+n-s)\gamma_{k}&=(s+1)(n\gamma_{k-1}+M_{k-1}-M_{k})\\
\Rightarrow\qquad\gamma_{k}&=\frac{s+1}{(n-1)(s+1)+1}(n\gamma_{k-1}+M_{k-1}-M_{k})\,.\end{split}

(3.13)

The above scheme has been tested numerically for
n=3
,
5
and
7
and it works.

We postulate that the series
G_{0}
is a
{}_{n-1}F_{n-2}
hypergeometric series and the root
r
has the form

r=\frac{sq}{p}{}_{n-1}F_{n-2}(a_{n},2a_{n},\dots,(n-1)a_{n};c_{n},2c_{n},\dots(n-2)c_{n};\xi)\,.

(3.14)

Here
a_{n}
and
c_{n}
are parameters to be determined.
For
n=3
, we know that
s=3
.
Working out the coefficients
\gamma_{k}
yields that the root is given by the hypergeometric series

r=\frac{3q}{p}F\Bigl(\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{3};\frac{1}{2};-\frac{D}{4p^{3}}\Bigr)\,.

(3.15)

This equals the root in eq. (
2.6
).
For
n>3

The above scheme does not work for even
n

For even
n
, the constraint equation on
s
is

\frac{s^{n}}{s+1}=-\frac{n^{n}}{(n-1)^{n-1}}\,.

(3.21)

The only real solution is a negative number
s=-n/(n-1)
.
However, the denominator
(n-1)(s+1)+1
equals zero if
s=-n/(n-1)
, leading to a division by zero in the formalism.
It is left for future research to find a partner series in the discriminant for even
n
