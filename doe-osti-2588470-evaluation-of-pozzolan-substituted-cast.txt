 EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The current Hanford Cast Stone formulation and tank closure grouts contain Class F fly ash, slag, and
Portland cement. The future availability of quality Class F fly ash has come into question as coal-fired
power plants are being converted to gas or taken offline. Consequently, alternative pozzolanic reagents are
being evaluated for fly ash replacement in cementitious matrices for treating radioactive waste and debris.

In 2023 and 2024, Savannah River National Laboratory (SRNL) was funded to evaluate natural pozzolans
for the replacement of Class F fly in the Savannah River Site (SRS) Saltstone formulation1, 2. Based on the
promising results and uncertain availability of Class F fly ash in the northwestern US, Hanford Tank Waste
Operations & Closure, LLC (H2C) requested SRNL to evaluate the potential for substituting natural
pozzolans in the Hanford West Area Pretreated Low Activity Tank Waste (PTW/LAW) Cast Stone
formulation1. Task 1 in this request was to determine the change in Cast Stone volume resulting from
stabilization/solidification of 1 L of PTW/LAW tank waste. The densities of three (Hess Pumice, tephra
pumice, and clinoptilolite zeolite) pozzolan-substituted Baseline Cast Stone mixes were determined, and
volume changes relative to the fly ash-Baseline Mix with a water-to-dry mix = 0.40 were calculated.

Solidification of 1L of LAW/PTW tank waste of the fly ash Cast Stone resulted in an increase of 1.75 L
(75.4% volume increase) for the waste form relative to the waste solution. The Hess pumice-, tephra
pumice-, and zeolite-substituted Cast Stone samples had a calculated volume increases relative to 1 L of
LAW/PTW of 77.9% (1.77 L), 82.0% (1.82 L), and 91.3% (1.91 L).

Each of the pozzolan-substituted Cast Stone waste forms resulted in higher volume increases relative to the
fly ash Cast Stone for water-to-dry mix = 0.4. These pozzolan-substituted Cast Stone waste forms had
volume changes (increase) relative to the volume of the fly ash Cast Stone was +1.4, +3.8 and + 9.1
volume % for the Hess pumice, Tephra pumice and zeolite, respectively.

Based upon the thick paste observed during Baseline Cast Stone testing, two natural pozzolans were
selected for higher waste loading calculations. Hess pumice substituted Cast Stone mixes calculated for
w/dm ratios of 0.6, 0.8, 1.0, 1.2, and 1.4 had relative volume decreases (compared to Baseline Cast Stone
with w/dm of 0.4) of 11.0, 19.0, 23.7, 26.9, and 29.2%, respectively compared to the 0.4 w/dm Baseline
mix. The Tephra pumice-substituted Baseline Cast Stone mixes calculated with w/dm ratios of 0.6, 0.8, 1.0,
1.2, and 1.4 also resulted in waste form volume decreases of 12.4, 20.0, 24.6, 27.6, and 29.8%, respectively.

1.0 Introduction

Hanford Tank Waste Operations & Closure, LLC (H2C) requested the Savannah River National Laboratory
to evaluate the volume increase when Hanford West Area Pretreated Low Activity (PTW/LAW) is
solidified/immobilized using natural pozzolan-substituted Cast Stone. This work is authorized in Statement
of Work (SOW) # 379664, Revision 0, dated March 5, 2025.1 This task is also relevant to other potential
Hanford cementitious waste forms and projects including the 1) waste forms resulting from the Liquid
Secondary Waste Form Development and Ammonia Mitigation for the Effluent Treatment Facility
Technology Development Plan (RPP-PLAN-62930)2; 2) Hanford tank closure grout formulations; 3)
cementitious waste forms for Direct Feed Low Activity Waste (DFLAW), and disposal of these waste forms
in the Hanford Integrated Disposal Facility (IDF) as outlined in the Hanford Site Secondary Waste
Roadmap3.

1.1 Background
The current Hanford Cast Stone formulation and the current Hanford tank closure grouts contain Class F
fly ash, slag, and Portland cement. The future availability of quality Class F fly ash has come into question
as coal-fired power plants are being converted to gas or taken out of service. Consequently, alternative
pozzolanic reagents are being evaluated for fly ash replacement in cementitious matrices for treating
radioactive waste and debris and other closure applications.

1.2 Objective

Prior to this project, the Department of Energy Office of Environmental Management Technology
Development (DOE-EM-TD) funded a review of natural pozzolans for Savannah River Site Saltstone4, 5.
Results from this work4 were reviewed by H2C engineers and limited testing was requested for the
PTW/LAW Cast Stone waste form1. The specific objectives of this scope were to:
 1. Determine the relative increase in waste form volume resulting from solidification of a unit volume
 (1L) of PTW/LAW liquid tank waste for pozzolan-substituted Cast Stone mixes made with three
 of the natural pozzolans tested for SRS Saltstone (Hess pumice, Tephra pumice, clinoptilolite
 zeolite).
 2. Evaluate retention of 11 Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) Land Disposal Restriction
 (LDR) inorganic contaminants using the same concentrations of contaminants as used in a previous
 study by Pacific Northwest National Laboratory (PNNL)6.

This document addresses the first objective.

2.4 Density and Fresh Property Measurements for Cast Stone Mixes with W/DM = 0.4
Fresh properties, static flow, free liquid, set time, gel time were measured according to SRNL grout
laboratory R&D Directions. These measurements were not requested but are standard methods for
evaluating processibility of grout mixes. Bulk wet densities (fresh waste forms) of the Baseline Cast Stone
and Pozzolan-substituted Cast Stone formulations were determined by two methods. The results were
averaged for input to the 1L of PTW/LAW to resulting Cast Stone relative volume calculations.

The small sample method is a standard bench top method for liquids and pourable grout. A known volume
density cup, i.e., 88.3 ml, was weighed then filled with grout and weighed again. The grout mass
corresponding to 88.3 ml was calculated as the difference in mass values. The density was calculated as
mass divided by volume.

In the second method, a larger sample made with 1L of PTW/LAW was used. The resulting grout plus
container + mixing impeller was weighed and then subtracted from the TARE weight of the container and
impeller to obtain the mass of this larger batch. The volume was calculated based on the measured density
determined in the small sample method and the grout mass calculated in the second method.

4.0 Conclusions
Future availability of quality Class F fly ash for use as a reliable pozzolan in concrete and cementitious
waste forms in the Richland/Hanford area is uncertain. DOE sites have a projected decades long need for
pozzolans and functional fillers for low activity cementitious waste forms, radioactive debris encapsulation,
tank closure grouts, and structural concrete. Natural pozzolans were evaluated as alternatives to Class F fly,
a byproduct of coal fired power plants, to support the long-term DOE site closure mission. In this series of
tests, three natural pozzolan-substituted Cast Stone mixes were evaluated with respect to changes in final
waste form volumes resulting from a direct replacement of Class F fly ash in the Caste Stone formulation.

In all cases, the volumes of the Hess Pumice, Tephra pumice and Zeolite-substituted Cast Stone waste forms
prepared with 1L of PTW/LAW increased relative to the Baseline fly ash mix because the densities of the
natural pozzolans are less than that of the Hanford fly ash. Consequently, for equal masses the volumes
were slightly greater than that of the Baseline Cast Stone at a water to dry mix ratio of 0.40.

The volume change of the waste form resulting from solidification of 1L of PTW/LAW was calculated for
Hess pumice substituted-and Tephra pumice-substituted Cast Stone mixes with w/dm ratios between 0.6
and 1.4. In all cases, the calculated? volume changes for the resulting waste forms decreased compared to
Baseline Cast Stone with w/dm of 0.4. The Hess pumice-substituted Baseline Cast Stone mixes calculated
with w/dm ratios of 0.6, 0.8, 1.0, 1.2, and 1.4 had relative volume decreases of 11.0, 19.0, 23.7, 26.9, and
29.2%, respectively compared to the 0.4 w/dm Baseline mix. The Tephra pumice-substituted Baseline Cast
Stone mixes calculated with w/dm ratios of 0.6, 0.8, 1.0, 1.2, and 1.4 also resulted in waste form volume
decreases of 12.4, 20.0, 24.6, 27.6, and 29.8%, respectively. A reduction in Cast Stone volume produced
per 1L of PTW/LAW offers potential for a reduction in disposed volume and cost.

This study was based on a project funded by DOE EM TD (PROJECT #SR071301NP-23) that identified,
down selected and provided initial test results for natural pozzolans for replacement of fly ash in
cementitious waste forms, grouts, and concretes8. The funding for this work from DOE EM Headquarters
is greatly appreciated.

5.0 Recommendations, Path Forward or Future Work
Additional testing is required to determine whether the natural pozzolan-substituted Cast Stone mixes meet
the EPA Method 1311 (RCRA TCLP leaching) requirements for classification as nonhazardous waste. This
work is currently in progress at the SRNL for 11 RCRA hazardous metals. This information is needed to
fully evaluate the potential for disposal of higher waste loading PTW/LAW Pumice-substituted Cast Stone
at an off-site LLW disposal facility.

Opportunities for further volume reduction of the Cast Stone relative to 1 L of PTW/LAW waste and for
enhanced RCRA metal stabilization may be achieved pending testing to evaluate:

  Changing the proportions of the three ingredients in the current Cast Stone Baseline and natural
 pozzolan-substituted Cast Stone mixes and/or
  Changing the components in the Baseline Cast Stone.
  Replacing Class F fly ash with a natural pozzolan such as pumice
  Developing inorganic reagent blends with a range of reagent proportions to accommodate variable
 waste compositions from the West Area tank closure.

 14

  Including organic polymer reagents for solidification of aqueous waste generated as the Hanford
 West Area tanks are closed.

The natural pozzolan Cast Stone formulations will also require the following characterization in order to be
considered for implementation as an option for PTW/LAW tank waste solidification at Hanford:
  Set time
  Isothermal calorimetry
  Compressive strength
  Diffusivity (Observed)
  EPA SW846 Method 1311 Toxicity Characterization Leaching Procedure (TCLP)
  Hydraulic conductivity
  Durability
  Mineralogy and phase evolution
  Scale-up process confirmation
