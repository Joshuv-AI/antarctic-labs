 Abstract

Inertial Fusion Energy (IFE) is one of the most promising pathways toward clean, carbon-free energy.
The recent demonstration of fusion ignition at Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory's National
Ignition Facility (NIF) established diamond (high-density carbon, HDC) as the benchmark ablator
material. However, further increases in fusion gain are limited by hydrodynamic instabilities
developing at the ablator–fuel interface during compression, motivating the search for next-
generation ablator materials.

Amorphous carbon (a-C) is a particularly attractive candidate because it combines exceptional
mechanical strength with the ability to incorporate high-Z dopants and oUers tunable properties
through controlled synthesis. Despite its promise, its behavior under the extreme pressures and
temperatures relevant to IFE remains poorly understood.

The objective of this project was to establish the fundamental high-energy-density physics of
amorphous carbon under dynamic compression. By combining dynamic compression experiments
with machine-learning-enabled large-scale molecular dynamics simulations, we investigated
shock-induced structural transformations, crystallization pathways to diamond, metastable liquid
formation, and the thermodynamic properties governing its response under IFE conditions. These
studies produced a predictive equation of state (EOS) and a comprehensive high-pressure phase
diagram for amorphous carbon.

The resulting EOS, phase diagram, and mechanistic understanding of shock-induced phase
transformations provide a scientiﬁc foundation for next-generation IFE target design and
hydrodynamic simulations. The project also created a highly collaborative research environment that
integrated advanced experiments with exascale simulations while providing interdisciplinary training
for graduate students and postdoctoral researchers.

2. Research Accomplishments

Experiments

We performed equation-of-state (EOS) and in situ X-ray diUraction (XRD) experiments at the OMEGA
EP Laser Facility to investigate the structural evolution of amorphous carbon (a-C) under dynamic
compression. Guided by predictive molecular dynamics (MD) simulations, the experiments were
designed to determine the metastability limits of a-C and identify the onset of shock-induced
crystallization to diamond.

In fall 2023, we successfully completed eight PXRDIP experiments using low-density glassy carbon
as a model amorphous carbon material. Double-shock compression enabled exploration of
pressures between 50 and 350 GPa. The XRD measurements provided clear evidence of shock-
induced diamond formation, with the strongest diUraction signal observed near 200 GPa, while no
diamond crystallization was detected near 100 GPa, in excellent agreement with MD predictions.

We also conducted XRD experiments at the High Energy Density (HED) instrument of the European
XFEL using amorphous carbon samples developed by the NIF Target Fabrication Team at Lawrence
Livermore National Laboratory. These data further improved our understanding of amorphous carbon
under IFE-relevant conditions.

Simulations

Our machine-learning MD (ML-MD) simulations on DOE exascale Frontier and Aurora
supercomputers investigated the atomic-scale evolution of the a-C microstructure under shock
compression, examining potential phase transitions to diamond or metastable supercooled liquid
carbon at high pressures and temperatures, as well as the complex states of carbon under conditions
relevant to IFE. These studies yielded a high-quality EOS through a combination of experimental
measurements and ML-MD simulations.

 Our quantum-accurate, billion-atom MD simulations uncovered several material
transformations of g-C along the Hugoniot (Fig. 4): (1) transformation to sp³ amorphous carbon at 20
GPa; (2) gradual transformation to nanocrystalline diamond at pressures above 36 GPa; (3) almost
full conversion to diamond at ~50 GPa, corresponding to the intersection of the Hugoniot with the
diamond melting line; (4) gradual decrease of the diamond fraction and corresponding increase of
the liquid fraction in the mixed-phase region of the Hugoniot along the diamond melting line; and (5)
complete melting at pressures above 100 GPa.

3. Training and Professional Development
 This project provided excellent training opportunities for students and postdocs. The project
involved several graduate students at USF: James Tunacao, Fraser Hanby, and Chamara
Somarathna. Students and postdocs were trained in ML-MD simulations as well as experimental
shot design, execution, and data analysis under the eUective mentorship of senior team members.
