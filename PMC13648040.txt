Visible-light driven degradation of tetracycline and ciprofloxacin using a Z-scheme FeS2@polythiophene heterostructure photocatalyst

Maruthasalam Pannerselvam, Vadivel Siva, Abdul Samad Shameem, Anbazhagan Murugan, Krishnasamy Padmavathi, Murugan Dineshkumar, Shaik Gouse Peera

Abstract

The development of efficient and reusable visible-light-driven photocatalysts is essential for the sustainable removal of emerging pharmaceutical contaminants from wastewater. In this study, a FeS2@polythiophene (FeS2@PTh) heterostructure has been synthesized using a simple in situ chemical oxidative polymerization method. The synthesized FeS2@PTh was used as a visible-responsive photocatalyst for the degradation of tetracycline (TC) and ciprofloxacin (CIP) from an aqueous medium. XPS analysis confirmed the existence of Fe2+, Fe3+, and S 2p species, with a significant interface between the FeS2 and PTh systems. FeS2@PTh exhibited higher degradation performance, removing 83% of TC (k = 1.422 × 10−2 min−1) and 85% of CIP (k = 1.687 × 10−2 min−1) under visible-light irradiation. Moreover, this study highlighted the excellent photostability and reproducibility of the photocatalysts, contributing to the development of sustainable photocatalytic materials for efficient water treatment.

Introduction

Energy crises and environmental pollution remain key issues despite the rapid development in building a sustainable society. The aquatic ecosystem is severely contaminated by everyday effluents from industrial wastes, and agricultural wastes that are dumped into natural water bodies. In particular, pharmaceutical residues are new contaminants that pose a major environmental issue due to their toxicity and capacity to promote bacterial resistance. Many antibiotic medications, including ciprofloxacin and tetracycline, have been overused recently to treat fever, and bacterial infections. The occurrence of these antibiotics in the environment is primarily attributed to their discharge from pharmaceutical manufacturing industries, hospital wastes, and various industrial wastes, which enter wastewater streams and are frequently transferred into freshwater systems, such as lakes and rivers, where they accumulate in soil. Through these pathways, TC and CIP pollutants may re-enter the food chain, affecting human and animal health. The persistence of TC and CIP in aquatic environments poses a series of ecological and public health challenges. Owing to their stable chemical structures, these compounds resist natural biodegradation, leading to their accumulation in water bodies. Therefore, the removal of these pollutants from wastewater using conventional treatment methods often proves ineffective, highlighting the urgent need for advanced and efficient remediation strategies.

Advanced oxidation processes (AOPs) have emerged as highly effective strategies for environmental remediation due to their ability to generate reactive species that can degrade persistent pollutants. Among these, heterogeneous photocatalysts have attracted considerable research interest owing to their sustainable development and efficiency in eliminating recalcitrant contaminants, ultimately breaking down and converting the pollutants into harmless and mineralised species using AOPs. Upon irradiation with light of sufficient energy, the electrons in the semiconductor photocatalyst are promoted from the valence band (VB) to the conduction band (CB), leaving behind positively charged holes (h+) in the VB. Photogenerated electron–hole pairs serve as a driving force that subsequently creates a redox mechanism on the catalyst surface. These photogenerated holes possess strong oxidative potential, reacting with adsorbed water molecules and hydroxyl ions (OH−) to produce highly reactive hydroxyl radicals ( ̇OH), simultaneously exciting electrons from the CB and reducing dissolved oxygen molecules to form superoxide radicals ( ̇O2−). These reactive species (ROS), along with (e−/h+) carriers, actively interact with pollutant molecules adsorbed on the photocatalyst surface. Through a sequence of oxidation and reduction reactions, complex organic contaminants are effectively broken down into simpler, non-toxic products, thereby completing the photocatalytic degradation process.

To date, a wide range of visible-light responsive semiconductors have been extensively explored for energy conversion and environmental remediation strategies. Among them, metal chalcogenides, such as MoS2, WS2, FeS2 and ZnS, have gained significant attention due to their suitable bandgap energies, strong absorption capacity over the visible region, and tuneable electronic properties, making them promising candidates for applications in pollutant degradation, hydrogen production and wastewater treatment. Recent studies have demonstrated that FeS2 based photocatalysts exhibit remarkable efficiency in the degradation of TC and CIP pollutants. For instance, FeS2@Co3O4–C composites have achieved 97.2% removal of TC at 20 min under visible light, as reported by Wang et al. In a related study, Liu et al. demonstrated persulfate-activated FeS2 CIP removal, with the synergistic sulphur species facilitating regeneration of Fe2+ from Fe3+ to achieve a CIP removal efficiency of 94.4% within 60 min. FeS2/SiO2 microspheres activated via the hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) system show that removal of CIP could be achieved efficiently at 100% within 60 min, as reported by Diao et al. Mohanty et al. reported a poly-m-aminophenol/CoFe2O4 heterojunction that achieved nearly 100% mineralization of BPA and ciprofloxacin within 60 min under ambient conditions. A visible-light driven Ag3PO4–WO3/coffee bean biochar photocatalyst achieved nearly 100% mineralisation of CIP within 60 min. Mishra et al. developed a Z-scheme g-C3N4/Gd2O3 composite that achieved 97% degradation of CIP and 96.5% degradation of TC under visible light irradiation within 120 min. This catalyst demonstrates good recyclability over five consecutive cycles. Mishra et al. developed a g-C3N4/Sm2O3 photocatalyst that demonstrated 90.6% CIP within 120 min under visible light. This enhanced activity was attributed to synergistic factors that influence visible-light absorption.

Conductive polymers, such as polyaniline (PANI), polypyrrole (PPy), and polythiophene (PTh), possess π-conjugated structures that enable visible-light absorption and charge transport. However, they often suffer from rapid electron–hole recombination. Ahmad et al. reported ciprofloxacin removal efficiencies of 82% (PANI), 75% (PPy), and 70% (PTh) after 660 min of sunlight irradiation. In our previous study, a MoS2@PTh heterojunction achieved 90% TC and 75% CIP degradation, attributed to improved charge separation and enhanced reactive oxygen species generation. In this current study, an efficient nanoscale FeS2@PTh composite was prepared using a simple synthesis route. A comprehensive investigation was carried out to explore the morphological, structural and physico-chemical properties. The FeS2@PTh composite achieved efficient removal of TC (83%) and CIP (85%) within 120 min. The enhanced photocatalytic activity of the FeS2@PTh heterostructure is attributed to its efficient charge separation capability, facilitated by the formation of a Z-scheme system. This composite also exhibits excellent recyclability, making it a promising nanoscale photocatalyst. In this context, the Z-scheme charge-transfer pathway significantly improves the degradation of TC and CIP, where highly reactive hydroxyl radicals ( ̇OH) play a dominant role by attacking and decomposing the pollutant molecules.

Experimental section

Materials

Potassium persulphate (K2S2O8, 99.99%), potassium iodide (KI, 99.0%) and iron disulfide (FeS2, 99.8%) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. Thiophene (98%), isopropyl alcohol (C3H8O, 99%), and anhydrous ferric chloride (FeCl3, 98%) were obtained from Loba Chemie Pvt Ltd. Tetracycline hydrochloride and ciprofloxacin hydrochloride were purchased from HiMedia.

Synthesis of polythiophene and FeS2@PTh

Polythiophene was synthesised by a simple chemical oxidative polymerisation method. FeCl3 (4.866 g) was dissolved in 50 mL of chloroform and magnetically stirred for 20 min. Then, the monomer form of thiophene (0.793 mL) was added dropwise to the solution until a homogeneous mixture was obtained. The formation of a black precipitate was allowed to proceed for 10 h with constant stirring. After the complete process, the resultant PTh was washed with a 1 mol L−1 HCl solution to remove residual FeCl3 until a colourless product was obtained. Finally, the product was dried under vacuum at 60 °C overnight to obtain polythiophene. To synthesise FeS2@PTh nanocomposite an in situ chemical oxidative polymerisation was performed by adding 0.3 g of FeS2 NPs directly to the FeCl3 (4.866 g) solution. Afterwards, the thiophene monomer (0.793 mL) was added dropwise to the solution. The reaction was carried out for 6 h with constant stirring. After the complete process, the product was washed several times with 1 mol L−1 HCl solution to remove residual FeCl3 until a colourless product was obtained. The product was dried at 60 °C for 24 h to obtain FeS2@PTh. The preparation process of FeS2@PTh is shown in .

Photocatalytic degradation experiments

Photocatalytic degradation experiments were carried out under visible-light source. Stock solutions (1 × 10−3 M) of TC and CIP were prepared using distilled water. Further solutions were prepared by diluting 6 mL of TC and 3 mL of CIP stock solutions to a final volume of 100 mL, yielding concentrations of 6 × 10−5 M (∼26.7 mg L−1) and 3 × 10−5 M (∼10 mg L−1), respectively. The optimised standard stock result was verified using a UV-visible spectrophotometer. In a typical experiment, 30 mg of catalyst was dispersed in 100 mL of solution. Before light irradiation, the suspension was magnetically stirred in the dark condition for 30 min to establish adsorption/desorption equilibrium. Subsequently, the solution was exposed to 160 W illumination. At regular time intervals, the settled particle were collected, centrifuged to remove catalyst particles, and analysed.

Materials characterisation

X-ray diffraction patterns were recorded using a Bruker D8 ECO Advance with Cu Kα radiation (λ = 1.54 Å). The optical properties were recorded using a JASCO V-750 spectroscopy in the range of 200–600 nm. The surface morphology characteristics were analysed using a FESEM-Sigma HV (Carl Zeiss) equipped with a Bruker Quantax 200-Z10. HR-TEM was performed with a Philips-TEM CM20. Dynamic light scattering (Malvern Analytical Limited) was used for particle size analysis. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS; Thermo Fisher Nexsa) was performed to evaluate the oxidation state and chemical composition. Photocatalytic activity was tested by HEBER Hml-Hvar-Compact-Lp-Mp46400 reactor.

Plausible degradation mechanism

To further understand the mechanism behind the working FeS2@PTh, the band level positions were estimated using the electronegativity-based approach. With X (FeS2 = 5.40 eV) and the hydrogen scale-free electron energy, the calculated band positions (ECB, −0.05 eV for FeS2 and −0.76 eV for PTh vs. NHE) and the CB of the composite (EVB was estimated at +1.85 eV and +1.35 eV vs. NHE) were obtained, and shown in . In , the band potential was estimated from UV-DRS and the values for FeS2 (1.90 eV) and PTh (2.12 eV). The Mott–Schottky measurements provide further information on the semiconductor behaviour, where negative and positive slopes were assigned to the p-type and n-type semiconductors of FeS2 and PTh, respectively, as provided in SI (Fig. S1). This allows us to understand the formation of a direct Z-scheme heterojunction that facilitates efficient charge separation. In the heterostructure photocatalyst, upon visible light irradiation of PTh and FeS2, an electron–hole pair is formed. In the conduction band of FeS2, photoexcited electron transferred from the PTh matrix participate in parallel redox reactions, converting dissolved oxygen into superoxide radicals, while reducing Fe3+ species to Fe2+. The photogenerated electrons in the conduction band of FeS2 recombine with holes in the valence band of PTh. Meanwhile, the remaining electrons in the conduction band of PTh directly reduce dissolved oxygen to form ( ̇O2−), subsequently generating ̇OH radicals, whereas holes in the VB of FeS2 participate in direct oxidation. In contrast, the favourable band alignment of the CB of PTh (−0.76 eV) is more negative than the potential (−0.33 eV vs. NHE), enabling the formation of superoxide radicals, as confirmed by the trapping results. However, in the valence band of FeS2 (+1.85 eV), ̇OH formation occurs indirectly via H2O2 intermediates. Meanwhile, OH is an active reactive species; both TC and CIP degrade through attack by ̇O2− and ̇OH, ultimately leading to mineralisation into CO2 and H2O.

Conclusions

A facile and effective strategy was used to synthesise the heterostructured FeS2@PTh photocatalyst, and its photocatalytic performance was evaluated. The optimised photocatalyst achieves the removal of TC (83%, k = 1.422 × 10−2 min−1) and CIP (85%, k = 1.687 × 10−2 min−1) under visible-light. Optical analysis revealed bandgap energies of PTh, FeS2, and the FeS2@PTh composite of 2.12, 1.90, and 2.0 eV, respectively. The intermediate optical bandgap of FeS2@PTh compared with the pristine components suggests effective interfacial electronic interaction between FeS2 and PTh. Radical trapping experiments were conducted, which demonstrated that the primary oxidants were hydroxyl radicals ( ̇OH), superoxide radicals ( ̇O2−) and secondary species of h+ (tested with potassium iodide) and e− (tested with potassium persulfate). Repeatability and reusability studies over four cycles for TC revealed negligible degradation in efficiency (79%). A direct Z-scheme pathway was proposed involving conventional electron transfer and charge separation kinetics. The optimised photocatalyst plays a synergistic role in the degradation of TC and CIP, and it offers an eco-friendly and efficient approach to wastewater treatment.
