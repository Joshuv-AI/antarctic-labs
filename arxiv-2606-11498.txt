Generic dc-automorphisms of two-sorted ultrametric spaces
Adam Bartoš 🖂 bartos@math.cas.cz, Wiesław Kubiś 🖂 kubis@math.cas.cz, Aleksandra Kwiatkowska 🖂 aleksandra.kwiatkowska@math.uni.wroc.pl, Maciej Malicki 🖂 mmalicki@mimuw.edu.pl

Abstract
We continue to study ultrametric spaces as two-sorted structures consisting of a set of points and of a linearly ordered set of distances, together with the dc-embeddings, which we introduced in our earlier paper “Universal homogeneous two-sorted ultrametric spaces”. The class of all finite two-sorted ultrametric spaces with dc-embeddings is Fraïssé whose limit we denote by 𝕌 \mathbb{U} . The main result of the article is that Aut ⁡ ( 𝕌 ) \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{U}) has a comeager conjugacy class. For that we show the cofinal amalgamation property of partial automorphisms and characterize amalgamation bases. In fact we develop a general strategy for showing cofinal amalgamation property for a broad class of categories. Furthermore, we show that there is no generic pair of automorphisms, we provide a detailed description of single orbits under dc-automorphisms, and we prove that any finite partial dc-automorphism, even in the presence of other orbits, can be extended to one that is closed or monotone.



Keywords:

ultrametric spaces, Fraïssé theory, automorphism groups, generic automorphisms

54E35, 03C50, 20B27, 18A22

Introduction

A metric space
(X,d)
is called an
ultrametric space
if for all
x,y,z\in X
, the ultrametric triangle inequality holds:
d(x,y)\leq\max\{d(x,z),d(y,z)\}
.
Consequently, every triangle is isosceles.
The
(open) ball
of center
x\in X
and radius
r>0
in an ultrametric space is the set
B_{r}(x)=\{y\in X\colon d(y,x)<r\}
.
Note that for any two balls
B
and
C
, they are either disjoint (
B\cap C=\emptyset
) or one is contained in the other (
B\subseteq C
or
C\subseteq B
). Moreover, every point in a ball can serve as its center.

In our previous paper
[
2
]
, we reframed ultrametric spaces as two-sorted structures consisting of a set of points and a linearly ordered set of distances, in order to construct a universal separable ultrametric space, which is impossible in the classical setting.
Instead of isometric embeddings, we used appropriate two-sorted embeddings called
dc-embeddings
(for “distance-carrying”) as morphisms.
Furthermore, we initiated a systematic study of two-sorted ultrametric spaces, ways of representing them, methods of constructing universal objects, and properties of their automorphism groups.
In particular, we studied the generic countable two-sorted ultrametric space
\mathbb{U}
.
We showed that the class
\mathfrak{U}_{\operatorname{fin}}
of all finite two-sorted ultrametric spaces is Fraïssé, and that its limit
\mathbb{U}
can be identified with the rational Urysohn ultrametric space, the Fraïssé limit of the class
\mathfrak{C}_{\operatorname{fin}}
of all classical finite rational ultrametric spaces.
Hence,
\mathbb{U}
is homogeneous both with respect to isometries and dc-automorphisms.
We denote the respective Polish groups by
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathbb{U})
and
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{U})
.

In the present paper we are interested in the existence of generic
n
-tuples of dc-automorphisms of
\mathbb{U}
.
They behave quite differently than isometries.
Unlike an isometry, a dc-automorphism can move distances around.
In fact, we showed
[
2
, Theorem 4.4]
that
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{U})
is canonically isomorphic to the topological semidirect product
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathbb{U})\rtimes\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Q}^{\geq 0})
, involving the automorphism group of the linear order on the rationals.

The situation with generic isometries is settled – by the result of Malicki
[
11
, Corollary 6.1]
, every Polish Urysohn ultrametric space has ample generics of isometries.
The argument applies to the countable rational Urysohn ultrametric space as well – by
[
11
, Lemma 4.2]
, the class
\mathfrak{C}_{\operatorname{fin}}
has the
Hrushovski property
(EPPA), i.e. every
A\in\mathfrak{C}_{\operatorname{fin}}
is contained in
B\in\mathfrak{C}_{\operatorname{fin}}
such that every partial isometry of
A
extends to a total isometry of
B
.
Since ultrametric spaces have a canonical amalgamation (as in Lemma
5.1
),
\mathfrak{C}_{\operatorname{fin}}
has also
amalgamation property with automorphisms
(APA), i.e. all
A\leq B,C
in
\mathfrak{C}_{\operatorname{fin}}
admit an amalgamation
D\in\mathfrak{C}_{\operatorname{fin}}
such that all coherent isometries
f\in\operatorname{Iso}(A)
,
g\in\operatorname{Iso}(B)
, and
h\in\operatorname{Iso}(C)
extend to an isometry of
D
.
The Fraïssé limit of a class with EPPA and APA automatically has ample generics (see
[
13
, Theorem 2.1.5]
).

This standard strategy for obtaining ample generics completely fails for dc-automorphisms – since finite linear orders have no nontrivial automorphisms, every finite total dc-automorphism is an isometry, and so
\mathfrak{U}_{\operatorname{fin}}
does not have EPPA.
In fact, we show that
\mathbb{U}
does not have a generic pair of dc-automorphisms.
On the other hand, a generic dc-automorphism exists, but we use a strategy based on so-called
determined partial automorphisms
in place of finite total automorphisms.

Let us summarize the results obtained in the article.

•

We provide a detailed description of single orbits under dc-automorphisms (Theorem
3.7
), which extends to partial dc-automorphisms (Corollary
3.9
).
Any orbit of a finite partial dc-automorphism, even in the presence of other orbits, can be extended to one that is closed or monotone (Theorem
3.10
).

•

There is a generic dc-automorphism of
\mathbb{U}
(Corollary
5.10
) but no generic pair (Corollary
6.2
).
Moreover, every orbit of the generic dc-automorphism is either closed or monotone, and the induced automorphism on distances is the generic automorphism of
\mathbb{Q}^{\geq 0}
(Corollary
5.13
).

•

Similarly, there is no generic pair of automorphisms of the Urysohn ultrametric space
\mathbb{U}^{\prec}
endowed with the generic convex order (Corollary
6.3
), and we believe
\mathbb{U}^{\prec}
has a generic automorphism (Question
6.4
).

•

To show the existence of a generic dc-automorphism of
\mathbb{U}
we prove the cofinal amalgamation property (CAP) of the category
\mathfrak{U}^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
of finite partial dc-automorphisms, and we characterize amalgamation bases (Theorem
5.9
).

•

To that end we develop a general strategy (Strategy
4.10
) for showing CAP of
\mathscr{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
for any so-called nice category of structures
\mathscr{C}
.
For such a category, every amalgamation base in
\mathscr{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
is a determined partial automorphism – the partial orbits can be completed to total ones in a unique way (Lemma
4.4
).
The strategy is summarized as follows:

–

First show that total (potentially infinite) automorphisms have AP.
Then amalgamation bases are exactly determined partial automorphisms (Theorem
4.5
)

–

Pick a full subcategory
\mathscr{S}\subseteq\mathscr{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
of so-called sufficient partial automorphisms and show that they have unique one-point orbit extensions, that the extensions lie in
\mathscr{S}
, and that
\mathscr{S}\subseteq\mathscr{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
is cofinal.
Then
\mathscr{S}
is a family of determined partial automorphism witnessing CAP (Proposition
4.9
).

–

If being in
\mathscr{C}
is also necessary for being an amalgamation base, we have a characterization.

In the cases of linear orders and ultrametric spaces, the amalgamation bases are exactly partial automorphisms whose orbits are stable, i.e. we cannot close an orbit or merge two orbits.
We guess this choice of
\mathscr{S}
does not work in general (Question
5.11
).

Preliminaries

2.1
General framework

We shall use the general framework of categories of structures introduced in
[
2
]
.
Recall that a category
\mathscr{C}
consists of a family of objects (sometimes called
\mathscr{C}
-objects) and of a composable family of morphisms (sometimes called arrows or
\mathscr{C}
-maps).
We also have the identity morphism
{\operatorname{id}_{X}}\colon X\to X
for every
\mathscr{C}
-object
X
.
A subcategory
\mathscr{D}\subseteq\mathscr{C}
is called
full
if every
\mathscr{C}
-map between
\mathscr{D}
-objects is a
\mathscr{D}
-map.
We will use some other standard category-theoretic notions such as functors and colimits. See a standard reference such as
[
10
]
for details.

Our categories consist of model-theoretic structures in a fixed signature (sometimes many-sorted, but we use only finitely many sorts), and of all embeddings.
We call them
categories of structures
.
For every category of structures
\mathscr{C}
, the full subcategory of all finite structures is denoted by
\mathscr{C}_{\operatorname{fin}}
.
Also, there is a notion of a substructure, i.e. of an embedding that is an inclusion on the level of sets.
The notation is
X\leq Y
, meaning that
X
is a substructure of
Y
and that
Y
is an extension of
X
.
Arguments are sometimes simplified by replacing embeddings with substructures. This is without loss of generality because, up to isomorphism, every embedding is an inclusion.

Recall that in general an
isomorphism
in a category
\mathscr{C}
is a
\mathscr{C}
-map
f\colon X\to Y
such that there is
\mathscr{C}
-map
f^{-1}\colon Y\to X
satisfying
f^{-1}\circ f={\operatorname{id}_{X}}
and
f\circ f^{-1}={\operatorname{id}_{Y}}
.
In a category of structures, it is equivalently a
\mathscr{C}
-map that is bijective (in every sort) as
\mathscr{C}
-maps are embeddings.
Isomorphisms
X\to X
are called
automorphisms
.
For every
\mathscr{C}
-object
X
the automorphism group of
X
is denoted by
\operatorname{Aut}(X)
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a category of finite structures.
For every
\mathscr{C}
-object
A
a
partial automorphism
of
A
is a
\mathscr{C}
-isomorphism
p
such that
\operatorname{dom}(p),\operatorname{rng}(p)\leq A
, i.e. an isomorphism between substructures of
A
.
An embedding of partial automorphisms
f\colon\langle A,p\rangle\to\langle B,q\rangle
is a
\mathscr{C}
-map
f\colon A\to B
such that
f\circ p=q\circ f
on
\operatorname{dom}(p)
, from which it follows that
f
restricts to (unique)
\mathscr{C}
-maps
\operatorname{dom}(p)\to\operatorname{dom}(q)
and
\operatorname{rng}(p)\to\operatorname{rng}(q)
.
We denote the category of partial automorphisms in
\mathscr{C}
and their embeddings by
\mathscr{C}^{*}
.
We can view
\mathscr{C}^{*}
as a category of structures. Indeed, if structures in
\mathscr{C}
are one-sorted, we turn
\langle A,p\rangle
into a three-sorted structure with sorts
A,\operatorname{dom}(p),\operatorname{rng}(p)
, and we add the embeddings
\operatorname{dom}(p)\to A
and
\operatorname{rng}(p)\to A
as well as both
p
and
p^{-1}
as operations. If
\mathscr{C}
is many-sorted, we do this for every sort.

More generally, for an
n
-tuple
\langle A,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\rangle
such that every
p_{i}
is a partial automorphism of
A
, an embedding
f\colon\langle A,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\rangle\to\langle B,q_{1},\ldots,q_{n}\rangle
is a
\mathscr{C}
-map
f\colon A\to B
that is an embedding from
\langle A,p_{i}\rangle
to
\langle B,q_{i}\rangle
for every
i
.
The corresponding category of
n
-tuples of partial automorphisms in
\mathscr{C}
is denoted by
\mathscr{C}^{*n}
.

Observation 2.1
.

Every functor
F\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{D}
between categories of finite structures lifts to a functor
F^{*n}\colon\mathscr{C}^{*n}\to\mathscr{D}^{*n}
as follows.

For every partial automorphism
p
of a
\mathscr{C}
-object
A
we have that
F(p)
is a
\mathscr{D}
-isomorphism between
F(\operatorname{dom}(p))\leq F(A)
and
F(\operatorname{rng}(p))\leq F(A)
, so
F(p)
is a partial automorphism of
F(A)
.
Hence, it is correct to define
F^{*n}(\langle A,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\rangle)=\langle F(A),F(p_{1}),\ldots,F(p_{n})\rangle
.
(Technically, since
F
is an abstract functor, it is not guaranteed that
F(\operatorname{dom}(p))
and
F(\operatorname{rng}(p))
are substructures of
F(A)
, only that they are embedded to
F(A)
.
But then, they can be canonically replaced by their images.)

Moreover, for every
\mathscr{C}^{*}
-map
f\colon\langle A,p\rangle\to\langle B,q\rangle
, the
\mathscr{D}
-map
F(f)\colon F(A)\to F(B)
restricts to
\mathscr{D}
-maps
\operatorname{dom}(F^{*}(p))\to\operatorname{dom}(F^{*}(p))
and
\operatorname{rng}(F^{*}(p))\to\operatorname{rng}(F^{*}(p))
.
Hence, for every
\mathscr{C}^{*n}
-map
f\colon\langle A,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\rangle\to\langle B,q_{1},\ldots,q_{n}\rangle
putting
F^{*n}(f)=F(f)
is well defined.

For a category of structures
\mathscr{C}
and a partial automorphism
\langle A,p\rangle\in\mathscr{C}^{*}
we consider the
orbit equivalence relation
(on every sort separately) defined by
a\sim_{p}b
if
p^{k}(a)=b
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
An
orbit
is an equivalence class of the orbit equivalence relation.
In particular,
trivial orbits

\{a\}
for
a\notin\operatorname{dom}(p)\cup\operatorname{rng}(p)
are allowed.
The orbit of
a
is sometimes denoted by
p^{\mathbb{Z}}(a)
.
An orbit
p^{\mathbb{Z}}(a)
is called
closed
if
p^{k}(a)=a
for some
k>0
.

We say that a category of structures
\mathscr{K}
is
hereditary
if for every
A\in\mathscr{K}
and every substructure
B\leq A
we have
B\in\mathscr{K}
.
Given
\langle A,p\rangle\in\mathscr{K}^{*}
and a substructure
B\leq A
, we consider the
restriction

\langle B,q\rangle=\langle A,p\rangle\mathord{\upharpoonright}_{B}
(
q
may also be denoted by
p\mathord{\upharpoonright}_{B}
) where
\operatorname{dom}(q)=\{b\in B:p(b)\in B\}
and
\operatorname{rng}(q)=\{b\in B:p^{-1}(b)\in B\}
.
If
\mathscr{K}
is hereditary, then
\operatorname{dom}(q),\operatorname{rng}(q)\in\mathscr{K}
, and so
\langle A,p\rangle\mathord{\upharpoonright}_{B}\in\mathscr{K}^{*}
.

2.2
Fraïssé theory

We shall recall notions from Fraïssé theory and adapt them to our context.
We follow the setup used in the previous paper
[
2
]
.
Here we additionally consider
weak
Fraïssé classes and
weak
Fraïssé sequences in order to apply Fraïssé theory to categories of partial automorphisms where the amalgamation property can hold only in trivial situations.

Let
\mathscr{K}
be a category of structures.
Fraïssé theory relates properties of the class
\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
of finite structures and of countable structures that are colimits of countable direct sequences from
\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
, i.e. up to isomorphism they are unions of countable increasing chains.
We denote this class by
\sigma\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
.
We assume that
\mathscr{K}
is
\sigma
-complete
in the sense that it is closed under countable unions and their isomorphic copies (i.e. under colimits of countable sequences taken in the ambient category of all structures of a given many-sorted signature).

A sequence
(A_{n},f_{n})
in
\mathscr{K}
consists of
\mathscr{K}
-objects
A_{n}
and
\mathscr{K}
-maps
f_{n}\colon A_{n}\to A_{n+1}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
We also introduce notation for compositions of the bonding maps:
f_{m}^{n}\colon A_{m}\to A_{n}
for
m\leq n\in\mathbb{N}
.
The colimit is denoted by
(A_{\infty},f^{\infty}_{n})
, i.e. it consists of a
\mathscr{K}
-object
A_{\infty}
and of
\mathscr{K}
-maps
f_{n}^{\infty}\colon A_{n}\to A_{\infty}
forming a cone.

Below we summarize the relevant properties of
\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
such as variants of the amalgamation, properties of objects from
\sigma\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
such as homogeneity and extension property, and we formulate a general Fraïssé theorem in our context.
As a rule of thumb, a weak Fraïssé-theoretic property involves an extra “guardian” embedding
i\colon A\to A^{\prime}
through which a general embedding considered has to factorize.

We define the following properties for
\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
.

(JEP)

The
joint embedding property
: for any
A,B\in\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
there is
C\in\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
, which embeds both
A
and
B
.

(AP)

The
amalgamation property
: for every
A\in\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
,
\alpha_{1}\colon A\to B
and
\alpha_{2}\colon A\to C
in
\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
there is
D\in\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
together with
\beta_{1}\colon B\to D
and
\beta_{2}\colon C\to D
such that
\beta_{1}\circ\alpha_{1}=\beta_{2}\circ\alpha_{2}
.
In that case, we say that
A
is an
amalgamation base
in
\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
.

(SAP)

The
strong amalgamation property
is a strengthening of the AP, where we additionally have
\beta_{1}(B)\cap\beta_{2}(C)=\beta_{1}(\alpha_{1}(A))(=\beta_{2}(\alpha_{2}(A)))
. In that case, we say that
A
is a
strong amalgamation base
.

(CAP)

The
cofinal amalgamation property
: any
A\in\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
embeds into
A^{\prime}\in\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
that is an amalgamation base in
\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
.

(WAP)

The
weak amalgamation property
:
for every
A_{0}\in\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
there is an embedding
i\colon A\to A^{\prime}
in
\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
that is an
amalgamable map
, i.e. for any
\alpha_{1}\colon A^{\prime}\to B
and
\alpha_{2}\colon A^{\prime}\to C
in
\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
there is
D\in\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
together with
\beta_{1}\colon B\to D
and
\beta_{2}\colon C\to D
such that
\beta_{1}\circ\alpha_{1}\circ i=\beta_{2}\circ\alpha_{2}\circ i
holds.

We say that
\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}\neq\emptyset
with countably many isomorphism types is a
Fraïssé class
if it satisfies JEP and AP, and it is a
weak Fraïssé class
if it satisfies JEP and WAP.

Note that we do not require
\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
to be hereditary.

Definition 2.2
.

We say that a structure
U\in\mathscr{K}

•

is
universal
for
\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
if for every
A\in\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
there is an embedding
f\colon A\to U
,

•

is

for
\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
if for every
A,B\in\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
such that
A,B\leq U
and every isomorphism

there is
h\in\operatorname{Aut}(U)
extending
f
, or equivalently, for every
A\in\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
and all embeddings

there is
h\in\operatorname{Aut}(U)
such that
h\circ g=f
,

•

is
weakly homogeneous
for
\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
if for every
A\in\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
and
f\colon A\to U
there is
A^{\prime}\in\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
and embeddings
i\colon A\to A^{\prime}
and

with

such that for every
g\colon A^{\prime}\to U
there is an isomorphism
h\colon U\to U
with
h\circ g\circ i=f^{\prime}\circ i
,

•

has the
extension property
/ is
injective
for
\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
if for every
A,B\in\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
and embeddings
f\colon A\to U
and

there is an embedding
h\colon B\to U
such that
h\circ g=f
,

•

has the
weak extension property
/ is
weakly injective
for
\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
if for every
A\in\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
and
f\colon A\to U
there is
A^{\prime}\in\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
and an embedding
i\colon A\to A^{\prime}
such that for every
B\in\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
and

there is an embedding

such that

.

Similarly we say that a sequence
(A_{n},f_{n})
in
\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}

•

is
cofinal
if for every
A\in\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
there is an embedding
f\colon A\to A_{n}
for some
n
,

•

has the
extension property
if for every
A,B\in\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
and embeddings
f\colon A\to A_{m}
and

there is an embedding

for some

such that

,

•

is

if for every

and an embedding

there is an embedding

for some

, i.e. it has the extension property for

,

•

is
weakly absorbing
if for every

m^{\prime}\geq m
such that for every

and an embedding

for some

where

,

•

is
Fraïssé
if it has the extension property (which under AP is equivalent to just being absorbing) and is cofinal,

•

is
weak Fraïssé
if it weakly absorbing and cofinal.

Now that we have all the definitions, the core of (weak) Fraïssé theory can be summarized by the following two theorems.
The Fraïssé case including references is already covered by
[
2
, Theorems 3.2 and 3.3]
.
The weak Fraïssé case was proved by Kubiś
[
8
, Theorems 3.7, 4.2 and 5.1]
in a more general setup.
For another exposition see
[
1
, Section 2.2]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
has a (weak) Fraïssé sequence if and only if it is a (weak) Fraïssé class.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\mathscr{K}
be a
\sigma
-complete category of structures, and let
U\in\sigma\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
.
The following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

U
is universal and (weakly) homogeneous for
\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
.

(2)

U
is universal and has the (weak) extension property for
\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
.

(3)

U
is the colimit of a (weak) Fraïssé sequence in
\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
.

Moreover, such object
U
is unique, and every sequence in
\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
with colimit
U
is (weak) Fraïssé.
In the Fraïssé case,
U
is universal for
\sigma\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
.

The unique object
U
from Theorem
2.4
is called the
(weak) Fraïssé limit
of
\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
(in
\mathscr{K}
), and by Theorem
2.3
it exists if and only if
\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
is a (weak) Fraïssé class.

Observation 2.5
.

For any cofinal subcategory
\mathscr{S}\subseteq\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
,
every weakly absorbing sequence
(A_{n},f_{n})
in
\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
can be intertwined with a sequence
(B_{n},g_{n})
with
B_{n}\in\mathscr{S}
for every
n
.
From this some properties of the limit may be concluded, as we will see in Corollary
5.13
.

For every structure
X\in\sigma\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
we endow its automorphism group
\operatorname{Aut}(X)
with the topology of pointwise convergence, turning it into a Polish group.
A basic open set is of the form
V_{\langle A,p\rangle}=\{f\in\operatorname{Aut}(X):\langle X,f\rangle\geq\langle A,p\rangle\}
where
A\leq X
and
\langle A,p\rangle\in\mathscr{K}^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\mathscr{K}
be a
\sigma
-complete category of structures such that
\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
is a Fraïssé class, and let
U
be the Fraïssé limit.
A
generic
n
-tuple of automorphisms
of
U
is
\langle h_{1},\ldots,h_{n}\rangle\in\operatorname{Aut}(U)^{n}
such that the conjugacy class

\{\langle gh_{1}g^{-1},\ldots,gh_{n}g^{-1}\rangle:g\in\operatorname{Aut}(U)\}

is comeager in
\operatorname{Aut}(U)^{n}
.

It turns out that existence of a generic
n
-tuple of automorphisms is closely connected to Fraïssé-theoretic properties of the class of finite partial automorphisms
\mathscr{K}^{*n}_{\operatorname{fin}}
.
However, partial automorphisms are essentially incompatible with AP.
By a correspondence of Kechris and Rosendal
[
7
, Theorem 6.2]
a generic
n
-tuple exists if and only if the class of finite partial automorphisms satisfies JEP and WAP.
A variant of the correspondence for
\omega
-categorical structures was independently proved by Ivanov
[
5
, Theorem 1.2]
.
We shall use the following more precise formulation, which explicitly mentions weak Fraïssé limit, in order to deduce properties of a generic automorphism in Corollary
5.13
via Observation
2.5
.
To our knowledge, this more precise formulation is folklore.

Let us also note that the original theorem is stated for classes of one-sorted structures. This is not a problem as every many-sorted structure can be turned into a one-sorted structure while preserving the notion of embedding, and hence any category of structures is equivalent to a category of one-sorted structures.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
\mathscr{K}
be a category of structures such that
\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}
is a Fraïssé class and let
U
be its Fraïssé limit.
Then for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\langle h_{1},\ldots,h_{n}\rangle\in\operatorname{Aut}(U)^{n}
the following are equivalent:

(1)

\langle h_{1},\ldots,h_{n}\rangle
is a generic
n
-tuple of automorphisms of
U
,

(2)

\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}^{*n}
is a weak Fraïssé class and
\langle U,h_{1},\ldots,h_{n}\rangle\in\mathscr{K}^{*n}
is the limit.

Hence, a generic
n
-tuple of automorphisms exists if and only if
\mathscr{K}_{\operatorname{fin}}^{*n}
has JEP and WAP.

We shall recall transfer principles formulated in
[
2
, Section 3.1]
.
These provide conditions for preservation of amalgamation and of the Fraïssé limit by a functor, and capture the situation of generalized reducts (images under forgetful functors) of a homogeneous structure being homogeneous in their respective categories.

Let us recall the relevant properties of functors.
A functor
F\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{D}
is

•

wide
/

if for every
\mathscr{D}
-object

there is a
\mathscr{C}
-object
X^{\prime}
such that

;

•

essentially wide
/
essentially surjective on objects
if for every
\mathscr{D}
-object
X
there is a
\mathscr{C}
-object
X^{\prime}
with
F(X^{\prime})
isomorphic to
X
;

•

cofinal
if for every
\mathscr{D}
-object
X
there is a
\mathscr{C}
-object
X^{\prime}
and a
\mathscr{D}
-map

.

Remark 2.8
.

We have the following implications for a functor
F
:

wide
\implies
essentially wide
\implies
cofinal.

We say that a functor

is

•

star-surjective
if for every
\mathscr{C}
object
X^{\prime}
and every
\mathscr{D}
-map

\mathscr{C}
-object
Y^{\prime}
and a
\mathscr{C}
-map

with

;

•

essentially star-surjective
if for every
\mathscr{C}
-object
X^{\prime}
and every
\mathscr{D}
-map
f\colon F(X^{\prime})\to Y
there is a
\mathscr{C}
-object
Y^{\prime}
and a
\mathscr{C}
-map
f^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}\to Y^{\prime}
and an
\mathscr{D}
-isomorphism
g\colon Y\to F(Y^{\prime})
with
F(f^{\prime})=g\circ f
;

•

absorbing
if for every
\mathscr{C}
-object
X^{\prime}
and every
\mathscr{D}
-map
f\colon F(X^{\prime})\to Y
there is a
\mathscr{C}
-object
Y^{\prime}
, a
\mathscr{C}
-map
f^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}\to Y^{\prime}
, and an
\mathscr{D}
-map

with

;

•

weakly absorbing
if for every
\mathscr{C}
-object

there is a
\mathscr{D}
-map

such that for every
\mathscr{D}
-map

there is a
\mathscr{C}
-object

, a
\mathscr{C}
-map

, and an
\mathscr{D}
-map

with

(wlog the map

may be of the form

for some
\mathscr{C}
-map

).

Remark 2.9
.

We have the following implications for a functor
F
:

star-surjective
\Rightarrow
essentially star-surjective
\Rightarrow
absorbing
\Rightarrow
weakly absorbing.

Remark 2.10
.

The above properties of functors
\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{D}
specialize to the corresponding properties of subcategories
\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathscr{D}
(via the inclusion functor) and of sequences in
\mathscr{D}
(viewed as functors
\mathbb{N}\to\mathscr{D}
).

Note that definitions of AP, CAP, and WAP make sense for arbitrary categories, and we use them in the following general proposition.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
and
\mathscr{D}
be categories and
F\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{D}
a functor.

(1)

If
\mathscr{C}
has AP and
F
is essentially wide and absorbing, then
\mathscr{D}
has AP.

(2)

If
\mathscr{C}
has CAP and
F
is cofinal and absorbing, then
\mathscr{D}
has CAP.

(3)

If
\mathscr{C}
has WAP and
F
is cofinal and weakly absorbing, then
\mathscr{D}
has WAP.

In particular, if
F
is wide and star-surjective, all the amalgamation properties are transferred forward.

A functor
F\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{D}
is
\sigma
-continuous
if for every sequence
(X_{n},f_{n})
in
\mathscr{C}
and its colimit
(X_{\infty},f^{\infty}_{n})
we have that
(F(X_{\infty}),F(f^{\infty}_{n}))
is a colimit of
(F(X_{n}),F(f_{n}))
in
\mathscr{D}
.
For categories of structures, where objects are (many-sorted) structures and morphisms are all embeddings, the colimit of a sequence is essentially the union of the structures, and so to check

-continuity, it is enough to show that for every point

there is
n\in\omega
and
x^{\prime}\in F(X_{n})
such that
f_{n}^{\infty}(x^{\prime})=x
.

Observation 2.12
.

If
\mathscr{C}
is a
\sigma
-complete category of structures, then so is
\mathscr{C}^{*}
with the colimit of

where

is the colimit of
(X_{n},f_{n})
and

is the colimit-induced isomorphism between the colimit of
(\operatorname{dom}(p_{n}))
and the colimit of
(\operatorname{rng}(p_{n}))
, and similarly for every

.
Hence, if

is a
\sigma
-continuous functor between
\sigma
-complete categories of structures, so is every

.

The first two cases of the following proposition come from
[

, Proposition 3.8]

2.3
Two-sorted ultrametric spaces

By
\mathfrak{L}
we denote the category of all linear orders
\langle L,\leq_{L}\rangle
and all embeddings, i.e. maps
f\colon L\to L^{\prime}
such that
x\leq_{L}y
if and only if
f(x)\leq_{L^{\prime}}f(y)
.
It is well-known that
\mathfrak{L}_{\operatorname{fin}}
is a Fraïssé class, whose limit is the linearly ordered set of rationals
\mathbb{Q}
.

We shall consider ultrametric spaces with linear orders as sets of distances.
First, let
\mathfrak{L}^{0}
denote the category of linear orders
\langle L,\leq_{L},0_{L}\rangle
with the least element
0_{L}
and of embeddings of linear orders preserving the least element.
As usual, the triple
\langle L,\leq_{L},0_{L}\rangle
is often denoted just by
L
, and
\leq_{L}
and
0_{L}
are often denoted just by
\leq
and
0
, respectively.
Clearly,
\mathfrak{L}^{0}
is a category equivalent to
\mathfrak{L}
.

In our previous paper
[
2
]
, we studied ultrametric spaces as two-sorted structures of the form
\langle X,d_{X},D_{X}\rangle
,
where
X
is a set of points,
D_{X}
is a set of distances, i.e. an
\mathfrak{L}^{0}
-object, and
d_{X}\colon X\times X\to D_{X}
is an ultrametric.
To be more specific,
d=d_{X}
is symmetric,
d(x,x^{\prime})=0
if and only if
x=x^{\prime}
, and
d
satisfies the ultrametric triangle inequality

d(x,x^{\prime\prime})\leq\max\{d(x,x^{\prime}),d(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})\}\qquad\text{for $x,x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}\in X$.}

Again, we shall denote
d_{X}
by
d
and
\langle X,d_{X},D_{X}\rangle
by
\langle X,D_{X}\rangle
or just
X
whenever convenient.
We shall refer to the two sorts as the
point sort
and the
distance sort
, respectively, when convenient.

A
distance-carrying embedding

f\colon\langle X,d_{X},D_{X}\rangle\to\langle Y,d_{Y},D_{Y}\rangle
(
dc-embedding
for short) is a one-to-one map
f\colon X\to Y
together with an
\mathfrak{L}^{0}
-map
D_{f}\colon D_{X}\to D_{Y}
such that

d_{Y}(f(x),f(x^{\prime}))=D_{f}(d_{X}(x,x^{\prime}))\qquad\text{for }x,x^{\prime}\in X.

Note that an
isometric embedding
corresponds to a dc-embedding
f\colon X\to Y
such that
D_{f}
is the inclusion
D_{X}\leq D_{Y}
, i.e. we have
d_{Y}(f(x),f(x^{\prime}))=d_{X}(x,x^{\prime})
.

Let
\mathfrak{U}
denote the category of ultrametric spaces and distance-carrying embeddings.
Clearly the assignment
(f\colon X\to Y)\mapsto(D_{f}\colon D_{X}\to D_{Y})
yields a functor
D\colon\mathfrak{U}\to\mathfrak{L}^{0}
with the restriction
D_{\operatorname{fin}}\colon\mathfrak{U}_{\operatorname{fin}}\to\mathfrak{L}^{0}_{\operatorname{fin}}
(which we may denote just by
D
).
Note that a
\mathfrak{U}_{\operatorname{fin}}
-object is an ultrametric space
X
such that both the set of points
X
and the set of distances
D_{X}
are finite.

Let
\mathfrak{I}^{E}\subseteq\mathfrak{U}
for
E\in\mathfrak{L}^{0}
denote the subcategory of all ultrametric spaces
X
with
D_{X}\leq E
and all isometric embeddings.
We say that an ultrametric space
X
is
precise
if
d_{X}\colon X\times X\to D_{X}
is surjective, i.e. every distance is achieved.
By
\mathfrak{I}^{E}_{\rm prec}\subseteq\mathfrak{I}^{E}

we mean the full subcategory consisting of precise spaces.
In this view, the category of classical ultrametric spaces is just
\mathfrak{I}^{\mathbb{R}^{\geq 0}}_{\rm prec}
.
A
precise partial dc-automorphism

p
of
A
is a partial automorphism such that all
\operatorname{dom}(p),\operatorname{rng}(p)\leq A
are precise.
Then for every
r\in\operatorname{dom}(D_{p})
we have
D_{p}(r)=D_{p}(d(x,y))=d(p(x),p(y))
for some
x,y\in A
, i.e. we can ignore the distance part of
D_{p}
.

Since every two-sorted ultrametric space
X
can be viewed both as a member of
\mathfrak{U}
and
\mathfrak{I}^{E}
(for any
E\in\mathfrak{L}^{0}
with
D_{X}\leq E
), we make a convention that
\operatorname{Aut}(X)=\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathfrak{U}}(X)
denotes the dc-automorphism group, while
\operatorname{Iso}(X)=\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathfrak{I}^{E}}(X)
denotes the isometry group, which does not depend on
E
.
We say that
X
is
dc-homogeneous
if it is homogeneous for
\mathfrak{U}_{\operatorname{fin}}
, and that
X
is
iso-homogeneous
if it is homogeneous for
\mathfrak{I}^{E}_{\operatorname{fin}}
.

Recall that the class of classical finite rational ultrametric spaces with isometric embeddings, which in our setup is isomorphic to the category
(\mathfrak{I}^{\mathbb{Q}^{\geq 0}}_{\rm prec})_{\operatorname{fin}}
, is Fraïssé and that its Fraïssé limit is the
countable rational Urysohn ultrametric space

\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{Q}}
.
In the previous paper
[
2
]
, we showed that finite two-sorted ultrametric spaces also form a Fraïssé class, with the same limit.

Theorem 2.15

(
[
2
, Theorem 3.11]
)
.

\mathfrak{U}_{\operatorname{fin}}
is a Fraïssé class, and its Fraïssé limit
\mathbb{U}
is dc-isomorphic to
\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{Q}}
.
In particular,
\mathbb{U}
is both dc-homogeneous and iso-homogeneous.

3
Description of a single orbit

In this section, we give a complete description of types of single orbits of automorphisms of two-sorted ultrametric spaces.

Let
\langle X,f\rangle
be a total dc-automorphism.
We shall analyze the orbit of a point
a\in X
, which consists of the points
a_{n}=f^{n}(a)
for
a\in\mathbb{Z}
.
First let us denote the sequence of the forward distances from
a
by
(r_{n})
, i.e.
r_{n}=d(a_{0},a_{n})
for every
n\geq 1
.

Let
L\geq 1
be the smallest integer such that
r_{L}=0
, if it exists; otherwise put
L=\infty
.
We call
L
the
length
of the orbit.
Clearly,
L<\infty
means that
f^{\mathbb{Z}}(a)
is a
closed
orbit of length
L
as
a_{L}=a_{0}
.

Let
N\geq 1
be the smallest integer such that
D_{f}(r_{N})\neq r_{N}
, if it exists; otherwise put
N=\infty
.
Note that for every
n<N
the distance
r_{n}
is a fixed point of
D_{f}
, and so
d(a_{k},a_{k+n})=r_{n}
for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
On the other hand,
r_{N}
induces the monotone orbit of
D_{f}
consisting of the distances
(D_{f})^{k}(r_{N})=d(a_{k},a_{k+N})
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Hence, we call the orbit
monotone
if
N<\infty
, and we call
N
the
monotone period
of the orbit.
A monotone orbit is either
decreasing
, i.e. when
D_{f}(r_{N})<r_{N}
, or
increasing
, i.e. when
D_{f}(r_{N})>r_{N}
.
We also call
r_{n}
a
fixed distance
or a
monotone distance
if
D_{f}(r_{n})=r_{n}
or
D_{f}(r_{n})\neq r_{n}
, respectively.

The cases of being closed or monotone are mutually exclusive since a closed orbit attains finitely many distances, while a monotone orbit attains infinitely many.
In the third case, when
L=N=\infty
, we call the orbit
horizontal
.
Horizontal orbits are of two subtypes depending on whether the set
\{r_{n}:n\geq 1\}
of all attained distances is finite or infinite.

Observation 3.1
.

The length
L
, the monotone period
N
, the set
\{r_{n}:1\leq n<N\}
of fixed distances, and so the classification of the orbit as being closed, monotone, or horizontal (with finitely or infinitely many distances) depends only on the orbit
f^{\mathbb{Z}}(a)
as a whole, not on the particular choice of the point
a
.
This is because
r_{n}=d(a_{k},a_{k+n})
for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
whenever
D_{f}(r_{n})=r_{n}
(which includes
r_{n}=0
).

Moreover, the invariants do not depend on the orientation of
f
, i.e. if we replace
f
by
f^{-1}
, we obtain the same
L
,
N
, and
\{r_{n}:1\leq n<N\}
. Only an increasing monotone orbit becomes decreasing monotone and vice versa.
This is because if
(a^{\prime}_{n})
and
(r^{\prime}_{n})
denote the points and distances with respect to
f^{-1}
, we have
r^{\prime}_{n}=d(a^{\prime}_{0},a^{\prime}_{n})=d(a_{0},a_{-n})=(D_{f})^{-n}(d(a_{n},a_{0}))=(D_{f})^{-n}(r_{n})=r_{n}
whenever
D_{f}(r_{n})=r_{n}
.

Let us adopt a convention that
n\leq N
means
n\in\{1,2,\ldots,N\}
if
N<\infty
, and
n\in\{1,2,\ldots\}
if
N=\infty
.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
r_{n}\notin\{r_{m}:m<n\}
for some
1<n\leq N
, then
r_{n}<\min\{r_{m}:m<n\}
.

Proof.

Let
1\leq m<n
.
Note that
d(a_{m},a_{n})=d(a_{0},a_{n-m})
since
n-m<N
.
We consider the triangle with vertices
a_{0},a_{m},a_{n}
, which attains the distances
r_{m},r_{n-m},r_{n}
.
Since,
r_{n}\neq r_{m},r_{n-m}
, by the ultrametric triangle inequality we have
r_{n}<r_{m}=r_{n-m}
.
∎

Let
(n_{i})
be the increasing enumeration of the set
\{n\leq N:r_{n}\notin\{r_{m}:m<n\}\}
of indices where a new distance (possibly up to
r_{N}
) appears.
So
n_{0}=1
, and we either obtain a finite sequence
(n_{i})_{0\leq i\leq k}
, or an infinite sequence
(n_{i})_{0\leq i<\infty}
.
It is easy to check that
for a closed orbit of length
L
,
(n_{i})
is finite with
n_{k}=L
,
for a monotone orbit of period
N
,
(n_{i})
is finite with
n_{k}=N
, and
for a horizontal orbit,
(n_{i})
can be either finite, or infinite, depending on the set of attained distances.
We call
(n_{i})
the
constructing sequence
for the orbit
f^{\mathbb{Z}}(a)
.
Again, it depends only on the orbit as a whole and agrees with the constructing sequence for
f^{-1}
by Observation
3.1
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Every element
n_{i+1}
of the constructing sequence is a non-trivial positive multiple of
n_{i}
, i.e.
n_{i+1}=l_{i}n_{i}
for some
l_{i}\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0,1\}
.

Proof.

By definition we have
n_{i+1}>n_{i}
, and so
n_{i+1}
can be uniquely written as
l_{i}n_{i}+j
for
l_{i}\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
and
j\in\{0,\ldots,n_{i}-1\}
.
Note that
d(a_{0},a_{n_{i}})=d(a_{n_{i}},a_{2n_{i}})=\cdots=d(a_{(l_{i}-1)n_{i}},a_{l_{i}n_{i}})=r_{n_{i}}
, and so by the ultrametric triangle inequality we have
r_{l_{i}n_{i}}\leq r_{n_{i}}
.
If
j>0
, then the triangle with the vertices
a_{0},a_{l_{i}n_{i}},a_{n_{i+1}}
attains the distances
r_{l_{i}n_{i}},r_{n_{i+1}},r_{j}
, and we have have
r_{n_{i+1}}<r_{l_{i}n_{i}}\leq r_{n_{i}}<r_{j}
, which is a contradiction.
Hence,
j=0
and
l_{i}\neq 1
since
n_{i+1}\neq n_{i}
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
m<n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Suppose that
N=\infty
or
N<\infty
and
N
does not divide
n-m
. Then
d(a_{m},a_{n})=r_{n_{i}}
where
n_{i}
is the largest element of the constructing sequence that divides
n-m
.

Proof.

It is enough to prove the claim in the case
m=0
since then

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
\langle A,p\rangle\in\mathfrak{U}^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
be a partial automorphism.
For every orbit
p^{\mathbb{Z}}(a)
of the point sort we can extend
\langle A,p\rangle
to
\langle B,q\rangle\in\mathfrak{U}^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
so that the extended orbit
q^{\mathbb{Z}}(a)
is closed or monotone.

Proof.

Let
\langle X,f\rangle\geq\langle A,p\rangle
be any extension to a total dc-automorphism.
If
f^{\mathbb{Z}}(a)
is closed or monotone, we are done as we can take a suitable finite restriction
\langle B,q\rangle\geq\langle A,p\rangle
.
Otherwise,
f^{\mathbb{Z}}(a)
is horizontal.
Let
a_{n}=f^{n}(a)
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
(n_{i})_{i\in I}
be the constructing sequence (finite or infinite) for
f^{\mathbb{Z}}(a)
, and let
u_{i}=d(a_{0},a_{n_{i}})
for every
i\in I
(so
\{u_{i}:i\in I\}
is the set of all positive distances used in the orbit
f^{\mathbb{Z}}(a)
).

Suppose there is an interval
[M,N]\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
such that
f^{\mathbb{Z}}(a)\cap A\subseteq\{a_{M},\ldots,a_{N}\}
and
d(a_{M},a_{N})\leq d(a_{M},b),d(a_{N},b)
(and so
d(a_{M},b)=d(a_{N},b)
) for every
b\in B\setminus\{a_{M},a_{N}\}
where
B=A\cup\{a_{M},\ldots,a_{N-1}\}
.
Then we can put
D_{B}=D_{A}\cup d_{X}[B\times B]
and
\langle B,q_{0}\rangle=\langle X,f\rangle\mathord{\upharpoonright}_{\langle B,D_{B}\rangle}
.
We have
D_{f}(d(a_{N-1},b))=d(a_{N},f(b))=d(a_{M},f(b))
for every
b\in\operatorname{dom}(q_{0})\setminus\{a_{N-1}\}
.
For
b=a_{N-1}
we have
D_{f}(d(a_{N-1},b))=0=d(a_{M},a_{M})
.
It follows that
q=q_{0}\cup\{a_{N-1}\mapsto a_{M}\}
with
D_{q}=D_{q_{0}}
is a partial automorphism of
B
with a closed orbit
q^{\mathbb{Z}}(a)
.

It remains to show that we can find a suitable interval
[M,N]
.
Note that by the ultrametric triangle inequality, for every
b\in A\setminus f^{\mathbb{Z}}(a)
there is at most one point
a_{m_{b}}
such that
d(a_{m_{b}},b)<u_{i}
for every
i\in I
, and let us put
m_{b}=0
if there is no such close point.
Let
[M_{0},N_{0}]
be such that
f^{\mathbb{Z}}(a)\cap A\subseteq\{a_{M},\ldots,a_{N}\}
and
\{m_{b}:b\in A\setminus f^{\mathbb{Z}}(a)\}\subseteq(M_{0},N_{0})
.

If the constructing sequence
(n_{i})_{i\in I}
is finite, we let
i=\max(I)
and we take any interval
[M,N]\supseteq[M_{0},N_{0}]
such that
n_{i}
divides
N-M
.
Then
d(a_{M},a_{N})
is the smallest distance
u_{i}
, and as
m_{b}\neq M,N
for any
b\in A\setminus f^{\mathbb{Z}}(a)
, we have
d(a_{M},b),d(a_{N},b)\geq u_{i}
and we are done.

If the constructing sequence
(n_{i})
is infinite, we need to be more careful.
We take
i
such that
K:=n_{i}-(N_{0}-M_{0})\geq\lvert A\setminus f^{\mathbb{Z}}(a)\rvert
, so that
[M(k),N(k)]:=[M_{0}-k,M_{0}-k+n_{i}]\supseteq[M_{0},N_{0}]
for every
0\leq k\leq K
.
In other words, we consider
K+1>\lvert A\setminus f^{\mathbb{Z}}(a)\rvert
possibilities for the interval
[M,N]
of length
n_{i}
, all of which lie in a common interval
[M_{1},N_{1}]
of length
n_{i+1}\geq 2n_{i}
.
Again by the ultrametric triangle inequality, for every
b\in A\setminus f^{\mathbb{Z}}(a)
there is at most one
n_{b}\in[M_{1},N_{1})
such that
d(a_{n_{b}},b)<u_{i}
, and hence there is
0\leq k\leq K
such that
\{M(k),N(k)\}
misses all the values
n_{b}
.
Therefore, for
[M,N]=[M(k),N(k)]
we have
d(a_{M},b),d(a_{N},b)\geq u_{i}=d(a_{M},a_{N})
for every
b\in A\setminus f^{\mathbb{Z}}(a)
.
Since also for every
M<n<N
we have
d(a_{M},a_{n}),d(a_{N},a_{n})>u_{i}
, we are done.
∎

4
Amalgamation bases and determined partial automorphisms

In this section, we present a general strategy (Strategy
4.10
), which applies to nice categories of structures (Definition
4.1
), for showing that the class of finite partial automorphisms has the CAP.

A structure
A
is
locally finite
if for every finite subset
F\subseteq A
the substructure of
A
generated by
F
is finite as well.

Definition 4.1
.

In order to collect the relevant assumptions, we say that a category of structures
\mathscr{C}
is
nice
if it is hereditary,
\sigma
-complete, consists of locally finite structures, and
\mathscr{C}_{\operatorname{fin}}
is Fraïssé.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a category of structures.
We say that
\langle A,p\rangle\in\mathscr{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
has a
unique totalization
(or is
determined
) if there is an extension
\langle A,p\rangle\to\langle\hat{A},\hat{p}\rangle
(in
\mathscr{C}^{*}
) to a total automorphism such that every extension
\langle A,p\rangle\to\langle X,f\rangle
to a total automorphism (in
\mathscr{C}^{*}
) admits a unique factorization
\langle\hat{A},\hat{p}\rangle\to\langle X,f\rangle
.
In other words, the orbits of
\langle A,p\rangle
can be completed to full orbits in a unique way (up to an isomorphism).
The extension
\langle\hat{A},\hat{p}\rangle\in\mathscr{C}^{*}
we call the
unique totalization
(of
\langle A,p\rangle
).

The concept of a determined partial automorphism appears already in Kuske–Truss
[
9
]
, where they show the existence of a generic automorphism of the universal partial order. The idea of extending a partial automorphism to a determined one was further used by Kaplan–Rzepecki–Siniora
[
6
]
, who show that there is a generic automorphism of the universal meet-tree. They point out that amalgamation bases are determined (
[
6
, Proposition 4.2]
) and have an analog of our Lemma
4.8
(
[
6
, Lemma 4.11]
). In their specific situation amalgamation bases correspond to determined partial automorphisms (
[
6
, Corollary 4.19]
).
Moreover, in order to show the CAP for finite meet-trees with partial automorphisms they show amalgamation of infinite meet-trees with total automorphisms (
[
6
, Lemma 4.16]
). That corresponds to our Corollary
5.6
.
We only found out about the ideas used in their article after completing our proofs. We further develop those ideas into a general strategy usable for a nice category of structures (Strategy
4.10
).

Observation 4.3
.

Note that if
\mathscr{C}
is nice then every
\langle A,p\rangle\in\mathscr{C}_{\operatorname{fin}}^{*}
admits an extension to a total automorphism
f
of a structure
X\in\sigma\mathscr{C}
, namely we let
X
be the Fraïssé limit and we apply the homogeneity.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a nice category of structures.
If
\langle A,p\rangle\in\mathscr{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
is an amalgamation base, then it is determined.

Proof.

Let
\langle A,p\rangle\leq\langle X,f\rangle
be any extension to a total automorphism and let
\{\langle a^{i}_{0},\ldots,a^{i}_{n_{i}}\rangle\colon i\in I\}
be an enumeration of the orbits (of all of the sorts) of
\langle A,p\rangle
.
We take
\langle\hat{A},\hat{p}\rangle
to be the substructure of
\langle X,f\rangle
generated by the set
\{f^{k}(a^{i}_{0}):k\in\mathbb{Z},i\in I\}
of elements of the full orbits.
Note that
\langle\hat{A},\hat{p}\rangle
is a total automorphism as
\hat{A}
is
f
-invariant.
Since
\mathscr{C}
is hereditary,
\langle\hat{A},\hat{p}\rangle\in\mathscr{C}^{*}
.

We show that for every other total extension
e\colon\langle A,p\rangle\to\langle Y,g\rangle
there is a unique
\mathscr{C}^{*}
-map
\langle\hat{A},\hat{p}\rangle\to\langle Y,g\rangle
extending

.
For every

let

be generated by
\{f^{k}(a^{i}_{0}):k\in[-n,n],i\in I\}
and let
B_{n}\leq Y
be generated by
\{g^{k}(e(a^{i}_{0})):k\in[-n,n],i\in I\}
.
Moreover, we put
p_{n}=\hat{p}\mathord{\upharpoonright}_{A_{n}}
and

and we let let

be such that
A\leq A_{n_{0}}
and
e[A]\leq B_{n_{0}}

Since
\mathscr{C}
is hereditary and locally finite,
A_{n},B_{n}\in\mathscr{C}_{\operatorname{fin}}
.
Since
\langle A,p\rangle
is an amalgamation base, the extensions
\langle A,p\rangle\leq\langle A_{n},p_{n}\rangle
and

can be amalgamated in
\mathscr{C}_{\operatorname{fin}}^{*}
, and necessarily
f^{k}(a^{i}_{0})
and

for

and

need to be identified in the amalgamation. Therefore we have a unique isomorphism
\langle A_{n},p_{n}\rangle\to\langle B_{n},q_{n}\rangle
and a unique embedding
\langle A_{n},p_{n}\rangle\to\langle Y,g\rangle

e
.
Together, we have a unique embedding
\langle\hat{A},\hat{p}\rangle\to\langle Y,g\rangle
extending
e
.
∎

In order to detect determined partial automorphisms and to show that they are cofinal, it is useful to work with one-point orbit extensions.

Definition 4.6
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a category of structures.
A
forward one-point orbit extension
of
\langle A,p\rangle\in\mathscr{C}^{*}
at
x\in A\setminus\operatorname{dom}(p)
is an extension
\langle B,q\rangle\geq\langle A,p\rangle
in
\mathscr{C}^{*}
such that
x\in\operatorname{dom}(q)
,
B
is generated by
A\cup\{q(x)\}
,
\operatorname{dom}(q)
is generated by
\operatorname{dom}(p)\cup\{x\}
,
\operatorname{rng}(q)
is generated by
\operatorname{rng}(p)\cup\{q(x)\}
.
Note that in the case of relational structures, we just extend the domain and the range by one point.
Also note that we may deal with many-sorted structures, so every point has the associated sort.

We say that two forward one-point orbit extensions
\langle B_{1},q_{1}\rangle,\langle B_{2},q_{2}\rangle\geq\langle A,p\rangle
at
x
are
isomorphic
, if
{\operatorname{id}_{A}}\cup\{q_{1}(x)\mapsto q_{2}(x)\}
extends to a (necessarily unique) isomorphism
\langle B_{1},q_{1}\rangle\to\langle B_{2},q_{2}\rangle
.
When we say that one-point orbit extensions are unique, we mean unique up to isomorphism.

Analogously we define
backward one-point orbit extensions
at
x\in A\setminus\operatorname{rng}(p)
and their isomorphisms.
In particular
\langle A,p\rangle\leq\langle B,q\rangle
is a backward one-point orbit extension at
x
if and only if
\langle A,p^{-1}\rangle\leq\langle B,q^{-1}\rangle
is a forward one-point orbit extension at
x
.

Observation 4.7
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a nice category of structures.
If
\langle A,p\rangle\in\mathscr{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
is determined, then it has unique one-point orbit extensions at all points, and these extensions are themselves determined.

Proof.

Let
\langle B_{1},q_{1}\rangle
and
\langle B_{2},q_{2}\rangle
be (without loss of generality forward) one-point orbit extensions of
\langle A,p\rangle
at
x
.
For
i=1,2
, let
\langle X_{i},f_{i}\rangle\in\mathscr{C}^{*}
be an extension of
\langle B_{i},q_{i}\rangle
to a total automorphism such that
X_{i}
is generated by the orbits
f_{i}^{\mathbb{Z}}(b)
for
b\in B_{i}
.
We first take any extension to a total automorphism, which exists since
\mathscr{C}_{\operatorname{fin}}
is Fraïssé, and then we restrict to the substructure generated by the orbits of the points in
B_{i}
.
The restriction is in
\mathscr{C}^{*}
since
\mathscr{C}
is nice.
It follows that both
\langle X_{1},f_{1}\rangle
and
\langle X_{2},f_{2}\rangle
are isomorphic to the unique totalization of
\langle A,p\rangle
, so there is a unique isomorphism
f\colon\langle X_{1},f_{1}\rangle\to\langle X_{2},f_{2}\rangle
extending
{\operatorname{id}_{A}}
.
Hence,
f(q_{1}(x))=q_{2}(x)
and the restriction of
f
gives the isomorphism of the one-point extensions
\langle B_{1},q_{1}\rangle
and
\langle B_{2},q_{2}\rangle
.
Moreover,
\langle X_{i},f_{i}\rangle
is the unique totalization of
\langle B_{i},q_{i}\rangle
for
i=1,2
.
∎

The following lemma and proposition can be viewed as a converse to the observation above.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a nice category of structures and let
(\langle A_{n},p_{n}\rangle)_{n}
be a countable increasing chain (finite or infinite) of one-point orbit extensions in
\mathscr{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
, i.e.
\langle A_{n+1},p_{n+1}\rangle
is a forward or backward one-point orbit extension of
\langle A_{n},p_{n}\rangle
at
x_{n}\in A_{n}
, and suppose that
\langle\hat{A},\hat{p}\rangle=\bigcup_{n}\langle A_{n},p_{n}\rangle
is a total automorphism.
Then
\langle\hat{A},\hat{p}\rangle
is the unique totalization of
\langle A_{0},p_{0}\rangle
if and only if every
\langle A_{n+1},p_{n+1}\rangle
is the unique forward/backward one-point orbit extension of
\langle A_{n},p_{n}\rangle
at
x_{n}
.

Proof.

The “only if” part follows from Observation
4.7
.
For the “if” part let
e_{0}\colon\langle A_{0},p_{0}\rangle\to\langle X,f\rangle
be any
\mathscr{C}^{*}
-map to a total automorphism.
We want to show that there is a unique extension
\hat{e}\colon\langle\hat{A},\hat{p}\rangle\to\langle X,f\rangle
.
We proceed by induction.
Suppose that we already have an extension
e_{n}\colon\langle A_{n},p_{n}\rangle\to\langle X,f\rangle
of
e_{0}
.
We extend it to
e_{n+1}\colon\langle A_{n+1},p_{n+1}\rangle\to\langle X,f\rangle
.
Let
\langle B,q\rangle\leq\langle X,f\rangle
be the forward/backward one-point orbit extension of the copy of
\langle A_{n},p_{n}\rangle
at
e_{n}(x_{n})
coming from the restriction of
\langle X,f\rangle
.
Then,
e_{n}\colon\langle A_{n},p_{n}\rangle\to\langle B,q\rangle
is a forward/backward one-point orbit extension at
x_{n}
, which is by uniqueness isomorphic to
\langle A_{n+1},p_{n+1}\rangle
, so we let
e_{n+1}\colon\langle A_{n+1},p_{n+1}\rangle\to\langle B,q\rangle\leq\langle X,f\rangle
be the isomorphism.
Finally, we put
\hat{e}=\bigcup_{n}e_{n}
.
As any
\hat{e}
needs to satisfy
\hat{e}(p_{n+1}(x_{n}))=f(\hat{e}(x_{n}))
for every
n
,
\hat{e}
is uniquely determined on
A_{n+1}
, given it is uniquely determined on
A_{n}
, and therefore we get uniqueness by induction as well.
∎

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a nice category of structures.
Suppose that
\mathscr{S}
is a full subcategory of
\mathscr{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
with the following properties.

(1)

Partial automorphisms from
\mathscr{S}
have a unique one-point orbit extension at every point.

(2)

One-point orbit extensions of partial automorphisms in
\mathscr{S}
are themselves in
\mathscr{S}
.

Then any
\langle A,p\rangle\in\mathscr{S}
is determined.
Consequently, if we additionally have that

(3)

\mathscr{S}
is cofinal in
\mathscr{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
,

then determined partial automorphisms are cofinal in
\mathscr{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
.

Proof.

Let
\langle A,p\rangle\in\mathscr{S}
.
Let
\langle X,f\rangle\geq\langle A,p\rangle
be any total automorphism in
\mathscr{C}^{*}
, which exists by Observation
4.3
, and let
\langle\hat{A},\hat{p}\rangle\leq\langle X,f\rangle
be generated by the finite union of orbits of
f
intersecting
A
.
We show that
\langle\hat{A},\hat{p}\rangle
is the unique totalization of
\langle A,p\rangle
.

There is a countable enumeration
(a_{n})
of
\hat{A}
(finite or infinite) such that
A=\{a_{n}:n\leq n_{0}\}
for some
n_{0}
and such that for every
n\geq n_{0}
we have
a_{n+1}=\hat{p}^{\varepsilon_{n}}(x_{n})
for some
\varepsilon_{n}\in\{-1,1\}
and
x_{n}\in A_{n}
where
A_{n}\leq\hat{A}
is the substructure generated by
\{a_{i}:i\leq n\}
.
If we put
p_{n}=\hat{p}\mathord{\upharpoonright}_{A_{n}}
, we have that for every
n
, either
\langle A_{n+1},p_{n+1}\rangle\geq\langle A_{n},p_{n}\rangle
is a forward/backward one-point orbit extension at
x_{n}
, or
\langle A_{n+1},p_{n+1}\rangle=\langle A_{n},p_{n}\rangle
.
By passing to a subsequence if needed, we may assume that we always have one-point extensions.

By Condition (2),
\langle A_{n},p_{n}\rangle\in\mathscr{S}
for every
n\geq n_{0}
, and so by Condition (1),
\langle A_{n+1},p_{n+1}\rangle
is the unique forward/backward one-point orbit extension at
x_{n}
.
Hence, by Lemma
4.8
,
\langle\hat{A},\hat{p}\rangle
is the unique totalization of
\langle A,p\rangle
.
If we additionally have Condition (3), then clearly determined partial automorphisms are cofinal in
\mathscr{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
.
∎

Strategy 4.10
.

Now we are ready to describe our strategy for proving CAP and simultaneously characterizing amalgamation bases in
\mathscr{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
for a nice category of structures
\mathscr{C}
.

(1)

We show that the full subcategory of
\mathscr{C}^{*}
consisting of total automorphisms has AP, so that we can apply Theorem
4.5
.

(2)

We pick a full subcategory
\mathscr{S}\subseteq\mathscr{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
of so-called sufficient partial automorphisms satisfying the assumptions of Proposition
4.9
.

(3)

Then by Proposition
4.9
and Theorem
4.5
it follows that
\mathscr{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
has CAP, the amalgamation bases are exactly the determined partial automorphisms, and
\mathscr{S}
forms a cofinal family of those.

(4)

If we also show that every amalgamation base / determined partial automorphism is necessarily in
\mathscr{S}
, then
\mathscr{S}
forms exactly the class of amalgamation bases / determined partial automorphisms.

The following notion often gives a necessary condition for being an amalgamation base, and in our application it even turns out to be sufficient.

Definition 4.11
.

For a category of structures
\mathscr{C}
and a partial automorphism
\langle A,p\rangle\in\mathscr{C}^{*}
we say that its orbit
p^{\mathbb{Z}}(a)
is
stable
if it does not merge with another orbit of
p
and does not become closed in any extension:
for every
\langle B,q\rangle\geq\langle A,p\rangle
in
\mathscr{C}^{*}
we have
q^{\mathbb{Z}}(a)\cap A=p^{\mathbb{Z}}(a)
, and if
q^{\mathbb{Z}}(a)
is closed, then already
p^{\mathbb{Z}}(a)
is closed.

Proposition 4.12
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a nice category of structures and suppose that
\mathscr{C}_{\operatorname{fin}}
has the strong amalgamation property.
If
\langle A,p\rangle\in\mathscr{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
is an amalgamation base, then every orbit of
\langle A,p\rangle
is stable.

Proof.

Suppose that some extension
\langle B,q\rangle\geq\langle A,p\rangle
in
\mathscr{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
merges or closes some orbits of
\langle A,p\rangle
, i.e. there is a sequence
a\mapsto t_{1}\mapsto\cdots\mapsto t_{n}\mapsto a^{\prime}
in
q
such that
a\in A\setminus\operatorname{dom}(p)
,
a^{\prime}\in A\setminus\operatorname{rng}(p)
, and
t_{i}\in B\setminus A
.
By passing to a minimal such subsequence we can assume that
t_{i}\notin A
for every
i=1,\ldots,n
.
(The case
n=0
also captures the situation of
a=a^{\prime}
becoming a fixed point.)
Since
\mathscr{C}
is nice, we can pass to substructures, so without loss of generality,
\langle B,q\rangle
is generated by
\langle A\cup\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}\},p\cup\{a\mapsto t_{1}\mapsto\cdots\mapsto t_{n}\mapsto a^{\prime}\}\rangle
, i.e.
B
is generated by
A\cup\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}\}
, and
\operatorname{dom}(q)
is generated by
\operatorname{dom}(p)\cup\{a,t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}\}
(and so
\operatorname{rng}(q)
is generated by
\operatorname{rng}(p)\cup\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{n},a^{\prime}\}
).

We will construct another extension
\langle B^{\prime},q^{\prime}\rangle\geq\langle A,p\rangle
not amalgamable with
\langle B,q\rangle
.
Let
R\leq B
be the substructure generated by
\operatorname{rng}(p)\cup\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}\}
, so
\operatorname{rng}(q)\geq R
is generated by
R\cup\{a^{\prime}\}
.
Without loss of generality
B\leq U
where
U
is the Fraïssé limit of
\mathscr{C}_{\operatorname{fin}}
.
We want to find a point
x\in U\setminus B
such that
{\rm{tp}}(x/R)={\rm{tp}}(a^{\prime}/R)
, by which we mean such that there is an isomorphism
f\colon\operatorname{rng}(q)\to Q
extending
{\operatorname{id}_{R}}\cup\{a^{\prime}\mapsto x\}
, where
Q\leq U
is the substructure generated by
R\cup\{x\}
.
We take a strong amalgam
C\in\mathscr{C}_{\operatorname{fin}}
of
B
and
\operatorname{rng}(q)
over
R
, view it as an extension of
B
, which we realize in
U
.
Hence we obtain an embedding
e\colon\operatorname{rng}(q)\to U
extending
{\operatorname{id}_{R}}
such that
x:=e(a^{\prime})\notin B
.

Let
f\colon\operatorname{rng}(q)\to Q
be the isomorphism witnessing
{\rm{tp}}(x/R)={\rm{tp}}(a^{\prime}/R)
, let
q^{\prime}\colon\operatorname{dom}(q)\to Q
be the composition
f\circ q
, and let
B^{\prime}\leq U
be the substructure generated by
A\cup\operatorname{dom}(q^{\prime})\cup\operatorname{rng}(q^{\prime})
.
Then
\langle B^{\prime},q^{\prime}\rangle
is an extension of
\langle A,p\rangle
in
\mathscr{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
that cannot be amalgamated with
\langle B,q\rangle
over
\langle A,p\rangle
.
This is because
s^{n+1}(a)=a^{\prime}\in A
for every extension
s\supseteq q
, while
(s^{\prime})^{n+1}(a)=x\not\in A
for every extension
s^{\prime}\supseteq q^{\prime}
.
∎

We shall illustrate Strategy
4.10
on the example of linear orders.
Then we proceed to our main application – ultrametric spaces with partial dc-automorphisms.

Proposition 4.13
.

(\mathfrak{L}^{0})^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
has CAP and for
\langle A,p\rangle\in(\mathfrak{L}^{0})^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

\langle A,p\rangle
is an amalgamation base;

(b)

\langle A,p\rangle
is determined;

(c)

every orbit of
\langle A,p\rangle
is stable.

Proof.

We follow the strategy above.
First, by Lemma
5.5
, which we will prove later, total automorphisms have AP.
Second, we let class
\mathscr{S}\subseteq(\mathfrak{L}^{0})^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
of sufficient partial automorphisms consist exactly of those with all orbits stable.
We need to check the assumptions (1), (2), (3) of Proposition
4.9
.

Clearly, (2) is satisfied since extending a stable orbit cannot make it unstable.
Also we have (3) since if some orbit(s) of a given partial automorphism
\langle A,p\rangle
can be closed or merged in an extension
\langle B,q\rangle\geq\langle A,p\rangle
, we can pass to that extension, which without loss of generality does not add any new orbits (we can always remove new orbits).
After finitely many steps all orbits become stable.

Now we show (1), the uniqueness of one-point extensions, i.e. for every
\langle A,p\rangle\in\mathscr{S}
and
x\in A\setminus\operatorname{dom}(p)
there is up to isomorphism a unique forward one-point orbit extension of
\langle A,p\rangle
at
x
in
(\mathfrak{L}^{0})^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
.
(It is enough to consider only forward one-point orbit extensions as
\langle A,p\rangle\in\mathscr{S}
if and only if
\langle A,p^{-1}\rangle\in\mathscr{S}
.)
So let
x\in A\setminus\operatorname{dom}(p)
.
Forward one-point orbit extensions are exactly of the form
\langle A\cup\{y\},p\cup\{x\mapsto y\}\rangle
for
y\notin\operatorname{rng}(p)
and
p[A_{0}]<y<p[A_{1}]
, where
A_{0}=\{a\in\operatorname{dom}(p):a<x\}
and
A_{1}=\{a\in\operatorname{dom}(p):x<a\}
.
Note that the sets
A_{i}
may be empty and the point
y
may or may not be in
A
.
However, if there is any
y\in A
such that
p[A_{0}]<y<p[A_{1}]
, then the corresponding extension would close or merge orbits, which would contradict the assumption.
Hence, the interval between
p[A_{0}]
and
p[A_{1}]
is empty in
A
and can be filled with a new point in a unique way up to isomorphism.

Since the assumptions (1), (2), (3) are satisfied, by Proposition
4.9
and Theorem
4.5
we have that
(\mathfrak{L}^{0})^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
has CAP and we have (a)
\Leftrightarrow
(b)
\Leftarrow
(c) in the present proposition.
The implication (a)
\Rightarrow
(c) follows from Proposition
4.12
.
∎

5
A generic dc-automorphism

We apply Strategy
4.10
to characterize amalgamation bases and show CAP of
\mathfrak{U}^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
(Theorem
5.9
), and consequently to prove the existence of a generic dc-automorphism of
\mathbb{U}
(Corollary
5.10
).

5.1
Total dc-automorphisms have amalgamation

We shall prove that the full subcategory of
\mathfrak{U}^{*}
consisting of total automorphisms has AP.
This is the main assumption of Theorem
4.5
.

The first step is to give a certain “free-like” amalgamation of (even infinite) ultrametric spaces – a strong amalgamation such that distances between points from the extensions are as large as possible.
This is essentially an instance of so-called
independent amalgamation
(see
[
14
, Section 3]
,
[
3
, Section 2]
,
[
12
, Definition 2.3]
, and Remark
5.3
), which is well-suited to independently amalgamate new orbits added by the respective extensions of automorphisms.

Recall that
\mathfrak{I}^{E}\subseteq\mathfrak{U}
for
E\in\mathfrak{L}^{0}
denotes the subcategory of all ultrametric spaces
X
with
D_{X}\leq E
and of all isometric embeddings.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
E
be a complete linear order in
\mathfrak{L}^{0}
(i.e. every subset has the infimum and the supremum) where
0
is isolated, i.e.
\min(E\setminus\{0\})
exists.
Then
\mathfrak{I}^{E}
has AP.
More precisely, for all spaces
X\leq Y_{i}
in
\mathfrak{I}^{E}
,
i=1,2
, with
Y_{1}\cap Y_{2}=X
, the following ultrametric on
Z=Y_{1}\cup Y_{2}
with
D_{Z}=E
extends
d_{Y_{1}}\cup d_{Y_{1}}
:

d_{Z}(x,y)=\inf_{a\in Y_{i}\cap Y_{j}}\max\{d_{Y_{i}}(x,a),d_{Y_{j}}(a,y)\}

for every
x\in Y_{i}
and
y\in Y_{j}
.

Proof.

As
d_{Y_{i}}(x,y)=\inf_{a\in A}\max\{d_{Y_{i}}(x,a),d_{Y_{i}}(a,y)\}
for every
x,y\in Y_{i}
and
A\subseteq Y_{i}
containing
x
or
y
, the map
d_{Z}
extends
d_{Y_{i}}
and is well-defined (if
x\in X
, it does not matter whether we view it as a member of
Y_{1}
or
Y_{2}
).
Clearly,
d_{Z}
is symmetric and
d_{Z}(x,x)=0
.
Since
0\in E
is isolated, if
d_{Z}(x,y)=0
, then
\max\{d_{Y_{i}}(x,a),d_{Y_{j}}(a,y)\}=0
for some
a
, and so
x=a=y
.

It remains to prove the ultrametric triangle inequality, i.e. for every
x\in Y_{i}
,
y\in Y_{j}
, and
z\in Y_{k}
we want

\displaystyle\inf_{a\in Y_{i}\cap Y_{k}}\max\{d_{Y_{i}}(x,a),d_{Y_{k}}(a,z)\}\leq\max\bigl\{

\displaystyle\inf_{b\in Y_{i}\cap Y_{j}}\max\{d_{Y_{i}}(x,b),d_{Y_{j}}(b,y)\},

\displaystyle\inf_{c\in Y_{j}\cap Y_{k}}\max\{d_{Y_{j}}(y,c),d_{Y_{k}}(c,z)\}\bigr\}.

Since every complete linear order is completely distributive, we may rewrite the right-hand side as

\inf_{b\in Y_{i}\cap Y_{j}}\inf_{c\in Y_{j}\cap Y_{k}}\max\{d_{Y_{i}}(x,b),d_{Y_{j}}(b,y),d_{Y_{j}}(y,c),d_{Y_{k}}(c,z)\}.

Since
\max\{d_{Y_{j}}(b,y),d_{Y_{j}}(y,c)\}\geq d_{Y_{j}}(b,c)
, it is enough to show

\inf_{a\in Y_{i}\cap Y_{k}}\max\{d_{Y_{i}}(x,a),d_{Y_{k}}(a,z)\}\leq\inf_{b\in Y_{i}\cap Y_{j}}\inf_{c\in Y_{j}\cap Y_{k}}\max\{d_{Y_{i}}(x,b),d_{Y_{j}}(b,c),d_{Y_{k}}(c,z)\}.

If
i=j
, the right-hand side is greater than
\inf_{b\in Y_{i},\,c\in Y_{i}\cap Y_{k}}\max\{d_{Y_{i}}(x,c),d_{Y_{k}}(c,z)\}
, and we are done (if
Y_{i}=\emptyset
, the desired inequality is trivially true).
If
j=k
, analogous argument works.
Otherwise, we have
i\neq j\neq k=i
, so
Y_{i}\cap Y_{j}=Y_{j}\cap Y_{k}=X
, and the inequality simplifies to

\inf_{a\in Y_{i}}\max\{d_{Y_{i}}(x,a),d_{Y_{i}}(a,z)\}=d_{Y_{i}}(x,z)\leq\inf_{b,c\in X}\max\{d_{Y_{i}}(x,b),d_{X}(b,c),d_{Y_{i}}(c,z)\}.\qed

Let
\mathfrak{U}^{*\langle E,\phi\rangle}\subseteq\mathfrak{U}^{*}
for
E\in\mathfrak{L}^{0}
and
\phi\in\operatorname{Aut}(E)
denote the subcategory whose objects
\langle X,p\rangle
are such such that
\langle D_{X},D_{p}\rangle\leq\langle E,\phi\rangle
and whose morphisms are
\mathfrak{U}^{*}
-maps
f\colon\langle X,p\rangle\to\langle Y,q\rangle
that are isometric embeddings, i.e.
D_{f}\subseteq{\operatorname{id}_{E}}
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
E\in\mathfrak{L}^{0}
be a complete linear order where
0
is isolated, and let
\phi\in\operatorname{Aut}(E)
.
Then the full subcategory of
\mathfrak{U}^{*\langle E,\phi\rangle}
consisting of total automorphisms has AP.

Proof.

Let
\langle X,f\rangle\leq\langle Y_{i},g_{i}\rangle
for
i=1,2
be ultrametric spaces with automorphisms such that
D_{X}\leq D_{Y_{i}}\leq E
and
D_{f}\subseteq D_{g_{i}}\subseteq\phi
.
Without loss of generality
Y_{1}\cap Y_{2}=X
.
Let
Z=\langle Y_{1}\cup Y_{2},d_{Z},E\rangle
be the free-like amalgamation obtained from Lemma
5.1
.
As
g_{i}\in\operatorname{Aut}(Y_{i})
and
f=g_{1}\cap g_{2}\in\operatorname{Aut}(X)
, the map
h=g_{1}\cup g_{2}
is a well-defined bijection on
Z
.

It remains to show that
\langle h,\phi\rangle\in\operatorname{Aut}(Z)
.
For every
x\in Y_{i}
and
y\in Y_{j}
we have

\displaystyle\phi(d_{Z}(x,y))

\displaystyle=\phi\bigl(\inf_{a\in Y_{i}\cap Y_{j}}\max\{d_{Y_{i}}(x,a),\,d_{Y_{j}}(a,y)\}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\inf_{a\in Y_{i}\cap Y_{j}}\max\{\phi(d_{Y_{i}}(x,a)),\,\phi(d_{Y_{j}}(a,y))\}

\displaystyle=\inf_{a\in Y_{i}\cap Y_{j}}\max\{d_{Y_{i}}(g_{i}(x),g_{i}(a)),\,d_{Y_{j}}(g_{j}(a),g_{j}(y))\}

\displaystyle=\inf_{a\in\operatorname{rng}(g_{i}\cap g_{j})=Y_{i}\cap Y_{j}}\max\{d_{Y_{i}}(h(x),a),\,d_{Y_{j}}(a,h(y))\}=d_{Z}(h(x),h(y)).\qed

Remark 5.3
.

As seen in the proof of the previous proposition, the amalgamation from Lemma
5.1
is independent in the sense that it is such an amalgamation
Y_{1}\to Z\leftarrow Y_{2}
of a span
Y_{1}\leftarrow X\to Y_{2}
in
\mathfrak{I}^{E}
that every lifting
\langle Y_{1},g_{1}\rangle\leftarrow\langle X,f\rangle\to\langle Y_{2},g_{2}\rangle
with respect to the forgetful functor
\mathfrak{U}^{*\langle E,\phi\rangle}\to\mathfrak{I}^{E}
admits an amalgamating lifting
\langle Y_{1},g_{1}\rangle\to\langle Z,h\rangle\leftarrow\langle Y_{2},g_{2}\rangle
.
This can be compared to the notion of
amalgamation property with automorphisms (APA)
coined by Siniora
[
13
]
and originating from Hodges–Hodkinson–Lascar–Shelah
[
4
]
.

To elevate the previous proposition from
\mathfrak{U}^{*\langle E,\phi\rangle}
to
\mathfrak{U}^{*}
, we will need the following.

Observation 5.4
.

For every
\langle X,D_{X}\rangle\in\mathfrak{U}
and every
\mathfrak{L}^{0}
-map
f\colon D_{X}\to E
there is the canonical lifting: the dc-embedding
\bar{f}\colon\langle X,D_{X}\rangle\to\langle X,E\rangle
with
D_{\bar{f}}=f
, i.e. we put
d_{\langle X,E\rangle}(x,y)=f(d_{X}(x,y))
and we let
\bar{f}\colon X\to X
be the identity.

The canonical liftings are compositional in the sense that for every dc-embedding
e\colon X\to Y
and
\mathfrak{L}^{0}
-maps
f\colon D_{X}\to E
and
g\colon D_{Y}\to E
such that
f=g\circ D_{e}
we have
e\circ\bar{f}=\bar{g}\circ e
where
e
is also viewed as an isometric embedding
\langle X,E\rangle\to\langle Y,E\rangle
.

Analogously, we have canonical liftings for partial automorphisms, i.e. for every
\langle X,p,D_{X},D_{p}\rangle\in\mathfrak{U}^{*}
and every
(\mathfrak{L}^{0})^{*}
-map
f\colon\langle D_{X},D_{p}\rangle\to\langle E,\phi\rangle
we have the lifting
\langle X,p,D_{X},D_{p}\rangle\to\langle X,p,E,\phi\rangle
.

Lemma 5.5
.

The full subcategory of
(\mathfrak{L}^{0})^{*}

Proof.

We can equivalently work in
\mathfrak{L}^{*}
.
Let
f_{i}\colon\langle X,p\rangle\to\langle Y_{i},q_{i}\rangle

be embeddings of automorphisms of linear orders.
Without loss of generality,
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are inclusions, and
Y_{1}\cap Y_{2}=X
.
Let
Y^{\prime}_{i}=Y_{i}\setminus X

q^{\prime}_{i}=q_{i}\restriction Y^{\prime}_{i}\in\operatorname{Aut}(Y^{\prime}_{i})

We put

and

Clearly

We need to extend the linear order to

Let

If there is

This is coherent with the definition of

We proceed analogously if there is

y_{1}>_{Y_{1}}x>_{Y_{2}}y_{2}

Finally, if

y_{1}<_{Z}y_{2}

Then

since

5.2
Amalgamation bases for partial dc-automorphisms

Let
\mathscr{S}\subseteq\mathfrak{U}^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
be the class of all finite partial dc-automorphisms whose all orbits of both sorts are stable, which is a necessary condition for being an amalgamation base by Proposition
4.12
.
We show that it is also sufficient.

Observation 5.7
.

We have that
\langle A,p\rangle\in\mathfrak{U}^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
is in
\mathscr{S}
if and only if every orbit of the point sort is stable and
\langle D_{A},D_{p}\rangle
is an amalgamation base in
(\mathfrak{L}^{0})^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
(or equivalently is determined in
(\mathfrak{L}^{0})^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
).

Proof.

The claim follows immediately from Proposition
4.13
once we observe that an orbit of
\langle A,p\rangle
of the distance sort is stable in
\mathfrak{U}^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
if and only if it is stable in
(\mathfrak{L}^{0})^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
as an orbit of
\langle D_{A},D_{p}\rangle
, i.e. we can close or merge an orbit of
D_{p}
in an extension
\langle E,\phi\rangle\geq\langle D_{A},D_{p}\rangle
if and only if we can close or merge the orbit in an extension
\langle B,q\rangle\geq\langle A,p\rangle
.
The last equivalence is true because every such extension
\langle B,q\rangle\geq\langle A,p\rangle
induces
\langle E,\phi\rangle=\langle D_{B},D_{q}\rangle\geq\langle D_{A},D_{p}\rangle
, and every extension
\langle E,\phi\rangle\geq\langle D_{A},D_{p}\rangle
induces
\langle A,p,E,\phi\rangle\geq\langle A,p,D_{A},D_{p}\rangle
, and we let
\langle B,q,D_{B},D_{q}\rangle=\langle A,p,E,\phi\rangle
.
∎

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
\langle A,p\rangle\in\mathscr{S}
.
For every
x\in A\setminus\operatorname{dom}(p)
there is a unique (up to isomorphism) forward one-point orbit extension
\langle A^{\prime},p^{\prime}\rangle
at
x
.
Moreover, every
r\in D_{A^{\prime}}\setminus D_{A}
is in an extension of an orbit of
D_{p}
.

Proof.

Let
\langle A^{\prime},p^{\prime}\rangle=\langle A\cup\{y\},p\cup\{x\mapsto y\}\rangle
be any such extension.
It is enough to show that for every
a\in A
the distance
d_{A^{\prime}}(a,y)
is determined by the distance part
D_{p^{\prime}}
and by an old distance.
Specifically,
d_{A^{\prime}}(a,y)=\max\{D_{p^{\prime}}(r_{a}),s_{a}\}
, where
r_{a},s_{a}\in D_{A}
depend only on
a
and
x
.
This is indeed enough since the extension
D_{p^{\prime}}\geq D_{p}
is unique up to isomorphism as
D_{p}
is determined by Observation
5.7
.

Let
a\in A
.
If
a\in\operatorname{rng}(p)
and so
a=p(a^{\prime})
for some
a^{\prime}\in A
, we have
d(a,y)=D_{p^{\prime}}(d(a^{\prime},x))
, and hence in the formula above we can put
r_{a}=d(a^{\prime},x)
and
s_{a}=0
.

Otherwise,
a\notin\operatorname{rng}(p)
.
Let
b\in\operatorname{dom}(p)
be such that the distance
d(p(b),a)
is minimal possible.
Then we define
r_{a}=d(b,x)
and
s_{a}=d(p(b),a)
.
We consider the triangle
y,a,p(b)
with distances
d(y,a)
,
s_{a}
, and
d(y,p(b))=D_{p^{\prime}}(r_{a})
.
Clearly, if
s_{a}\neq D_{p^{\prime}}(r_{a})
, then
d(y,a)=\max\{s_{a},D_{p^{\prime}}(r_{a})\}
and we are done.
Otherwise, we have
d(y,a)=s_{a}=D_{p^{\prime}}(r_{a})
and we are also done, or
d(y,a)<s_{a}=D_{p^{\prime}}(r_{a})
.
We claim the last case contradicts our assumptions as then
q=p\cup\{x\mapsto a\}
induces a partial automorphism of
A
.
For that it is enough to show that
\phi(d(x,t))=d(a,p(t))
for every
t\in\operatorname{dom}(p)
, where
\phi
is the unique totalization of
D_{p^{\prime}}
in
(\mathfrak{L}^{0})^{*}
, as then letting
D_{q}
be the restriction of
\phi
to
d[\operatorname{dom}(q)^{2}]
turns
q
into a partial dc-automorphism.
Let
t\in\operatorname{dom}(p)
.
We have
d_{A^{\prime}}(y,a)<s_{a}=d(p(b),a)\leq d(p(t),a)
.
And therefore by considering the triangle
y,a,p(t)
in
A^{\prime}
we have

d(a,p(t))=d_{A^{\prime}}(y,p(t))=D_{p^{\prime}}(d(x,t))=\phi(d(x,t))=D_{q}(d(x,t)).

The moreover part of the proposition follows from
d_{A^{\prime}}(a,y)=\max\{D_{p^{\prime}}(r_{a}),s_{a}\}
.
∎

Theorem 5.9
.

\mathfrak{U}^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
has CAP and for
\langle A,p\rangle\in\mathfrak{U}^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

\langle A,p\rangle
is an amalgamation base;

(b)

\langle A,p\rangle
is determined;

(c)

every orbit of
\langle A,p\rangle
is stable;

(d)

every orbit of the point sort of
\langle A,p\rangle
is stable and
\langle D_{A},D_{p}\rangle
is an amalgamation base in
(\mathfrak{L}^{0})^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
.

Proof.

We follow Strategy
4.10
.
First, we showed in Corollary
5.6
that total automorphisms have AP.
Our class of sufficient partial automorphisms
\mathscr{S}
is defined by (c), or equivalently (d), by Observation
5.7
.
We need to check the assumptions (1), (2), (3) of Proposition
4.9
.

Assumption (1) for the point sort follows from Proposition
5.8
(as in the proof of Proposition
4.13
, it is enough to consider the forward case).
For the distance sort (1) follows from Observation
5.7
as the distance part is determined.

As a stable orbit cannot become unstable in an extension, to show (2) it is enough to observe that a one-point orbit extension at
x
does not add any new orbits.
If
x
is a distance, we only extend an existing orbit of the distance sort, and if
x
is a point, we extend an existing orbit of the point sort and possibly extend an existing orbit of the distance sort, but by Proposition
5.8
no new orbit of the distance sort is added.

To show (3), we inductively close or merge existing orbits as in the proof of Proposition
4.13
, but first we take care of all orbits from the point sort, which may introduce new orbits of the distance sort, and then we take care of all orbits of the distance sort, and this does not interfere with the point sort.

Since the assumptions (1), (2), (3) are satisfied, by Proposition
4.9
and Theorem
4.5
we have that
\mathfrak{U}^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
has CAP and we have (a)
\Leftrightarrow
(b)
\Leftarrow
(c) in the present proposition.
The implication (a)
\Rightarrow
(c) follows from Proposition
4.12
.
∎

Corollary 5.10
.

The countable Urysohn ultrametric space
\mathbb{U}
has a generic dc-automorphism.

Proof.

By the Theorem
5.9
,
\mathfrak{U}^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
has CAP, and since the empty automorphism
\langle\emptyset,\emptyset\rangle
is total and so an amalgamation base, JEP follows from amalgamation.
Hence,
\mathbb{U}
has a generic automorphism by the theorem of Kechris and Rosendal (Theorem
2.7
).
∎

In both
(\mathfrak{L}^{0})^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
and
\mathfrak{U}^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
the amalgamation bases are exactly partial automorphisms with stable orbits.
We believe this is not the case in general, but we failed to find a simple counterexample.

Question 5.11
.

Is there a nice category of structures
\mathscr{C}
such that
\mathscr{C}_{\operatorname{fin}}
has SAP, the full subcategory of
\mathscr{C}^{*}
of total automorphisms has AP, and
\mathscr{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
has CAP, but not every
\langle A,p\rangle\in\mathscr{C}^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
with stable orbits is determined?

Coming back to
\mathfrak{U}^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
,
let us define a subfamily
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathscr{S}
of all amalgamations bases
\langle A,p\rangle
where we additionally suppose that
\langle A,p\rangle
is precise, i.e. the spaces
A
,
\operatorname{dom}(p)
, and
\operatorname{rng}(p)
use all distances, and that every orbit of the point sort is closed or monotone.

Proposition 5.12
.

The family
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathscr{S}
is cofinal, i.e. every partial automorphism
\langle A,p\rangle\in\mathfrak{U}^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
has an extension in
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, by Theorem
5.9
, we already have
\langle A,p\rangle\in\mathscr{S}
.
We apply Theorem
3.10
to extend every orbit of the point sort, one by one, so that it becomes closed or monotone.
Clearly, every orbit of the point sort of the resulting extension
\langle A^{\prime},p^{\prime}\rangle
remains stable, but we may have added some new orbits of the distance sort.
We consider an extension
\langle E,f\rangle\geq\langle D_{A^{\prime}},D_{p^{\prime}}\rangle
in
(\mathfrak{L}^{0}_{\operatorname{fin}})^{*}
where all orbits are stable and without loss of generality
f(r)=r
for
r=\max(E)
.
By passing to a suitable orbit extension if needed, we may additionally assume that
E=\operatorname{dom}(f)\cup\operatorname{rng}(f)
(so every
t\in E
is a part of an increasing or decreasing or fix-point orbit) and that the end-points of the increasing and decreasing orbits of
f
are not elements of
D_{\operatorname{dom}(p^{\prime})}
and
D_{\operatorname{rng}(p^{\prime})}
.

Finally we pass to an extension
\langle A^{\prime},p^{\prime}\rangle\leq\langle A^{\prime\prime},p^{\prime\prime}\rangle\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}
with
\langle D_{A^{\prime\prime}},D_{p^{\prime\prime}}\rangle=\langle E,f\rangle
.
For every increasing or decreasing orbit
t=(t_{i})_{0\leq i<k}
of
\langle E,f\rangle
we consider the space
M_{t}=\{a_{i}:0\leq i\leq k\}
with the distance
d(a_{i},a_{j})=t_{i}
for
0\leq i<j\leq k
for the increasing case and
d(a_{i},a_{j})=t_{j-1}
for the decreasing case, and its partial automorphism
m_{t}(a_{i})=a_{i+1}
for
0\leq i<k
.
Note that
\operatorname{dom}(m_{t})
uses all the distances
t_{i}
, but the last one;
\operatorname{rng}(m_{t})
uses all the distance
t_{i}
, but the first one.
Next, for every fixed point
x\in E\setminus\{r\}
we consider the space
F_{x}=\{b_{0},b_{1}\}
with the distance
d(b_{0},b_{1})=x
, and its partial automorphism
q_{x}(b_{i})=b_{i}
, so that the distance
x
is used both in
\operatorname{dom}(q_{x})
and
\operatorname{rng}(q_{x})
.
Finally we put the parts together: we let

\textstyle\langle A^{\prime\prime},p^{\prime\prime}\rangle=\langle A^{\prime},p^{\prime}\rangle\cup\bigcup_{t}\langle M_{t},m_{t}\rangle\cup\bigcup_{x}\langle F_{x},q_{x}\rangle

and let
d(y,z)=r
for
y
and
z
from different parts.

Clearly, every orbit of the point sort is closed or monotone, and
\langle D_{A^{\prime\prime}},D_{p^{\prime\prime}}\rangle=\langle E,f\rangle
, so all orbits of the distance sort are stable.
By the additional assumptions on
\langle E,f\rangle
, the spaces
A^{\prime\prime}
,
\operatorname{dom}(p^{\prime\prime})
,
\operatorname{rng}(p^{\prime\prime})
are precise.
It remains to show that orbits of the point sort are stable.
As every orbit is already closed or monotone, it is enough to show that the orbits cannot be merged.
Since we started with
\langle A^{\prime},p^{\prime}\rangle
, no two orbits from
p^{\prime}
can be merged.
Clearly, no new fixed-point orbit can be merged with anything.
Every extension of a new monotone orbit satisfies
d(y,z)<r
for each pair of its points since
D_{p^{\prime\prime}}(r)=r
, and therefore cannot be merged with any other orbit of
\langle A^{\prime\prime},p^{\prime\prime}\rangle
as the distance between different orbits is
r
.
∎

Corollary 5.13
.

Every orbit of the point sort of the generic dc-automorphism
g
of
\mathbb{U}
is closed or monotone, and
D_{g}
is the generic automorphism of
D_{\mathbb{U}}=\mathbb{Q}^{\geq 0}
.

Proof.

For both parts we use Theorem
2.7
, which says that the generic automorphism is the limit of the corresponding weak Fraïssé class of partial automorphisms.
For the first part use Observation
2.5
to obtain the generic dc-automorphism as a colimit of a sequence from any cofinal subclass of
\mathfrak{U}_{\operatorname{fin}}^{*}
.
We can take the subclass of amalgamation bases
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}
, which is cofinal by Proposition
5.12
, or even the class of all finite partial dc-automorphisms with only closed or monotone orbits, which is cofinal already by Theorem
3.10
.

For the second part can we apply Corollary
2.14
to the forgetful functor
D\colon\mathfrak{U}\to\mathfrak{L}^{0}
as
D^{*}_{\operatorname{fin}}
is clearly wide and star-surjective by Observation
5.4
.
∎

6
No generic pair of dc-automorphisms

In this section we show that the countable Urysohn ultrametric space
\mathbb{U}
has no generic pair of dc-automorphisms.

The following result was originally proved by Hodkinson in an unpublished note.

Theorem 6.1

(Hodkinson, see Truss
[
15
, Theorem 2.4]
)
.

There is no generic pair of automorphisms of
\langle\mathbb{Q},\leq\rangle
.
Equivalently, the class
(\mathfrak{L}_{\operatorname{fin}})^{*2}
does not have WAP.

Clearly the category
(\mathfrak{L}_{\operatorname{fin}}^{0})^{*2}
is equivalent to
(\mathfrak{L}_{\operatorname{fin}})^{*2}
, therefore
(\mathfrak{L}_{\operatorname{fin}}^{0})^{*2}
does not have WAP as well.

Corollary 6.2
.

The class
\mathfrak{U}_{\operatorname{fin}}^{*2}
does not have WAP, and so
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{U})
does not have a generic pair.

Proof.

We will use the contraposition of the transfer principle from Proposition
2.11
(3) applied to the forgetful functor
(D_{\operatorname{fin}})^{*2}\colon(\mathfrak{U}_{\operatorname{fin}})^{*2}\to(\mathfrak{L}^{0}_{\operatorname{fin}})^{*2}
.
Observation
5.4
implies that the functor
(D_{\operatorname{fin}})^{*2}
is star-surjective.
Clearly, for every
\langle E,\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\rangle\in(\mathfrak{L}^{0})^{*2}
we may consider
X=\langle\emptyset,E\rangle
and
p_{i}=\langle\emptyset,\phi_{i}\rangle
for
i=1,2
, showing that
(D_{\operatorname{fin}})^{*2}
is wide.
Hence,
(\mathfrak{U}_{\operatorname{fin}})^{*2}
does not have WAP, since by Theorem
6.1
,
(\mathfrak{L}^{0}_{\operatorname{fin}})^{*2}
does not have WAP.
Therefore, there is no generic pair of dc-automorphisms of
\mathbb{U}
by Theorem
2.7

∎

In the previous paper
[
2
, Section 3.3]
we studied the category
\mathfrak{U}^{\prec}_{\operatorname{fin}}
of finite convexly ordered ultrametric spaces.
We showed that it is Fraïssé and that its limit
\mathbb{U}^{\prec}
is the rational Urysohn ultrametric space
\mathbb{U}
endowed with a generic convex order
[
2
, Theorem 3.16]
.
The distance functor
(D^{\prec}_{\operatorname{fin}})^{*2}\colon(\mathfrak{U}^{\prec}_{\operatorname{fin}})^{*2}\to(\mathfrak{L}^{0})^{*2}
is again wide and star-surjective by an analogous argument, and so we obtain the following.

Corollary 6.3
.

The class
(\mathfrak{U}^{\prec}_{\operatorname{fin}})^{*2}
does not have WAP, and so
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{U}^{\prec})
does not have a generic pair.

Question 6.4
.

Does
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{U}^{\prec})
have a generic dc-automorphism?

We believe that our proof for
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{U})
should extend to
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{U}^{\prec})
.
