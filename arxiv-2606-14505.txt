Hölder regularity up to the boundary for the g-Laplacian on Reifenberg flat domains Universidade Federal do Ceará. Departamento de Matemática. Fortaleza - CE, Brazil. E-mail address: alanpio@ufc.br
by

Abstract
We establish boundary Hölder regularity for weak solutions to a class of nonlinear elliptic Dirichlet problems with nonstandard growth posed on Reifenberg flat domains. More precisely, for any prescribed exponent α ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \alpha\in(0,1) , we show that weak solutions are α \alpha -Hölder continuous up to the boundary provided that the Reifenberg flatness parameter is sufficiently small. The proof combines an iterative boundary decay argument with an ABP-type maximum principle in the Orlicz–Sobolev setting. Keywords : Reifenberg flat domains; Holder regularity; Orlicz spaces; g-Laplacian. AMS Subject Classification: Primary 35B65, 35J87; Secondary 35R05, 46E35

Introduction and main results

In this manuscript, we investigate boundary regularity for weak solutions to the quasilinear elliptic equation

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Delta_{g}u&=f\quad\text{in }\Omega,\\
u&=\psi\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded domain.
The operator
\Delta_{g}
is the so-called g-Laplace operator, defined by

\Delta_{g}u:=\operatorname{div}\left(\frac{g(|\nabla u|)}{|\nabla u|}\nabla u\right),

where
g:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is the derivative of a Young function G, satisfying the following structural conditions:

•

(
PC
)
Primitive condition:

G^{\prime}(t)=g(t),\quad g\in C^{0}([0,+\infty)])\cap C^{1}((0,+\infty))

•

(
QC
)
Quotient condition:
There exist positive constants
\delta_{0}\leq g_{0}
such that

\delta_{0}\leq\frac{t\cdot g^{\prime}(t)}{g(t)}\leq g_{0},\quad\forall t>0.

Originally introduced by Lieberman in
[
1
]
, these assumptions offer a natural analytical framework for studying boundary regularity in singular or degenerate elliptic equations with non-standard growth. Clearly, this operator recovers the classical p-Laplacian by taking
g(t)=t^{p-1}
with
\delta_{0}=g_{0}=p-1
, respectively. A different example consists of a function
g(t)=t^{a}\log(bt+c)
with
a,b,c>0
that satisfies
(QC)
for
\delta_{0}=a
and
g_{0}=a+1.

Moreover, the class of functions satisfying the conditions introduced by Lieberman is broad, since any positive linear combination of functions satisfying
(PC)
and
(QC)
also satisfies these conditions. Furthermore, the product and composition of functions satisfying
(PC)
and
(QC)
also satisfy these conditions.

Boundary regularity for nonlinear PDEs has long been a central topic in analysis, particularly in elliptic and parabolic problems where the behavior of weak solutions near the boundary presents delicate analytical difficulties. In this context, the geometry of the domain and the regularity of the boundary data are crucial in determining the degree of regularity that can be achieved.

Under suitable regularity assumptions on the domain, weak solutions to the Dirichlet problem (
1.1
) are known to enjoy corresponding regularity properties; see, for instance,
[
5
]
. In this paper we investigate boundary regularity under substantially weaker geometric assumptions, namely when the underlying domain is a Reifenberg flat domain. This means that for the domain
\Omega,
at each boundary point and every scale the boundary of the domain is between two hyperplanes separated by a distance which depends on the scale.

The notion of Reifenberg-flat sets was first introduced by Reifenberg in 1960
[
3
]
while he was working on the Plateau problem. Since then, these sets have played an important role in the study of minimal surfaces. Over the past decades, a substantial body of work has been devoted to the study of elliptic and parabolic equations on Reifenberg flat domains. Interest in this setting has continued to grow, reflecting the importance of Reifenberg flatness as a natural geometric framework extending beyond the classical smooth-domain theory. Among the contributions in this direction are the works of Byun, Wang, Zhou and Yang
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
, Lemenant and Sire
[
15
]
, and Milakis and Toro
[
12
]
(see also the references therein), which address various regularity and qualitative properties of solutions in Reifenberg flat domains.

More recently, the works
[
13
,
6
,
11
]
have introduced new approaches to the study of boundary regularity for solutions in Reifenberg flat domains. These developments have led to boundary regularity results, together with quantitative estimates, in a variety of settings, including the Laplace equation, linear elliptic equations in both divergence and non-divergence form, fully nonlinear elliptic equations, the
p
-Laplace equation, and the fractional Laplacian. Furthermore, in
[
14
]
, sharp boundary regularity estimates were established for solutions to nonlocal elliptic equations associated with operators comparable to the fractional Laplacian in Reifenberg flat domains. Our goal is to extend that theory to the framework considered here. We establish an ABP-type estimate for the difference of weak solutions to (
1.1
), which provides the main tool for obtaining the regularity results established in this paper. Our main operator, the
g
-Laplacian, displays either degenerate elliptic or singular behavior and, unlike the homogeneous
p
-Laplacian, lacks homogeneity. This absence of homogeneity creates additional challenges and forces us to develop new arguments at some stages of the analysis.

We now introduce some notation that establishes the framework for the main results. The first of these is a notion of Reifenberg flat domain introduced in
[
6
]
.

.

We say that

is
\delta-
Reifenberg flat from the exterior at

(isometric to the original coordinate system) such that

in this coordinate system and

Remark 1.2
.

We say that
\Omega
satisfies the exterior cone condition with

\delta
at

and a unit vector

\delta

at 0.

.

Preliminary tools

In this section, we collect some auxiliary results that will be used throughout the paper.
Associated with the
N
-function
G
, we consider the Orlicz and Orlicz–Sobolev spaces.

Definition 2.1
.

Given an
N
-function
G
, we define the Orlicz space
L^{G}(\Omega)
as the set of measurable functions
h
in
\Omega
such that

\int_{\Omega}G(|h(x)|)\,dx<\infty,

where we adopt the Luxemburg norm

\|h\|_{L^{G}(\Omega)}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\int_{\Omega}G\left(\frac{|h(x)|}{\lambda}\right)\,dx\leq 1\right\}.

Associated with the Orlicz space, we define the Orlicz–Sobolev space
W^{1,G}(\Omega)
as the set of all measurable functions
h
such that
h
and all its distributional derivatives
D_{i}h
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, belong to
L^{G}(\Omega)
. In this case, we endow
W^{1,G}(\Omega)
with the norm

\|h\|_{W^{1,G}(\Omega)}=\|h\|_{L^{G}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla h\|_{L^{G}(\Omega)}.

Remark 2.2
.

We observe that, under assumptions
(PC)–(QC)
,
L^{G}(\Omega)
and
W^{1,G}(\Omega)
are reflexive Banach spaces (see for instance
[
2
]
).

Definition 2.3
.

A function
u\in W^{1,G}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
is called a weak supersolution
(respectively, subsolution) to
\Delta_{g}u=f
if, for every nonnegative test
function
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, the following inequality holds:

(2.1)

\int_{\Omega}\frac{g(|\nabla u|)}{|\nabla u|}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx\geq-\int_{\Omega}f\phi\,dx\qquad(\text{respectively, }\leq).

We say that
u
is a weak solution to
\Delta_{g}u=f
if

\int_{\Omega}\frac{g(|\nabla u|)}{|\nabla u|}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx=-\int_{\Omega}f\phi\,dx,

for all
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Whe
f\equiv 0
we say that
u
is g-harmonic.

Remark 2.4
.

One can show by a density argument that test functions can be taken in
0\leq\phi\in W^{1,G}_{c}(\Omega),
which denotes the functions in
W^{1,G}(\Omega)
that have compact support (see
[
4
,
2
]
).

3
Boundary Hölder regularity: Proof of Theorem
1.4

First, observe that, up to the addition of a constant (which preserves the class of solutions) we may assume without loss of generality that

\psi(0)=0.
Let
K=||u||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\cap B_{1})}+[\psi]_{C^{\alpha}(0)}
and denote
\Omega_{r}=\Omega\cap B_{r}.
To establish that
u\in C^{\alpha}(0)
, it suffices to prove the following statement: there exist universal constants
\delta,\rho\in(0,1)
and
M\geq 1
such that, for every integer
k\geq 0
, one has

(3.1)

||u||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\rho^{k}})}\leq MK\rho^{k\alpha}.

We prove (
3.1
) by induction on
k
. The case
k=0
is immediate. Assume that (
3.1
) holds for some
k\geq 0
. We shall show that it also holds for
k+1
.

Set
r=\frac{\rho^{k}}{2}.
Since
\Omega
is
\delta
-Reifenberg flat from the exterior at the origin, there exists a coordinate system
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
such that

B_{r}(0)\cap\Omega\subset B_{r}(0)\cap\{x_{n}>-\delta r\}.

We now introduce the following auxiliary function for
\gamma>0
:

(3.2)

h(x)=\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{-\gamma}-\left|x+\left(\delta+\frac{1}{2}\right)e_{n}\right|^{-\gamma}.

We claim that
\Delta_{g}h\leq 0,
for
\gamma>0
large enough. Indeed, if
\lambda=\min\{1,\delta_{0}\}
and
\Lambda=\max\{1,g_{0}\}

\displaystyle\Delta_{g}(-|x|^{-\gamma})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}a^{ij}D_{ij}(-|x|^{-\gamma})

\displaystyle=\gamma|x|^{-(\gamma+2)}\sum_{k=1}a^{kk}-\gamma(\gamma+2)|x|^{-(\gamma+4)}\sum_{i\neq j}a^{ij}x_{j}x_{i}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma|x|^{-(\gamma+2)}n\Lambda\frac{g(|\nabla(-|x|^{-\gamma})|)}{|\nabla(-|x|^{-\gamma})|}-\gamma(\gamma+2)|x|^{-(\gamma+4)}\lambda\frac{g(|\nabla(-|x|^{-\gamma})|)}{|\nabla(-|x|^{-\gamma})|}|x|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma|x|^{-(\gamma+2)}\frac{g(|\nabla(-|x|^{-\gamma})|)}{|\nabla(-|x|^{-\gamma})|}[n\Lambda-(\gamma+2)\lambda]\leq 0

provided that
\gamma
is chosen so that

\frac{n\Lambda}{\lambda}\leq\gamma+2.

Let
T_{r}:=\{(x^{\prime},0);|x^{\prime}|<r\},\,\tilde{B}_{r}^{+}:=B_{r}^{+}-\delta re_{n},\,\,\tilde{T}_{r}:=T_{r}-\delta re_{n}
and
\tilde{\Omega}_{r}:=\Omega\cap\tilde{B}_{r}^{+}.
Then
\Omega_{r/2}\subset\tilde{\Omega}_{r}\subset\Omega_{\rho^{k}}.
Now we define the following barrier function

(3.3)

\tilde{h}(x)=MK\rho^{k\alpha}h\left(\frac{x}{r}\right).

We claim that
\tilde{h}
satisfies:

\begin{cases}\Delta_{g}\tilde{h}\leq 0&\text{in }\tilde{B}_{r}^{+};\\
\tilde{h}\geq 0&\text{in }\tilde{B}_{r}^{+};\\
\tilde{h}\geq MK\rho^{k\alpha}&\text{on }\partial\tilde{B}_{r}^{+}\setminus\tilde{T}_{r};\\
\tilde{h}(-\delta re_{n})=0.\end{cases}

The first inequality has already been established. To verify the third, we note that
\gamma>1
and

\partial\tilde{B}_{r}^{+}\setminus\tilde{T}_{r}=\{x;|x+\delta re_{n}|=r,\,x_{n}>-\delta r\}

then follows from convexity, provided that
\gamma
is chosen sufficiently large

\left|\frac{x}{r}+\left(\delta+\frac{1}{2}\right)e_{n}\right|^{-\gamma}\leq 2^{-\gamma}\left(1+\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{-\gamma}\right)\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{-\gamma}-1.

That is, on
\partial\tilde{B}_{r}^{+}\setminus\tilde{T}_{r},
we have

\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{-\gamma}-\left|\frac{x}{r}+\left(\delta+\frac{1}{2}\right)e_{n}\right|^{-\gamma}\geq 1,

It follows that
\tilde{h}(x)\geq MK\rho^{k\alpha}h\left(\frac{x}{r}\right)\geq MK\rho^{k\alpha}.

Finally, the second inequality and the fact that
\tilde{h}(-\delta re_{n})=0
are obviously.

Let

\delta=\rho.

It follows from the Mean Value Theorem that

h\left(\frac{x}{r}\right)\leq\tilde{C}\rho\quad\text{in}\quad\{(x^{\prime},x_{n});x^{\prime}=0,\,-\delta r\leq x_{n}\leq 2\rho r\},

where
\tilde{C}=3\gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{-\gamma-1}.

Thus, we obtain

\tilde{h}\leq MK\rho^{(K+1)\alpha}\tilde{C}\rho^{1-\alpha}\quad\text{in}\quad\{(x^{\prime},x_{n});x^{\prime}=0,\,-\delta r\leq x_{n}\leq 2\rho r\}.

On
\Omega\cap\partial\tilde{B}_{r}^{+}=\Omega\cap(\partial\tilde{B}_{r}^{+}\setminus\tilde{T}_{r}),
the induction hypothesis gives that

u\leq||u||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\rho^{k}})}\leq MK\rho^{k\alpha}\leq\tilde{h}.

On the other hand, on
\partial\Omega\cap\tilde{B}_{r}^{+},
since
u=\psi
and
\tilde{h}\geq 0
we have

u\leq\tilde{h}+||\psi||_{L^{\infty}}.

Therefore, the Comparison Principle
[
7
, Lemma 2.8]
yields

u(x)\leq\tilde{h}(x)+||\psi||_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega\cap\tilde{B}_{r}^{+})}\quad\forall x\in\tilde{\Omega}_{r}.

Thus, using the fact that
\psi\in C^{\alpha}(0),
we obtain in
\tilde{\Omega}_{r}\cap\{(x^{\prime},x_{n});x^{\prime}=0,\,-\delta r\leq x_{n}\leq 2\rho r\}
for a constant
C=C(n,\delta_{0},g_{0})>0

u\leq\tilde{h}+||\psi||_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega\cap\tilde{B}_{r}^{+})}\leq\left(C\rho^{1-\alpha}+\frac{C}{M\rho^{\alpha}}\right)MK\rho^{(k+1)\alpha}.

We choose
\rho\in(0,1)
sufficiently small and M sufficiently large so that

\left(C\rho^{1-\alpha}+\frac{C}{M\rho^{\alpha}}\right)\leq 1.

Then,
u\leq MK\rho^{(k+1)\alpha}
in the strip
\{(x^{\prime},x_{n});x^{\prime}=0,\,-\delta r\leq x_{n}\leq 2\rho r\}.

By placing suitable translates of the barrier (with
v(x^{\prime},-\delta r)=0
for some
x^{\prime}\in B_{\rho^{k+1}})
and repeating the above argument, we obtain

\sup_{\Omega_{\rho^{k+1}}}u\leq KM\rho^{(k+1)\alpha}.

Similarly, one shows that

\inf_{\Omega_{\rho^{k+1}}}u\geq-KM\rho^{(k+1)\alpha},

which gives

||u||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\rho^{k+1}})}\leq MK\rho^{(k+1)\alpha}.

This completes the induction and hence the proof.

4
ABP-Type Maximum Principle

We need to put in a little more effort for the corresponding Poisson equation, because the difference between two solutions no longer satisfies any equation. The key tool for overcoming this difficulty is an ABP-type estimate. Prior to its derivation, we establish the following technical lemma, which can be viewed as an improvement of a result from
[
11
]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\phi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a nonnegative nonincreasing function.
Assume that for
h>k

\phi(h)\leq\frac{c_{0}}{\min\{(h-k)^{\gamma_{1}},(h-k)^{\gamma_{2}}\}}\max\{\phi(k)^{\beta_{1}},\phi(k)^{\beta_{2}}\}

where
c_{0}>0,\,\,\,0<\gamma_{1}<\gamma_{2}
and
1<\beta_{1}<\beta_{2}.

Then

\phi(r)=0\quad\text{where}\quad r=\max\left\{\left(c_{0}2^{\gamma_{2}(1+\frac{1}{\beta_{1}-1})+2}\phi(0)^{\theta}\right)^{1/\gamma_{1}},\left(c_{0}2^{\gamma_{2}(1+\frac{1}{\beta_{1}-1})+2}\phi(0)^{\theta}\right)^{1/\gamma_{2}}\right\}

and
\theta
is a constant given by

\theta=\begin{cases}\beta_{2},&\text{if }\phi(0)>1\\
\dfrac{\beta_{1}-1}{2},&\text{if }\phi(0)\leq 1.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let

for integers

Since

and
\phi
is nonnegative nonincreasing function, it is enough to prove that for any integer

\phi(k_{m})\leq\frac{\phi(0)}{2^{m\eta}}\quad\text{where}\quad\eta=\frac{\gamma_{2}}{\beta_{1}-1}+1.

We prove by induction. For
m=0
it holds clearly, suppose that it holds
for
m
and we need to prove that it holds for
m+1.

Since,

\displaystyle\min\{(k_{m+1}-k_{m})^{\gamma_{1}},(k_{m+1}-k_{m})^{\gamma_{2}}\}

\displaystyle=\min\{(r2^{-m-1})^{\gamma_{1}},(r2^{-m-1})^{\gamma_{2}}\}

\displaystyle\geq 2^{-(m+1)\gamma_{2}}\min\{r^{\gamma_{1}},r^{\gamma_{2}}\},

it follows denoting
\zeta:=\gamma_{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{\beta_{1}-1}\right)+2

\phi(k_{m+1})\leq 2^{(m+1)\gamma_{2}}\frac{c_{0}}{c_{0}2^{\zeta}\phi(0)^{\theta}}\max\{\phi(0)^{\beta_{1}},\phi(0)^{\beta_{2}}\}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\phi(k_{m+1})

\displaystyle\leq 2^{(m+1)\gamma_{2}}\cdot 2^{-\zeta}\cdot\phi(0)^{-\theta}\max\left\{\left(\frac{\phi(0)}{2^{m\eta}}\right)^{\beta_{1}},\left(\frac{\phi(0)}{2^{m\eta}}\right)^{\beta_{2}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\phi(0)}{2^{m\eta}}\cdot 2^{(m+1)\gamma_{2}-\zeta-m\eta(\beta_{1}-1)}\cdot\max\{\phi(0)^{\beta_{1}-\theta-1},\phi(0)^{\beta_{2}-\theta-1}\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\phi(0)}{2^{m\eta}}\cdot 2^{-\eta}

\displaystyle=\frac{\phi(0)}{2^{(m+1)\eta}},

where we use that
2^{(m+1)\gamma_{2}-\zeta-m\eta(\beta_{1}-1)+\eta}\leq 1
and
\max\{\phi(0)^{\beta_{1}-\theta-1},\phi(0)^{\beta_{1}-\theta-1}\}\leq 1.

∎

The proof follows the same strategy as that of Theorem

. Therefore, we may assume that
