Intelligent prediction of thermodynamic performance in MHD Oldroyd B trihybrid nanofluids using artificial neural networks
Authors: Mamoon Aamir, Chemseddine Maatki, Aqsa Zafar Abbasi, Rajab Alsayegh, Karim Kriaa, Walid Hassen, Lioua Kolsi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This research aims to describe the flow characteristics and entropy creation of an Oldroyd-B tri hybrid nanofluid under conditions of MHD with heat transfer through a hybrid numerical—machine learning framework. The nonlinear boundary layer governing equations for momentum and heat transport are transformed to a commonly used ordinary differential equations (ODE) form via similarity transformations and solved using MATLAB's bvp4c solver. A mesh independence study has verified numerical. The main innovation of this study is combining ANN modelling with the nonlinear numerical calculation of Oldroyd-B tri hybrid nanofluid flow to create an accurate predictive surrogate modelling tool for complex thermofluid systems. Data collected from the bvp4c solver was then used to train a feed forward ANN, using Levenberg–Marquardt backpropagation algorithm. The trained ANN was able to accurately predict velocity, temperature and entropy creation, with regression accuracies above 0.999 and mean square error values less than \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${10}^{-6}$$\end{document}, indicating a high degree of predictive power. Parametric analysis indicated that increasing the magnetic parameter caused a large reduction in velocity field (approximately 18–25%) as a result of the Lorentz force acting in against the flow. Also, the generation of heat increased the temperature profile by approximately 20%, therefore increasing Entropy Generation within the thermal boundary layer. In addition, tri-hybrid nanoparticles have better thermal conductivity and heat transfer performance than nanofluids made from conventional materials because of their ability to improve the thermal performance of fluids. A predictive framework for ANN-based modelling of nonlinear fluid transport has been developed that reduces the computational cost of obtaining accurate numerical solutions compared with traditional methods. This new framework has the potential to allow engineers and scientists to model advanced nanofluids with greater accuracy than previous approaches, thereby providing valuable information for thermal management, high-performance cooling systems, and energy conversion technologies.

Introduction

Effective management of thermal energy is a central function in numerous industrial and engineering applications, such as high-performance cooling systems, chemical reactors, microelectronic devices, heat exchangers, biomedical procedures, and radiators. Among the numerous solutions that have been devised for enhancing thermal performance, the use of nanofluids, base fluids containing nanoscale particles, has become of significant interest as a result of their improved thermal conductivity and better heat transport properties. The enhanced thermophysical characteristics of nanofluids are directly attributed to the high thermal conductivity and surface area to volume ratio of nanoparticles, resulting in enhanced convective heat transfer rates compared with traditional fluids. Due to this advantage, nanofluids have been widely researched in various thermal systems including drug delivery, chemical processing, thermal control, and energy conversion technologies. There is a considerable amount of literature that has investigated the impact of nanoparticles on heat transfer enhancement. Ellahi et al. [1] considered the role of nanoparticles on magnetic field under thermal transport, whereas Zeeshan et al. [2] maximized nanoparticle-included fluid performance in magnetically affected environments. Zeeshan et al. [3] also investigated the effect of hybrid nanoparticles on combined convective heat transfer in Casson fluids and Ellahi et al. [4] showed improved heat transfer by using nanoparticle suspensions. Tlili [5] examined flow and thermal properties in eccentric annuli for internal heat generation using Al2O3 and Ni nanoparticles in an ethylene glycol base fluid. Algehyne et al. [6] conducted numerical simulations of hybrid nanofluid flow along a vertical permeable sheet, and Hassan et al. [7] investigated MHD flow of hybrid nanofluids over a bidirectional stretching sheet with Ag and MoS2 nanoparticles using various base fluids (EG, EO, and water), and they concluded that Ag–MoS2/water was the most efficient in terms of heat transfer. Arif et al. [8] utilized the finite element method (FEM) for investigating thermal enhancement in Williamson fluids with mono (CuO–blood) and hybrid (CuO–Al2O3–blood) suspensions, and Ali et al. [9] found that hybrid nanoparticles in a porous medium enhanced shear stress but decreased the heat transfer rate. Further research [10–13] has uniformly proved the better thermal behavior of nanoparticle suspensions in different fluid systems. The internal heat generation and Joule (Ohmic) heating further affect thermal transport in electrically conducting fluids, especially in the presence of a magnetic field. Ohmic dissipation and volumetric heat generation can have a major effect on temperature distribution, influence boundary layer formation, and lower the efficiency of the system if not appropriately addressed. A. Few studies have been performed to investigate the effects of these phenomena: Shamshuddin et al. [14] investigated steady nanofluid flow along a vertical surface with internal heating and Ohmic dissipation, and Ajibade et al. [15] presented analytical solutions for viscous MHD flows with internal heat generation.

Artificial neural network framework

Artificial neural networks (ANNs) have emerged as powerful computational tools for solving highly nonlinear differentiable equations associated with complex transport processes in thermal-fluid systems. Since ANNs represent nonlinear mappings, have the ability to learn dynamic behaviors, and are able to capture complex parameter dependencies, they are often used in modeling multi-physics problems wherein traditional numerical methods struggle with convergence or stability challenges. Recently, ANNs have successfully been implemented to model fluid dynamics and heat transfer processes including non-Newtonian rheology, magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) effects, and coupled thermal–electromagnetic fields [16, 17]. Neural network models using optimization methods, including Levenberg–Marquardt and scaled conjugate gradient methods, have shown high accuracy for modeling flow characteristics, entropy generation, and heat transfer rates of nanofluids. Gupta and Wakif [18] are examining heat and mass transfer in the flow of nanofluid between two discs using neural networks. An innovative feature of this research is the use of the Levenberg–Marquardt artificial neural network (ANN) to analyse the training, testing, and validation procedures. The constructed ANN model's validity was shown by its accuracy throughout the training, validation, and testing stages. Gupta [19] examined the mass and heat transport of a ternary hybrid nanofluid between two parallel plates with an angled magnetic field using an artificial neural network. The effects of several physical factors on temperature, concentration, and velocity are discussed and shown graphically. This work builds off this modeling framework to examine the thermohydrodynamics of an Oldroyd-B tri-hybrid nanofluid under the influences of Joule heating, heat generation, and magnetic fields. The integrated intelligent solver and non-Newtonian transport model enables improvements for predictive capability and computational efficiency, enhances understanding of entropy generation, and develops high-performance thermal devices.

In spite of the huge advancements in nanofluid and non-Newtonian fluid research, available literature has mostly focused on mono- and bi-nanoparticle suspensions. Much fewer efforts have gone towards tri-hybrid nanofluids, particularly under magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) conditions in conjunction with Joule heating and internal heat generation—factors that considerably influence entropy production and thermal performance. Additionally, the recent development of artificial neural networks (ANNs) has provided new prospects for more efficient solution of highly nonlinear differential equations governing transport phenomena with enhanced accuracy and computational efficiency. The merging of ANN-based solvers with multi-physics flow models offers a strong platform for the precise prediction of velocity, temperature, and entropy fields, thus facilitating the design of next-generation heat transfer systems.

Driven by these shortcomings, this paper examines the thermohydrodynamic behavior attributes of an Oldroyd-B viscoelastic fluid with tri-hybrid nanoparticles (Al2O3,TiO2, and SiO2) in an ethylene glycol base fluid utilizing intelligence computing. The model takes into consideration the effects of combined Joule heating, internal heat generation, as well as an externally imposed magnetic field. Application of Oldroyd-B fluids

There has been significant research into using artificial intelligence (AI), machine learning (ML) and other computational techniques to examine multiphase systems involving complex nanofluid or magneto hydrodynamic (MHD) behavior over the last several years. Machine learning frameworks such as Tensor Flow have been used for the analysis of thermal and entropy generation from nanofluids [20]; hybrid models such as response surface methodology (RSM) & artificial neural networks (ANN) combined with sensitivity analysis have been used for improving heat transfer and energy efficiency in parabolic trough solar collectors and aerospace systems [21, 22]; and stochastic AI methods have been utilized to propel MHD fluids with peristaltic motion and Reiner-Philippoff fluids [23], develop ANN-based models of two-dimensional magnetized nanofluid flow [24] and optimize the Thermophysical properties of radially magnetized hybrid nanofluids [25]. AI driven methods have also been applied to electromagnetically driven Casson hybrid nanofluid flow [26], bio convective nanofluid flows with gyrotactic microorganisms [27] and the thermodynamic effects of aggregated nanoparticles [28]; additionally, sensitivity analyses were performed on hypersonic glide vehicle propulsion systems subject to magnetic fields and nanofluid effects [29] and advanced cooling techniques for aircraft propulsion systems using Carreau trihybrid nanofluids [30]. A range of complementary numerical and optimization methods have been developed; for example, there is research into porous cavity and porous block entropy generation [31] and machine learning for both dust and bio-magnetic hybrid nano-fluids [32–34]. There have also been studies of semi-analytical rheological methods applied to biomedical flows [35] along with examples of magnetic dipole and radiative effects in permeable materials [36]. All of the above studies contribute to the development of a multi-layer neural network model for mixed convection MHD Prandtl nano-fluids containing bio-logical microorganisms [37]. These studies demonstrate that the combination of advanced numerical models with machine learning models and thermofluid dynamics provides a powerful framework for improved prediction accuracy and optimization and energy-efficient design of thermal systems.

Tri-hybrid nanofluids have been a focus of much research in recent years because adding three types of nanoparticles to a base fluid will increase Thermophysical properties more than adding just one type or combining two types of nanoparticle together (hybrid). In fact, because of their combined effect, multiple nanoparticles have been shown to increase thermal conductivity, heat transport capabilities and thermal stability compared to either mono or hybrid nanofluids. Thus, tri-hybrid nanofluids are becoming more common to find in modern thermal systems and energy engineering applications. In this paper, we use the tri-hybrid Thermophysical model developed in the paper referenced [38] as the basis for our study and demonstrate the authors’ derivation and validation on the governing equations for tri-nanofluids. This reference study looks at how to physically model tri-hybrid nanofluids whereas our study uses a different method to study the properties of such complex nanofluid systems by testing whether we could use machine learning technologies to accurately predict their thermodynamic behavior. To do this we used an artificial neural network trained by Levenberg–Marquardt backpropagation algorithm to approximate the numerical solutions of the velocity, temperature and entropy generation profiles of the Oldroyd-B tri-hybrid nanofluid model.

Problem formulating

In this study, we investigate the entropy generation characteristics of an Oldroyd-B viscoelastic fluid, where the base fluid is ethylene glycol \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$({C}_{2}{H}_{6}{O}_{2}),$$\end{document} mixed with various nanoparticles and flowing over a non-uniformly moving surface. The base fluid is assumed to have internal volumetric heat generation, and when subjected to an external magnetic field, it experiences Ohmic (Joule) dissipation. Three types of nanoparticles, alumina (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Al_2O_3$$\end{document}), titania (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$TiO_2$$\end{document}), and silica (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$SiO_2$$\end{document}) are uniformly dispersed into the base fluid to form a homogeneous tri-hybrid nanofluid. The wall temperature is considered non-uniform and varies quadratically with the horizontal coordinate, while the ambient temperature is maintained constant. The mathematical model that governs the analysis at hand centers on the conservation of mass, momentum, and energy for an incompressible, steady, two-dimensional Oldroyd-B viscoelastic nanofluid composed of ethylene glycol (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${C}_{2}{H}_{6}{O}_{2}$$\end{document}) as its base fluid with the homogeneous suspension of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Al_2O_3, TiO_2,$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$SiO_2$$\end{document} nanoparticles forming a tri-hybrid nanofluid with increased thermophysical properties. The flow is considered within the confines of a non-uniformly moving surface that experienced a transverse magnetic field that gives rise to Lorentz forces and Joule (Ohmic) heating and an internal volumetric heat generation would likewise affect the thermal effects. The governing equations are derived under the assumptions of thin boundary layers to govern the effects of the flow and heat transfer phenomena. The continuity equation (Eq. 1) ensures conservation of mass through the condition of a divergence-free velocity field. The momentum equation (Eq. 2) includes the inertia of the fluid, viscous diffusion and viscoelastic effects related to relaxation and retardation times as well as electromagnetic body forces produced by the application of the magnetic field, thereby accounting for the nonlinear viscous response along with the viscoelastic stress and magnetohydrodynamic forces on the boundary layer. In the momentum equation for the magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) model, the magnetic interaction term indicates how the Lorentz force acts on the fluid due to its conductivity and an external magnetic field applied to it. In this paper, the assumption that the magnetic Reynolds number is small enough that can be neglible to the induced magnetic field from the fluid and only consider the applied magnetic field [38]. The energy equation (Eq. 3) governs the thermal transport process, incorporating the effects of heat conduction, viscous dissipation, Joule heating, and internal heat generation.

The physical and thermal behavior of the system is further constrained by the boundary conditions defined in Eq. 4, which prescribe the velocity distribution, suction or injection velocity, and surface temperature along the non-uniformly moving boundary. These conditions ensure that the velocity and temperature fields adhere to the physical characteristics of the flow near the surface, while asymptotically satisfying free-stream conditions far from the wall. To enable analytical tractability and numerical solvability, a similarity transformation is incorporated in Eq. 5 that simplifies the governing partial differential equations into a set of coupled, nonlinear ordinary differential equations defined in terms of dimensionless similarity variables. This transformation encapsulates the dominant dynamics of the viscoelastic nanofluid system, emphasizing the role of pertinent physical parameters while largely demystifying the computational intricacy. The new momentum and energy equations, provided in Eqs. 6 and 7, model the coupled transport processes under the influence of magnetohydrodynamic, viscoelastic stress, and thermal enhancement caused by nanoparticles. Equation 6, for instance, models the momentum transport within the boundary layer in terms of inertial forces, viscous shear, elastic stresses involving relaxation and retardation times, and Lorentz force due to the applied magnetic field. Equation 7 controls the thermal field, including conductive and convective heat transfer, viscous dissipation expressed in terms of the Eckert number, Joule heating, and internal heat generation or absorption expressed by the parameter λ. The Prandtl number and magnetic interaction parameter also become important factors in controlling the temperature field and entropy generation. The corresponding dimensionless boundary conditions given by Eqs. 8 and 9, determine the near-wall and far-field velocity and temperature profiles, guaranteeing the mathematical well-posedness of the problem as well as the physical correctness of the solutions.

Methodology

The transformed equations governing the boundary layer (the transforming process creates the similarity conditions) are a set of nonlinear ordinary differential equations subject to two sets of boundary conditions for each equation. Due to the high degree of nonlinearity and coupling of the two systems of equations (for velocity and temperature), analytical solutions are typically not practical to implement; thus a numerical method was employed to solve for the solution profiles.

In this case, the set of equations was solved using a boundary measure problem (symmetrical approximation) solution technique and this data was then used to create an artificial neural network (ANN) model to predict the behaviour of the thermodynamic properties of the tri-hybrid nanofluid system.

The changes in the heat transport characteristics of fluid systems with the inclusion of multiple types of nanoparticles are due to the increased effective thermal conductivity and modified viscosity that the multiple types of nanoparticles provide. The presence of tri-hybrid nanoparticles helps enhance heat transfer from the working fluid; however, there are greater thermal gradients and higher viscous interactions, hence producing higher entropy near the boundary of the wall. In contrast, the tri-hybrid nanofluid generates a higher amount of local entropy, but the tri-hybrid nanofluids also have a superior heat transfer efficiency. Therefore, there is a tradeoff between improving heat transfer and producing thermodynamic irreversibility in the design of a practical thermal system. Conclusion

The present research examines the thermo-hydrodynamic properties of an Oldroyd-B viscoelastic tri-hybrid nanofluid (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$A{l}_{2}{O}_{3}-Ti{O}_{2}-Si{O}_{2}/$$\end{document} ethylene glycol) through the Levenberg–Marquardt-based Artificial Neural Network (ANN) method. The model includes the magnetic interaction, Joule heating, and internal heat generation/absorption phenomena to investigate the nonlinear behavior of viscoelastic transport. The boundary-layer approximation-derived governing equations were reduced to nonlinear ODEs via similarity transformations and solved numerically. The governing equations were solved numerically using MATLAB’s bvp4c solver and a mesh independence study was performed in order to confirm the accuracy and convergence of the numerical results. In addition, an artificial neural network (ANN) was constructed using the Levenberg–Marquardt backpropagation algorithm to act as a surrogate model of prediction for the numerical data. The results provide a number of important physical insights on how heat transfer and entropy production mechanisms work. First, the magnetic field creates a resistive Lorentz force, which greatly diminishes the velocity of the fluid and increases the thickness of the momentum boundary layer and therefore modifies how heat is transported throughout the system. Second, the heat generation parameter significantly increases the thickness of the thermal boundary layer and increases the production of entropy in the system. Third, the viscoelastic characteristics of the Oldroyd-B fluid change how momentum is distributed and change how thermal and mechanical energy are coupled together. Finally, the addition of tri-hybrid nanoparticles increases the thermal conductivity of the base fluid, which results in higher rates of heat transfer than is found with conventional nanofluids.

Engineering perspective

The findings from this research will benefit engineers in regards to providing essential design information regarding systems in which effective heat transfer and minimizing the amount of entropy produced are of importance. The enhanced thermal conductivity of the Oldroyd-B tri-hybrid nanofluid can be utilized to design a working fluid used in such applications like aerospace thermal control systems, turbine blade cooling, microelectronics heat sinks, polymer extrusion processes, and solar thermal collectors. The data obtained regarding the magnetic field strength and internal heat generation allow for effective regulation of flow resistance, temperature distribution throughout the system, and reducing the amount of thermodynamic irreversibility. The surrogate model created using an artificial neural network allows for thermal performance to be predicted quickly, without continuously performing numerical simulations, thus being very helpful for design optimization, performing parametric studies, and providing real-time control of advanced thermal systems.

Limitations

The current results hold significant value for thermal management systems, micro scale cooling devices, energy conversion systems, polymer processing flows, and advanced heat exchange technologies because they provide better thermal conductivity and optimized entropy generation which contributes greatly to the efficiency of the system. While the current findings do have value there are a number of limitations associated with the current study. Firstly, this study forms a steady-state, 2-dimensional analysis for boundary layer flow; however, other real-world physical phenomena such as thermal radiation, aggregation of nanoparticles, and unsteady flow are not addressed in this study. Additionally, this study developed an artificial neural network (ANN) model based on data generated from numerical simulations and not from experimental data which may limit validation against actual database. Lastly, this study did not include any evaluation of pumping power or flow stability requirements which are also very important in making the results relevant to engineers.

Future work

These results may be built upon in future work by considering the many physical phenomena from real life, like thermal radiation or chemical reactions. Likewise, there is potential for future work to build upon the present analysis by considering unsteady flow or three-dimensional fluid motion to better predict real-world events. Additionally, using new architectures in machine learning will assist in predicting complex nonlinear thermofluid systems; this includes deep neural networks, physics informed neural networks, and hybrid machine learning using optimization-based training methods. Future studies can extend the current model for radiative heat transfer, unsteady and three-dimensional flow cases, or hybrid nanoparticles with bio-inspired base fluids for biomedical heat management. Also, use ANN as a fast surrogate for optimization, but warn that extrapolation needs retraining or solver checks.

The discussed LMBFFNN approach can be coupled with higher-order soft-computing methods like genetic algorithms, adaptive neuro-fuzzy inference systems (ANFIS), or deep neural networks to optimize parameters that control entropy reduction and heat transfer enhancement. In addition, experimental confirmation of tri-hybrid nanofluids under MHD and internal heating conditions can further validate the computational results of the present study. Also, this study can be extended for QNN [39, 40] for the simulation of Oldroyd-B tri-hybrid nanofluid. Also, ANN can be implemented to the flow studies in engineering application e.g., [41, 42, 43].
