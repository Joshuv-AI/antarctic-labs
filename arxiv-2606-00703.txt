Information-Theoretic Lower Bounds for Bit-Constrained Stochastic Optimization via a Reduction to Compressed Gaussian Mean Estimation
Munsik Kim

Abstract
Low-precision pretraining (FP8, MXFP4, NVFP4) is now standard for frontier language models, yet the literature is almost entirely achievability : algorithms and empirical scaling laws, with no matching characterization of what is information-theoretically possible. We study a B B -bit quantized stochastic first-order oracle in which an optimizer interacts for T T rounds and receives, each round, a B B -bit adaptive, public-coin description of its stochastic gradient. Our main contribution is an exact reduction (Lemma 1 ) from optimizing a strongly convex quadratic family to interactively compressed Gaussian mean estimation: under the B B -bit oracle the query carries no information, so optimization collapses exactly onto a sequential distributed-estimation problem. This yields two unconditional lower bounds—a communication bound T ​ B = Ω ⁡ ( d ) TB=\Omega(d) (Theorem 1 ) and a statistical bound T = Ω ⁡ ( σ 2 ​ d / ε 2 ) T=\Omega(\sigma^{2}d/\varepsilon^{2}) (Theorem 2 )—and the sharp product-form bound T = Ω ⁡ ( ( σ 2 ​ d / ε 2 ) ​ max ⁡ { 1 , d / B } ) T=\Omega\!\big((\sigma^{2}d/\varepsilon^{2})\max\{1,d/B\}\big) (Theorem 3 ), also unconditional: in the Gaussian-location model the Fisher-information–mutual-information constraint of 8 —a B B -bit transcript carries at most O ⁡ ( T ​ B / σ 2 ) O(TB/\sigma^{2}) of Fisher trace about the mean, so bits rather than dimension limit the recoverable information—combined with the multivariate van Trees inequality ( 16 ) gives the bound directly, without the bounded-likelihood-ratio truncation of 11 . We give a near-matching achievability result with exact per-round bit accounting (Theorem 4 ) under a bounded-dynamic-range oracle, tight up to a logarithmic factor; the lower bound is for truly Gaussian (unbounded) gradients, and closing this oracle gap is left open. A sequential rate–distortion perspective (Appendix E ) extends the reduction to temporally correlated and drifting oracles and corrects an earlier conjecture: positive noise correlation raises the bound by 1 + ρ 1 − ρ \tfrac{1+\rho}{1-\rho} rather than relaxing it (Corollary 2 ). We are deliberately conservative about interpretation: the bounds give an information-theoretic baseline for any low-bit gradient path, not an optimality claim about deployed FP4 systems.

Problem Setup

Conventions (fixed once, used throughout).

\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
is Euclidean. Logarithms and
information quantities are in bits;
h_{\mathrm{b}}(p)=-p\log p-(1-p)\log(1-p)
. We adopt the
per-coordinate
variance convention
: the oracle noise is
\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}I_{d})
, so each coordinate has variance

\sigma^{2}
and the total variance is
\sigma^{2}d
. (Under a total-variance-
\sigma^{2}
convention every
rate below relocates a factor of
d
.) For
v\in\{\pm 1\}^{d}
we write
\theta_{v}=\delta v
for a scale

\delta>0
chosen per theorem. “Accuracy
\varepsilon
” always means the optimization gap

f(\hat{x})-f^{\star}\leq\varepsilon^{2}
. We fix the tie-break
\mathrm{sign}(0):=+1
; no bound below depends on this choice.

Definition 1

(
B
-bit quantized stochastic first-order oracle)
.

Fix
f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
,
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, noise level
\sigma>0
, bit budget
B\in\mathbb{N}
, horizon

T
, and shared randomness
U
. For
t=1,\dots,T
: (i) the optimizer chooses
x_{t}\in\mathcal{X}
as a
function of
U,M_{1:t-1}
; (ii) the oracle returns
g_{t}
with
\mathbb{E}[g_{t}\mid x_{t}]=\nabla f(x_{t})
, noise
independent across
t
; (iii) an encoder emits
M_{t}=Q_{t}(g_{t},x_{t},M_{1:t-1},U)\in\{0,1\}^{B}
. The output

\hat{x}=\hat{x}(M_{1:T},U)
depends on the transcript only.

B=\infty
recovers the oracle of
1
. The dependence of
Q_{t},x_{t}
on
M_{1:t-1}

makes the protocol
interactive
; with shared randomness
U
this is the public-coin blackboard
model of communication complexity.

Remark 1

(The encoder is deliberately strong)
.

Because
Q_{t}
sees
x_{t}
, in the quadratic reduction below it can subtract the query and access

\theta_{v}-\xi_{t}
directly. This is
stronger
than a practical low-precision quantizer, which does
not center the gradient using the true query. Granting a stronger encoder only strengthens a lower
bound, so this is safe for Sections
5
–
6
; but it is one reason the
upper bound and any practical reading (Section
8
) must be stated with care.

The Reduction

Lemma 1

(Optimization
\Longleftrightarrow
compressed Gaussian mean estimation)
.

For
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
set
f_{\theta}(x)=\tfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert x-\theta\right\rVert^{2}
; each
f_{\theta}
is
1
-strongly convex,

1
-smooth, with minimizer
\theta
and
f_{\theta}(\theta)=0
. If the oracle noise is

\xi_{t}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}I_{d})
i.i.d., then the
B
-bit oracle for
f_{\theta}
is
information-theoretically equivalent to one where the encoder observes
Y_{t}=\theta+\xi_{t}^{\prime}
,

\xi_{t}^{\prime}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}I_{d})
i.i.d., and emits
M_{t}=\tilde{Q}_{t}(Y_{t},M_{1:t-1},U)
. The query
x_{t}

carries no information about
\theta
beyond
(U,M_{1:t-1})
, and for any estimator
\hat{x}
,

f_{\theta}(\hat{x})-f_{\theta}(\theta)=\tfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert\hat{x}-\theta\right\rVert^{2}
.

The reduction holds verbatim for any parameter set
\Theta\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We instantiate it
over two classes: the
hypercube

\Theta=\{\delta v:v\in\{\pm 1\}^{d}\}
(with
\theta_{v}=\delta v
and

f_{v}:=f_{\theta_{v}}
),
used for the Fano and Assouad bounds of §
5
, and the
continuous cube

\Theta=[-\delta,\delta]^{d}
, used for the van Trees bound of §
6
. Nothing in the proof
below uses the discreteness of
\theta
.

Proof.

\nabla f_{\theta}(x_{t})=x_{t}-\theta
, so realize
g_{t}=(x_{t}-\theta)+\xi_{t}
; then
\mathbb{E}[g_{t}\mid x_{t}]=\nabla f_{\theta}(x_{t})
.
The encoder knows
x_{t}
and forms
Y_{t}:=x_{t}-g_{t}=\theta-\xi_{t}
; writing
\xi_{t}^{\prime}:=-\xi_{t}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}I_{d})

gives
Y_{t}=\theta+\xi_{t}^{\prime}
, and
Q_{t}(g_{t},x_{t},\cdot)=\tilde{Q}_{t}(Y_{t},\cdot)
since
g_{t}=x_{t}-Y_{t}
. The law
of
Y_{t}
is
\mathcal{N}(\theta,\sigma^{2}I_{d})
, independent of
x_{t}
. Since
x_{t}=x_{t}(U,M_{1:t-1})
is
deterministic given
(U,M_{1:t-1})
, the conditional law of
Y_{t}
does not depend on
x_{t}
, so
x_{t}
adds
no information about
\theta
beyond
(U,M_{1:t-1})
. The identity is immediate from
f_{\theta}(\theta)=0
.
∎

For the hypercube instantiation
\theta_{v}=\delta v
, since each
\theta_{v,j}=\pm\delta
,
projecting
\hat{x}
onto
[-\delta,\delta]^{d}
only decreases
\left\lVert\hat{x}-\theta_{v}\right\rVert
; assume this WLOG.

5
Base Lower Bounds

Theorem 1

(Communication bound)
.

In the setting of Lemma
1
, let
V\sim\mathrm{Unif}(\{\pm 1\}^{d})
,
\hat{V}_{j}:=\mathrm{sign}(\hat{x}_{j})
,

p_{j}:=\mathbb{P}[\hat{V}_{j}\neq V_{j}]
,
\bar{p}:=\tfrac{1}{d}\sum_{j}p_{j}
. Then
TB\geq d(1-h_{\mathrm{b}}(\bar{p}))
.
Consequently, if
\mathbb{E}_{V}\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\hat{x}-\theta_{V}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\alpha\,d\,\delta^{2}
with
\alpha<\tfrac{1}{2}
, then

TB\geq d(1-h_{\mathrm{b}}(\alpha))=\Omega(d)
, i.e.
T=\Omega(d/B)
.

Proof.

Since
\hat{V}
is a function of the public-coin transcript
(M_{1:T},U)
, the chain

V\to(M_{1:T},U)\to\hat{V}
is Markov. As

U\perp V
,
I(V;M_{1:T},U)=I(V;M_{1:T}\mid U)
. By DPI, the chain rule (now conditioned on
U
), and

M_{t}\in\{0,1\}^{B}
,

I(V;\hat{V})\leq I(V;M_{1:T}\mid U)=\sum_{t=1}^{T}I(V;M_{t}\mid M_{1:t-1},U)\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T}H(M_{t}\mid M_{1:t-1},U)\leq TB.

(1)

Since the
V_{j}
are i.i.d. uniform,
H(V)=d
, and by subadditivity, monotonicity of conditioning, and
binary Fano,

I(V;\hat{V})=d-H(V\mid\hat{V})\geq d-\sum_{j}H(V_{j}\mid\hat{V}_{j})\geq\sum_{j}(1-h_{\mathrm{b}}(p_{j}))\geq d(1-h_{\mathrm{b}}(\bar{p})),

(2)

the last step by concavity of
h_{\mathrm{b}}
. Combining gives
TB\geq d(1-h_{\mathrm{b}}(\bar{p}))
. If
\hat{V}_{j}\neq V_{j}
then

\hat{x}_{j},\theta_{v,j}
have opposite signs (or
\hat{x}_{j}=0
), so
(\hat{x}_{j}-\theta_{v,j})^{2}\geq\delta^{2}
;
hence
\mathbb{E}_{V}\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\hat{x}-\theta_{V}\right\rVert^{2}\geq\delta^{2}d\bar{p}
, forcing
\bar{p}\leq\alpha
and the claim.
∎

Theorem 2

(Statistical bound)
.

For every horizon
T
there is a scale
\delta^{2}=\sigma^{2}/(4T)
such that, over the family

\{f_{v}:v\in\{\pm 1\}^{d}\}
of Lemma
1
at that scale and for any
B
(including
\infty
),

\inf_{\hat{x}}\max_{v}\mathbb{E}_{v}\left\lVert\hat{x}-\theta_{v}\right\rVert^{2}\geq\sigma^{2}d/(16T)
. Hence achieving

\max_{v}\mathbb{E}_{v}[f_{v}(\hat{x})-f_{v}^{\star}]\leq\varepsilon^{2}
requires
T\geq\sigma^{2}d/(32\varepsilon^{2})=\Omega(\sigma^{2}d/\varepsilon^{2})
.

Proof.

Assouad: since
(\hat{x}_{j}-\theta_{v,j})^{2}\geq\delta^{2}\mathbf{1}[\mathrm{sign}(\hat{x}_{j})\neq v_{j}]
,

\max_{v}\mathbb{E}_{v}\left\lVert\hat{x}-\theta_{v}\right\rVert^{2}\geq\tfrac{d\delta^{2}}{2}\min_{\mathrm{Ham}(v,v^{\prime})=1}(1-\mathrm{TV}(P_{v},P_{v^{\prime}}))
,
where
P_{v}
is the joint law of the full transcript
(U,M_{1:T})
under
\theta_{v}
. For neighbors differing
in coordinate
j
, only the
T
observations in coordinate
j
differ, so for the uncompressed laws

(U,Y_{1:T})
, using
U\perp(V,Y)
,

\mathrm{KL}(P^{Y}_{v}\|P^{Y}_{v^{\prime}})=T\cdot\frac{(2\delta)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}=\frac{2T\delta^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}
. Pinsker gives

\mathrm{TV}(P^{Y}_{v},P^{Y}_{v^{\prime}})\leq\delta\sqrt{T}/\sigma
; choosing
\delta^{2}=\sigma^{2}/(4T)
yields
\mathrm{TV}\leq\tfrac{1}{2}
,
so
\max_{v}\mathbb{E}_{v}\left\lVert\hat{x}-\theta_{v}\right\rVert^{2}\geq\tfrac{d\delta^{2}}{4}=\sigma^{2}d/(16T)
. For any compression the
transcript
(U,M_{1:T})
is a function of
(U,Y_{1:T})
, so
\mathrm{TV}(P_{v},P_{v^{\prime}})\leq\mathrm{TV}(P^{Y}_{v},P^{Y}_{v^{\prime}})
by DPI
and the bound persists. The gap conversion is the identity of Lemma
1
.
∎

Corollary 1

(Combined
\max
-form bound)
.

Fix
\alpha\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2})
with
1-h_{\mathrm{b}}(\alpha)\geq\tfrac{1}{2}
(e.g.
\alpha=0.11
). Every algorithm
achieving
\max_{v}\mathbb{E}_{v}[f_{v}(\hat{x})-f_{v}^{\star}]\leq\varepsilon^{2}
obeys
T\geq c^{\prime}\max\{\sigma^{2}d/\varepsilon^{2},\ d/B\}
for
an absolute
c^{\prime}>0
.

Proof.

The statistical term is Theorem
2
(its instance uses
\delta^{2}=\sigma^{2}/(4T)
). For the
communication term, use a
separate
instance of the same quadratic class with scale

\delta_{0}^{2}:=2\varepsilon^{2}/(\alpha d)
: the gap bound
\tfrac{1}{2}\delta_{0}^{2}d\bar{p}\leq\mathbb{E}_{V}\mathbb{E}[f_{V}(\hat{x})-f_{V}^{\star}]\leq\varepsilon^{2}

gives
\bar{p}\leq\alpha
(valid for any scale
\delta_{0}
; no SNR restriction is needed for the

1
yields
TB\geq d(1-h_{\mathrm{b}}(\alpha))\geq d/2
, i.e.
T\geq d/(2B)
. We regard the lower bound as holding over the union of the quadratic instances

\{f_{v}:v\in\{\pm 1\}^{d}\}
across all scales
\delta>0
; the two branches simply select different scales
from this class, so their maximum is valid. Tightness fails because the two bottlenecks are treated
separately; Section
6
removes this. Appendix
B.3
illustrates the gap
between the max-form and product-form scalings numerically.
∎

6
The Product-Form Bound

The
\max
-form does not capture that a coarse message inflates the
effective variance
, giving a

product

\max\{1,d/B\}
. We first show why elementary arguments cannot reach the product, then
isolate the exact missing ingredient, then prove the product form modulo that single ingredient.

Proposition 1

(Elementary per-coordinate information bound)
.

For
V_{j}\sim\mathrm{Unif}\{\pm 1\}
and
Y_{j}=\delta V_{j}+\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})
,
I(V_{j};Y_{j})\leq\frac{1}{2\ln 2}\cdot\frac{\delta^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}

bits. Consequently
I(V;M_{1:T}\mid U)\leq I(V;Y_{1:T})\leq T\,I(V;Y_{1})\leq\frac{Td\delta^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}\ln 2}
.

Proof.

I(V_{j};Y_{j})=h(Y_{j})-h(Y_{j}\mid V_{j})
with
h(Y_{j}\mid V_{j})=\tfrac{1}{2}\log(2\pi e\sigma^{2})
. Since

\mathrm{Var}(Y_{j})=\sigma^{2}+\delta^{2}
, the maximum-entropy bound gives
h(Y_{j})\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\log(2\pi e(\sigma^{2}+\delta^{2}))
,
so
I(V_{j};Y_{j})\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\log(1+\delta^{2}/\sigma^{2})\leq\frac{\delta^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}\ln 2}
(using
\log_{2}(1+x)\leq x/\ln 2
).
For the second claim, since
U\perp(V,Y_{1:T})
and
M_{1:T}
is a function of
(Y_{1:T},U)
, the conditional
DPI gives
I(V;M_{1:T}\mid U)\leq I(V;Y_{1:T}\mid U)=I(V;Y_{1:T})
; with
Y_{t}
conditionally i.i.d. given
V
,

I(V;Y_{1:T})=H(Y_{1:T})-\sum_{t}H(Y_{t}\mid V)\leq\sum_{t}[H(Y_{t})-H(Y_{t}\mid V)]=T\,I(V;Y_{1})
by subadditivity,
and

Lemma 2

(Bits limit transcript Fisher trace;

Theorem 3

(Product-form lower bound, unconditional)
.

Consider the family
\{f_{\theta}(x)=\tfrac{1}{2}\|x-\theta\|^{2}:\theta\in[-\delta,\delta]^{d}\}
under the
B
-bit

\delta^{2}=4\pi^{2}\varepsilon^{2}/d

\varepsilon^{2}\leq\sigma^{2}d/(16\pi^{2})

T\;\geq\;\frac{\sigma^{2}d^{2}}{4\,\varepsilon^{2}\,\min\{2\ln 2\cdot B,\;d\}}\;=\;\Omega\!\Big(\frac{\sigma^{2}d}{\varepsilon^{2}}\,\max\!\Big\{1,\frac{d}{B}\Big\}\Big).

Proof.

By the reduction (Lemma

of
\theta

\sup_{\theta}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\|\hat{\theta}-\theta\|^{2}\leq 2\varepsilon^{2}

conditions (i)–(iii) of Lemma

\sup_{\theta}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\|\hat{\theta}-\theta\|^{2}\;\geq\;\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\|\hat{\theta}-\theta\|^{2}\;\geq\;\frac{d^{2}}{\sup_{\theta}\mathrm{Tr}I_{\Pi}(\theta\mid U)+\mathcal{I}(\lambda)}\;\geq\;\frac{d^{2}}{\dfrac{T\min\{2\ln 2\,B,d\}}{\sigma^{2}}+\dfrac{\pi^{2}d}{\delta^{2}}},

Choose

Corollary 2

(Correlated gradient noise)
.

Suppose the gradient noise is stationary and temporally correlated,

\xi_{t}=\rho\,\xi_{t-1}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}\,\eta_{t}

7
Achievability: A Matching Upper Bound under Bounded Dynamic Range

We turn the assembly into a full proof. The scheme is a public-coin compression operator followed by
stochastic gradient descent; the two ingredients are an unbiased bounded-bit compressor with controlled
second moment (Lemma
3
) and a standard strongly convex SGD rate under a state-dependent
second-moment bound (Theorem
4
).

Lemma 3

(Unbiased
B
-bit compression under bounded dynamic range)
.

Fix a known bound
G>0
and let
q:=\lceil\log_{2}d\rceil+1
. For every bit budget
B\geq 2q
there is a
public-coin pair (encoder of
exactly
at most
B
bits, decoder)
\mathcal{C}_{B}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
such
that for every
g\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\|g\|_{2}\leq G
,

\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{C}_{B}(g)\mid g]=g\qquad(\text{unbiased}),\qquad\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathcal{C}_{B}(g)\|^{2}\,\big|\,g\big]\leq\omega_{B}\,\|g\|^{2}+\frac{G^{2}}{s},

where
s:=\min\{d,\lfloor B/q\rfloor\}
and
\omega_{B}:=d/s\leq\max\{1,\,2q\,d/B\}=O\!\big(\max\{1,\tfrac{d\log d}{B}\}\big)
.

The proof (a fixed-grid stochastic quantizer on a public-coin random subset, with no scale
channel) is in Appendix
A
.

Remark 5

(Role of the rotation; the
\log d
factor)
.

The bound above uses only
\|Hg\|=\|g\|
, so it holds even for
H=I
; the random Hadamard rotation is
included because it is the practically relevant device for replacing the
\Theta(\log d)
-bit quantizer
by an
O(1)
-bit quantizer (the FP4 regime): after rotation the coordinates of
\tilde{g}
are balanced,

|\tilde{g}_{i}|=O(\|g\|\sqrt{\log d/d})
with high probability, which suggests that with additional
high-probability coordinate-balancing and bounded-dynamic-range bookkeeping one could take

s=\Theta(B)
and remove the
\log d
factor
(
33
;
2
;
3
)
. We do

not
prove this here; the self-contained
\Theta(\log d)
-bit version above already matches
Theorem
3
up to the logarithmic factor acknowledged there.

Theorem 4

(Achievability under a bounded-dynamic-range oracle)
.

Let
f
be
\mu
-strongly convex and
L
-smooth, with a stochastic first-order oracle

g_{t}=\nabla f(x_{t})+\xi_{t}
satisfying
\mathbb{E}[\xi_{t}\mid x_{t}]=0
,
\mathbb{E}[\|\xi_{t}\|^{2}\mid x_{t}]\leq\sigma^{2}d
, and a
known almost-sure bound
\|g_{t}\|\leq G
(bounded gradients). Assume
B\geq 2(\lceil\log_{2}d\rceil+1)
and
run SGD with compressed gradients
\hat{g}_{t}=\mathcal{C}_{B}(g_{t})
(Lemma
3
with this

) and
diminishing steps
\eta_{t}=\beta/(\gamma+t)
,
\beta>1/\mu
,
\gamma=\Theta(\omega_{B}L/\mu)
. Then
\hat{g}_{t}

is unbiased for
\nabla f(x_{t})
, satisfies
\mathbb{E}[\|\hat{g}_{t}\|^{2}\mid x_{t}]\leq\omega_{B}\|\nabla f(x_{t})\|^{2}+M

with
M=\omega_{B}\sigma^{2}d+G^{2}/s
, and
(
9

\mathbb{E}[f(x_{T})-f^{\star}]\;\leq\;\frac{\nu}{\gamma+T},\qquad\nu=\max\Big\{\tfrac{\beta^{2}LM}{2(\beta\mu-1)},\,(\gamma+1)\,\mathbb{E}[f(x_{1})-f^{\star}]\Big\}.

Consequently
\mathbb{E}[f(x_{T})-f^{\star}]\leq\varepsilon^{2}
after

T=O\big(\tfrac{LM}{\mu^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}+\tfrac{\gamma(f(x_{1})-f^{\star})}{\varepsilon^{2}}\big)

quadratic family of Lemma
1
(
\mu=L=1
) at the hard scale
\delta^{2}=4\pi^{2}\varepsilon^{2}/d
,
started at
x_{1}=0
(so
f(x_{1})-f^{\star}\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\delta^{2}d=2\pi^{2}\varepsilon^{2}
),
under the additional
bounded-gradient assumption

\|g_{t}\|\leq G
a.s. with
G^{2}=O(\sigma^{2}d)
(so

and the initial term is dominated). Then

T=O\!\Big(\frac{\omega_{B}\,\sigma^{2}d}{\varepsilon^{2}}\Big)=O\!\Big(\frac{\sigma^{2}d}{\varepsilon^{2}}\,\max\!\Big\{1,\frac{d\log d}{B}\Big\}\Big)=\widetilde{O}\!\Big(\frac{\sigma^{2}d}{\varepsilon^{2}}\,\max\!\Big\{1,\frac{d}{B}\Big\}\Big),

matching Theorem
3
up to a
\log d
factor. The bounded-gradient assumption
G^{2}=O(\sigma^{2}d)

is exactly the per-coordinate noise scale; it is an
assumption
, not a consequence of the Gaussian
oracle, since Gaussian noise has no almost-sure norm bound. This is the one place where the achievability
oracle differs from the Gaussian lower-bound oracle (Section
8
).

The proof (the compressor’s moments fed into the strongly convex SGD rate, then the quadratic
specialization) is in Appendix
A
.

Consequence.
Theorems

and
4

\log d

.
The broader practical reading is deferred to Section

the model’s native setting (confirming the rate, illustrating the oracle gap, and—in

8
Discussion

We close with the practical reading of the bounds (Section
8.1
),
their limitations (Section
8.2
), and the open directions they leave
(Section
8.3
).

8.1
Practical reading

The lower bound is for the Gaussian oracle and the upper bound for a bounded-dynamic-range oracle;
modern low-precision training recipes

(
37
;
13
;
26
;
35
)
sit between these
idealised models. The following implications follow without leaving the safe scope.

•

Bit-width alone is not the right metric.
The bound scales with the effective
per-round payload, not nominal bit-width: scale metadata, block-scaling overhead,
occasional high-precision fallbacks, and overflow codes all consume from the same
B
-bit
budget. Comparing recipes by “nominal FP4 vs. FP8” is weaker than comparing them by

effective bits per gradient update
.

•

Dynamic-range control is part of the information channel.
The bounded-range
assumption of Theorem
4
is what lets the encoder spend exactly
B
bits
with no scale channel. Mechanisms practical recipes use—random Hadamard transforms,
block scaling, clipping policies, and selective high-precision layers—are exactly the
engineering moves that bring the empirical channel closer to that bounded-range model.

•

Stochastic rounding is not optional.
Theorem
4
uses an

unbiased
compressor (Lemma
3
); deterministic rounding would
introduce a per-step bias that no second-moment improvement can erase. This is consistent
with empirical reports that stochastic rounding is essential on the gradient path

(
37
;
26
)
.

•

Correlation must be read with care.
Real gradient sequences are temporally and
structurally correlated. Appendix
E

(Corollary
2
, Remark
10
) shows that correlation of the

noise

raises
the bit cost, whereas predictability of the optimum’s

trajectory
lowers it; predictive or residual-coded compressors are the natural way to
exploit the latter.

8.2
Limitations

(L1) Independent noise.

The main theorems assume noise independent across rounds.
Appendix
E
removes this assumption via a sequential rate–distortion analysis and finds the
effect is the opposite of the naive intuition: correlated
noise
raises the effective bit cost
(Corollary
2
), whereas a predictable
drift
lowers it
(Theorem
5
).

(L2) Variance convention.

Per-coordinate variance
\sigma^{2}
(Section
3
);
the alternative convention relocates a factor of
d
.

(L3) Strong encoder.

The oracle of Definition
1
centers the gradient
using the query (Remark
1
), stronger than a real quantizer; safe for lower bounds,
a caveat for interpretation.

(L3
′
) The two oracles differ.

The lower bound is for the Gaussian oracle with

\xi_{t}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}I_{d})
, whose gradient has no almost-sure norm bound. The achievability
(Theorem
4
) assumes a bounded-dynamic-range oracle,
\|g_{t}\|\leq G
a.s. with

G^{2}=O(\sigma^{2}d)
, which is what lets a fixed grid spend exactly
B
bits with no scale channel.
The two rates match up to a
\log d
factor, but the match is
across two oracle models
, not
within one; closing it (see Section
8.3
) is left open. We state this rather
than absorb it into the constants. Appendix
E
addresses it only in an
expected-rate

sense and only for the drifting model; the fixed-length static gap remains open.
Appendix
B.2
illustrates the gap numerically.

(L4) Proved vs. imported.

Lemma
1
,
Theorems
1
–
2
, Corollary
1
,
Proposition
1
, and Theorem
3
are proved here.
Theorem
3
invokes two external inequalities, cited as such: the
Fisher-trace/mutual-information bound of
8
(whose Gaussian
T=1

reprove in Appendix
D
) and the multivariate van Trees inequality

(
16
)
. Theorem

is proved here, invoking the strongly convex SGD

9
and standard quantization/sparsification primitives

(
3
;
33
;
40
;
32
;
29
)
.

(L5) Scope of the practical reading.

The idealised model omits optimizer state, block
scaling, accumulation precision, and non-quadratic landscapes. We do not claim that any deployed
FP4 or FP8 system is minimax-optimal; the claim is that the rotation-plus-stochastic-rounding
motif of practical recipes admits a clean information-theoretic rationale inside this model.

8.3
Open directions

Closing the oracle gap.

The most direct technical step is to remove (L3
′
): give an
unbiased
B
-bit compressor for truly Gaussian (unbounded) gradients, matching
Theorem
3
up to logarithms. Three routes:
clipping with controlled bias

(a high-probability analysis showing the bias decays fast enough to leave the rate intact);

overflow handling
(a rare “escape” code for out-of-range values, paid for at expected

B
bits per round); and
variable-length coding
(spending more bits only when the value is
large, expected
B
). Each is a clean, well-posed problem.

Correlated and drifting gradients (resolved).

A first version of this paper posed the
correlated-gradient case as a conjecture, suggesting that temporal correlation with mixing coefficient

\rho
would
relax
the
d/B
penalty in proportion to the predictability
1-\rho
.
Appendix
E
resolves the question through a sequential rate–distortion analysis—and

corrects
that intuition. For a fixed optimum with stationary AR(1) gradient noise, positive

raises
the bound,

T=\Omega\!\Big(\tfrac{1+\rho}{1-\rho}\cdot\tfrac{\sigma^{2}d}{\varepsilon^{2}}\max\{1,d/B\}\Big)\qquad(\text{Corollary~\ref{cor:correlated}}),

scaling every term by
\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{eff}}/\sigma^{2}=\tfrac{1+\rho}{1-\rho}
rather than relaxing

d/B
alone (Remark
10
); the quantity that genuinely lowers the bit requirement is
predictability of the optimum’s
trajectory
(slow drift), not correlation of the noise. The same
appendix sketches a drifting optimum via the data-rate theorem
(
24
)
and
remote Gaussian SRD
(
34
)
, and—by quantizing the
innovation
rather than the raw

(
19
)
—points to an
expected-rate
achievability route that needs
only finite differential entropy rather than an a.s. bound; the exact fixed-length per-round gap
(L3
′
) remains open. The heavy-tailed regime
(
30
)
remains a further axis.

A clean single-round information question.

Appendix
D
reduces a
self-contained alternative proof of Theorem

(Conjecture

The product-form bound (Theorem

•

D.4
A single-round contraction: what the maximal-correlation route does and does not give

Reversing the Markov chain turns the “downstream SDPI” obstacle into a question about the backward

Lemma 5

(Rigorous ingredients, single round)

1.

(Reversal and SDPI.)

skewed low-entropy (rare-event) messages, for which the ratio
I(V;M)/I(Y;M)
strictly exceeds

I(V;M)\leq\rho_{m}^{2}\,H(M)
is
false

implication chain
I(V;M\mid U)\leq\eta_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{Y},P_{V\mid Y})\,B
, which yields the single-round

iff

\eta_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{Y},P_{V\mid Y})=O(\mathrm{SNR})
.

Conjecture 1

(Single-round KL contraction is
O(\mathrm{SNR})
)
.

For the binary hypercube Gaussian-location channel with
\mathrm{SNR}\leq 1
,

\eta_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{Y},P_{V\mid Y})\leq C\,\mathrm{SNR}
for an absolute constant
C
. Under this conjecture,

I(V;M\mid U)\leq C\,\mathrm{SNR}\cdot B
, i.e. the single-round (
T=1
) case of
the target inequality (

⋆

‣
D
) holds with
C_{\star}(d)=C
(no
\mathrm{polylog}
at
T=1

We do not prove Conjecture
1
. We know
\eta_{\mathrm{KL}}\geq\rho_{m}^{2}=\Theta(\mathrm{SNR})

(item 2–3), so if true the rate is the right one; bounding
\eta_{\mathrm{KL}}

from above
by

is an instance of the same Gaussian-channel strong-data-processing estimate that the
distributed-DPI literature establishes by more involved means. We flag this honestly: the
maximal-correlation argument settles the
\chi^{2}
contraction but not the mutual-information contraction,
and therefore does
not
by itself make even the single-round bound self-contained.

D.5
The multi-round gap

Even granting Conjecture
1
, the multi-round bound does not follow by naive
telescoping. By the chain rule
I(V;M_{1:T}\mid U)=\sum_{t}I(V;M_{t}\mid M_{1:t-1},U)
, and conditioned on a
prefix the round-
t
term involves
V
drawn from the posterior
\mu_{m}:=P(V\mid M_{1:t-1}=m,U)
rather
than the uniform prior. The relevant coefficient is then that of the
posterior
channel, and the
maximal correlation under
\mu_{m}
need not stay
O(\mathrm{SNR})
: if a prefix could force the coordinates
of
V
to be strongly correlated, a fresh Gaussian look would behave like many looks at one bit and the
per-round contraction would degrade. Controlling this is exactly the inductive information-cost step of
the distributed-DPI argument, which we do not carry out.

Remark 6

(Numerical observations, not used in any proof)
.

The following exploratory computations are not invoked anywhere above. For
product
posteriors
(independent, possibly tilted coordinates) the maximal correlation appears to
decrease
as the
posterior concentrates (e.g. at
\mathrm{SNR}=0.1
it falls from
\approx 0.09
toward
0.01
as a
coordinate’s posterior mass moves from
0.5
to
0.97
). For
strongly correlated
posteriors it
appears to grow (e.g. at
d=8
,
\mathrm{SNR}=0.1
it rises from
\approx 0.09
for independent
coordinates to
\approx 0.44
when all coordinates are forced equal, exceeding
2\,\mathrm{SNR}=0.2
).
These observations suggest that multi-round safety hinges on whether a bounded-bit prefix can induce
strongly correlated posteriors; we make no formal claim and use none of this in any proof.

Hence multi-round safety reduces to a single inductive statement:
a transcript of

(t-1)B
bits cannot drive the posterior
\mu_{m}
to a maximal correlation with a fresh Gaussian look
exceeding
O(\mathrm{SNR})
.
This is precisely the content the distributed data-processing inequality of

11
supplies through its information-cost induction, and is the one ingredient we
do not establish. We note that an earlier draft of this analysis incorrectly claimed the per-round
contraction is automatically uniform over prefixes “because the SDPI coefficient is a channel
property”; the maximal-correlation computation above shows that claim is false—the coefficient
depends on the (posterior) input distribution—and isolates the genuine difficulty.

D.6
A rigorous compression bound via Gaussian centroids

The maximal-correlation route of §
D.4
stalls because it targets the contraction coefficient directly.
A different route proves cleanly the statement that “
B
bits carry only
B
dimensions’ worth of
Gaussian score energy,” which is the mechanism by which
d
is replaced by
B
. This does not by itself
close the target inequality (

⋆

‣
D
), but it isolates the remaining gap more sharply.

Lemma 6

(Gaussian centroid bound)
.

Let
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d})
and let
M
be any random variable with
Z\to M
(a channel output of
Z
).
Then
\mathbb{E}\big\|\mathbb{E}[Z\mid M]\big\|^{2}\leq 2\,I(Z;M)\leq 2\,H(M)
(nats).

Proof.

For any probability measure
Q\ll\gamma:=\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d})
, the Donsker–Varadhan variational formula

(
15
, Lemma 6.2.13)
with the
linear test function
f(z)=\langle a,z\rangle
(for which
\log\mathbb{E}_{\gamma}e^{f}=\tfrac{1}{2}\|a\|^{2}
) gives

D(Q\|\gamma)\geq\langle a,\mathbb{E}_{Q}Z\rangle-\tfrac{1}{2}\|a\|^{2}
; maximizing over
a
at
a=\mathbb{E}_{Q}Z
yields

D(Q\|\gamma)\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\|\mathbb{E}_{Q}Z\|^{2}
. Apply this with
Q=P_{Z\mid M=m}
and average:

I(Z;M)=\mathbb{E}_{m}D(P_{Z\mid M=m}\|\gamma)\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{m}\|\mathbb{E}[Z\mid M=m]\|^{2}
. Finally
I(Z;M)\leq H(M)
.
∎

Lemma 7

(Bits limit Fisher trace)
.

Let
Y=\theta+\sigma Z
with
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d})
, and let
M=Q(Y,U)\in\{0,1\}^{B}
with
U\perp(Z)
. Let

J_{\theta}(M\mid U)
be the Fisher information of the message about
\theta
. Then at every
\theta
,

\mathrm{Tr}J_{\theta}(M\mid U)\;=\;\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\,\mathbb{E}\big\|\mathbb{E}[Z\mid M,U]\big\|^{2}\;\leq\;\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}}\,I(Z;M\mid U)\;\leq\;\frac{2\ln 2}{\sigma^{2}}\,B.

For an adaptive
T
-round protocol with fresh noise each round, Fisher information adds across rounds, so

\mathrm{Tr}J_{\theta}(M_{1:T}\mid U)\leq 2\ln 2\,TB/\sigma^{2}
.

Proof.

At fixed
\theta
and fixed
U=u
, the score for outcome
m
is

\nabla_{\theta}\log P_{\theta}(M=m)=\sigma^{-2}\mathbb{E}[Y-\theta\mid M=m]=\sigma^{-1}\mathbb{E}[Z\mid M=m]
, so

\mathrm{Tr}J_{\theta}(M\mid U{=}u)=\sigma^{-2}\mathbb{E}\|\mathbb{E}[Z\mid M,u]\|^{2}
; Lemma
6
(with
Z
a function
of fresh noise at fixed
\theta,u
) bounds this by
\tfrac{2}{\sigma^{2}}I(Z;M\mid u)\leq\tfrac{2\ln 2}{\sigma^{2}}H(M\mid u)\leq\tfrac{2\ln 2}{\sigma^{2}}B
;
average over
u
. Across rounds, the transcript score is the sum of conditional per-round scores, which
are martingale increments, so the Fisher informations add and each round contributes at most

2\ln 2\,B/\sigma^{2}
.
∎

Lemma
7
is the rigorous form of the compression mechanism: regardless of
dimension, a
B
-bit-per-round transcript carries at most
O(TB/\sigma^{2})
total Fisher trace about the
mean, not
O(Td/\sigma^{2})
.
What remains
to obtain the target inequality (

⋆

‣
D
) is a conversion from
this Fisher-trace bound to the hypercube-prior mutual information
I(V;M_{1:T}\mid U)
with
\theta=\delta V
.
The natural Gaussian conversion
I(V;M)\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}(\Sigma_{\pi}J)=\tfrac{\delta^{2}}{2}\mathrm{Tr}J
(prior
covariance
\Sigma_{\pi}=\delta^{2}I_{d}
) would finish the single-round case, but it is
not
valid at
finite SNR: for the hypercube prior
I(V;M)
can exceed
\tfrac{\delta^{2}}{2}\mathrm{Tr}J
(the bound is the
small-perturbation limit, and the discrete prior contributes positive higher-order terms). The weaker
form
I(V;M)\leq c\,\delta^{2}H(M)
, and the coordinate-Hellinger sensitivity

\sum_{j}\mathbb{E}_{V_{-j}}H^{2}(\,\cdot\,)\leq c\,\tfrac{\delta^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}H(M)
, are the natural targets, but a rigorous
proof of either in the regime
\mathrm{SNR}\leq 1
(rather than
\mathrm{SNR}\to 0
) is the residual step. Thus Lemma
7
reduces the otherwise opaque assumption (

⋆

‣
D
) to a single,
concrete finite-SNR Fisher-to-Bayes conversion (which the main text bypasses entirely via van Trees).

D.7
Status summary

We have reduced the target mutual-information inequality (

⋆

‣
D
) as follows. The single-round case is
not
proved: the

E.1
A drifting quadratic oracle

Definition 2

(Linear–Gaussian oracle)
.

The optimum follows linear–Gaussian dynamics

\theta_{t+1}=A\theta_{t}+w_{t}
,
w_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{iid}}}{{\sim}}\mathcal{N}(0,W)

f_{t}(x)=\tfrac{1}{2}\|x-\theta_{t}\|^{2}

x_{t}

\mathcal{F}_{t-1}:=\sigma(M^{t-1},U)

\xi_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{iid}}}{{\sim}}\mathcal{N}(0,V)

. The encoder

Y_{t}:=x_{t}-g_{t}=\theta_{t}-\xi_{t}

M_{t}=\mathcal{E}_{t}(Y^{t},M^{t-1},U)\in\{0,1\}^{B}

, so
\hat{\theta}_{t}:=x_{t}
is
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}

(a one-step predictor). Write
D_{t}:=\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\|x_{t}-\theta_{t}\|^{2}
and

\bar{D}_{\infty}:=\limsup_{T}\tfrac{1}{T}\sum_{t\leq T}D_{t}
.

Remark 7

(One model, two regimes)
.

Two regimes fit this template. The

(Model B; the focus of
§
E.2
–

) is Definition
2

Lemma 8

(Sequential reduction; query irrelevance)
.

Under Definition
2

\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
-measurable query sequence, the joint

Proof.

Y_{t}=x_{t}-g_{t}=x_{t}-((x_{t}-\theta_{t})+\xi_{t})=\theta_{t}-\xi_{t}

is the case

,

∎

E.2
Lower bound I: a self-contained entropy-power bound

For a random vector
Z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{G}
, write the conditional entropy power
(bits)
N(Z\mid\mathcal{G}):=\tfrac{1}{2\pi e}2^{2h(Z\mid\mathcal{G})/d}
; for a Gaussian
\mathcal{N}(\mu,\Sigma)

one has
N=(\det\Sigma)^{1/d}
.

Lemma 9

(Rate, observation, time, and distortion bounds)
.

Let
N_{t}:=N(\theta_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1})
and
N_{t}^{+}:=N(\theta_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t})
. For any encoder, with
no distributional assumption on the (non-Gaussian) posteriors:

(R)

N_{t}^{+}\geq N_{t}\,2^{-2B/d}
, since

h(\theta_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1})-h(\theta_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t})=I(\theta_{t};M_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1})\leq H(M_{t})\leq B
.

(O)

N_{t}\geq(\det\bar{P}^{\mathrm{KF}}_{t})^{1/d}=:N^{\mathrm{KF}}_{t}
, where
\bar{P}^{\mathrm{KF}}_{t}

is the Kalman one-step prediction covariance: since
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\subseteq\sigma(Y^{t-1},U)
and

\theta_{t}\mid Y^{t-1}
is Gaussian, no predictor measurable w.r.t.
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
beats the Kalman
prediction error.

(T)

N_{t+1}\geq|\det A|^{2/d}N_{t}^{+}+\det(W)^{1/d}
, by the entropy-power inequality and

w_{t}\perp(\theta_{t},\mathcal{F}_{t})
(Remark
7
keeps this independence).

(D)

D_{t}\geq\tfrac{d}{2}\,N_{t}
, since
\inf_{\hat{\theta}_{t}\in\mathcal{F}_{t-1}}\mathbb{E}\|\hat{\theta}_{t}-\theta_{t}\|^{2}=\mathrm{Tr}\bar{P}_{t}\geq d(\det\bar{P}_{t})^{1/d}\geq d\,N_{t}
.

Proof.

(R) Differential-entropy reduction equals conditional mutual information, bounded by
H(M_{t})\leq B
bits;
exponentiate by
2/d
. (O) Any
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
-measurable predictor has error at least the Kalman
prediction error, as
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\subseteq\sigma(Y^{t-1},U)
and
(\theta_{t},Y^{t-1})
are jointly
Gaussian. (T) Entropy-power inequality
(
14
, Thm. 17.7.3)
with
N(A\theta_{t}\mid\cdot)=|\det A|^{2/d}N(\theta_{t}\mid\cdot)
.
(D) Optimality of the conditional mean, then AM–GM
(\det)^{1/d}\leq\mathrm{Tr}/d
and the maximum-entropy
inequality
N\leq(\det)^{1/d}
.
∎

Theorem 5

(Self-contained tracking lower bound)
.

Let
a_{\star}:=|\det A|^{2/d}2^{-2B/d}
and
w_{\star}:=\det(W)^{1/d}
. If
a_{\star}<1
, equivalently

B>\log_{2}|\det A|
, then

\bar{D}_{\infty}\ \geq\ \frac{d}{2}\,N_{\star},\qquad N_{\star}=\max\Big\{\underbrace{\tfrac{\det(W)^{1/d}}{1-|\det A|^{2/d}2^{-2B/d}}}_{\text{rate floor}},\ \underbrace{(\det\bar{P}^{\mathrm{KF}}_{\infty})^{1/d}}_{\text{observation floor}}\Big\},

where
\bar{P}^{\mathrm{KF}}_{\infty}
is the stabilizing solution of the Kalman prediction Riccati

\bar{P}=A(\bar{P}^{-1}+V^{-1})^{-1}A^{\top}+W
(it exists for any
A
since
C=I
is detectable). For stable

A
(
|\det A|\leq 1
, the typical optimization-tracking regime) the rate floor is finite at every

B\geq 0
, so the binding constraint is usually the observation floor. The divergence

\bar{D}_{\infty}\to\infty
for
B<\log_{2}|\det A|
, and with it the Nair–Evans data-rate threshold

(
24
)
, arises only when
A
has unstable modes (
|\det A|>1
; e.g. a scalar
source with
|a|>1
).

Proof.

By (O),
N_{t}\geq(\det\bar{P}^{\mathrm{KF}}_{t})^{1/d}\to(\det\bar{P}^{\mathrm{KF}}_{\infty})^{1/d}
, so

\liminf_{t}N_{t}\geq(\det\bar{P}^{\mathrm{KF}}_{\infty})^{1/d}
. Composing (R) and (T) of
Lemma

gives
N_{t+1}\geq a_{\star}N_{t}+w_{\star}
; iterating from any

N_{t}\geq a_{\star}^{t}N_{0}+w_{\star}\frac{1-a_{\star}^{t}}{1-a_{\star}}
, and when

this yields

\liminf_{t}N_{t}\geq w_{\star}/(1-a_{\star})
(the unique fixed point of the contraction

\Phi(N)=a_{\star}N+w_{\star}
). Combining the two,

\liminf_{t}N_{t}\geq N_{\star}=\max\{w_{\star}/(1-a_{\star}),\,(\det\bar{P}^{\mathrm{KF}}_{\infty})^{1/d}\}
. Finally,

\bar{D}_{\infty}=\limsup_{T}\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t\leq T}D_{t}\geq\frac{d}{2}\,\liminf_{T}\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t\leq T}N_{t}\geq\frac{d}{2}\liminf_{t}N_{t}\geq\frac{d}{2}N_{\star}

using (D) and that a Cesàro mean has
\liminf
at least that of the sequence. The prediction Riccati has
a unique stabilizing solution
\bar{P}^{\mathrm{KF}}_{\infty}\succ 0
because

makes

detectable

(

)
. When

(i.e.

∎

Because the encoder observes the

Proposition 3

(Directed-information converse, with the observation floor)

E.4
Achievability via innovation quantization (expected-rate)

Lemma 11

(Dithered-lattice innovation quantizer)
.

Let the encoder maintain the Kalman one-step prediction

(equivalently

(Expected-rate achievability)

of that covariance; substituting it into the Kalman recursion gives a perturbed prediction Riccati whose
stabilizing solution is, at high rate (where the lattice operates near the Gaussian rate–distortion
function, the overload probability is negligible, and the entropy-coded
average
rate is
B
),
within
c_{d}
of the remote-NRDF distortion of Proposition
3
. This is the
innovation-quantization achievability of
19
, valid whenever the noise has
finite differential entropy and hence requiring no a.s. bound on
g_{t}
. The matching is in the
average-rate sense; converting it to an exact fixed-length
\{0,1\}^{B}
code per round is the open
fixed-length question (L3
′
).
∎

Remark 9
.

Existing single-machine quantizers
(
3
, e.g.)
quantize the
raw
gradient
under an a.s. norm bound; quantizing the innovation is what simultaneously exploits temporal correlation
and removes that bound. The high-rate space-filling constant
c_{d}
and an overload tail (absorbed into

E.5
Specialization and the correlated-gradient correction

We now treat Model A: a
fixed
optimum
\theta
with stationary AR(1) gradient noise

\xi_{t}=\rho\,\xi_{t-1}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}\,\eta_{t}
,
\eta_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{iid}}}{{\sim}}\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}I)
,
stationary variance
\sigma^{2}
,
|\rho|<1
.

We restate the main-text corollary and give the full argument, then generalize it.

Proof of Corollary
2
.

By Lemma
1
,
Y_{t}=x_{t}-g_{t}=\theta-\xi_{t}
. The causal, invertible transform

\tilde{Y}_{t}:=Y_{t}-\rho Y_{t-1}=(1-\rho)\theta-\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}\,\eta_{t}
(
t\geq 2
) depends only on

\{\eta_{t}\}
, so
\{\tilde{Y}_{t}\}_{t\geq 2}
is i.i.d. and
(Y_{1},\tilde{Y}_{2},\dots,\tilde{Y}_{T})
is a bijective
(hence sufficient) function of
Y^{T}
, losing no information about
\theta
. Normalizing,

\tilde{Y}_{t}/(1-\rho)\sim\mathcal{N}(\theta,\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{eff}}I)
with

\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{eff}}=\frac{1-\rho^{2}}{(1-\rho)^{2}}\,\sigma^{2}=\frac{1+\rho}{1-\rho}\,\sigma^{2}.

The encoder (which under Definition
2
sees
Y^{t}
) can form
\tilde{Y}_{t}
causally and the
decoder can invert; the
B
-bit budget is unchanged, so under this causal-history encoder the two
minimax risks are equal and Theorem
3
applies with
\sigma^{2}\mapsto\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{eff}}
.
The map
Y^{T}\mapsto(Y_{1},\tilde{Y}_{2},\dots,\tilde{Y}_{T})
is a bijection, so the
T
-round correlated problem
has the same minimax risk as the
(T\!-\!1)
-sample i.i.d. problem at variance

\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{eff}}
together with the single Gaussian observation
Y_{1}
(variance
\sigma^{2}
); the
latter shifts the per-round risk by
O(1/T)
and is dominated. Finally, a causal-history encoder is
stronger than the memoryless encoder of Definition
1
(Remark
1
), so the
lower bound transfers
a fortiori
to the original oracle.
∎

Remark 10

(What the correlated-gradient conjecture got wrong)
.

Positive noise correlation
raises
the bound by the factor
\tfrac{1+\rho}{1-\rho}\geq 1
: it does
not relax the
d/B
penalty in isolation but scales
all
terms by
\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{eff}}/\sigma^{2}
.
Three independent computations agree: (a) the whitening above; (b) the AR(1) noise spectrum

S_{\xi}(\omega)=\tfrac{(1-\rho^{2})\sigma^{2}}{1-2\rho\cos\omega+\rho^{2}}
evaluated at the DC component that
carries
\theta
,
S_{\xi}(0)=\tfrac{1+\rho}{1-\rho}\sigma^{2}
; and (c) the effective sample size

\mathrm{Var}(\bar{\xi})=\tfrac{\sigma^{2}}{T}\sum_{k}\rho^{|k|}=\tfrac{\sigma^{2}}{T}\tfrac{1+\rho}{1-\rho}
. What

does
relax the bit requirement is predictability of the optimum’s
trajectory
(slow drift,

\rho(A)\to 1
with small
W
), via the rate floor of Theorem
5
—not correlation of the
noise. The original conjecture conflated the two.

Corollary 3

(General PSD noise; consistency)
.

If
\{\xi_{t}\}
is stationary with matrix spectral density
S_{\xi}(\omega)
, estimating the fixed

\theta
(the DC component) has effective noise covariance
S_{\xi}(0)
, and the bound is obtained by
reverse-waterfilling over the eigenvalues of
S_{\xi}(0)
as a corollary of the SDP of
Lemma
10
. Setting
\rho=0
(or
A=I
,
W=0
) gives
\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{eff}}=\sigma^{2}
and
recovers Theorem
3
exactly.
