Null limit of large-charge correlators in planar 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 Super-Yang-Mills theory
Benjamin Basso, Thiago Fleury, Erkan Kaluç, Didina Serban

Abstract
We present a conjecture for the double-logarithmic behavior of the logarithm of large-charge correlators in the null limit in planar 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 Super-Yang-Mills theory. Generalizing earlier results for four- and five-point functions, our proposal predicts this behavior for n n -point functions to all loops in terms of the tilted cusp anomalous dimension. In the dual amplitude description, it reproduces the expected small-mass behavior of massive amplitudes in the equal-mass limit.



I
Introduction

Correlation functions of chiral primary operators are among the most fundamental observables in planar
\mathcal{N}=4
Super-Yang-Mills theory, encoding much of the theory’s OPE data. While difficult to determine in general, they exhibit rich structural properties and admit deep connections to scattering amplitudes in special kinematic limits.

A striking example is provided by the duality between scattering amplitudes and large-charge correlators
Caron-Huot and Coronado 2022
;
Bargheer et al. 2025a
. It is most naturally formulated by considering the “ten-dimensional null limit”,
(x_{i}-x_{i+1})^{2}+(y_{i}-y_{i+1})^{2}\rightarrow 0
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, of the generating function for correlators of chiral primaries with arbitrary R-charges,

G_{n}=\sum_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}\geqslant 2}\Big\langle\,\prod_{i\,=\,1}^{n}\frac{1}{k_{i}}\textrm{Tr}\,(y_{i}\cdot\phi(x_{i}))^{k_{i}}\,\Big\rangle\,,

(1)

where
y\cdot\phi=\sum_{I=1}^{6}y_{i}^{I}\phi^{I}
and
y_{i}^{2}=0
. This limit singles out contributions in which a large R-charge is exchanged between neighboring operators, causing
G_{n}
to factorize into the square of a polygon correlator
\mathbb{M}_{n}(\{x_{i},y_{i}\})

Caron-Huot and Coronado 2022
;
Bargheer et al. 2025a
.
According to a recent proposal, these polygons map in dual momentum space,
p^{\mu}_{i}=x^{\mu}_{i,i+1}=x^{\mu}_{i}-x^{\mu}_{i+1}
, to Coulomb-branch scattering amplitudes, where the
y_{i}
determine the external masses
m_{i}^{2}=(y_{i}-y_{i+1})^{2}
, while loop particles remain massless, as illustrated in Fig.
1
.
This extends to the massive case the well-known factorization of twist-two correlators (
k_{i}=2
) into Wilson loops, dual to maximally helicity-violating (MHV) amplitudes, in the null-polygon limit
Alday et al. 2011a
;
Alday and Maldacena 2007
;
Drummond et al. 2008a
;
Brandhuber et al. 2008
;
Drummond et al. 2010
.

Figure 1:
Cartoon of the duality between large-charge correlators and scattering amplitudes for
n=5
. The large charge exchanged between operators splits the correlator into two independent polygons in the front and back (left panel). Each polygon is dual to a scattering amplitude in dual momentum space (right panel), with massive external particles,
p_{i}^{2}=x_{i,i+1}^{2}\neq 0
, and massless internal ones.

In the null limit where
m_{i}^{2}=-x_{i,i+1}^{2}\rightarrow 0
, with all Coulomb-branch parameters set to zero
(y_{i}\rightarrow 0)
, polygon correlators are expected to behave similarly to massless amplitudes. However, the resulting infrared behavior turns out to be surprisingly different.
Calculations at low multiplicity reveal a double-logarithmic behavior that departs from the Sudakov behavior of conventionally regulated amplitudes
Bern et al. 2005
;
Alday et al. 2010
;
Henn et al. 2011
;
Caron-Huot and Coronado 2022
;
Alday et al. 2026
, with the infrared logarithms no longer governed by the universal cusp anomalous dimension
\Gamma_{\textrm{cusp}}
, but instead by a new set of coefficients characteristic of polygon correlators.

The simplest example of polygon correlator where such a behavior can be observed is the four-point case,
\mathbb{M}_{4}=\mathbb{O}_{0}
, known as the octagon
Coronado 2020
. Owing to integrability, this quantity can be computed exactly at finite coupling
g=\sqrt{\lambda}/(4\pi)

Coronado 2020
;
Kostov et al. 2019
;
Belitsky and Korchemsky 2020a
. In the null limit its logarithm exhibits a remarkably simple double-logarithmic scaling
that persists to all loop orders
Coronado 2020
;
Kostov et al. 2019
;
Belitsky and Korchemsky 2020b
;
Caron-Huot and Coronado 2022
,

\displaystyle\log{\bP_4}

(2)

\displaystyle\approx-\frac{\Gamma_{\textrm{oct}}}{16}\log^{2}{\left(\prod_{i\,=\,1}^{4}\frac{x_{i,i+1}^{2}}{x_{i,i+2}^{2}}\right)}+\frac{g^{2}}{4}\log^{2}{\left(\frac{x_{12}^{2}x_{34}^{2}}{x_{23}^{2}x_{41}^{2}}\right)}+\mathcal{C}_{4}\,,

up to power-suppressed corrections in the
x_{i,i+1}^{2}
. Here
\Gamma_{\textrm{oct}}
is the octagon anomalous dimension
Belitsky and Korchemsky 2020b

\Gamma_{\textrm{oct}}(g)=\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}\log{\cosh{(2\pi g)}}\,,

(3)

and
\mathcal{C}_{4}
is a coupling-dependent constant that is likewise known exactly. Another quantity known in closed form is the one-loop contribution for arbitrary
n

Drukker and Plefka 2009
;
Bargheer et al. 2018
,

\log{\bP_{n}}=g^{2}\,\mathbb{M}^{\textrm{1-loop}}_{n}+\mathcal{O}\left(g^{4}\right)\,.

(4)

In the null-polygon limit,
l_{i}=\log x_{i,i+1}^{2}\rightarrow-\infty
, it exhibits the logarithmic scaling
Alday et al. 2011a
(see Appendix
A
)

\!\mathbb{M}_{n}^{\textrm{1-loop}}\approx-\sum_{i\,=\,1}^{n}(l_{i}-l^{\prime}_{i})(l_{i+1}-l^{\prime}_{i})+2A_{n}^{\textrm{BDS}}-\frac{5n}{2}\zeta_{2}\,,

(5)

where
l^{\prime}_{i}=\log x_{i,i+2}^{2},\,\zeta_{2}=\pi^{2}/6,
and
A_{n}^{\textrm{BDS}}
coincides with the finite part of the Bern-Dixon-Smirnov (BDS) ansatz for scattering amplitudes
Bern et al. 2005
. Perturbative results for
n=5
, currently available up to three loops
Bork et al. 2022
;
Bercini et al. 2024
;
Bargheer et al. 2025a
;
Belitsky et al. 2025
;
Belitsky et al. 2026a
;
Belitsky et al. 2026b
, further confirmed this behavior and motivated conjectures for the all-loop dependence of the corresponding coefficients
Belitsky et al. 2026a
.

In this Letter, we propose an all-loop formula for the double-logarithmic asymptotics of
\log{\bP_n}
in the null limit. The formula reproduces all known perturbative results and applies to polygons with an arbitrary number of sides. Its structure parallels that of massless scattering amplitudes in origin limits
Basso et al. 2020
;
Basso et al. 2023
, and is expressed in terms of the tilted cusp anomalous dimension.

II
Main formula

Based on the evidence above, we expect that in the null limit
l_{i}=\log{x^2_{i,i+1}}\rightarrow-\infty
, the polygon correlator
\mathbb{M}_{n}
develops large logarithms that exponentiate as

\log{\bP_n}=\mathcal{Q}_{n}(\{l_{i}\})+\ldots\,,

(6)

where
\mathcal{Q}_{n}
is a quadratic form and the dots denotes subleading contributions. This form must respect the discrete dihedral symmetry of the polygon, generated by cyclic shift
l_{i}\rightarrow l_{i+1}
and reflection
l_{i}\rightarrow l_{n-i+2}
, with
l_{i+n}=l_{i}
. This forces
\mathcal{Q}_{n}
to be circulant,

\mathcal{Q}_{n}(\{l_{i}\})=\sum_{i,j\,=\,1}^{n}a_{ij}\,l_{i}l_{j}\,,

(7)

with
a_{ij}=a(i-j)
and
a(j)=a(n+j)=a(-j)
. Standard discrete Fourier analysis then diagonalizes it, yielding

\mathcal{Q}_{n}(\{l_{i}\})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\alpha\,\in\,\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{n}}\widehat{a}_{\alpha}\,\Big|\sum_{k\,=\,1}^{n}e^{2ik\alpha}\,l_{k}\Big|^{2}\,,

(8)

where the sum runs over angles
\alpha
corresponding to the
n
-th roots of unity,
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{n}=\{\alpha:e^{2in\alpha}=1,\alpha\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2]\}
. The Fourier coefficients are defined as

\widehat{a}_{\alpha}\equiv\sum_{j\,=\,0}^{n-1}a(j)\,e^{2ij\alpha}\,,

(9)

and they satisfy
\widehat{a}_{\alpha+\pi}=\widehat{a}_{\alpha}
and
\widehat{a}_{-\alpha}=\widehat{a}_{\alpha}
, reflecting the dihedral symmetry.

Our main conjecture is that the eigenvalue
\widehat{a}_{\alpha}
is governed by the tilted cusp anomalous dimension
\Gamma_{\alpha}(g)
,

\widehat{a}_{\alpha}=-\frac{1}{4}\cos{(2\alpha)}\,\Gamma_{\alpha}(g)\,,

(10)

for arbitrary coupling
g
and any
n
.

The tilted cusp anomalous dimension
\Gamma_{\alpha}(g)
is a function of the tilt angle
\alpha
and the coupling
g
. It was obtained in
Basso et al. 2020
by deforming the Beisert-Eden-Staudacher (BES) equation for the cusp anomalous dimension
Beisert et al. 2007
, and was originally introduced to describe the double-logarithmic behavior of massless MHV scattering amplitudes in the origin limits, where multiple cross ratios are simultaneously taken to zero. In our setup, the same function controls the null limit of polygon correlators evaluated at discrete angles
\alpha\in\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{n}
. It obeys
\Gamma_{\alpha}=\Gamma_{-\alpha}=\Gamma_{\alpha+\pi}
, in agreement with the symmetry properties of
\widehat{a}_{\alpha}
.

Explicit expressions for
\Gamma_{\alpha}(g)
are obtained by solving the tilted BES equation
Basso et al. 2020
. In particular, at weak coupling one finds

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\alpha}(g)=4g^{2}\big\{1-4c^{2}\zeta_{2}g^{2}+8c^{2}(3+5c^{2})\zeta_{4}g^{4}

(11)

\displaystyle-8c^{2}\left[(25+42c^{2}+35c^{4})\zeta_{6}+4s^{2}(\zeta_{3})^{2}\right]g^{6}+\mathcal{O}\left(g^{8}\right)\big\}\,,

where
\zeta_{k}=\sum_{j\,=\,1}^{\infty}j^{-k}
is the Riemann zeta function and
c=\cos{\alpha},s=\sin{\alpha}
. Higher-order terms can be generated iteratively.
At special values of
\alpha
, it reproduces known anomalous dimensions, notably
\Gamma_{\textrm{cusp}}=\Gamma_{\pm\pi/4}
,
\Gamma_{\textrm{oct}}=\Gamma_{0}
, and
\Gamma_{\pi/2}=4g^{2}
to all loops.

Combining this with (
9
) and (
10
), one immediately verifies consistency with the all-order formula (
2
) for
n=4
. In this case the relevant angles are
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{4}=\{0,\pm\pi/4,\pi/2\}
, but only
\alpha=0
and
\alpha=\pi/2
contribute to the final result, since the prefactor
\cos{(2\alpha)}
removes the contribution from
\Gamma_{\textrm{cusp}}
, in complete agreement with (
2
).

As an additional check, one reproduces the double-logarithmic behavior
\mathcal{Q}_{n}=-g^{2}\sum_{i}l_{i}l_{i+1}+\ldots
of the one-loop all-
n
formula (
5
).
At this order,
\Gamma_{\alpha}
is independent of
\alpha
, and all angular dependence required to match this behavior comes from the factor
\cos(2\alpha)
in (
10
).

III
Conformal invariance and minimal ansatz

Since the original correlator
\mathbb{M}_{n}
is a conformally invariant function of the operator positions, it is natural to require that the quadratic form (
6
) be completed into a function of conformal cross ratios.
In principle, for generic
n
, this completion is not unique, and different choices may lead to different subleading divergent terms in (
6
).
Among these, the simplest and most natural uplift uses only next-to-nearest-neighbor distances
l^{\prime}_{i}=\log{x^2_{i,i+2}}
.
Accordingly, we consider a decomposition into a divergent and finite part

\log{\bP_{n}}\approx\mathcal{Q}^{\textrm{min}}_{n}+\mathcal{F}_{n}\,,

(12)

where each term is separately conformally invariant. In particular,
\mathcal{F}_{n}
is a finite function of the cross ratios of the null polygon.

To construct the ansatz for the divergent part, we generalize
\mathcal{Q}_{n}
to a quadratic form
\mathcal{Q}^{\textrm{min}}_{n}
built form the
2n
distances
l_{i}=\log{x^2_{i,i+1}}
and
l^{\prime}_{i}=\log{x^2_{i,i+2}}
,

\mathcal{Q}^{\textrm{min}}_{n}=\sum_{i,j}\,a_{ij}l_{i}l_{j}+2\sum_{i,j}\,b_{ij}l_{i}l^{\prime}_{j}+\sum_{i,j}c_{ij}\,l^{\prime}_{i}l^{\prime}_{j}\,.

(13)

As before, dihedral symmetry implies that the coefficients depend only on
i-j
modulo
n
, together with
a(-i)=a(i)
,
b(-i)=b(1+i)
,
c(-i)=c(i)
. To impose conformal invariance, it suffices to consider spacetime inversions,
x_{i}^{\mu}\rightarrow x_{i}^{\mu}/x_{i}^{2}
, under which

\log{x_{ij}^2}\rightarrow\log{x_{ij}^2}-\log{x_{i}^2}-\log{x_{j}^2}\,.

(14)

Requiring
\mathcal{Q}^{\textrm{min}}_{n}
to be invariant under this transformation, for arbitrary
\log{x^2_{i}}
and
\log{x^2_{ij}}
, yields linear recurrence relations among the coefficients in (
13
), which can be again solved by Fourier transform (
9
), giving

\displaystyle\widehat{b}_{\alpha}=-e^{i\alpha}\frac{\cos\alpha}{\cos{(2\alpha)}}\,\widehat{a}_{\alpha}\,,\quad\widehat{c}_{\alpha}=\left(\frac{\cos\alpha}{\cos{(2\alpha)}}\right)^{2}\widehat{a}_{\alpha}\,.

(15)

These expressions are well defined provided
\alpha\neq\pm\pi/4
, or equivalently
n\neq 4k
with
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence, in this case, the conformally invariant quadratic form (
13
) is uniquely determined by the double-divergent part
\mathcal{Q}_{n}
.

The result can in fact be directly obtained from (
8
) by replacing
l_{i}\rightarrow\ell_{i}
,

\mathcal{Q}^{\textrm{min}}_{n}=-\frac{1}{4n}\sum_{\alpha\,\in\,\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{n}}\cos{(2\alpha)}\,\Gamma_{\alpha}\,\Big|\sum_{j\,=\,1}^{n}e^{2ij\alpha}\,\ell_{j}\Big|^{2}\,,

(16)

where
\ell_{i}=\log{\u_i}
, with
\chi_{i}
the unique cross ratio constructed from
x_{i,i+1}^{2}
and next-to-nearest-neighbor distances
\{x^{2}_{j,j+2}\}

Alday et al. 2011a
;
Alday et al. 2011b
. It reads

\chi_{i}=x^{2}_{i,i+1}\prod_{j\,=\,0}^{2k}\left(x^{2}_{i+2j\sigma,i+2j\sigma+2\sigma}\right)^{\varepsilon(j)}\,,

(17)

for
n=4k+2-\sigma
, with
\sigma=\pm 1
, and

\!\!\chi_{i}=x^{2}_{i,i+1}\prod_{m\,=\,0}^{1}\prod_{j\,=\,0}^{k}\!\left(x^{2}_{i+2j+m,i+2j+m+2}\right)^{\varepsilon(j)/2}\,,

(18)

for
n=4k+2
, with
k=1,2,\ldots,
and
\varepsilon(j)=(-1)^{j+1}
in both cases.

When
n=4k
, the solution (
15
) is singular due to poles at
\alpha=\pm\pi/4
. These poles do not affect
\widehat{b}_{\alpha}
, however, because
\widehat{a}_{\alpha}
has a simple zero at the same points. As a result, the ansatz (
13
) remains well-defined for the full divergent part and arbitrary
n
.

The situation is different for
\widehat{c}_{\alpha}
, where the poles persist. They signal that, for
n=4k
, the divergent part cannot be completed to a conformally invariant quadratic form using only terms of the form
l^{\prime}_{i}\,l^{\prime}_{j}
. Completion in this case necessarily requires including additional kinematic variables, such as distances between next-to-next-to-nearest neighboring cusps.

This obstruction is tied to the fact that the cross ratios
\chi_{i}
cannot be defined when
n=4k

Alday et al. 2011b
. It is analogous to the obstruction encountered in the construction of the BDS-like ansatz for massless amplitudes, which formally corresponds to the ansatz (
10
) for
\widehat{a}_{\alpha}
with
\Gamma_{\alpha}\rightarrow\Gamma_{\textrm{cusp}}
. This analogy suggests a natural way to obtain an expression that remains valid for arbitrary
n
, namely by subtracting a contribution controlled by the BDS ansatz and
\Gamma_{\textrm{cusp}}
.

In the null limit, this leads to the decomposition

\log{\bP_n}\approx\frac{1}{4}\Gamma_{\textrm{cusp}}\,\mathbb{M}_{n}^{\textrm{1-loop}}+\Delta\mathcal{Q}^{\textrm{min}}_{n}+\mathcal{R}_{n}\,,

(19)

where
\Delta\mathcal{Q}^{\textrm{min}}
is obtained from the ansatz (
13
) by replacing
\Gamma_{\alpha}
with
\Delta\Gamma_{\alpha}=\Gamma_{\alpha}-\Gamma_{\textrm{cusp}}
in (
10
) and
solving (
15
) for the modified coefficients.
The term
\mathcal{R}_{n}
denotes a conformally invariant remainder function, which is finite in the null limit. Unlike
\mathcal{Q}_{n}^{\textrm{min}}
and
\mathcal{F}_{n}
, both
\Delta\mathcal{Q}_{n}^{\textrm{min}}
and
\mathcal{R}_{n}
start at two loops in the weak-coupling expansion.

The rationale for this splitting is that the singularities at
\alpha=\pm\pi/4
are removed from
\Delta\mathcal{Q}_{n}^{\textrm{min}}
,
the pole in
\widehat{c}_{\alpha}
being now canceled by the extra zero in
\Delta\Gamma_{\alpha}
. This allows us to write a conformally invariant formula valid for any
n
as follows

\!\!\Delta\mathcal{Q}^{\textrm{min}}_{n}=-\frac{1}{16n}\sum_{\alpha\,\in\,\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{n}}\frac{\Delta\Gamma_{\alpha}(g)}{\cos{(2\alpha)}}\,\Big|\sum_{j\,=\,1}^{n}e^{2ij\alpha}\log{\U_j}\Big|^{2}\,,

(20)

with the cross ratios

U_{i}=\frac{x^{2}_{i,i+1}x^{2}_{i+2,i+3}}{x^{2}_{i,i+2}x^{2}_{i+1,i+3}}\,.

(21)

This form is manifestly more local in the index
i
, since the cross ratios
U_{i}
are built only from neighboring points, in contrast to the more non-local cross-ratios
\chi_{i}
. When the latter exist,
U_{i}=\chi_{i}\,\chi_{i+2}
, and substituting this into (
20
) and (
19
) reproduces formula (
16
) and (
12
).

IV
Check in equal-mass limit

As a further check, we consider the symmetric limit
l_{i}=l
for all
i
, with
l\rightarrow-\infty
. In the amplitude picture, this corresponds to the equal-mass regime
m_{i}=m
, with
m\to 0
. In this limit, the correlator should reproduce the factorization formula
Belitsky et al. 2025
;
Bork et al. 2022
;
Korchemsky 1989
;
Belitsky et al. 2023

\log{\bP_n}\approx-\frac{\Gamma_{\textrm{oct}}}{4}\sum_{i\,=\,1}^{n}(l-l^{\prime}_{i})^{2}+F_{n}\,,

(22)

where
l=\log{m^2}
and
F_{n}
is another function that remains finite as
m\rightarrow 0
. This expression reflects the factorization of infrared divergences into Sudakov form factors associated with neighboring particles in the planar ordering.
Unlike conventional regulators, however, the divergences are governed by
\Gamma_{\textrm{oct}}
rather than
\Gamma_{\textrm{cusp}}
.

This behavior may appear at odds with our proposal, which involves several anomalous dimensions, but in the equal-mass limit
l_{j}\to l
,

\sum_{j\,=\,1}^{n}e^{2ij\alpha}l_{j}\rightarrow nl\delta_{\alpha,0}\,,\qquad\alpha\in\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{n}\,,

(23)

where
\delta_{\alpha,0}
denotes the Kronecker delta. Consequently, all terms with
\alpha\neq 0
are projected out, both in the quadratic and linear divergences in
l
in the ansatz (
13
). The only surviving contribution is therefore the one proportional to
\Gamma_{\textrm{oct}}
, in complete agreement with the structure of (
22
).

Equivalently,
\Gamma_{\textrm{oct}}
controls the overall mass dependence, whereas the anomalous dimensions
\Gamma_{\alpha}
with
\alpha\neq 0
encode the dependence on mass ratios, which becomes trivial in the equal-mass limit.

The dependence on
\alpha
is not entirely lost, however, it survives in the finite part
F_{n}
. Indeed, our conjecture can be reformulated as a conformal Ward identity for
F_{n}
. Under the transformation (
14
), it transforms as

F_{n}\rightarrow F_{n}+\sum_{i\,=\,1}^{n}\delta_{i}F_{n}\log{x^2_{i}}\,,

(24)

to leading order in
\log{x^{2}_{i}}
, with

\displaystyle\delta_{i}F_{n}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\Gamma_{\textrm{oct}}}{2}(l^{\prime}_{i}+l^{\prime}_{i-2})

(25)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\alpha\,\in\,\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{n}}\frac{\Gamma_{\alpha}}{n}\cos^{2}{\alpha}\sum_{j\,=\,1}^{n}\cos{(2 j\alpha)}\,l^{\prime}_{j+i-1}\,.

This equation can be viewed as a deformation of the familiar anomaly equation for the finite part of massless amplitudes
Drummond et al. 2010
;
Alday et al. 2011b
, which is recovered upon replacing
\Gamma_{\alpha},\Gamma_{\textrm{oct}}
with
\Gamma_{\textrm{cusp}}
and summing over the
\alpha
’s.

V
Weak coupling results

To perform more advanced checks of our conjecture, it is convenient to first carry out the sum over the angles
\alpha
’s in (
10
) and rewrite the result in the form

\!\mathcal{Q}_{n}=-\gamma_{0}(g)\sum_{i\,=\,1}^{n}\,l_{i}l_{i+1}-\sum_{k\,=\,1}^{\infty}\gamma_{k}(g)\!\sum_{i\,=\,1}^{n}\,l_{i}(l_{i+1+k}+l_{i+1-k})\,,

(26)

where
\gamma_{k}
is the
k
-th Fourier coefficient of
\Gamma_{\alpha}
, defined as

\gamma_{k}(g)=\int\limits_{0}^{\pi/2}\frac{d\alpha}{2\pi}\cos{(2k\alpha)}\,\Gamma_{\alpha}(g)\,,

(27)

or, equivalently,

\Gamma_{\alpha}(g)=4\gamma_{0}(g)+8\sum_{k\,=\,1}^{\infty}\cos{(2k\alpha)}\gamma_{k}(g)\,.

(28)

Note that the dependence on
n
only enters through the range of the sums in (
26
) and the periodicity condition
l_{i}=l_{i+n}
, whereas the coefficients
\gamma_{k}
are independent of
n
.

This representation reveals that, at weak coupling, a new polynomial structure
\propto\bigl(l_{i}l_{i+L}+l_{i}l_{i+2-L}\bigr)
appears at each loop order
L
, coupling logarithms associated with edges separated by up to
L
steps along the polygon (for
L\leqslant\lfloor n/2\rfloor
). This follows since the
L
-loop coefficient of
\Gamma_{\alpha}
is a polynomial of degree
L-1
in
\cos{(2\alpha)}
, as can be seen in (
11
). Consequently, the coefficient (
27
) obeys the scaling
\gamma_{k}=\mathcal{O}(g^{2k+2})
, and at
L
loops the sum in (
26
) truncates to
k\leqslant L
.

Up to four loops, the coefficients required are

\displaystyle\gamma_{0}=g^{2}-2\zeta_{2}g^{4}+27\zeta_{4}g^{6}-\!\left(\frac{627}{2}\zeta_{6}+4\zeta_{3}^{2}\right)g^{8}\!+\!\mathcal{O}\left(g^{10}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}=-\zeta_{2}g^{4}+16\zeta_{4}g^{6}-\frac{1597}{8}\zeta_{6}g^{8}+\mathcal{O}\left(g^{10}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\gamma_{2}=\frac{5}{2}\zeta_{4}g^{6}-\left(\frac{189}{4}\zeta_{6}-2\zeta_{3}^{2}\right)g^{8}+\mathcal{O}\left(g^{10}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\gamma_{3}=-\frac{35}{8}\zeta_{6}g^{8}+\mathcal{O}\left(g^{10}\right)\,,

which fully determine the divergent part for arbitrary
n
.

26

;
Bork et al. 2022

Belitsky et al. 2026b
;
Belitsky et al. 2026a

\displaystyle c_{0}=-\gamma_{1}(g)\,,\quad c_{1}=-\gamma_{0}(g)-\gamma_{2}(g)-\gamma_{3}(g)\,,

\displaystyle c_{2}=-\gamma_{1}(g)-\gamma_{2}(g)\,-\gamma_{3}(g)\,,

\displaystyle\!\!c_{0}

\displaystyle=\zeta_{2}g^{4}-16\zeta_{4}g^{6}+\frac{1597}{8}\zeta_{6}g^{8}\,,

(32)

\displaystyle\!\!c_{1}

\displaystyle=-g^{2}+2\zeta_{2}g^{4}-\frac{59}{2}\zeta_{4}g^{6}+\left(2\zeta^{2}_{3}+\frac{2921}{8}\zeta_{6}\right)g^{8}\,,

\displaystyle\!\!c_{2}

\displaystyle=\zeta_{2}g^{4}-\frac{37}{2}\zeta_{4}g^{6}+\left(-2\zeta_{3}^{2}+\frac{1005}{4}\zeta_{6}\right)g^{8}\,,

up to
\mathcal{O}(g^{10})
corrections. Truncating at three loops, we find perfect agreement with the results of
Belitsky et al. 2026b
;
Belitsky et al. 2026a
!

The four-loop expressions are predictions. They differ from those conjectured in
Belitsky et al. 2026b
;
Belitsky et al. 2026a
which also rely on the tilted cusp anomalous dimension but involve a different choice of angles

\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{5}=\{0,\pm\pi/5,\pm 2\pi/5\}
.

For completeness, let us also quote the higher-loop conformal formula for generic
n
. As discussed earlier, this is most naturally done using
\Delta\mathcal{Q}^{\textrm{min}}
, which starts at two loops and reads

\Delta\mathcal{Q}^{\textrm{min}}_{n}=\frac{1}{2}\zeta_{2}g^{4}\sum_{i\,=\,1}^{n}\log^{2}{U_{i}}+\mathcal{O}(g^{6})\,,

(33)

for any

with
U_{i}(=\chi_{i}\chi_{i+2})
defined in (

n
-gon

;
Bargheer et al. 2025b

\Delta\mathcal{Q}^{\textrm{min}}_{n}=-\frac{\tilde{\gamma}_{1}}{2}\sum_{i\,=\,1}^{n}\log^{2}{U_{i}}-\sum_{k\,=\,2}^{\infty}\tilde{\gamma}_{k}\sum_{i\,=\,1}^{n}\log{\U_{i}}\log{\U_{i+k-1}}\,,

(34)

\tilde{\gamma}_{k}=\sum_{m\,=\,0}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}\gamma_{k+2m}=\mathcal{O}\left(g^{2k+2}\right)

VI
Conclusions

In this Letter, we proposed an all-loop conjecture for the behavior of polygon correlators in the null limit in planar
\mathcal{N}=4
Super-Yang-Mills theory. The conjecture rests on a small set of natural assumptions, drawing on the symmetries of the correlator and insights into the general structure of scattering amplitudes in origin limits. It reproduces all available data. Further non-perturbative tests may be obtained using integrability, in particular through the hexagonalization approach to correlation functions
Basso et al. 2026
.

Uplifting our result to a function of cross ratios through a minimal ansatz, we find that polygon correlators admit the decomposition (
19
) in the null limit. This mirrors the twist-two case, where the correlator factorizes into a null polygonal Wilson loop multiplied by a recoil factor that captures the null-limit divergences
Alday et al. 2011a
;
Alday and Bissi 2013
;
Bercini et al. 2021
;
Chen et al. 2025
. Our result takes the same form, with

J_{n}=e^{\Delta\mathcal{Q}_{n}^{\textrm{min}}}

(35)

playing the role of the recoil factor. Like its twist-two counterpart,
J_{n}
depends only on the vanishing cross ratios
U_{i}
defined in (
21
) and, at weak coupling, exhibits analogous long-range contributions of the form
\sim g^{2k+2}\log{\U_i}\log{\U_{i+k-1}}
(see Appendix
B
). The large-charge recoil factor is, however, considerably simpler, owing to its Gaussian structure.

An important open question is whether the finite remainder
\mathcal{R}_{n}
in (
19
) coincides with the remainder function of scattering amplitudes (up to scheme-dependent constants) as for twist-two correlators
Alday et al. 2011a
;
Chen et al. 2025
. We know that the equality with massless amplitudes holds exactly at integrand level
Caron-Huot and Coronado 2022
;
Bargheer et al. 2025a
. We also expect
\mathcal{R}_{n}
(or, more precisely,
\mathcal{F}_{n}
in (
12
)) to satisfy the same Steinmann relations and belong to the same space of transcendental functions as MHV amplitudes
Caron-Huot et al. 2016
, suggesting this identification may persist beyond the integrand level.

A key difference, however, is that a large-charge correlator has many particles running along its boundary, rather than the single fast particle that frames the Wilson loop in the twist-two case. This distinction may substantially alter the Wilson-loop picture, leaving a direct interpretation of the remainder less clear.

A direct comparison at two loops is the next natural step, building on the recent results for polygon integrands
Bargheer et al. 2025a
;
Bargheer et al. 2025b

and off-shell amplitudes
Belitsky 2025
. The first non-trivial test arises at
n=6
, where a genuine remainder function appears, just as for scattering amplitudes and Wilson loops
Drummond et al. 2008b
;
Bern et al. 2008
;
Drummond et al. 2009
;
Goncharov et al. 2010
.

More generally, it would be interesting to study broader classes of Coulomb-branch amplitudes, such as those considered in
Alday et al. 2025
, which interpolate more smoothly between the off-shell and on-shell regimes. This may help clarify whether the walking behavior identified there is a feature of the divergent part alone or extends to the finite remainder as well.

Finally, another powerful framework for studying the null-polygon limit is the Stampedes approach
Olivucci and Vieira 2022a
;
Olivucci and Vieira 2022b
. It operates in the double-scaling limit, where the cusp variables
t^{2}_{i}=g^{2}l_{i-1}l_{i}
are kept finite while
g^{2}\rightarrow 0
and
l_{i}\rightarrow-\infty
, and has enabled nontrivial tests of diagrammatic results at higher loops
Bercini et al. 2024
;
Belitsky et al. 2025
;
Bargheer et al. 2025a
. While our analysis extends beyond this limit for empty polygons with
y^{2}_{ij}=(y_{i}-y_{j})^{2}\rightarrow 0
, it does not readily apply when some
y^{2}_{ij}
remain finite, unlike the Stampedes approach. It would be interesting to explore whether this complementarity can provide access to more general null-polygon configurations at higher loops.

Appendix A
One-loop result

The structure of
n
-point functions of arbitrarily polarized half-BPS operators at one loop was determined in
Drukker and Plefka 2009
from the complete set of one-loop Feynman diagrams. For polygon correlators, the contributions can be resummed into a closed-form expression, in agreement with both the integrated one-loop polygon integrand of
Bargheer et al. 2025a
and the integrability results of
Fleury and Komatsu 2018
;
Bargheer et al. 2018
in two-dimensional kinematics. In the limit where all the R-charge contractions

\log\mathbb{M}^{\textrm{1-loop}}_{n}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\,=\,1}^{n}\sum_{j\,=\,i+2}^{n+i-2}\Phi(z_{ij},\bar{z}_{ij})\,,

(36)

where

\displaystyle\Phi(z,\bar{z})=\frac{z+\bar{z}+2}{z-\bar{z}}\left[\textrm{Li}_{2}(-z)+\frac{1}{2}\log{(z\bar{z})}\log{\left(1+z\right)}-(z\leftrightarrow\bar{z})\right]\,,

(37)

and
\textrm{Li}_{2}(x)=\sum_{k\,=\,1}^{\infty}x^{k}/k^{2}
denotes the dilogarithm function. The variables
z_{ij}
and
\bar{z}_{ij}
parametrize the cross ratios associated with the four points

(38)

Note that this parametrization differs from that commonly used in the literature by a sign flip,

and

The null-polygon limit corresponds to

and
\bar{z}_{i,i+2}\rightarrow 0
, while the remaining

are held fixed. In this limit,

z_{ij}\approx\frac{1-V_{ij}}{U_{ij}}\rightarrow\infty\,,\qquad\bar{z}_{ij}\approx\frac{V_{ij}}{1-V_{ij}}\,.

(39)

In this appendix, we show that the one-loop contribution
\mathbb{M}^{\textrm{1-loop}}_{n}

5

\log\mathbb{M}^{\textrm{1-loop}}_{n}\approx-\sum_{i\,=\,1}^{n}(l_{i}-l^{\prime}_{i})(l_{i+1}-l^{\prime}_{i})+2A_{n}^{\textrm{BDS}}-\frac{5n}{2}\zeta_{2}\,,

(40)

where

To translate the results of
Bern et al. 2005
into our notations, we identify

(42)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{r\,=\,2}^{m-1}\textrm{Li}_{2}\,(1-V_{i-1,i+r})+\frac{\delta_{n,2m}}{2}\textrm{Li}_{2}\,(1-V_{i-1,i+m-1})\,,

\displaystyle=-\frac{2-\delta_{n,2m}}{4}\left(l_{i}^{(m)}-l_{i+m+1}^{(m)}\right)\left(l_{i+1}^{(m)}-l_{i+m}^{(m)}\right)\,.

(43)

To avoid lengthy formulas, we included the dependence on the parity of

Appendix B
Comparison with
J
factor of twist-two operators

In this appendix, we compare our results with the known expression for correlators of twist-two operators in the null-polygon limit. General arguments imply that, in this limit, the correlator factorizes into a null polygonal Wilson loop in the adjoint representation multiplied by a recoil factor
J^{\textrm{adj}}_{n}

Alday et al. 2011a
;
Alday and Bissi 2013
;
Bercini et al. 2021
;
Chen et al. 2025
. This factor captures the interaction between the state propagating along the Wilson-loop contour and the color flux tube it generates. It was first computed at two loops in
Alday et al. 2011a
. Its all-loop expression was derived in
Alday and Bissi 2013
for
n=4
using conformal-bootstrap techniques, and generalized to higher
n
-point functions in
Bercini et al. 2021
.

In terms of the cross-ratios
U_{i}
defined in (
21
), the recoil factor takes the form

J^{\textrm{adj}}_{n}=e^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Delta_{i,i+1}}\prod_{i\,=\,1}^{n}G\left(\frac{f}{4}\log{\U_i}-\frac{h}{2}\right)\,,

(58)

where
\Delta_{i,i+1}
is a diffusion operator acting on the variables
\log{U_i}
,

\Delta_{i,i+1}=-\frac{4}{f}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\log{\U_i}\,\partial\log{\U_{i+1}}}\,,

(59)

and

G(x)=e^{\gamma_{\textrm{E}}x}\Gamma(1+x)=\exp\left[\sum_{k\,=\,2}^{\infty}\frac{(-x)^{k}}{k}\zeta_{k}\right]\,.

(60)

Here,
\Gamma
denotes the Gamma function and
\gamma_{\textrm{E}}
the Euler–Mascheroni constant. Moreover,
f=\Gamma_{\textrm{cusp}}^{\textrm{adj}}=2\Gamma_{\textrm{cusp}}
is the cusp anomalous dimension in the adjoint representation, while
h=\mathcal{O}\left(g^{4}\right)
denotes the virtual anomalous dimension of twist-two operators, which is known to all loop orders from integrability
Freyhult and Zieme 2009
;
Fioravanti et al. 2009
.

The logarithm of this factor,
\log J^{\textrm{adj}}_{n}
, plays a role analogous to our conformal ansatz
\Delta\mathcal{Q}_{n}^{\textrm{min}}
. Both quantities depend on the cross ratios
U_{i}
defined in (
21
), which vanish in the null limit and parametrize the approach to the null polygon. Since the operators inserted at the cusps are different in the two setups, there is no reason to expect the corresponding expressions to coincide. In particular,
\log J^{\textrm{adj}}_{n}
is not a quadratic function of logarithms at higher loops. Nevertheless, at weak coupling the two quantities exhibit remarkably similar long-range contributions of the form
\log U_{i}\,\log U_{i+k-1}
. These terms appear at order
\mathcal{O}\left(g^{2k+2}\right)
in both cases and come with closely related coefficients.

To see this, we may set
h\to 0
in (
58
), since
h
affects only subleading logarithmic terms at a given loop order. We may also truncate the expansion (
60
) at quadratic order,

G(x)\longrightarrow\exp\left[\frac{x^{2}}{2}\zeta_{2}\right]\,,

(61)

since higher powers of
x
contribute to the long-range quadratic terms only through subleading powers of the coupling. The term proportional to
\log{\U_i}\log{\U_{i+k-1}}
in
\log{J^{\adj}_{n}}
then arise from the action of the chain of diffusion operators

\Delta_{i,i+1}\Delta_{i+1,i+2}\cdots\Delta_{i+k-2,i+k-1}

(62)

on the monomials

\left(\frac{f^{2}}{32}\zeta_{2}\right)^{k}\log^{2}{U_{i}}\log^{2}{U_{i+1}}\cdots\log^{2}{U_{i+k-1}}\subset\prod_{i\,=\,1}^{n}G\left(\frac{f}{4}\log{\U_i}-\frac{h}{2}\right)\,.

(63)

Hence, focusing on the quadratic terms, we may write

\displaystyle\log{J^{\adj}_{n}}\big|^{\textrm{Gaussian}}=-\frac{1}{2}d_{1}\sum_{i\,=\,1}^{n}\log^{2}{U_{i}}-\sum_{k\,=\,2}^{\infty}d_{k}\sum_{i\,=\,1}^{n}\log{\U_i}\log{\U_{i+k-1}}\,,

(64)

with

d_{k}\approx(-1)^{k}\frac{f^{k+1}}{4^{k+1}}\zeta_{2}^{k}\,,

(65)

to leading order at weak coupling,
f=\mathcal{O}\left(g^{2}\right)
. This is precisely the structure observed in (
33
) for
\Delta Q_{n}^{\textrm{min}}
. The fact that the same pattern emerges in two rather different setups suggests that the scaling of long-range contributions with the coupling may be a universal feature of the null limit of correlators of local operators. It would be interesting to understand whether this behavior admits a simple diagrammatic interpretation or a reformulation within the framework of
Alday et al. 2011a
;
Chen et al. 2025
.

Finally, it is worth noting that the coefficients in (
65
) and (
33
) match
exactly
at weak coupling upon formally replacing

f\rightarrow\Gamma_{\textrm{cusp}}\approx 4g^{2}\,,

(66)

that is, by using the cusp anomalous dimension appropriate to a fundamental Wilson loop rather than to a Wilson loop in the adjoint representation. Indeed, under this replacement one finds
d_{k}\approx\tilde{\gamma}_{k}
with

\tilde{\gamma}_{k}\approx\gamma_{k}\approx(-1)^{k}\zeta_{2}^{k}g^{2k+2}\,,

(67)

to leading order at weak coupling. This result follows from the definition of
\gamma_{k}
as the Fourier coefficient of
\Gamma_{\alpha}
(see eq. (
27
)) and from the fact that the
L
-loop contribution in
\Gamma_{\alpha}(g)=\sum_{L=1}^{\infty}\Gamma^{(L)}_{\alpha}g^{2L}
has the form

\Gamma^{(L)}_{\alpha}=4(-2\zeta_{2}\cos^{2}{\alpha})^{L-1}+\ldots\,,

(68)

where the dots denote terms with lower powers of
\cos^{2}{\alpha}
.
