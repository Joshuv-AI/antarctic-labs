Multi-level π \pi -junction in a proximitized Ge/SiGe quantum dot probed by an on-chip superconducting microwave resonator
Luigi Ruggiero 1, Vera Jo Weibel 1, Pauline Drexler 2, Carlo Ciaccia 1, Christian Olsen 1, Dominique Bougeard 2, Christian Schönenberger 3, 4, Andrea Hofmann 1, 3, *

Abstract
1 University of Basel, Klingelbergstrasse 82, 4056 Basel, Switzerland 2 University of Regensburg, Universitätsstraße 31, 93053 Regensburg, Germany 3 Swiss Nanoscience Institute, Klingelbergstrasse 82, 4056 Basel, Switzerland 4 YQuantum, Parkstrasse 1, 5234 Villigen, Switzerland * Corresponding author. Email: andrea.hofmann@unibas.ch Using on-chip microwave measurements, we investigate multilevel π \pi -junctions formed by proximitized quantum dot (QD) in a germanium (Ge) / silicon-germanium (SiGe) heterostructure. In the multilevel regime, where several QD orbitals contribute simultaneously to superconducting transport, the Josephson ground state is no longer determined solely by the occupation of a single orbital. By combining DC transport and microwave techniques, we identify the qualitative signatures of multilevel π \pi -junctions in both their gate-voltage dependence and microwave response. In particular, we observe combinations phase transitions that are sharp or smooth in gate voltage and which exhibit distinct inductive and dissipative signatures. Such multilevel Josephson transport has previously been observed primarily in exceptionally clean systems such as carbon nanotubes. Our results establish proximitized Ge as a platform for investigating hybrid superconductor/semiconductor physics and demonstrate the integration of gate-defined superconducting quantum devices with high-quality on-chip microwave resonators.



I
Introduction

Hybrid semiconductor–superconductor devices provide a versatile platform for exploring the interplay of superconductivity, Coulomb interaction, and quantum confinement.
QDs
coupled to superconducting reservoirs are of particular interest because they yield
Josephson junctions (JJs)
whose ground state can be controlled electrically
5
. These junctions have been studied extensively in the single-level limit
4
;
9
;
25
;
22
, where transport is governed by a single orbital, as well as in the limit of metallic islands involving a large number of orbitals
2
;
8
. Between these two extremes lies the multi-level regime, where the single-particle energy remains relevant but several orbitals simultaneously contribute to transport
29
;
32
. Experimental studies of this regime are scarce
31
;
7
and have so far relied on transport measurements, whereas microwave techniques provide additional access
3
to the inductive and dissipative response of the junction.

Planar
Ge
/
SiGe

quantum wells (QWs)
have recently emerged as a platform that combines gate-defined
QDs

16
;
24
;
13
;
17
, the superconducting proximity effect
12
;
30
;
33
;
1
, and superconducting microwave resonators
26
;
24
. Recent advances in
Ge
-based hybrid superconductor-semiconductor devices have enabled proximitized
QDs

21
;
10
, gate-tunable transmon qubits
27
;
20
and high-transparency
Andreev-Bound-States (ABSs)

15
;
18
. These developments now make it possible to study the phase-dependent transport through proximitized quantum dots within a planar semiconductor platform using combined
Direct-current (DC)
and
Radio-frequency (RF)
methods. While control over the superconducting phase
\varphi_{\mathrm{0}}
, which determines the phase minimizing the junction ground-state energy, has previously been demonstrated using

in nanowires
31
;
28
and carbon nanotubes
7
;
6

;
23
;

In a

-

-

;
31

7

The
JJ
, a weak link between two superconducting current leads, is a basic building block of a superconducting hybrid device. To lowest order, the supercurrent across a
JJ
follows a sinusoidal current-phase relation,
I(\phi)\propto\sin{(\varphi+ \subtxt{\varphi}{0})}
, where
\varphi
is phase difference across the superconducting leads. In general the
JJ
energy is minimized at a phase
\varphi_{\mathrm{0}}
=0,\pi
, if time reversal symmetry holds. In a
SC
-
QD
-
SC
type of
JJ
, where a superconducting current is carried by discrete
QD
levels, the phase offset is determined by the relative size of the competing interactions. While the Coulomb interaction, characterized by the charging energy
U
, enforces sequential tunneling through the
QD
, the superconducting correlations influenced by the induced gap
\Delta^{*}
and the tunnel coupling
\Gamma
to the leads, drives coherent transport of
CPs
across the junction. Low
QD
excitation energies
\delta
facilitate co-tunneling events.

When
\Delta^{*}\sim\Gamma\sim U<<\delta
, the
QD
ground state is either a singlet or a doublet
21
. Tunneling through a single
QD
level is favored, and the phase offset of the
SC
-
QD
-
SC
junction is determined by the spin multiplicity of the
QD
ground state. This is the single-level regime. On the other hand, when
\delta<U
, also co-tunneling processes to different orbitals strongly affect the transport, which puts the junction in a multi-level regime.

Figure 1
(b) shows the case of a single-level
\pi
-junction, where the
\pi
shift is mediated by spin-exchange processes during tunneling through a singly occupied
QD
level forming a spin-degenerate doublet ground state. The
CP
is transported from left to right across the
QD
by a
\ket{\uparrow}
tunneling to the right, while
\ket{\downarrow}
occupies the
QD
in a meta-stable state.
\ket{\downarrow}
recombines with
\ket{\uparrow}
in the right lead to form a
CP
while the state inside the
QD
is again filled with a
\ket{\uparrow}
from the left lead. On the other hand, if the
QD
is empty or doubly occupied, the ground state is a spin singlet, and the tunneling of the
CP
occurs without phase accumulation. In this regime, the
QD
occupation and the according ground state multiplicity directly determine the phase offset to take the values
0
or
\pi
, which result in different contribution to the total current as sketched in
Figure 1
(c). In contrast, a multi-level
\pi
-junction describes a regime where
\delta
is small, and permutation of the spins also occur due to high order co-tunneling processes, this time involving several
QD
orbitals, as illustrated in
Figure 1
(d). The multi-level process is not directly dependent on the parity of the charges in the
QD
and the probability of a
\pi
shift is non-zero for both odd and even occupation
29
. In this regime, the 0-
\pi
transition is either sharp or smooth. While a sharp transition denotes a change of occupation in the
QD
, the smooth transition purely depends on the involved orbitals. This is illustrated in
Figure 1
(e), where we sketch the
I_{\mathrm{c}}
behavior as a function of
QD
occupation for a situation with four spin-degenerate
QD
states with a small energy spacing
\delta
compared to
\Delta
and the thermal energy

29
. At charge
n
, two of these states are occupied and two are empty, determining an electron-hole symmetry point for this set of (nearly-)degenerate states. At higher or lower charge occupation, smooth transitions occur due to the weight with which the transport is electron-like or hole-like with respect to the given charge on the
QD
. In the simple case of one spin-degenerate state, the picture again simplifies to the single-level regime shown in
Figure 1
(c).

III
Discussion

Using a combination of DC and RF experiments, we have drawn a clear picture of 0-
\pi
transitions occurring in a
SC
-
QD
-
SC

JJ
in a planar heterostructure. In particular, we perform a detailed analysis of a multi-level
\pi
-junction, where 0-
\pi
-transitions not only occur due to a change in ground state parity, but also due to weight shifts of co-tunneling processes via low-energy excited
QD
states. The fingerprints of the smooth transitions and their change in slope are inherently different from the better known
\pi
-junction with pronounced even-odd alternations. The latter occur in small
QDs
where the single-particle level spacing is large, or in systems where different co-tunneling processes add incoherently and thus average out. In our experiment, we demonstrate the successful formation of a
QD
in our hybrid heterostructure, in which tunneling paths through excited states add phase-coherently.

Differences in the two types of transitions are visible also in the frequency shift and quality factor as a function of flux, insights that we obtained from the additional information encoded in the microwave signal. This demonstrates the potential of on-chip microwave technology for studying the properties of high quality
QD
on planar
Ge
/
SiGe

QWs
. Together with the large induced gap
\Delta^{*}
of about
240\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro eV}
, our hybrid heterostructure is well suited for studying individual Andreev bound states, crossed Andreev reflection and eventually emerging topological states
19
.
