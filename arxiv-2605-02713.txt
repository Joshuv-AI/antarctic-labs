Abstract
Whereas classical invariance principles for ergodic Markov chains address the situation in which the time horizon of observations is large, the quality of approximation is questionable when this is not the case anymore – even when starting the Markov chain in the invariant law. In this article, we prove quantitative and functional limit theorems for additive functionals along triangular arrays of stationary Gaussian Markov processes when the mixing time t mix t_{\text{mix}} scales sub-, super- and proportionately to the number of observations n n . Our major finding is a phase transition at t mix ≍ n t_{\text{mix}}\asymp n , together with the identification and interrelation properties of the emerging new limit processes at and before the mixing time.

Introduction and main result

Additive functionals
S_{n}(f)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}f(X_{k})
of stochastic processes
(X_{k})
constitute one of the most fundamental objects in probability theory. The literature on this topic is extensive and spans a wide range of probabilistic frameworks, including general dependency structures (
Durrett and Resnick, 1978
,
Davis, 1983
,
Dehling et al., 1986
), self-similar Gaussian processes (
Hong et al., 2024
) and predominantly Markov processes; see
Bhattacharya and Lee, 1988
,

Jain, 1990
,
Pacheco and Prabhu, 1996
,
Ney and de Acosta, 1998
,
Chen, 1999
,
Maxwell and Woodroofe, 2000
,
Jara et al., 2009
for the discrete case and
Getoor and Sharpe, 1973
,
Maisonneuve, 1975
,
Nevison, 1976
,
Dynkin, 1977
,
Glover, 1980
,
Glover, 1981a
,

,
Kaspi, 1988
,
Dynkin, 1991
,
Marcus and Rosen, 1996
,
Chen et al., 2008
,
Kuwae, 2010
,
Chen et al., 2019
,
Deuschel et al., 2021
for time-continuous processes and the corresponding integral functional.

Classical (functional) limit theorems for positive recurrent Markov chains describe the behavior of the (sequential) additive functional when the time horizon of observations is large. Indeed, if the Markov chain is stationary ergodic and
f
is square-integrable with respect to the invariant measure, the sequential limit is a Brownian motion, under additional assumptions such as the existence of a solution to the Poisson equation (
Bhattacharya and Lee, 1988
) or under conditions on the moments of
f
and the growth of the conditional mean
\mathbb{E}[S_{n}(f)|X_{0}]
(
Maxwell and Woodroofe, 2000
), the latter within a logarithmic term of being necessary. However, if the time horizon is not large as often present in physical applications, the distributional approximation by the Brownian motion seems to be inadequate, even in case of ergodic Markov chains starting in their invariant law.

To approach the small-sample effect in an asymptotic framework, we study the additive functional along a triangular array of stationary processes with diverging mixing times.
For technical convenience, we carry out our analysis in the context of discrete-time stationary Gaussian Markov processes, which reduces to the fundamental example of autoregressive processes (cf. Lemma
A.1
). On the one hand, stationary autoregressive processes which are not constant in time are geometrically ergodic and represent the classical setup for near-unit-root analysis (cf.
Chan and Wei, 1987
,
Cox, 1991
,
Cox and Llatas, 1991
,
Bhattacharyya et al., 1997
, and
Buchmann and Chan, 2007
), while on the other hand, the theory of Gaussian processes provides well-developed mathematical tools which allow for a profound analysis, leaving technical issues aside.
To this end, let
X_{1,n},\dots,X_{n,n}
be consecutive observations of a stationary time series satisfying the recursion

\displaystyle X_{j,n}=\alpha_{n}X_{j-1,n}+\sigma_{n}\varepsilon_{j}\quad\text{for }j\in\mathbb{N},

(1.1)

where
(\varepsilon_{j})
are iid
\mathcal{N}(0,1)

\pi_{n}=\mathcal{N}\big(0,\sigma_{n}^{2}/(1-\alpha_{n}^{2})\big)
denotes its invariant distribution and

the local mixing time in total variation distance

\displaystyle t_{\text{mix},n}(\varepsilon,\delta):=\inf\bigg\{m\geq 0:\sup_{|x|\leq k_{n}(\delta)}d_{TV}\Big(\mathcal{L}_{x}(X_{m,n}),\pi_{n}\Big)\leq\varepsilon\bigg\}\ \text{ for }\varepsilon,\delta\in(0,1),

(1.2)

1.1

\alpha_{n}

is related to
\alpha_{n}

•

if
\sigma_{n}^{2}=\sigma^{2}
is constant while
\alpha_{n}\nearrow 1

•

If

As in the classical literature for near-unit-root analysis of AR(1) processes, we parametrize

Note that for all ranges of

Beyond the phase transition.
From the phase transition at
\beta=1
on, the parameter
\sigma_{n}^{2}
starts to matter. This is getting especially apparent if the additive functional is not evaluated at a fixed polynomial
\sum_{q=m}^{p}c_{q}H_{q}
as in Figure
1
but at
f_{n}=\sum_{q=m}^{p}c_{q}\delta_{n}^{q}H_{q}(\cdot/\delta_{n})
for some family
(c_{q})
and
\delta_{n}^{2}=\sigma_{n}^{2}/(1-\alpha_{n}^{2})
, i.e.
\mathcal{N}(0,\delta_{n}^{2})=\pi_{n}
. As Theorems
3.5
,
4.4
, and
5.3
reveal, for
\sigma_{n}^{2}\gg(1-\alpha_{n}^{2})
, the asymptotics is then governed by the highest Hermite coefficient (order-driven regime). For
\sigma_{n}^{2}=\sigma^{2}(1-\alpha_{n}^{2})
, all Hermite coefficients contribute to the limit. If
\sigma_{n}^{2}\ll(1-\alpha_{n}^{2})
, a classical pattern re-emerges for this choice of
f_{n}
, with the asymptotics solely determined by the Hermite rank.
Observe that in both two last regimes, the recursion (
1.1
) approaches the constant relation
X_{t}=X_{t-1}
, indicating (degenerate) long-range dependence, but the Hermite-rank as a key quantity arises exclusively in the latter one. However, it evolves here to a degenerate process from the phase transition on, bypassing the traditional Dobrushin-Major-Taqqu long-memory limit regime.

Preview of the proof

In view of the targeted variation bounds, the proof is conducted as follows.

•

\beta<1
:
The proof relies on the Wiener chaos decomposition of the additive functional, expressed in terms of multiple Wiener-Itô integrals together with a quantitative version of the Fourth-Moment theorem (
Nualart and Peccati, 2005
,
Nourdin et al., 2010
). The contractions in the remainder are explicitly evaluated and bounded by means of symmetry considerations, a log-convexity argument and Chebychev’s sum inequality.

•

\beta=1
:
For the triangular array of AR(1)-observations
(X_{1,n},\ldots,X_{n,n}),\,n\in\mathbb{N}
, we exploit the spectral representation, building upon the seminal ideas of
Dobrushin and Major, 1979
originally developed in the context of long-range dependent time series. Using the self-similarity of complex Brownian motion within the multiple Wiener-Itô representation of
\hat{S}_{n,t}(H_{q})
and the isometry, the result is traced back to proving convergence of the respective kernels in
L^{2}
. Here, a sharp bound is needed, which we build especially on Jordan’s inequality. The multivariate extension over different chaos orders employs the result of
Bai and Taqqu, 2013
.

•

\beta>1
:
The proof relies on convergence of the finite-dimensional marginals in
L^{2}
, taking advantage of the recursive structure and orthogonality relations inherent to the Wiener chaos decomposition.

Limit process for
\beta=1

The processes
W_{q,\gamma}
in the limit for
\beta=1
are so-called tempered Hermite processes as introduced in
Sabzikar, 2015
, motivated by modeling of turbulence. As such, they are continuous with stationary increments, not self-similar and possess the equivalent time domain representation as multiple Wiener-Itô integral

\displaystyle W_{q,\gamma}(t)=K_{q}^{(\gamma)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{q}}^{{}^{\prime}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\prod_{r=1}^{q}e^{-\gamma(s-x_{r})}\,\mathbf{1}_{\{x_{r}\leq s\}}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)\mathrm{d}B(x_{1})\cdots\mathrm{d}B(x_{q}),\quad t\in[0,1],

(1.3)

with prime indicating the integration off the diagonals (i.e.
x_{i}=x_{j}
,
i\not=j
).
Interestingly, the limit processes for
\beta=1
depend continuously on
\gamma
with respect to the topology of weak convergence, and reveal the limiting processes for
\beta<1
(Brownian motion) and
\beta>1
as
\gamma\to\infty
and
\gamma\to 0
, respectively (Theorem
6.1
). Note that the previous restriction
\gamma\in(0,1)
was only made to guarantee that (
1.1
) is stationary for
every

n\in\mathbb{N}
. For the component
W_{q,\gamma}
, we also provide explicit bounds on the total variation distance of the marginals.

Theorem 1.1

(Total variation bound)
.

For any
q\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a positive constant

such that the following bounds are satisfied.

\displaystyle(i)

\displaystyle\quad d_{TV}\left(\mathcal{L}\big(W_{q,\gamma}(1)\big),\mathcal{N}(0,1)\right)\leq C(q)\gamma^{-1/2}(1+a_{q}(\gamma)),\qquad\hskip 28.45274pt\gamma\geq 1,

\displaystyle(ii)

\displaystyle\quad d_{TV}\left(\mathcal{L}\big(W_{q,\gamma}(1)\big),\mathcal{L}\left(\frac{H_{q}(Z)}{\sqrt{q!}}\right)\right)\leq C(q)\gamma^{1/(4q)}(1+b_{q}(\gamma)),

where
a_{q}(\gamma)\to 0
as
\gamma\to\infty
and
b_{q}(\gamma)\to 0
as
\gamma\to 0
for every
q\in\mathbb{N}
.

The boundary case
\sigma_{n}^{2}=1-\alpha_{n}^{2}

If
\sigma_{n}^{2}=1-\alpha_{n}^{2}
in the recursion (
1.1
), the invariant distribution
\pi_{n}
does not depend on
n
and equals

. In this case, the results displayed in Figure

even apply to any

Finally, we also provide explicit bounds on the total variation distance between the distribution of

Theorem 1.2

(Quantitative limit theorems)
.

Let
\sigma_{n}^{2}=1-a_{n}^{2}
with
\alpha_{n}=\left(1-\frac{\gamma}{n^{\beta}}\right)
for some constants
\gamma\in(0,1)
. For any

\displaystyle(i)

\displaystyle\quad d_{TV}\left(\mathcal{L}\big(\hat{S}_{n,1}(H_{q})\big),\mathcal{L}(B(1))\right)\leq C(q)\gamma^{-\frac{1}{2}}n^{\frac{\beta-1}{2}}(1+o_{n}(1)),

\displaystyle\text{for any }\beta\in(0,1);

\displaystyle(ii)

\displaystyle\quad d_{TV}\left(\mathcal{L}\big(\hat{S}_{n,1}(H_{q})\big),\mathcal{L}(W_{q,\gamma}(1)\big)\right)\leq f_{q}(\gamma)\,n^{-\frac{1}{9q}}(1+o_{n}(1)),

\displaystyle(iii)

\displaystyle\quad d_{TV}\left(\mathcal{L}\big(\hat{S}_{n,1}(H_{q})\big),\mathcal{L}\Big(\frac{H_{q}\left(Z\right)}{\sqrt{q!}}\Big)\right)\leq C(q)\gamma^{\frac{1}{4q}}n^{\frac{1-\beta}{4q}}(1+o_{n}(1)),

2.1
Asymptotic variances, covariances and leading terms of
\hat{S}_{n,t}(f)

In this subsection, we derive explicit expressions for the asymptotic variance of the additive functional for a polynomial
f
in case
\sigma_{n}^{2}\gg(1-\alpha_{n}^{2})
and
\sigma_{n}^{2}\ll(1-\alpha_{n}^{2})

if

In case (i), the multiplication theorem for Hermite polynomials yields

\displaystyle\left(\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}X_{1,n}}\right)^{k-2l}\left(\mathrm{Var}X_{1,n}-1\right)^{l}\asymp\left(\mathrm{Var}X_{1,n}\right)^{k/2}(1+o_{n}(1)).

and
\kappa_{1}\hat{S}_{n,t_{1}}(H_{q})+\ldots+\kappa_{d}\hat{S}_{n,t_{d}}(H_{q})
remains a multiple Wiener-Itô integral of order
q
,

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\kappa_{j}\hat{S}_{n,t_{j}}(H_{m}),\ldots,\sum_{j=1}^{d}\kappa_{j}\hat{S}_{n,t_{j}}(H_{p})\right)\longrightarrow_{\mathcal{D}}\mathcal{N}\big(0,V\cdot\mathrm{I}_{p-m+1}\big)

as
n\to\infty
by Theorem 1 of
Peccati and Tudor, 2005
, where
\mathrm{I}_{k}
denotes the

identity matrix. By the continuous-mapping theorem again, the latter implies that

\sum_{j=1}^{d}\kappa_{j}\sum_{q=m}^{p}b_{q}\hat{S}_{n,t_{j}}(H_{q})\longrightarrow_{\mathcal{D}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\kappa_{j}\sum_{q=m}^{p}b_{q}B_{q}(t_{j})

as
n\to\infty
, with independent Brownian motions

. Again by the Cramér-Wold device,

\displaystyle\Bigg(\,\sum_{q=m}^{p}b_{q}\hat{S}_{n,t_{1}}(H_{q}),\ldots,\sum_{q=m}^{p}b_{q}\hat{S}_{n,t_{d}}(H_{q})\Bigg)\longrightarrow_{\mathcal{D}}\Bigg(\sum_{q=m}^{p}b_{q}B_{q}(t_{1}),\ldots,\sum_{q=m}^{p}b_{q}B_{q}(t_{d})\Bigg)

as
n\to\infty
. Together with (
3.1
) and Lemma
2.5
,

\displaystyle\left(\hat{S}_{n,t}(f)\right)_{t\in[0,1]}

\displaystyle=\left(\,\sum_{q=m}^{p}w_{n,q}\hat{S}_{n,t}(H_{q})\right)_{t\in[0,1]}\longrightarrow_{\mathcal{D}}\left(\,\sum_{q=m}^{p}b_{q}B_{q}(t)\right)_{t\in[0,1]}\overset{\mathcal{D}}{=}\big(B(t)\big)_{t\in[0,1]}

in
D[0,1]
as
n\to\infty
, as
\sum_{q=m}^{p}b_{q}^{2}=1
.

For cases (i) and (iii), we make first use of Lemma
2.1
to extract the leading components. As these are polynomials in
Z_{j,n}
, the proof is analogous to the one for (ii).

Step 3:
f\in L^{2}\left(\mathcal{N}(0,1)\right)

First note that as a consequence of Step 2, there exists a sequence

with

such that

where

denotes the dual bounded Lipschitz metric, metrizing weak convergence. Therefore it is sufficient to prove

\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\big|\hat{S}_{n,t}\big(f-f^{(p_{n})}\big)\big|\longrightarrow_{\mathbb{P}}0
.
To this aim, let

T_{i,n}:=\sum_{q=p_{n}+1}^{\infty}c_{q}H_{q}(Z_{i,n});\qquad S_{j,n}:=\sum_{i=1}^{j}T_{i,n};\qquad S_{l}^{*(n)}=\max_{1\leq j\leq l}\big|S_{j,n}\big|.

Observe that
T_{i,n}
is stationary in
i
for fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbb{E}\big(T_{i,n}\big)^{2}=\sum_{q=p_{n}+1}^{\infty}c_{q}^{2}q!<\infty
by Parseval’s identity. Consider the filtration

\big(\mathcal{F}_{i}\big)_{i\in\{0,\ldots,n\}}\text{ with }\mathcal{F}_{i}:=\sigma\big(Z_{0,n},\varepsilon_{k}:k\leq i\big).

Clearly
T_{i,n}\in\mathcal{F}_{i}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then Corollary 1 of
Peligrad et al., 2007
is applicable with
p=2
(cf. also its first version in
Peligrad and Utev, 2005
), and we obtain together with Lemma
5.1
that
\|S_{l}^{*(n)}\|_{2}
is bounded by

\displaystyle C_{2}^{1/2}l^{1/2}\left[\|T_{i,n}\|_{2}+240\sum_{k=1}^{l}k^{-1/2}\|\mathbb{E}\big[T_{k,n}|\mathcal{F}_{0,n}\big]\|_{2}\right]

\displaystyle\,=C_{2}^{1/2}l^{1/2}\left[\sqrt{\hskip-1.42262pt\sum_{q=p_{n}+1}^{\infty}\hskip-5.69054ptc_{q}^{2}q!}+240\sum_{k=1}^{l}k^{-1/2}\bigg\Arrowvert\hskip-1.42262pt\sum_{q=p_{n}+1}^{\infty}\hskip-5.69054ptc_{q}\mathbb{E}\big[\alpha_{n}^{qk}H_{q}(Z_{0,n})+R_{q}(Z_{k,n})|\mathcal{F}_{0,n}\big]\bigg\Arrowvert_{2}\right]

\displaystyle\,\leq C_{2}^{1/2}l^{1/2}\left[\sqrt{\sum_{q=p_{n}+1}^{\infty}c_{q}^{2}q!}+240\,\sqrt{\sum_{q=p_{n}+1}^{\infty}c_{q}^{2}q!}\sum_{k=1}^{l}k^{-1/2}\alpha_{n}^{k}\right],

where we have used in the last inequality that
\mathbb{E}\big[R_{q}(Z_{k,n})|\mathcal{F}_{0,n}\big]=0
. Hence, with

\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\left|\hat{S}_{n,t}\big(f-f^{(p_{n})}\big)\right|=\frac{S_{n}^{*(n)}}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}S_{n,1}(f)}},

Chebychev’s inequality reveals

\mathbb{P}\bigg(\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\left|\hat{S}_{n,t}\big(f-f^{(p_{n})}\big)\right|>\lambda\bigg)\leq\frac{C_{2}\,n}{\mathrm{Var}S_{n,1}(f)\,\lambda^{2}}\sum_{q=p_{n}+1}^{\infty}c_{q}^{2}q!\left[1+240\,\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{-1/2}\alpha_{n}^{k}\right]^{2}

for every
\lambda>0
, which converges to
0
as
n\to\infty
by the subsequent Lemma
3.4
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

For any
\beta>0
,

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\Big(1-\frac{\gamma}{n^{\beta}}\Big)^{k}\leq\begin{cases}1+\sqrt{\frac{n^{\beta}\pi}{\gamma}}&\text{ if }\beta<1,\\
1+2\sqrt{n}&\text{ if }\beta\geq 1.\end{cases}

Proof.

As
k^{-1/2}\big(1-\gamma/n^{\beta}\big)^{k}
is monotone decreasing in
k
, the integral criterium leads to

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\Big(1-\frac{\gamma}{n^{\beta}}\Big)^{k}\leq 1-\frac{\gamma}{n^{\beta}}+\int_{1}^{n}\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}\Big(1-\frac{\gamma}{n^{\beta}}\Big)^{x}\mathrm{d}x.

(3.2)

If
\beta<1
, observe that
1-y\leq e^{-y}
for
y\in[0,1]
and substituting
z:=x\,(\gamma/n^{\beta})
leads to

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\Big(1-\frac{\gamma}{n^{\beta}}\Big)^{k}\leq 1+\sqrt{\frac{n^{\beta}}{\gamma}}\int_{\gamma/n^{\beta}}^{\gamma n^{1-\beta}}z^{-1/2}e^{-z}\mathrm{d}z.

Extending the integration boundaries and recalling that
\int_{0}^{\infty}z^{-1/2}e^{-z}\mathrm{d}z=\Gamma(1/2)=\sqrt{\pi}
completes the proof for
\beta\in(0,1)
. For
\beta\geq 1
, the right-hand side in (
3.2
) is bounded by
1+2\sqrt{n}
.
∎

Remark 4.1
.

Note that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{q}}\left|\frac{e^{i\left(\sum_{r=1}^{q}\lambda_{r}\right)t}-1}{i\left(\sum_{r=1}^{q}\lambda_{r}\right)}\prod_{r=1}^{q}\frac{1}{i\lambda_{r}+\gamma}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\lambda_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}\lambda_{q}\leq t^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{\gamma}\right)^{q}<\infty\qquad\text{for all $t\in[0,1]$}

and therefore, by the isometry property of multiple Wiener-Itô integrals, the limit process in Figure
1
and the subsequent statements is well-defined.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
(ii).

We exploit the spectral representation of
Z_{1,n},\ldots,Z_{n,n}
together with the expression of
S_{n,t}(H_{q})
as a multiple Wiener-Itô integral. Specifically,

\displaystyle H_{q}\left(Z_{j,n}\right)=(\sigma_{n}^{2})^{q/2}\int_{[-\pi,\pi]^{q}}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}e^{ij\left(\sum_{r=1}^{q}\lambda_{r}\right)}\prod_{r=1}^{q}\phi^{(n)}(\lambda_{r})\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{B}_{0}(\lambda_{1})\ldots\mathrm{d}\tilde{B}_{0}(\lambda_{q})

(4.1)

for
j=1,\ldots,n
, where
\tilde{B}_{0}(-\lambda)=\overline{\tilde{B}_{0}(-\lambda)}
,
\tilde{B}_{0}
restricted to
\mathbb{R}_{+}
is some complex Brownian motion, and

\displaystyle\phi^{(n)}(\lambda):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{\gamma}{n}\right)^{k}e^{-ik\lambda}\right).

(4.2)

We now consider the one-dimensional marginals. Summing over
j
reveals for any
t\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle S_{n,t}(H_{q})

\displaystyle=(\sigma_{n}^{2})^{q/2}\int_{[-\pi,\pi]^{q}}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}\hskip-5.69054pt\frac{e^{i\left(\sum_{r=1}^{q}\lambda_{r}\right)\left(\lfloor(n-1)t\rfloor+1\right)}-1}{e^{i\left(\sum_{r=1}^{q}\lambda_{r}\right)}-1}\prod_{r=1}^{q}\phi^{(n)}(\lambda_{r})\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{B}_{0}(\lambda_{1})\ldots\mathrm{d}\tilde{B}_{0}(\lambda_{q}).

Exploiting the self-similarity
n^{-1/2}\tilde{B}_{0}(nA)\overset{\mathcal{D}}{=}\tilde{B}_{0}(A)
for each set
A\in\mathcal{B}[-\pi,\pi]
, we find

\displaystyle\big(S_{n,t}(H_{q})\big)_{t\in[0,1]}

\displaystyle\overset{{\mathcal{D}}}{=}\bigg(n^{1+\frac{q}{2}}(\sigma_{n}^{2})^{q/2}\int_{[-n\pi,n\pi]^{q}}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}\psi_{n}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{q},t)\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{B}_{0}\left(\lambda_{1}\right)\ldots\mathrm{d}\tilde{B}_{0}\left(\lambda_{q}\right)\bigg)_{t\in[0,1]}

with

\displaystyle\psi_{n}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{q},t):=\frac{e^{i\left(\sum_{r=1}^{q}\lambda_{r}/n\right)\left(\lfloor(n-1)t\rfloor+1\right)}-1}{n\left(e^{i\left(\sum_{r=1}^{q}\lambda_{r}/n\right)}-1\right)}\frac{1}{n^{q}}\prod_{r=1}^{q}\phi^{(n)}\left(\frac{\lambda_{r}}{n}\right).

(4.3)

Dividing by
{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}{S}_{n,1}(H_{q})}}
yields

\displaystyle\hat{S}_{n,1}(H_{q})

\displaystyle\overset{{\mathcal{D}}}{=}K_{q,n}^{(\gamma)}\int_{[-n\pi,n\pi]^{q}}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}\psi_{n}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{q},1)\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{B}_{0}\left(\lambda_{1}\right)\ldots\mathrm{d}\tilde{B}_{0}\left(\lambda_{q}\right),

where further reducing the fraction and letting
n\to\infty
reveals

\displaystyle K_{q,n}^{(\gamma)}:=\frac{n^{1+\frac{q}{2}}(\sigma_{n}^{2})^{q/2}}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}{S}_{n,1}(H_{q})}}\longrightarrow\sqrt{\frac{2^{q-1}q^{2}\gamma^{q+2}}{q!\,\left(\exp(-\gamma q)-1+\gamma q\right)}}=:K_{q}^{(\gamma)}

(4.4)

and more precisely
\big(K_{q,n}^{(\gamma)}-K_{q}^{(\gamma)}\big)^{2}\leq C_{\gamma,q}^{2}n^{-2}
for a constant
C_{\gamma,q}>0
.
Next, we evaluate
\mathbb{E}\big(\hat{S}_{n,1}(H_{q})-W_{n,q}(1)\big)^{2}
, where

\displaystyle W_{n,q}(1)=K_{q,n}^{(\gamma)}\int_{[-n\pi,n\pi]^{q}}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}\psi(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{q},1)\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{B}_{0}\left(\lambda_{1}\right)\ldots\mathrm{d}\tilde{B}_{0}\left(\lambda_{q}\right)

(4.5)

with

\displaystyle\psi(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{q},1):=\begin{cases}\frac{e^{i\left(\sum_{r=1}^{q}\lambda_{r}\right)}-1}{i\sum_{r=1}^{q}\lambda_{r}}\frac{1}{\left(2\pi\right)^{q/2}}\prod_{r=1}^{q}\frac{1}{i\lambda_{r}+\gamma}&\text{ if }\sum_{r=1}^{q}\lambda_{r}\neq 0;\\
\,\frac{1}{\left(2\pi\right)^{q/2}}\prod_{r=1}^{q}\frac{1}{i\lambda_{r}+\gamma}&\text{ if }\sum_{r=1}^{q}\lambda_{r}=0.\end{cases}

(4.6)

Using the isometry for multiple Wiener-Itô integrals (equation (5.5.62) in
Peccati and Taqqu, 2011
), we obtain for arbitrary
M\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}

\mathbb{E}\big(\hat{S}_{n,1}(H_{q})-W_{n,q}(1)\big)^{2}=\big(K_{q,n}^{(\gamma)}\big)^{2}\big(I_{M,n}+I_{M,n}^{c}\big),

where

\displaystyle I_{M,n}

\displaystyle=\int_{[-M,M]^{q}}\left|\psi_{n}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{q},1)-\psi(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{q},1)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\lambda_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}\lambda_{q}\qquad\text{and}

\displaystyle I_{M,n}^{c}

\displaystyle=\int_{[-n\pi,n\pi]^{q}\setminus{[-M,M]^{q}}}\left|\psi_{n}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{q},1)-\psi(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{q},1)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\lambda_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}\lambda_{q}.

Note that
\psi_{n}\to\psi
pointwise in
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{q}
as
n\to\infty
. As furthermore
|\psi_{n}|,|\psi|\leq\gamma^{-q}
, which can be deduced by means of the inequality
|1-ce^{-ix}|\geq 1-c
, for
c\in[0,1]
,
I_{M,n}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, by the theorem of dominated convergence. More specifically, Taylor expansions in numerator and denominator of the components of
\psi_{n}
and
\psi
reveal the existence of a constant
C_{\gamma,q}>0
such that

\displaystyle I_{M,n}\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}\frac{q^{2}}{2^{q+2}}\gamma^{2-q}+C_{\gamma,q}\bigg(\frac{\pi^{q}}{\gamma^{q}}\frac{(1+M^{4})^{2}}{n^{4}}+\frac{1+M^{4}}{n^{3}}\bigg)

(4.7)

as long as
n\geq M
and
n\geq 1+M
.

As concerns
I_{M,n}^{c}
, we first identify a bound on
\left|\psi_{n}\right|^{2}
. Note that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{e^{i\left(\sum_{r=1}^{q}\lambda_{r}/n\right)n}-1}{n\big(e^{i\left(\sum_{r=1}^{q}\lambda_{r}/n\right)}-1\big)}\right|=\left|\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{e^{ij\left(\sum_{r=1}^{q}\lambda_{r}/n\right)}}{n}\right|\leq 1.

For the second factor in (
4.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{n^{q}}\prod_{r=1}^{q}\phi^{(n)}\left(\frac{\lambda_{r}}{n}\right)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2q}}\prod_{r=1}^{q}\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left[\left(1-\frac{\gamma}{n}\right)e^{-i\lambda/n}\right]^{k}\right)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2q}}\prod_{r=1}^{q}\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\frac{1}{1-2(1-\gamma/n)\cos(\lambda/n)+(1-\gamma/n)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n^{2q}}\prod_{r=1}^{q}\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\frac{1}{1-2(1-\gamma/n)\left(1-\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}\left(\frac{\lambda}{n}\right)^{2}\right)+(1-\gamma/n)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2q}}\prod_{r=1}^{q}\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\frac{1}{(\gamma/n)^{2}+2(1-\gamma/n)\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}\big(\frac{\lambda}{n}\big)^{2}}\right),

where we we have used the identity
1-\cos(x)=2\sin(x/2)^{2}
together with
Jordan’s inequality
\sin^{2}(x/2)\geq x^{2}/\pi^{2}
for
x\in[-\pi,\pi]
in the inequality. The last expression is bounded by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{q}}\prod_{r=1}^{q}\bigg(\gamma^{2}+\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}\lambda_{r}^{2}\bigg)^{-1}

(4.8)

for
n\geq 2\gamma
, which delivers the required uniform bound on
\arrowvert\psi_{n}\arrowvert^{2}
. Analogously,

\arrowvert\psi\arrowvert^{2}\leq\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{q}}\prod_{r=1}^{q}\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}+\lambda_{r}^{2}}.

Now observe that
\mathbb{R}^{q}\setminus[-M,M]^{q}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{q}\{\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{q})\in\mathbb{R}^{q}:|\lambda_{j}|>M\}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{q}A_{j}
. Therefore, with
|\psi_{n}(\lambda,1)-\psi(\lambda,1)|^{2}\leq 2|\psi_{n}(\lambda,1)|^{2}+2|\psi(\lambda,1)|^{2}
,

\displaystyle I_{M,n}^{c}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\bigcup_{j=1}^{q}A_{j}}\frac{2}{(2\pi)^{q}}\Bigg[\prod_{r=1}^{q}\bigg(\gamma^{2}+\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}\lambda_{r}^{2}\bigg)^{-1}+\prod_{r=1}^{q}\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}+\lambda_{r}^{2}}\Bigg]\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{(2\pi)^{q}}\sum_{j=1}^{q}\left[\int_{A_{j}}\prod_{r=1}^{q}\bigg(\gamma^{2}+\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}\lambda_{r}^{2}\bigg)^{-1}\mathrm{d}\lambda+\int_{A_{j}}\prod_{r=1}^{q}\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}+\lambda_{r}^{2}}\mathrm{d}\lambda\right].

For fixed

Fubini’s theorem and the inequality
(\gamma^{2}+(2/\pi^{2})x^{2})^{-1}\leq((2/\pi^{2})x^{2})^{-1}
provide

\displaystyle\int_{A_{j}}\prod_{r=1}^{q}\bigg(\gamma^{2}+\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}\lambda_{r}^{2}\bigg)^{-1}\mathrm{d}\lambda\leq\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}\left(\int_{|x|>M}\frac{1}{x^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\right)\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{\gamma\sqrt{2}}\right)^{q-1}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{M}\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{\gamma\sqrt{2}}\right)^{q-1}.

A similar bound can be determined for the second integral, which gives

\int_{A_{j}}\prod_{r=1}^{q}\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}+\lambda_{r}^{2}}\mathrm{d}\lambda\leq\frac{2}{M}\left(\frac{\pi}{\gamma}\right)^{q-1}.

As a consequence,

\displaystyle I_{M,n}^{c}\leq C_{q}\gamma^{-(q-1)}M^{-1}\quad\text{ for $n\geq 2\gamma$ and some constant $C_{q}>0$}.

(4.9)

The upper bound for
\mathbb{E}\big(W_{n,q}(1)-W_{q,\gamma}(1)\big)^{2}
, namely

2\big(K_{q,n}^{(\gamma)}-K_{q}^{(\gamma)}\big)^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{\gamma}\right)^{q}+2\big(K_{q}^{(\gamma)}\big)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{q}\setminus[-n\pi,n\pi]^{q}}\left|\psi(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{q},1)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\lambda_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}\lambda_{q}\leq C_{\gamma,q}n^{-1},

.

4.7

\displaystyle(g_{t,\gamma}\otimes_{r}g_{t,\gamma})(\bm{x},\bm{y})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{r}}\prod_{i=1}^{r}h_{\gamma,s}(z_{i})h_{\gamma,u}(z_{i})\mathrm{d}\bm{z}\right]\left(\prod_{j=1}^{q-r}h_{\gamma,s}(x_{j})\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{q-r}h_{\gamma,u}(y_{j})\right)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}\big(\rho_{\gamma}(s,u)\big)^{r}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{q-r}h_{\gamma,s}(x_{j})\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{q-r}h_{\gamma,u}(y_{j})\right)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}u.

Now we calculate its
L^{2}
-norm. Integrating the square of last expression over

(\bm{x},\bm{y})
, and using again Fubini’s theorem leads to

\displaystyle\|g_{t,\gamma}\otimes_{r}g_{t,\gamma}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2q-2r})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,t]^{4}}\big(\rho_{\gamma}(s,u)\big)^{r}\big(\rho_{\gamma}(s^{\prime},u^{\prime})\big)^{r}\big(\rho_{\gamma}(s,s^{\prime})\big)^{q-r}\big(\rho_{\gamma}(u,u^{\prime})\big)^{q-r}\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}\mathrm{d}u^{\prime}

\displaystyle=(2\gamma)^{-2q}\int_{[0,t]^{4}}e^{-\gamma\left(r|s-u|+r|s^{\prime}-u^{\prime}|+(q-r)|s-s^{\prime}|+(q-r)|u-u^{\prime}|\right)}\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}\mathrm{d}u^{\prime}.

We now determine a bound for
\|g_{t,\gamma}\otimes_{r}g_{t,\gamma}\|^{2}
. Set

\displaystyle I_{q,r}(\gamma;t)

\displaystyle:=\int_{[0,t]^{4}}e^{-\gamma\left(r|s-u|+r|s^{\prime}-u^{\prime}|+(q-r)|s-s^{\prime}|+(q-r)|u-u^{\prime}|\right)}\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}\mathrm{d}u^{\prime}

and consider
J(s,s^{\prime};\gamma):=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\gamma\left(r|s-u|+(q-r)|u-u^{\prime}|+r|s^{\prime}-u^{\prime}|\right)}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}u^{\prime}
, such that
I_{q,r}(\gamma;t)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\gamma(q-r)|s-s^{\prime}|}J(s,s^{\prime};\gamma)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}
. For each fixed
s,s^{\prime}
, bound the inner
u^{\prime}
-integral with

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\gamma r|s^{\prime}-u^{\prime}|}e^{-\gamma(q-r)|u-u^{\prime}|}\mathrm{d}u^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\gamma r|s^{\prime}-u^{\prime}|}e^{-\gamma(q-r)|u-u^{\prime}|}\mathrm{d}u^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-2\gamma r|y|}\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-2\gamma(q-r)|y|}\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\gamma\sqrt{r(q-r)}}.

Thus,

\displaystyle J(s,s^{\prime};\gamma)\leq\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\gamma r|s-u|}\frac{1}{\gamma\sqrt{r(q-r)}}\mathrm{d}u\leq\frac{1}{\gamma\sqrt{r(q-r)}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\gamma r|y|}\mathrm{d}y=\frac{2}{\gamma^{2}r^{3/2}\sqrt{q-r}}.

So we can provide the following estimate for all
\gamma\geq 1
:

\displaystyle I_{q,r}(\gamma;t)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\gamma^{2}r^{3/2}\sqrt{q-r}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\gamma(q-r)|s-s^{\prime}|}\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\gamma^{2}r^{3/2}\sqrt{q-r}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\gamma(q-r)|y|}\mathrm{d}y=\frac{C_{q,r,t}}{\gamma^{3}}

with
C_{q,r,t}=4(r(q-r))^{-3/2}
.
Next, we can bound
\|f_{t,\gamma}\otimes_{r}f_{t,\gamma}\|^{2}\leq\big(K_{q}^{(\gamma)}\big)^{4}\frac{C_{q,r,t}}{2^{2q}}\gamma^{-(2q+3)}
. As shown in Section
4
,

\displaystyle K_{q}^{(\gamma)}:=\sqrt{\frac{q^{2}\gamma^{q+2}}{q!\,2^{1-q}\left(\exp(-\gamma q)-1+\gamma q\right)}}

with
\left(\exp(-\gamma q)-1+\gamma q\right)\asymp q\gamma
for
\gamma\to\infty
, hence

\displaystyle\big(K_{q}^{(\gamma)}\big)^{4}\asymp\left(\frac{2^{q-1}q}{q!}\right)^{2}\gamma^{2q+2},\qquad\gamma\to\infty.

Combining the previous bounds, we can write by Lemma 14.22 in
Kallenberg, 2021
for some constant
C^{\prime}_{q,r,t}>0
,

\displaystyle\|f_{t,\gamma}\tilde{\otimes}_{r}f_{t,\gamma}\|^{2}\leq\|f_{t,\gamma}\otimes_{r}f_{t,\gamma}\|^{2}\leq C^{\prime}_{q,r,t}\gamma^{2q+2}\gamma^{-(2q+3)}=C^{\prime}_{q,r,t}\gamma^{-1},\qquad\gamma\geq 1,

(6.3)

from which the statement follows.

(ii)
The argument for
q=1
is identical to the one in (i). Let
q\geq 2
.
We show that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\left|\sum_{q=1}^{p}c_{q}W_{q,\gamma}(t)-t\sum_{q=1}^{p}c_{q}\frac{H_{q}(Z)}{\sqrt{q!}}\right|^{2}\,\right]\xrightarrow[\gamma\searrow 0]{}0,

(6.4)

providing a quantitative bound. Applying the stochastic Fubini theorem (cf. Theorem 2.1 in
Pipiras and Taqqu, 2010
) and considering
h_{\gamma,s}(x)=e^{-\gamma(s-x)}\mathbf{1}_{\{x\leq s\}}
, we write

\displaystyle W_{q,\gamma}(t)=K_{q}^{(\gamma)}\int_{0}^{t}T_{q,\gamma}(s)\mathrm{d}s,\qquad t\in[0,1],

with

\displaystyle T_{q,\gamma}(s):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{q}}^{{}^{\prime}}\prod_{j=1}^{q}h_{\gamma,s}(x_{j})dB(x_{1})\cdots dB(x_{q})=I_{q}\big(h_{\gamma,s}^{\otimes q}\big),\qquad s\in[0,1].

Observe that
W_{q,\gamma}(t)-Y_{q,\gamma}(t)=K_{q}^{(\gamma)}\int_{0}^{t}\big(T_{q,\gamma}(s)-T_{q,\gamma}(0)\big)\mathrm{d}s
, with
Y_{q,\gamma}(t):=K_{q}^{(\gamma)}tT_{q,\gamma}(0)
. By Jensen’s inequality and Fubini’s theorem, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|W_{q,\gamma}(t)-Y_{q,\gamma}(t)|^{2}\right]\leq\big(K_{q}^{(\gamma)}\big)^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}\left[\big(T_{q,\gamma}(s)-T_{q,\gamma}(0)\big)^{2}\right]\mathrm{d}s.

(6.5)

By equation (5.5.62) in
Peccati and Taqqu, 2011
,
\mathbb{E}[T_{q,\gamma}(s)T_{q,\gamma}(u)]=q!{\rho_{\gamma}(s,u)}^{q}
for
s,u\geq 0
, with
\rho_{\gamma}(s,u)
given in (
6.2
). Thus, by using
1-e^{-x}\leq x
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\big(T_{q,\gamma}(s)-T_{q,\gamma}(0)\big)^{2}\right]=\mathbb{E}[T_{q,\gamma}(s)^{2}]+\mathbb{E}[T_{q,\gamma}(0)^{2}]-2\mathbb{E}[T_{q,\gamma}(s)T_{q,\gamma}(0)]\leq 2q!(2\gamma)^{-q}\gamma qs

and by equation (
6.5
)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|W_{q,\gamma}(t)-Y_{q,\gamma}(t)|^{2}\right]\leq\big(K_{q}^{(\gamma)}\big)^{2}2q!(2\gamma)^{-q}\gamma q\int_{0}^{1}s\mathrm{d}s=\big(K_{q}^{(\gamma)}\big)^{2}qq!(2\gamma)^{-q}\gamma.

We now observe that
\big(K_{q}^{(\gamma)}\big)^{2}(2\gamma)^{-q}q!=1+\frac{q}{3}\gamma+\mathcal{O}(\gamma^{3})
for

. This yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|W_{q,\gamma}(t)-Y_{q,\gamma}(t)|^{2}\right]\leq 2q\gamma(1+o_{\gamma}(1)),

(6.6)

where the Landau symbol

here is intended for
\gamma\searrow 0

\bar{h}_{\gamma,0}(x)=\sqrt{2\gamma}h_{\gamma,0}(x)

, and define

\displaystyle Z:=I_{1}(\bar{h}_{\gamma,0})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bar{h}_{\gamma,0}(x)dB(x)=\sqrt{2\gamma}\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{\gamma x}dB(x)\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1).

By Itô’s polynomial representation (cf. Theorem 14.25 in

I_{q}(\bar{h}_{\gamma,0}^{\otimes q})=H_{q}\left(I_{1}(\bar{h}_{\gamma,0})\right)

Y_{q,\gamma}(t)=K_{q}^{(\gamma)}tI_{q}\big(h_{\gamma,0}^{\otimes q}\big)=tK_{q}^{(\gamma)}(2\gamma)^{-q/2}I_{q}\big(\bar{h}_{\gamma,0}^{\otimes q}\big)=tK_{q}^{(\gamma)}(2\gamma)^{-q/2}H_{q}(Z)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\left|Y_{q,\gamma}(t)-\frac{t}{\sqrt{q!}}H_{q}(Z)\right|^{2}=\left|K_{q}^{(\gamma)}(2\gamma)^{-q/2}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{q!}}\right|^{2}\cdot|H_{q}(Z)|^{2}.

By using

and
K_{q}^{(\gamma)}(2\gamma)^{-q/2}-1/\sqrt{q!}=\frac{q}{6\sqrt{q!}}\gamma+\mathcal{O}(\gamma^{3})

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\left|Y_{q,\gamma}(t)-\frac{t}{\sqrt{q!}}H_{q}(Z)\right|^{2}\right]=q!\left(K_{q}^{(\gamma)}(2\gamma)^{-q/2}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{q!}}\right)^{2}=\frac{q^{2}}{36}\gamma^{2}(1+o_{\gamma}(1)).

(6.7)

Now, Theorem 4.4 in
Nourdin and Poly, 2013
reveals

\displaystyle d_{TV}\left(\mathcal{L}\big(W_{q,\gamma}(1)\big),\mathcal{L}\left(\frac{H_{q}(Z)}{\sqrt{q!}}\right)\right)\leq\tilde{C}_{2}(q)\Bigg(\mathbb{E}\bigg(W_{q,\gamma}(1)-\frac{H_{q}(Z)}{\sqrt{q!}}\bigg)^{2}\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{4q}}.

Then the assertion follows by (
6.6
) and (
6.7
) as
\gamma\to 0
.

Appendix A
Discrete-time stationary Gauss-Markov process

The following lemma is well-known, yet we did not find an appropriate discrete-time reference in the literature and therefore provide a proof for the reader’s convenience. It states that after centering, the class of stationary, Gaussian discrete-time Markov processes only contains AR(1) processes.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
(X_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be a stationary Gaussian Markov process. Then after centering with
\mathbb{E}X_{0}=\mu
, the process is either constant in time or satisfies the recursion

X_{k}-\mu=\alpha(X_{k-1}-\mu)+\varepsilon_{k},\quad k\geq 1,

with
\varepsilon_{k}\overset{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\mathcal{N}\big(0,\mathrm{Var}(X_{0})(1-\alpha^{2})\big)
and
|\alpha|<1
.

Proof.

Let
\mathrm{Var}(X_{0})>0
and
\alpha=\mathrm{Cov}(X_{0},X_{1})/\mathrm{Var}(X_{0})
. Then
|\alpha|\leq 1
by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and
\mathrm{Var}(X_{1})=\mathrm{Var}(X_{0})
. If
\alpha=1
, the process is constant in time whereas
\alpha=-1
contradicts stationarity. Thus, it remains to study
|\alpha|<1
.
Proposition 14.7 in
Kallenberg, 2021
reveals

\mathrm{Cov}(X_{0},X_{k+1})\mathrm{Var}(X_{1})=\mathrm{Cov}(X_{0},X_{1})\mathrm{Cov}(X_{1},X_{k+1}).

By stationarity,
\mathrm{Var}(X_{1})=\mathrm{Var}(X_{0})
and
\mathrm{Cov}(X_{1},X_{k+1})=\mathrm{Cov}(X_{0},X_{k})
, hence

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}(X_{0},X_{k+1})=\frac{\mathrm{Cov}(X_{0},X_{1})}{\mathrm{Var}(X_{0})}\mathrm{Cov}(X_{0},X_{k})=\alpha\,\mathrm{Cov}(X_{0},X_{k}).

(A.1)

Next, define
\varepsilon_{k}:=X_{k}-\mu-\alpha\,(X_{k-1}-\mu)
. We show that
\varepsilon_{k}\perp\!\!\!\perp\{X_{j}:j\leq k-1\}
. To this aim, note that for
j\leq k-1
,

\mathrm{Cov}(\varepsilon_{k},X_{j})=\mathrm{Cov}(X_{k},X_{j})-\alpha\,\mathrm{Cov}(X_{k-1},X_{j})

by definition of
\varepsilon_{k}
. Now, if
j=k-1
, stationarity reveals

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}(\varepsilon_{k},X_{k-1})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Cov}(X_{k},X_{k-1})-\alpha\,\mathrm{Var}(X_{k-1})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Cov}(X_{0},X_{1})-\frac{\mathrm{Cov}(X_{k},X_{k-1})}{\mathrm{Var}(X_{0})}\mathrm{Var}(X_{0})=0.

If
j<k-1
, we obtain with equation (
A.1
),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}(\varepsilon_{k},X_{j})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Cov}(X_{k},X_{j})-\alpha\,\mathrm{Cov}(X_{k-1},X_{j})

\displaystyle=\alpha\,\mathrm{Cov}(X_{k-1},X_{j})-\alpha\,\mathrm{Cov}(X_{k-1},X_{j})=0.

Since
\varepsilon_{k}
and
\{X_{j}:j\leq k-1\}
are jointly normally distributed,
\varepsilon_{k}
is even independent of
\{X_{j}:j\leq k-1\}
.
\varepsilon_{k}
is clearly centered with variance

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(\varepsilon_{k})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Var}(X_{0})+\alpha^{2}\mathrm{Var}(X_{0})-2\alpha\,\mathrm{Cov(X_{0},X_{1})}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Var}(X_{0})+\alpha^{2}\mathrm{Var}(X_{0})-2\alpha^{2}\mathrm{Var}(X_{0})=\mathrm{Var}(X_{0})(1-\alpha^{2}).

As concerns
l<k
, we write
\varepsilon_{l}=X_{l}-\mu-\alpha\,(X_{l-1}-\mu)
. Then
\varepsilon_{l}
is represented as a function of
X_{l}
and
X_{l-1}
, both in the past of time-step
k
. Due to previous observations,
\varepsilon_{k}\perp\!\!\!\perp\varepsilon_{l}
and more generally
\varepsilon_{k}\perp\!\!\!\perp(\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{k-1})
. By induction, the sequence
(\varepsilon_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is i.i.d.
Finally, isolating
X_{k}
in the defining equation for
\varepsilon_{k}
, one recovers the defining equation of an AR(1) process, which proves the assertion.
∎

Appendix B
Mixing time

Lemma B.1
.

Let
X_{1,n},\dots,X_{n,n}
be observations satisfying recursion (
1.1
). Then there exist constant
c,C>0
such that

c\min\bigg\{1,\frac{|x|\alpha_{n}^{j}\sqrt{1-\alpha_{n}^{2}}}{\sigma_{n}}+\alpha_{n}^{2j}\bigg\}\leq\Big\|\mathcal{L}_{x}\big(X_{j,n}\big)-\pi_{n}\Big\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq C\min\bigg\{1,\frac{|x|\alpha_{n}^{j}\sqrt{1-\alpha_{n}^{2}}}{\sigma_{n}}+\alpha_{n}^{2j}\bigg\}.

In particular,
t_{\text{mix},n}\asymp n^{\beta}/\gamma
for
\alpha_{n}=(1-\gamma/n^{\beta})
with the local mixing time
t_{\text{mix},n}
in (
1.2
).

Proof.

Starting the recursion (
1.1
) from a deterministic initial condition
X_{0}^{(n,x)}=x
, the
j
-th iteration equals

\displaystyle X_{j}^{(n,x)}=\alpha_{n}^{j}x+\sum_{k=1}^{j}\alpha_{n}^{j-k}\sigma_{n}\varepsilon_{k},

whereas
\mathcal{L}\big(X_{j,n}\big)=\pi_{n}
for the unique stationary solution. With the notation

\displaystyle\mu_{n,j}:=\mathcal{L}\big(X_{j}^{(n,x)}\big)=\mathcal{N}\left(m_{n,j},s_{n,j}^{2}\right),\qquad\text{ with }\quad m_{n,j}=\alpha_{n}^{j}x,\quad s_{n,j}^{2}=\frac{\sigma_{n}^{2}}{1-\alpha_{n}^{2}}(1-\alpha_{n}^{2j});

\displaystyle\pi_{n}=\mathcal{L}\big(X_{j,n}\big)=\mathcal{N}\left(0,s_{n,\infty}^{2}\right),\qquad\text{ with }\quad s_{n,\infty}^{2}=\frac{\sigma_{n}^{2}}{1-\alpha_{n}^{2}},

we determine an upper and lower bound for the total variation distance of
\mu_{n,j}
and
\pi_{n}
. For the upper bound, consider
\nu_{n,j}:=N\big(m_{n,j},s_{n,\infty}^{2}\big)
, that is, the Gaussian measure with stationary variance but shifted mean
m_{n,j}
. By the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\|\mu_{n,j}-\pi_{n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq\|\mu_{n,j}-\nu_{n,j}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}+\|\nu_{n,j}-\pi_{n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.

Next, for any
\sigma^{2}>0
and
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\|N(m_{1},\sigma^{2})-N(m_{2},\sigma^{2})\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=2\,\Phi\!\left(\frac{|m_{1}-m_{2}|}{2\sigma}\right)-1\leq\frac{|m_{1}-m_{2}|}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\sigma},

(B.1)

where
\Phi
denotes the distribution function of the standard normal distribution.
Applying this inequality with
m_{1}=m_{n,j}=\alpha_{n}^{j}x
,
m_{2}=0
, and
\sigma=s_{n,\infty}
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\|\nu_{n,j}-\pi_{n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq\frac{|\alpha_{n}^{j}x|}{\sqrt{2\pi}s_{n,\infty}}.

For the distance of normal laws with different variances, recall that by the first Pinsker inequality (cf. Lemma 2.5 (i) in
Tsybakov, 2009
),
\|\mu_{n,j}-\nu_{n,j}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq\sqrt{KL(\mu_{n,j},\nu_{n,j})/2}
, where
KL(\cdot,\cdot)
is the Kullback-Leibler divergence. Elementary algebra reveals

KL(\mu_{n,j},\nu_{n,j})=\log\bigg(\frac{s_{n,\infty}}{s_{n,j}}\bigg)-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{s_{n,j}^{2}}{2s_{n,\infty}^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\Big(-\log\big(1-\alpha_{n}^{2j}\big)-\alpha_{n}^{2j}\Big).

Expanding the logarithm provides
-\log\big(1-\alpha_{n}^{2j}\big)=\alpha_{n}^{2j}+\alpha_{n}^{4j}/2+\mathcal{O}_{n}(\alpha_{n}^{6j})
, whence
KL(\mu_{n,j},\nu_{n,j})=\mathcal{O}_{n}(\alpha_{n}^{4j})
and thus
\|\mu_{n,j}-\nu_{n,j}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq C\alpha_{n}^{2j}
. Combining the two bounds gives the claimed upper bound

\displaystyle\|\mu_{n,j}-\pi_{n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq C_{1}|x|\frac{\alpha_{n}^{j}}{s_{n,\infty}}+C_{2}\alpha_{n}^{2j}\leq C\left(|x|\frac{\alpha_{n}^{j}}{s_{n,\infty}}+\alpha_{n}^{2j}\right).

(B.2)

As concerns the lower bound, set

\displaystyle A=\begin{cases}[0,\infty),\quad x\geq 0,\\
(-\infty,0),\quad x<0.\end{cases}

Then
\|\mu_{n,j}-\pi_{n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\geq|\mu_{n,j}(A)-\pi_{n}(A)|
by definition of total variation, where
\pi_{n}(A)=1/2
for each value of
x
. For
\mu_{n,j}(A)
, first suppose that
x\geq 0
. Thus
m_{n,j}\geq 0
,
A=[0,\infty)
and

\mu_{n,j}(A)=\mathbb{P}\big(X_{j}^{(n,x)}\geq 0\big)=\mathbb{P}\Bigg(\frac{X_{j}^{(n,x)}-m_{n,j}}{s_{n,j}}\geq\frac{-m_{n,j}}{s_{n,j}}\Bigg)=1-\Phi\bigg(\frac{-m_{n,j}}{s_{n,j}}\bigg)=\Phi\bigg(\frac{m_{n,j}}{s_{n,j}}\bigg).

If
x<0
, then
m_{n,j}<0
,
A=[-\infty,0)
and analogously

\mu_{n,j}(A)=\mathbb{P}\big(X_{j}^{(n,x)}<0\big)=\mathbb{P}\Bigg(\frac{X_{j}^{(n,x)}-m_{n,j}}{s_{n,j}}<\frac{-m_{n,j}}{s_{n,j}}\Bigg)=\Phi\bigg(\frac{-m_{n,j}}{s_{n,j}}\bigg)=\Phi\bigg(\frac{|m_{n,j}|}{s_{n,j}}\bigg).

Therefore,
\mu_{n,j}(A)=\Phi(|m_{n,j}|/s_{n,j})\geq 1/2
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
and hence,

\|\mu_{n,j}-\pi_{n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\geq\Phi\bigg(\frac{|m_{n,j}|}{s_{n,j}}\bigg)-\frac{1}{2}=\Phi\bigg(\frac{|x|\alpha_{n}^{j}}{s_{n,j}}\bigg)-\frac{1}{2}\geq\Phi\bigg(\frac{|x|\alpha_{n}^{j}}{s_{n,\infty}}\bigg)-\frac{1}{2}.

Now let
w:=(|x|\alpha_{n}^{j})/s_{n,\infty}
and
\phi(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-t^{2}/2}
. If
0\leq w\leq 1
, then

\Phi(w)-\frac{1}{2}=\int_{0}^{w}\phi(t)\mathrm{d}t\geq\int_{0}^{w}\phi(1)\mathrm{d}t=\phi(1)w,

while

\Phi(w)-\frac{1}{2}\geq\Phi(1)-\frac{1}{2}=\int_{0}^{1}\phi(t)\mathrm{d}t\geq\int_{0}^{1}\phi(1)\mathrm{d}t=\phi(1)

if
w>1
. Hence,
\Phi(w)-\frac{1}{2}\geq\phi(1)\min\{1,w\}
for any
w\geq 0
and thus,

\displaystyle\|\mu_{n,j}-\pi_{n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\geq\phi(1)\min\bigg\{1,\frac{|x|\alpha_{n}^{j}}{s_{n,\infty}}\bigg\}.

(B.3)

Now consider the set
B=[-s_{n,\infty},s_{n,\infty}]
and the random variable
Y\sim\lambda_{n,j}:=\mathcal{N}(0,s_{n,j}^{2})
. Then

\lambda_{n,j}(B)=\mathbb{P}(-s_{n,\infty}\leq Y\leq s_{n,\infty})=\mathbb{P}\bigg(-\frac{s_{n,\infty}}{s_{n,j}}\leq\frac{Y}{s_{n,j}}\leq\frac{s_{n,\infty}}{s_{n,j}}\bigg)=2\Phi\bigg(\frac{s_{n,\infty}}{s_{n,j}}\bigg)-1.

Next,
s_{n,j}<s_{n,\infty}
implies
\lambda_{n,j}(B)>\pi_{n}(B)
and consequently,

\|\lambda_{n,j}-\pi_{n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\geq\lambda_{n,j}(B)-\pi_{n}(B)=2\Phi\bigg(\frac{s_{n,\infty}}{s_{n,j}}\bigg)-2\Phi(1)=2\Big[\Phi\big((1-\alpha_{n}^{2j})^{-1/2}\big)-\Phi(1)\Big].

By a Taylor expansion,
(1-\alpha_{n}^{2j})^{-1/2}=1+\alpha_{n}^{2j}/2+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{n}^{4j})
. Together with
\Phi^{\prime}(x)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}\exp(-x^{2}/2)=:\phi(x)
, the mean value theorem yields

\Phi\big((1-\alpha_{n}^{2j})^{-1/2}\big)-\Phi(1)=\phi(y)\big((1-\alpha_{n}^{2j})^{-1/2}-1\big)\geq\phi(1)\,\frac{\alpha_{n}^{2j}}{2}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{n}^{4j})

for an appropriate intermediate value
y\in[1,(1-\,\alpha_{n}^{2j})^{-1/2}]
.
By the reverse triangle inequality and the latter equation, we then write

\displaystyle\|\mu_{n,j}-\pi_{n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}

\displaystyle\geq\|\lambda_{n,j}-\pi_{n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}-\|\mu_{n,j}-\lambda_{n,j}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\phi(1)}{2}\,\alpha_{n}^{2j}-\|\mu_{n,j}-\lambda_{n,j}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\phi(1)}{2}\,\alpha_{n}^{2j}-\frac{|x|\alpha_{n}^{j}}{\sqrt{2\pi}s_{n,j}},

(B.4)

where the last inequality follows from (
B.1
). Thus (
B.4
), combined with (
B.3
), leads to

\displaystyle\|\mu_{n,j}-\pi_{n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\geq\begin{cases}\phi(1)\min\bigg\{1,\frac{|x|\alpha_{n}^{j}}{s_{n,\infty}}\bigg\},\qquad&\text{ if }\quad\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{2\pi}s_{n,\infty}}\geq\frac{\phi(1)}{2}\alpha_{n}^{j};\\
\frac{\phi(1)}{2}\,\alpha_{n}^{2j},\qquad\hskip 56.9055pt&\text{ if }\quad\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{2\pi}s_{n,\infty}}<\frac{\phi(1)}{2}\alpha_{n}^{j}.\end{cases}

In particular, for arbitrary
|x|\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}

\|\mu_{n,j}-\pi_{n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\geq\max\bigg\{\phi(1)\min\bigg\{1,\frac{|x|\alpha_{n}^{j}}{s_{n,\infty}}\bigg\},\frac{\phi(1)}{2}\alpha_{n}^{2j}\bigg\}.

Using consecutively
\max\{a+b\}\geq(a+b)/2
for
a,b\geq 0
and
\min\{a,b\}+c\geq\min\{a,b+c\}
for
c\geq 0
, the latter display implies

\displaystyle\|\mu_{n,j}-\pi_{n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\geq c\min\bigg\{1,\frac{|x|\alpha_{n}^{j}}{s_{n,\infty}}+\alpha_{n}^{2j}\bigg\},

(B.5)

with
c=\phi(1)/4
. As
0\leq\|(\cdot)-(\cdot)\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq 1
, the combination of (
B.2
) and (
B.5
) yields

c\min\bigg\{1,\frac{|x|\alpha_{n}^{j}}{s_{n,\infty}}+\alpha_{n}^{2j}\bigg\}\leq\|\mu_{n,j}-\pi_{n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq C\min\bigg\{1,\frac{|x|\alpha_{n}^{j}}{s_{n,\infty}}+\alpha_{n}^{2j}\bigg\}.

To evaluate the asymptotic order in
n
of
t_{\text{mix},n}
, we need to extract for any
0<\delta,\eta<1
the order of the smallest possible index
j
such that

k_{n}(\delta)\frac{\alpha_{n}^{j}}{s_{n,\infty}}+\alpha_{n}^{2j}\leq\eta,

where
k_{n}(\delta)=\inf\{k>0:\pi_{n}\left(\left[-k,k\right]\right)\geq 1-\delta\}\asymp s_{n,\infty}
. As
\alpha_{n}=(1-\gamma/n^{\beta})
, one immediately reads off
t_{\text{mix},n}\asymp n^{\beta}/\gamma
.
