Executive Summary: On May 8, 2017, Hanover residents voted overwhelmingly to
transition to 100% renewable electricity by 2030 and heating, cooling and transportation
by 2050. In so doing, we became the first municipality in the country to make this
commitment by popular vote.
To accomplish these goal town leaders developed a plan to address first transitioning
municipal energy uses to renewable forms of energy, through on-site generation of the
municipal electrical load, load reduction through energy efficiency improvements to town
facilities and infrastructure. Significant progress has been accomplished in the
generation of energy with 90%+ of the 2024 municipal load being offset by generation
and through various energy efficiency projects.

The utility owned street lighting was identified as a large energy consumer of 165 MWh
annually (7% of the annual municipal load) due to old inefficient high-pressure sodium
and mercury vapor streetlights with no ability to reduce wattage during periods with low
lighting needs. The Hanover LED Streetlight Replacement Project replaced utility
owned streetlights with town owned controllable (dimmable and trimmable) LED lights
that can be adjusted by location and time of night. Coupled with ownership of the
streetlights transfer to the town, the town’s share of the project pay back is 11 months
and for the complete project including federal share the payback is approximately 3
years.

With the completion of lighting upgrade, the annual streetlighting energy load has
dropped by 60% to 67 MWh in the first year of operation (7/1/24 - 6/30/25). This
amounts to an approximate $90,000 annual savings or a local 1% tax rate impact.
Additionally, over 200,000 pounds of CO2 will be saved annually.

3. Project Objectives:

 The project goal is to reduce streetlight energy consumption, CO2, lighting load,
 provide for the ability to vary lighting levels by location and reduce annual expenses.
 Over the life of the streetlights (15 years) expected CO2 is expected to be reduced
 by 1,400 tons. Project objectives go/no go were to (1) negotiate with the utility to
 install town owned and controlled streetlights (2) determining stranded costs for
 existing streetlights, (3) develop a Request for Proposals (RFP), contracting with a
 vendor to provide BABA compliant, cloud based controllable LED streetlights, (4)
 commission the lighting controls.

4. Project Results and Discussion:

 Project objectives and outcomes were met, although the timeline was extended
 much longer than anticipated due to several factors. Two (2) no cost projects
 extensions were requested and received to address the delayed response from the
 utility at various points in the project and to provide additional time to search for
 BABA compliant ornament light fixtures (not originally identified in the project). The
 project was not successful at finding BABA compliant ornamental fixtures (not
 installed in the project) and were not successful obtaining a waive of the BABA
 requirements.

 All the streetlights installed in the project were BABA compliant.

 The town contracted separately not part of the project with the vendor to install
 ornamental fixtures which although were assembled in the U.S.A., the components
 that made up the fixtures were not substantially made in the U.S.A.

 Task 1, Negotiate stranded costs with utility was delayed due to slow response
 and verification by the utility as assert records were incomplete (10-week delay).

 Task 2 RFP development, issued and contractor selection proceeded without
 any delays. A vendor was found using an open bidding process that was able to
 provide BABA compliant equipment (retrofit LED lighting and cellular controls).

  Task (3) Light replacement - the installation of the retrofit lighting and controls was
  delayed by the utility which required the vendor’s subcontracted installer to be
  authorized to work on the utility’s infrastructure, which took several months to obtain.

  Task (4) Lighting Controls commissioning was performed with little delay and
  happened within one month after lights were installed.

5. Significant Accomplishments and Conclusions:

  The Town was successful meeting the project objectives to replace inefficient
  streetlights with Town owned controllable (Verizon City Hub) streetlights (S900D
  Series DOB Roadway Luminaire, P900D Series DOB Luminaire 25W - 100W &
  C901 Series Carriage Luminaire manufactured by Affinity LED, LLC of Dover,
  NH. The lighting provides effective efficient cloud based controllable illumination at
  levels and durations appropriate for both high active downtown areas around the
  urban core and in neighborhoods which desire dark sky’s during the late nights and
  early mornings.

 Map of lights

 Example Lighting Schedule

 The only negative outcome was the inability to replace existing ornamental
 streetlights as part of the project due to the lack of BABA compliant retrofit fixtures.
 The Town was able to accomplish this goal in a separate town only funded project.

 Two challenges were the delays experienced in negotiating with the utility due to
 their lack of verifiable asset data and installation requirements and the inability to
 find BABA compliant retrofit fixtures that would work in existing infrastructure. Had
 there been more time for a waiver request or the retrofit replacement fixtures had
 been identified sooner, the Town may have been able to include the ornament retrofit
 lights in the project.

6. Path Forward: There are no immediate plans to replace any additional streetlights.
