01
 Executive
 Summary
 The Institute for Nuclear Science to Inspire the
 next Generation of a Highly Trained workforce
 (INSIGHT) is a resource enter that was funded
 by the Department of Energy Office of Nuclear
 Physics within the Office of Science in 2021as
 part of a two-year pilot program focusing on a
 “Research Traineeships to Broaden and
 Diversify Nuclear Physics” (DE-FOA-0002456).
 The INSIGHT evaluation team developed
 specific metrics tailored to address the goals of
 this FOA and worked with all 18 sites funded by
 this program. The awards were split into three
 cohorts: Phase I, which included sites with a
 start date in the Summer of 2021, and Phase II,
 which included sites starting in the Summer of
 2022, and Phase III that started in the Summer
 of 2023. All cohorts were completed by Spring
 of 2025.

 The evaluation efforts used an extended case
 study approach to evaluate all cohorts, as well
 as the program elements’ impact on the overall
 program. The evaluation findings summarized
 below will be used to aid the INSIGHT resource
 center and other interested stakeholders in
 gaining perspectives regarding the
 implementation process of programs, quality of
 partnership development and engagement, and
 a nuanced understanding of how program
 elements impact students and ultimately
 impact students decision(s) regarding their
 participation in the nuclear physicist pipeline.

02
 Executive
 Summary
 PHASE I EVALUATION
 The evaluation for Phase 1 focused on 4
 aspects: implementation, institutional
 stakeholders and participants, experiences, and
 impact. A total of 39 students and 18 faculty
 and mentors were involved in this study,
 corresponding to 70% of the total expected
 number of participants that were either funded
 or participated in this program. The major
 findings were that students overall mainly had
 positive experiences (83% strongly agreed), felt
 supported (83% strongly agreed), and were
 motivated (83% strongly agreed) to pursue
 careers in nuclear physics and/or engineering.
 It was also found that administrative
 challenges did contribute to any negative
 experience that occurred.

03
 Executive
 Summary
 PHASE II AND FINAL EVALUATION

 The evaluation for Phase II focused on
 participants, experiences, programmatic
 changes, and impact. In Phase II we track two
 cohorts of students with the continuation of
 students from Phase I and the first year of
 Cohort II. Phase II and the final evaluation also
 included an analysis of the practices and
 experiences of coaches as well as mentors and
 faculty leads. Major findings were that students
 overwhelmingly enjoyed their experiences in the
 program. Students were motivated to pursue
 both graduate study and ultimately careers in
 nuclear physics and engineering.

 Overall, students not only had mostly positive
 experiences in the program, but also, of the
 students that graduated from their institutions
 in Phase II and Phase III, a majority of
 respondents also indicated that they had
 entered or were considering entering Physics
 graduate programs/careers. Likewise, a
 majority of the respondents indicated they were
 interested in engaging in research projects. A
 need for increased advertising for the program,
 more practical, hands-on experiences,
 information regarding graduate school
 application processes, support with housing,
 increased networking opportunities, and more
 seminars and interactions with active nuclear
 physicists.

 04
 Major Goals
 MAJOR GOALS OF EVALUATION

PHASE I

The major goals of Phase 1 evaluation were to gain insights regarding
improving the development, adaptation, implementation, and replication of
evidence-based practices and support strategies to ultimately increase under-
represented groups’ participation in nuclear physics. Additionally, the evaluation
goals focused on gaining perspective regarding the implementation process of
all programs, the quality of partnership development and engagement, and a
nuanced understanding of how program elements impacted students and
ultimately impacted decision(s) regarding their participation in the nuclear
physicist pipeline.

Specifically for Phase 1, the evaluation focused on the initial implementation of
the program; activities of institutional stakeholders, mentors, and coaches;
quality of experiences and activities; and impact on students.

PHASE II & FINAL
EVALUATION
The major goals of Phase II and final evaluation expanded the goals of Phase I
to gain deeper perspectives of the student experiences from both cohorts, as
well as the impact of coaches, along with mentors. Specifically, the evaluation
focused on the impact of program practices, including the use of coaches and
research on student experiences, as well as the future plans of graduating
students regarding nuclear physics.

 05
 Major Goals

 ACCOMPLISHMENTS UNDER THESE GOALS
The evaluation activities for Phase I, Phase II, and the final evaluation included
a pre-survey for students and mentors, student focus group discussions held at
the beginning of program participation, as well as Phase I, Phase II, and Phase
III check-in interviews with students, mentors, coaches, and site leads. Lastly,
students who graduated from the program upon completion of all phases were
given post-surveys regarding their experiences. This information was compiled
to provide a complete view of the program’s impact. The findings of these
studies are presented in this report.

 Nuclear Physics summEr school for undEr Represented Students (NuPEERS)
 2024 Pilot at Dillard University
 Report

Abstract
On July 13-15, 2024, a pilot Nuclear Physics summEr school for undEr Represented
Students (NuPEERS2024) was held at Dollard University. The school targeted
undergraduate students from Minority Serving Institutions (MSIs) that are part of the
Institute for Nuclear Science to Inspire the next Generation of a Highly Trained workforce
(INSIGHT) DoE funded NP-RENEW sites and their partners. NuPEERS2024 had three
main goals: (1) a three day in-person school that included both lectures and hands-on
activities, (2) leverage the INSIGHT network to optimize the NuPEERS material for long
term impact, and (3) acquire information for a sustained engagement and participation of
students from MSI physics departments in NuPEERS. A total of fifteen (15) undergraduate
students, three (3) graduate students and five (5) lecturers participated in this pilot school.

1. Introduction
Dillard University, a Historically Black College located in New Orleans, LA, in collaboration
with the Institute for Nuclear Science to Inspire the next Generation of a Highly Trained
workforce (INSIGHT) housed at the Facility for Rare Isotope Beams (FRIB) on the
campus of Michigan State University, organized on June 13-16, 2024 a pilot summer
school targeting undergraduate students from Minority Serving Institutions (MSIs). The
2024 Nuclear Physics summEr school for undEr Represented Students (NuPEERS) pilot
school included fifteen students that are part of one of the REaching a New Energy
Sciences Workforce (RENEW) awards from the Department of Energy Office of Nuclear
Physics participated. NuPEERS is designed to provide a complementary venue to train
students in the field of nuclear physics across MSIs. INSIGHT leveraged an existing sub-
award from MSU to organize NuPEERS.

The school will also leverage two existing programs: the Dillard University Women In
STEM Highschool Experience Summer program (DU-WISHES) that is occurring on June
10-21, 2024 and a partnership with Fermi National Accelerator Lab (Fermilab). The former
will provide costs reduction for some of the logistics (e.g., lodging, meals) and enhanced
the impact of NuPEERS through a career panel for young girls. The latter involves an
existing pipeline that placed two students from DU at Fermilab, one is a team leader and
the second is one of the DEI office administrators, through a panel focusing on

opportunities at national labs. Both panels are included in the pilot NuPEERS agenda to
expand nuclear physics across MSIs by building a bridge to graduate school for workforce
development.

NuPEERS focuses on the INSIGHT RENEW sites and their partners and will bring fifteen
undergraduate students from these institutions, five graduate students to administer and
grade assignments as well as provide tutoring assistance to all undergraduate students,
and five professionals, faculty and postdocs, to prepare and deliver lectures on basic and
applied nuclear physics topics, including preparing assignments. The large majority of all
the participants are expected to be from marginalized groups involved in basic and
applied nuclear science. NuPEERS will also organize a one-day meeting for ten
administrative assistants from MSIs and involve twenty undergraduate students across
MSIs to assist in other logistics of the school.

Goal 1 – three-day summer school: the targeted RENEW-NP program within the DoE
Office of Science provides a unique innovative way to provide opportunities to
marginalized groups in the field of nuclear physics, thus establishing a bridge for this
population to not only be exposed to but also considering a graduate education and/or a
career in this field. There is only one nuclear physics school in the U.S. that is held in the
summer for undergraduate students (e.g., nuclear science summer school (NS3) housed
within FRIB/MSU) where few students could benefit. The pilot NuPEERS school at DU is
a dedicated summer school targeting 15 under-represented undergraduate students from
the INSIGHT RENEW sites and their partners that will receive lectures and homework
assignments in basic and applied nuclear physics along with conducting a hands-on
activity, e.g., Zeeman effect experiment. Five graduate students will mentor the
undergraduate students and serve as research assistants to guide for the completion of
the homework and hands-on activity (e.g., Zeeman effect) assignments, as well as grade
these assignments during the NuPEERS school. Ten Administrative Assistants from MSIs
will assist in the local MSI coordination that will participate in NuPEERS by ensuring
students do register in a timely manner, assist the students in the travel process, and
following travel reports, including interfacing with the NuPEERS management team to
address any logistical matter. A joint certificate for attendance to the school will be issued
to the participants signed by Dillard University, Facility for Rare Isotope Beams, National
Society of Black Physicists, Fermi National Accelerator Lab, and Thomas Jefferson
National Accelerator Facility.

Goal 2 – optimizing the NuPEERS material for long term impact: the learning
environment at Minority Serving Institutions is drastically different from R1 institutions.
While existing summer programs in nuclear science have proven to be highly effective in
training the next generation of students, they often have difficulty in recruiting from

marginalized groups. Furthermore, students from these groups often find themselves
isolated (being the sole or one of very few students that look like them) and overwhelmed
(the content is not adapted to their school curriculum). Understanding how to best
optimize the content and delivery of the material for NuPEERS is thus paramount for the
long-term success of this summer school instead of being a one-time event. The pilot
NuPEERS school at DU will support 20 undergraduate students across the INSIGHT
RENEW sites and their partners to assist in the development of its overall content and
framework, thus becoming a program that is tailored to and built as a grassroot from these
populations. These students that are not part of the 15 NuPEERS participants will work
with the school organizers (DU: PI-Darwish; FRIB: PI-Gueye) as well as the NuPEERS
participants to be trained in all aspect of the school such as the NuPEERS website,
hands-on experiment, homework assignments, etc., along with mentoring interactions
with the NuPEERS undergraduate students. All students involved in NuPEERS will
become ambassadors at their own institution.

Goal 3 – sustaining engagement and participation of students in NuPEERS: the
large majority of physics departments at Minority Serving Institutions do not have nuclear
physics faculty and/or nuclear physics research. Therefore, marginalized students
enrolled in physics programs have seldom access to be exposed to this field, resulting in
possible lack of support at their institution, procrastination in applying to future schools
such as NuPEERS, and isolation to continuing some level of engagement with their peers
in nuclear physics. The pilot NuPEERS school at DU will support 10 administrative
assistants across the INSIGHT RENEW sites and their partners to understand the
benefits of nuclear science and assist the school organizers (DU: PI-Darwish; FRIB: PI-
Gueye) as well as the NuPEERS participants by leveraging their local resources to
expand the NuPEERS impacts in their departments.
