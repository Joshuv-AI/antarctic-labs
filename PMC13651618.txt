A Streamlined One-Pot
Extraction Method Enables High-Sensitivity
Top-Down Proteomic Characterization of Phospholamban from Biopsy-Scale
Cardiac Tissue

Isabella R. Clemmer, Holden T. Rogers, Mallory C. Wilson, Zhan Gao, Hsin-Ju Chan, Zachery R. Gregorich, Farhan Raza, Ying Ge

Abstract

Top-down
proteomics (TDP) enables comprehensive characterization
of intact proteoforms, but the analysis of membrane proteoforms remains
challenging because of their hydrophobicity and relatively low abundance.
To address these challenges, we previously developed Azo, a photocleavable
surfactant that enables efficient solubilization and top-down analysis
of membrane proteins. Subsequently, we established an Azo-enabled
TDP method permitting the comprehensive characterization of phospholamban
(PLN), a transmembrane protein that plays crucial roles in calcium
handling, directly from cardiac tissue. However, the original workflow
required at least 10 mg of human cardiac tissue, making it unsuitable
for biopsy-scale and other sample-limited studies. Herein, we developed
a streamlined Azo-enabled one-pot extraction strategy for high-sensitivity
top-down analysis of PLN that reduces the required tissue input to
1 mg while preserving analytical performance. Using online liquid
chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry, we achieved highly
reproducible detection, quantification, and post-translational modification
localization of endogenous PLN proteoforms from 1 mg of human cardiac
tissue. Notably, the streamlined strategy reduced the tissue required
for PLN protein extraction 10-fold while retaining the same capacity
for comprehensive characterization in the one-pot extraction. Collectively,
these results establish a practical strategy for TDP of PLN proteoforms
from biopsy-scale and other sample-limited myocardial specimens.

Introduction

Top-down proteomics
(TDP) directly analyzes
intact proteins, enabling
comprehensive characterization of proteoforms
,
 by
preserving the molecular connectivity among post-translational modifications
(PTMs), genetic variants, and alternative splice isoforms.
−

 This unique capability provides insights into proteoform biology,
protein function, and disease-associated molecular mechanisms that
cannot be readily resolved using peptide-centric approaches.
,
 Membrane proteins play critical roles in cellular signaling, transport,
and communication across biological membranes, making them important
drug targets.
,
 However, analysis of intact membrane
proteins is particularly challenging due to their hydrophobicity,
low abundance, and reduced ionization efficiency.
−

To address these challenges, our laboratory developed a photocleavable
surfactant, Azo, enabling efficient solubilization and top-down analysis
of membrane proteins.
,
 Using Azo, we subsequently established
a TDP workflow for comprehensive proteoform characterization of phospholamban
(PLN), a 52-amino-acid transmembrane
regulator of sarco/endoplasmic reticulum calcium ATPase 2a (SERCA2A)
in the heart.
−

 PLN plays a central role in regulating cardiac calcium homeostasis,
and its activity is dynamically regulated by PTMs including phosphorylation
and S-palmitoylation.
,
 Dysregulation of PLN has been
implicated in the pathogenesis of heart failure and inherited cardiomyopathies
with prominent arrhythmias.
−

 The previously established workflow employed a two-stage
extraction strategy consisting of cytosolic depletion followed by
membrane protein solubilization in Azo surfactant buffer, enabling
identification, quantification, and site-specific localization of
PLN PTMs. However, it required a minimum
of 10 mg of tissue due to sample loss over multiple extraction and
cleanup steps, making it unsuitable for biopsy-scale and other sample-limited
studies.

These limitations highlight the need for simplified
sample preparation
strategies that minimize sample loss and enable top-down proteomic
analysis from a limited biological material. One-pot sample preparation
strategies have increasingly been developed to reduce sample handling
and protein losses, thereby improving the sample efficiency for limited-input
proteomics. In particular, the nanodroplet processing in a one-pot
trace sample platform (nanoPOTS) has been adapted for top-down proteomics,
enabling intact proteoform analysis from low numbers of mammalian
cells by minimizing sample loss during sample preparation. Similarly, other one-pot approaches have incorporated
magnetic-bead-based protein capture and intact proteoform elution
for low-input top-down analysis. In our
laboratory, one-pot processing has also been applied to single-muscle-cell
top-down proteomics to improve the recovery of intact proteoforms. Collectively, these studies demonstrate the
potential of one-pot strategies to reduce sample loss and extend top-down
proteomics to increasingly sample-limited biological systems.

Building on these advances, we developed a streamlined azo-enabled
one-pot extraction strategy for high-sensitivity top-down analysis
of PLN that reduces the required tissue input from 10 mg to 1 mg of
cardiac tissue. The streamlined one-pot extraction strategy eliminates
the cytosolic depletion step and incorporates sonication-assisted
extraction in a photocleavable Azo surfactant buffer to minimize sample
loss. Using this strategy, we demonstrate reproducible detection,
identification, quantification, and comprehensive characterization
of PLN, including phosphorylated and palmitoylated PLN proteoforms,
from biopsy-scale tissue inputs with analytical performance comparable
to that of the conventional workflow. This work establishes a practical
strategy for the biopsy-compatible TDP interrogation of PLN proteoforms.

Materials and Methods

Reagents and Chemicals

The reagents used were purchased
from MilliporeSigma (Burlington, MA, USA), unless otherwise noted.
Buffers contained liquid chromatography (LC)-mass spectrometry (MS)-grade
water from Milli-Q (Millipore, Corp., Billerica, MA, USA). Isopropanol
(IPA), acetonitrile (ACN), and methanol (MeOH) were obtained from
Fisher Scientific (Fair Lawn, NJ, USA).

Human Cardiac Tissue Collection

Left-ventricular myocardial
tissue from nonfailing adult donors was obtained from the University
of Wisconsin (UW)-Madison Organ and Tissue Donation Program. The Institutional
Review Board (IRBs) of UW-Madison (study no. 2013–1264) approved
the collection procedures for human heart tissue.

Protein Extraction
and Sample Preparation

To compare
the streamlined one-pot extraction method with the previously established
two-stage extraction method, both protocols
were performed in parallel using cardiac tissue from nonfailing donor
hearts, with all extraction procedures conducted at 4 °C. For
the streamlined one-pot method, cardiac tissues from nonfailing donors
were cut into 1 mg pieces, approximating biopsy-scale tissue samples.
Unlike the previously established workflow for 10 mg of cardiac tissue,
these samples were not cryopulverized and did not undergo cytosolic
depletion. Instead, the tissue was immediately homogenized with a
Teflon pestle in 100 μL of Azo buffer [25 mM ammonium bicarbonate
(ABC)] pH 8.0, 0.4% Azo in 25 mM ABC, 10 mM l-methionine,
5 mM tris­(2-carboxyethyl) phosphine hydrochloride (TCEP), 1 mM phenylmethylsulfonyl
fluoride (PMSF), 2.5 mM ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA), and
1× Halt protease and phosphatase inhibitor cocktail (Thermo Scientific;
Catalog # 78444)] and vortexed vigorously. To compensate for the lack
of cryopulverization and ensure efficient protein solubilization,
samples were then sonicated in a water bath located in a cold room
(4 °C) for 10 min. The homogenate was centrifuged (21,000 g for 25 min, 4 °C), and the supernatant was removed
().

For the previously established two-stage extraction
method, 10 mg pieces of cardiac tissue
were cut and
cryopulverized under liquid nitrogen. Cytosolic proteins were depleted
twice by being homogenized with a Teflon pestle in 100 μL of
ABC buffer [25 mM ABC pH 8.0, 10 mM l-methionine, 5 mM TCEP,
1 mM PMSF, 2.5 mM EDTA, and 1× Halt protease and phosphatase
inhibitor cocktail]. After centrifuging (21,000 g for 25 min, 4 °C), samples were homogenized in 100 μL
Azo buffer, and centrifuged again (21,000 g for 25
min, 4 °C). The supernatant was then centrifuged one additional
time to pellet any cell debris (Figure S1).

For both methods, proteins were prepared for MS analysis
using
a MeOH/chloroform/water (MCW) precipitation as described previously (Supplemental Methods). Protein pellets were then resolubilized in 80% formic acid in
water and diluted with 10:10:80 IPA:ACN:water before being irradiated
with ultraviolet (UV) light for 1 min to degrade any remaining Azo.
All extraction steps for both protocols were performed at 4 °C.

Top-Down LC-Tandem MS (MS/MS) Analysis

LC-MS/MS analysis
was performed as previously described, with the same parameters for samples prepared from the 1 mg and
10 mg extraction analysis. Briefly, protein samples were normalized
to a concentration of 0.5 mg/mL and 4 μL (2 μg of total
protein) was injected into the system. Samples were analyzed using
reversed-phase LC (RPLC) on a Waters NanoAcquity ultraperformance
LC system coupled to an Impact II quadruple time-of-flight (QTOF)
mass spectrometer (Bruker Daltonics). The RPLC method includes 0.2%
formic acid in water for mobile phase A (MPA) and 0.2% formic acid
in IPA/ACN for mobile phase B (MPB). A 1 h gradient was performed,
going from 10% to 90% MPB (0–5 min, 10% B; 5–45 min,
15%–65% B; 45–50 min, 65%–90% B, 50–55
min, 90% B; 55–60 min, 10% B) on a home-packed column (C2,
150 × 0.5 mm, 5-μm particle size, 300 Å pore size).
A constant flow rate of 20 μL/min was used, and the column temperature
was set at 60 °C. MS parameters can be found in the Supporting
Information (Supplemental Methods). MS1
was performed for intact analysis, and targeted MS/MS was performed
for selected PLN proteoforms with collisional activated dissociation
(CAD) set between 20 and 50 eV on additional injection replicates.
The instrument was tested for reliability and reproducibility using
injection replicates, and a loading curve was run to demonstrate that
the amount of sample injected was within the linear response range
of the instrument (Figure S2).

Data Analysis

LC-MS/MS data was analyzed with Bruker
Daltonics Data Analysis software (ver. 4.3). Extracted ion chromatograms
(EIC) were generated from the protein’s most abundant proteoforms’
charge state values to determine elution times of each proteoform
and mass spectra were generated by averaging across the total ion
chromatogram (TIC) during those elution times. Mass spectra were deconvoluted
using Maximum Entropy set at a resolution of 60,000 and relative abundances
were calculated using intensity values with the sophisticated numerical
annotation procedure (SNAP) peak-picking algorithm with the quality
factor threshold set at 0.4, the absolute intensity threshold at 300,
and the max charge state at 20. Total phosphorylation levels (Pi/mol
protein) of PLN were calculated as described previously (Supplemental Methods). MASH Native (Version 1.1) was used
to analyze tandem mass spectra from targeted MS/MS. An error tolerance
of 20 ppm was used for fragment verification.

Statistical Analysis

Statistical comparisons of proteoform
levels between the 1 mg and 10 mg samples were performed using a Mann–Whitney
U test. All p-values less than 0.05 were considered
statistically significant.

Results and Discussion

High-Sensitivity
TDP Enables Reproducible Detection of PLN Proteoforms
from Biopsy-Scale Cardiac Tissue

To enable proteoform analysis
from biopsy-scale cardiac tissue, we developed a streamlined one-pot
Azo-enabled extraction method for high-sensitivity TDP (). During initial method development,
different tissue input amounts (10, 5, 2, and 1 mg) were evaluated
to determine the feasibility of reducing the sample requirement while
maintaining robust PLN proteoform characterization. Based on these
experiments, 1 mg, the lowest tissue input evaluated in this study,
was selected for the streamlined one-pot extraction method to enable
biopsy-scale analysis and further evaluated in a side-by-side comparison
with the conventional two-stage extraction method. In contrast to
our previously established conventional two-stage extraction method
(Figure S1), this one-pot extraction method eliminates cryopulverization and
cytosolic depletion and incorporates direct sonication in Azo buffer,
minimizing sample loss while maintaining robust PLN extraction and
reproducibility. The streamlined one-pot extraction method yielded
approximately 30–60 μg of total protein from 1 mg of
cardiac tissue (1–2 μg/μL in a total extract volume
of 30 μL). Left ventricular tissue from a nonfailing donor heart
was analyzed using both the newly developed one-pot extraction method
(1 mg tissue) and the conventional two-stage extraction method (10
mg tissue), with all extractions and analyses performed in parallel
for direct comparison. Each method was performed in triplicate. The
TICs acquired using the 1 h elution gradient were highly reproducible
for both methods (Figure S3). The TICs
differed because the conventional two-stage extraction depleted cytosolic
proteins (A,
C), however both extraction methods reproducibly detected comparable
PLN proteoform profiles (E, F).

EICs revealed reproducible elution
of mono- and
bis-phosphorylated
PLN proteoforms between 45.0 and 47.5 min ( and S4), whereas
the palmitoylated bis-phosphorylated proteoform eluted between 49.2
and 49.7 min (data not shown). High-resolution mass spectra confirmed
the presence of previously reported PLN proteoforms through mass shifts corresponding to N-terminal acetylation
(+42.01 Da), phosphorylation (+79.96 Da), and S-palmitoylation (+238.23
Da).

Importantly, deconvoluted spectra demonstrated highly similar
PLN
proteoform profiles between the one-pot extraction (1 mg) and the
conventional two-stage extraction (10 mg) methods (E, F), indicating that the
10-fold reduction in tissue input did not result in observable loss
of proteoform diversity or spectral quality. Furthermore, all detected
PLN proteoforms were measured within 3 ppm of their theoretical masses
in both workflows (G, H), demonstrating excellent mass accuracy despite a likely
reduction in PLN recovery due to the lack of cytosolic protein depletion.
Although the streamlined one-pot extraction method incorporated a
10 min sonication step at 4 °C to facilitate tissue disruption
and protein extraction, we did not observe detectable changes in the
PLN proteoform distribution or PTM characterization attributable to
the sonication treatment under the conditions used in this study.
Furthermore, we observed no evidence of acid-induced modifications,
as no additional or unexpected modifications consistent with formic
acid-induced artifacts were detected. These results demonstrate that
the streamlined one-pot extraction strategy enables reproducible detection
of low-abundance PLN proteoforms from biopsy-scale cardiac tissue
with a 10-fold reduction in tissue input.

Quantitative Assessment
of PLN Phosphorylation Is Preserved
Following a 10-fold Reduction in Tissue Input

Having established
reproducible detection of PLN proteoforms using the streamlined one-pot
extraction method, we next evaluated whether the reduced tissue input
affected quantitative measurements. The total PLN phosphorylation
levels were calculated from deconvoluted mass spectra. The one-pot
extraction method (1 mg tissue) yielded 1.89 ± 0.03 Pi/mol protein,
compared with 1.91 ± 0.02 Pi/mol protein for the conventional
two-stage extraction method (10 mg tissue) (I). No statistically significant difference
was observed between the two extraction methods (Mann–Whitney
U test, p = 0.9999), suggesting that the streamlined
one-pot extraction method provides comparable quantitative measurements
of PLN phosphorylation. The comparable phosphorylation levels observed
between these two extraction methods indicate that reducing tissue
input in the streamlined one-pot extraction method preserves the biological
information required for PLN proteoform analysis, which is particularly
important for the detection of conditions where PLN phosphorylation
is known to change such as heart failure and certain genetic diseases.
−

 Together, these results demonstrate that quantitative assessment
of PLN phosphorylation can be reliably achieved from only 1 mg of
human cardiac tissue, expanding the applicability of TDP to sample-limited
studies.

The Streamlined One-Pot Extraction Method Enables High-Confidence
PTM Localization

Although the same amount of total protein
(2 μg) was injected for both workflows, the composition of the
injected samples differed. The conventional two-stage extraction method
(10 mg) included cytosolic depletion, enriching membrane proteins
such as PLN, whereas the one-pot extraction (1 mg) workflow retained
the full proteome. Consequently, the relative amount of PLN injected
was expected to be lower in the one-pot workflow due to increased
contribution from abundant cytosolic proteins. To determine whether
this reduced amount affected MS/MS-based proteoform characterization,
mono- and bis-phosphorylated PLN proteoforms were subjected to targeted
CAD-MS/MS using collision energies of 20, 30, 40, and 50 eV. Fragment
ions from all collision energies were combined to maximize sequence
coverage ( and S5).

For the bis-phosphorylated proteoform, 49 fragment
ions (36 b-ions
and 13 y-ions) were identified following the streamlined one-pot extraction
method (1 mg tissue), compared with 48 fragment ions (36 b-ions and
12 y-ions) following the conventional two-stage extraction method
(10 mg tissue). Both extraction methods achieved identical sequence
coverage of 71.2% with 36 bond cleavages out of 52 possible backbone
cleavages (). For the monophosphorylated proteoform, 43 fragment ions with 33
bond cleavages were identified following the one-pot extraction method,
compared with 47 fragment ions and 32 bond cleaves following the conventional
two-stage extraction method. The corresponding sequence coverages
were 63.5% and 61.5%, respectively (Figure S5). Importantly, preservation of sequence coverage enabled confident
localization of phosphorylation sites to Ser16 and Thr17, consistent
with previous studies.
,,
 These results demonstrate that the substantial reduction in tissue
input in the streamlined one-pot extraction method does not diminish
confidence in PTM localization or proteoform identification. Although
CAD enabled confident localization of the PLN phosphorylation sites
in the present study, electron-based fragmentation methods or ultraviolet
photodissociation (UVPD) could potentially improve the preservation
and characterization of labile PTMs.
−

 The S-palmitoylated PLN proteoform was assigned based on its intact
MS1 mass and chromatographic elution rather than targeted MS/MS characterization.
Importantly, the site of PLN S-palmitoylation has previously been
localized to Cys36 using targeted MS/MS and a range of collision energies
in our previous report. Thus, the present
study focused on detection of the S-palmitoylated proteoform by intact
mass rather than relocalizing the modification site.

Furthermore,
the present study was designed to evaluate the streamlined
one-pot extraction method through targeted PLN proteoform analysis
rather than global top-down proteomic profiling; therefore, we did
not perform comprehensive proteoform identification or determine the
total number of proteoforms detected. Given the demonstrated utility
of Azo for membrane protein solubilization in our previous studies,
,
 we anticipate that the streamlined one-pot method may be applicable
to additional membrane protein targets. Future studies will systematically
evaluate its applicability to other membrane proteins and cardiac
protein classes, including myofilament, mitochondrial, and histone
proteins, and assess its overall proteoform coverage.

Collectively,
these results establish that the streamlined one-pot
extraction method enables comprehensive top-down PLN proteoform analysis
from biopsy-scale tissue inputs. Endomyocardial biopsies provide a
unique opportunity to provide direct access to myocardial tissue for
diagnosis, investigate molecular changes during disease progression,
and/or in response to therapeutic interventions.
,
 However, their limited tissue yield has historically constrained
comprehensive proteoform analysis. Consequently, most previous studies
of heart failure rely on tissue obtained at end-stage disease, where
distinct pathogenic mechanisms have often converged on common remodeling
pathways.
−

 By reducing tissue requirements to 1 mg, the present method expands
the feasibility of longitudinal and patient-specific top-down proteomic
studies using endomyocardial biopsies to identify early proteoform
alterations and monitor molecular responses to therapeutic interventions
to therapy.

Conclusion

In this study, we developed
a streamlined
one-pot extraction method
for high-sensitivity TDP that enables reproducible detection and quantification
of PLN proteoforms from only 1 mg of human cardiac tissue. By implementing
the Azo-enabled one-pot extraction strategy with online RPLC-MS/MS
analysis, we achieved analytical performance comparable to our previously
established two-stage extraction method with a 10-fold reduction in
tissue requirements. The ability to reproducibly detect and comprehensively
characterize PLN proteoforms from biopsy-scale tissue inputs substantially
improves sample efficiency while preserving valuable clinical specimens.
These advances improve the feasibility of sensitive PLN proteoform
characterization from limited cardiac tissue inputs and provide a
foundation for future studies investigating proteoform remodeling
in cardiovascular diseases.
