Public Executive Summary
Light-emitting diodes (LEDs), in particular InGaN-based LED devices, have achieved remarkable
success in solid-state lighting, contributing to 25% of energy savings already. However, more than
two thirds of the electricity is still lost as heat in current white LEDs today. Furthermore, expected
population growth and increasing demand for lighting necessitate a more efficient approach,
which can only be realized by solving the issue of the green gap (i.e., the inefficiency of the state-
of-the-art green LEDs). This green gap is a fundamentally limiting issue in conventional hexagonal
nitride LEDs, leading to 19% wall-plug efficiency (WPE) at 100 A/cm2, while the department of
energy goal is 30% (in 2025) and 55% (in 2035).

Novel cubic nitride LEDs are proposed as a promising solution to the green gap, tripling the
efficiency with 30% less indium required in the green and beyond spectrum, due to well-
documented advantages in literature. However, there are theoretical and experimental issues in
the current approach that need to be innovated in order to translate these material advantages
into functional devices and address the green gap with the cubic nitride approach.

In this project, the realization of cubic nitride LEDs was attempted targeting a WPE of ≥ 50% under
injection current density of ≥ 100 A/cm2 and at a junction temperature of ≤ 85°C. The project
adds significant number of new insights on cubic nitride LEDs, including (i) a method to synthesize
phase-pure low-defect-density cubic gallium nitride (GaN), (ii) high-efficiency cubic green LED
design rules, (iii) revised material parameters that were misunderstood for over twenty (20)
years, and (iv) scaling down and enabling (sub)-micron cubic nitride devices. The effectiveness of
the technology is proved by the demonstration of efficiency six times higher than conventional
cubic nitride technologies, while economic feasibility is demonstrated via the patterned silicon
(100) substrate fabrication from a commercial CMOS foundry. The project also gives insight into
achieving high speed, high-efficiency light sources by scaling down the devices, potentially usable
for optical communication, and achieving cubic nitride power electronics, for true CMOS-
compatible GaN devices.

Accomplishments and Objectives
The first goal of the project was to prepare U-grooved Si substrates based on the PI’s patent (U.S.
Patent 10,027,086) with an error less than 3% in all design parameters. This was achieved by
collaborating with a CMOS fab to generate U-grooved silicon substrates with CMOS-compatible
process. The second technical goal was to demonstrate at least 80% of the U-grooves in a
die/wafer covered with 100% pure cubic GaN and grow novel cubic phase direct-emitting green
(530 ≤ λ ≤ 550) LED structures atop. The cubic GaN growth was achieved with 100 % purity and
green LED layers with 522 nm emission were grown with 16.0 ± 1.6 % indium content and
demonstrated 32.0 ± 0.6 % internal quantum efficiency, which is more than six times higher than
traditional cubic quantum wells. The third goal of the project was to fabricate and characterize
novel direct-emitting green LEDs, targeting direct-emitting green LED with a wall-plug efficiency
of ≥ 30% under injection current density of ≥ 100A/cm2 and at a junction temperature of ≤ 85°C.
The successful LED fabrication process ranging from large to micro- or submicron-LEDs was
demonstrated with EQE droop-free behavior.

This award allowed the University of Illinois to demonstrate several key objectives. The focus of
the project was on building a direct-emitting flip-chip bonded green LED with a wall-plug
efficiency of ≥ 50 % under injection current density of 100 A/cm2 and at a junction temperature
of ≤ 85°C.

 possible in conventional 3C-SiC platform but feasible in U-grooved Si

Project Activities
The project focuses on realizing high wall-plug-efficiency green LEDs based on cubic green nitride
technology, on scalable Si (100) platform with 100% phase-pure material. With a proprietary
aspect ratio phase trapping technique based on silicon patterning and proprietary design for
high-efficiency green active layers, the team achieved the demonstration of phase-pure cubic
active layer stripes emitting 522 nm on a 1 cm2 die with 16.0% of indium content, which has ~30
% lower indium (an expensive, rare element) content than traditional hexagonal LEDs for green
emission, and record 32% internal quantum efficiency, which is more than six times higher
efficiency than what is reported in the literature for conventional cubic active layers on 3C-SiC.
Further, electrically injected cubic green LEDs with droop-free nature has been demonstrated
and the path towards achieving higher efficiency and streamlining the process for
commercialization has been established.
