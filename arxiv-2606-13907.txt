Nonsimplicial toric Nullstellensatz and stacky GKZ theory
Christine Berkesch, Daniel Erman, David Favero

Abstract
. We introduce a variant of the Cox ring using ℚ \mathbb{Q} -Cartier divisors and use this to remedy various deficiencies of nonsimplicial toric varieties. Our main applications are: Cox’s ideal-variety correspondence, an explicit classification of subschemes and sheaves in terms of multigraded modules, an associated toric Deligne-Mumford stack, and a stacky extension of the GKZ/Mori theory of toric varieties.

To begin, note that one key property of quotient stacks (all stacks in this paper are quotient stacks), which is that quasi-coherent sheaves on a quotient

are naturally equivalent to

.
This allows for concrete descriptions of sheaves and subschemes in terms of ideals and graded modules on
\check{S}
(or

For background on stacks, there are several major references such as
[
21

,
7
,
3

Let

denote a category of quasi-coherent sheaves.
Further, write
\operatorname{Mod}_{\operatorname{Cl}(X)}(S)
for the category of
\operatorname{Cl}(X)

\operatorname{Mod}_{\operatorname{Cl}(X),B}(S)
for the full subcategory of modules

, where each element

.

When

, it is well known that the category

is the Verdier quotient
\operatorname{Mod}_{\mathbb{Z}}(S)/\operatorname{Mod}_{\mathbb{Z},\mathfrak{m}}(S)
, where
\mathfrak{m}
denotes the graded maximal ideal of

.
In short, studying quasi-coherent sheaves on

is equivalent to studying
\mathbb{Z}
-graded

\mathfrak{m}
-torsion

is simplicial, but not smooth, then the corresponding result is slightly more complicated. In this case, the category of sheaves on the canonical stack

is more closely connected to multigraded commutative algebra than the category of sheaves on the variety

.
The following result is essentially a stacky rephrasement of Cox’s results about sheaves on simplicial toric varieties

]
.

Proposition 3.1

(Module-Sheaf Correspondence, Simplicial Case)
.

If
X
is a simplicial toric variety, then there are equivalences of categories:

\operatorname{QCoh}({\mathcal{X}}_{\mathrm{can}})=\frac{\operatorname{Mod}_{\operatorname{Cl}(X)}(S)}{\operatorname{Mod}_{\operatorname{Cl}(X),B}(S)}\quad\text{ and }\quad\operatorname{QCoh}(X)=\frac{\operatorname{Mod}_{\operatorname{Pic}}(S_{\operatorname{Pic}})}{\operatorname{Mod}_{\operatorname{Pic},B_{\operatorname{Pic}}}(S_{\operatorname{Pic}})}.

Thus, if one is interested in understanding modules over the multigraded polynomial ring
S
, then
{\mathcal{X}}_{\mathrm{can}}
is the “right” geometric object to consider, whereas
X
itself is more closely associated to the study of modules over the subring
S_{\operatorname{Pic}}
.

Example 3.2
.

Let

be a weighted projective variety.
In this case, its Cox ring is

with degrees

, while

is the Veronese subring consisting of all degrees that are multiples of

, so that

.

1.6

,

, and

in that example) all yield the same ideal sheaf on

, since

implies that the ideals

,
I_{2}
, and

each determine distinct sheaves on the stack

.
Thus the stack

is more closely tied to the regular ring

, where the variety

is more closely tied to the singular subring

.
∎

Proposition 3.4

If

\widehat{H}

is the category of
H
-equivariant quasi-coherent sheaves on
\operatorname{Spec}(R)

Proof.

By definition, for a diagonalizable algebraic group,

\Gamma(H,{\mathcal{O}}_{H})=\mathbb{C}[\widehat{H}]=\bigoplus_{\chi\in\widehat{H}}\mathbb{C}.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be an
H
-equivariant quasi-coherent sheaf on
\operatorname{Spec}(R)
, meaning there is an isomorphism
\varphi\colon\sigma^{*}\mathcal{F}\to p^{*}\mathcal{F}
, where
\sigma,p\colon G\times Y\to Y

denote the group action map and projection onto the second factor, respectively.
Setting

,
\Gamma(\varphi)
induces a map

\displaystyle\nu_{M}=\Gamma(\varphi)|_{M}\colon M\subseteq M[\widehat{H}]

\displaystyle\to M[\widehat{H}]=\bigoplus_{\chi\in\widehat{H}}M\otimes\chi,

\displaystyle m\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle\mapsto\qquad\qquad\textstyle\sum m_{\chi}\otimes\chi.

Thus

has the structure of a
\widehat{H}
-graded

-module via

.

Conversely, a

-graded

induces a map

where a homogeneous

has
\nu_{M}(m\otimes 1)=m\otimes\chi
. Taking the associated sheaf then induces an element of

Hence the usual equivalence
