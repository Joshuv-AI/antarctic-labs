Public Executive Summary
The project, "Advanced Facility Design and AI/ML Enabled Safeguards to Establish Secure,
Economical Recycling of Fast Reactor Fuels," represents a significant advancement in nuclear
fuel recycling technology. It integrates cutting-edge multimodal sensor fusion, machine learning
(ML), and digital twin (DT) technologies to address challenges in material safeguarding, process
optimization, and regulatory compliance for pyroprocessing facilities. This research has
significantly enhanced the understanding of pyrochemical fuel recycling processes by
developing innovative tools and methodologies. The Multimodal Safeguards Monitoring Unit
(MSMU) combines electroanalytical techniques, Raman spectroscopy, and differential thermal
analysis (DTA) to enable high-fidelity, near-real-time material accountancy measurements.
Machine learning techniques, such as Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) autoencoders, are
utilized to detect anomalies in material balances and sensor data, improving the reliability of
safeguards monitoring. Additionally, digital twin technology has been established to provide
real-time system-level monitoring and diagnostics, integrating physics-based models with
sensor data to optimize process safety and efficiency.
The project has demonstrated the technical effectiveness and economic feasibility of combined
multimodal monitoring tools, sensor fusion, and digital twins in support of fuel reprocessing.
The MSMU enables near-real-time monitoring with high precision, comparable to traditional
off-line methods like ICP-OES, but with significantly reduced analysis time. Sensor fusion
improves anomaly detection accuracy, as evidenced by high Area Under Curve (AUC) values in
Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) analyses. Digital twins enhance diagnostics and
operational efficiency by integrating real-time data with predictive models. Automation of
monitoring and analysis reduces staffing and manual activities, lowering operational costs.
Optimized sensor sets minimize the number of sensors required, further reducing costs while
maintaining high diagnostic capabilities. Furthermore, the project's Techno-Economic
Assessment (TEA) modeling and commercialization outreach have provided reliable cost
estimates, supporting the economic viability of deploying a commercial-scale recycling facility.
The project offers substantial benefits to the public. By enabling the recycling of fast reactor
fuels, it reduces nuclear waste volume and radiotoxicity, contributing to sustainable nuclear
energy practices. The development of an economical front-end fuel supply chain and a
responsible back-end closed fuel cycle supports the deployment of advanced nuclear reactors,
enhancing energy security. The integration of safeguards technologies ensures adherence to
domestic regulations, fostering public trust in nuclear energy. Additionally, the selection of Oak
Ridge, TN, as the site for a commercial recycling facility has shown that reprocessing provides
pathways for economic growth through job creation and investment.
In conclusion, this project has successfully demonstrated innovative technologies that address
critical challenges in nuclear fuel recycling. Its contributions to science, technology, and public
welfare position it as a transformative initiative in the field of advanced nuclear energy
systems.

Machine Learning
This work concerns deep learning applied to molten salt chemometric data for a
reconstruction-based anomaly detection algorithm utilizing a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM)
autoencoder, a specific type of artificial neural network (ANN) architecture. The basis of a
reconstruction-based anomaly detection algorithm is to train a model on normal operation
data, using statistical techniques to analyze the discrepancy or loss between the model
prediction and the true value. For a well-trained model, if a given unseen sample is similar to
the training data, then the loss for that sample should be significantly lower compared to a
sample that is dissimilar to the training data. We can utilize the historical losses from the
training data to build a threshold based on some sufficiently large standard deviation, where
any loss above that threshold is a statistically significant anomaly. This methodology assumes
that our model is well-trained, where the success of reconstruction-based anomaly detection is
tied to the predictive capabilities of the model. LSTM neurons are a specific type of ANN which
have been shown to be state of the art for any sort of sequential data, which is true for LSV,
Raman spectroscopy, and DTA data. An autoencoder is a specific compression learning ANN
architecture where layers are arranged such that data is first compressed through increasingly
small layers in a substructure known as an encoder. The data is then re-expanded back to the
original dimensions through an opposing decoder substructure. Compression learning forces
the model to learn the patterns of normal operation, which is ideal for anomaly detection as

Electrorefiner Real Time Digital Twin
The real-time electrorefiner digital twin is a computational model that simulates the
electrochemical operation of an electrorefiner used in pyroprocessing for nuclear fuel recycling.
The model couples electrode reaction kinetics, described by Butler–Volmer equations, with
mass transfer processes governed by Fick’s first law of diffusion. Under steady-state conditions,
it equates the partial currents from electrode kinetics and mass transfer to solve for electrode
potentials, species concentrations, and deposition rates. The model is implemented in Python
within the PRO-AID platform and solves the electrochemical and mass balance equations in real
time. Its governing equations are derived from the validated DyER submodel of the AMPYRE
code developed at Argonne National Laboratory.
The model assumes solid electrodes operating in constant-current mode. Post-electron-transfer
chemical reactions and surface processes (e.g., adsorption and crystallization) are assumed to
have negligible time constants; thus, system dynamics are governed primarily by electron
transfer kinetics and diffusion through the boundary layer between the electrode surface and
the bulk salt. The diffusion layer thickness for each species is assumed constant.
The model was verified against test results from DyER. Its performance was further
demonstrated in a scenario where fresh KCl–LiCl salt, corresponding to a 10% increase in moles,
was added during operation. The digital twin correctly predicted an approximately 10%
increase in salt volume, along with corresponding changes in bulk uranium concentration and
cathode deposition rates.

Electrorefiner Lumped Parameter Model
To support real-time fault diagnostics of the electrorefiner during steady-state operation, a
lumped-parameter model was developed. This simplified analytical model predicts ER cell
voltage as a function of applied current and bulk uranium concentration in the salt. The total
cell voltage is expressed as the sum of electrode overpotentials and the ohmic drop across the
salt. Each electrode overpotential includes an activation component (from charge transfer
kinetics) and a concentration component (from the concentration gradient between the
electrode surface and the bulk salt).
The model assumes the applied current is sufficiently high to justify a Tafel approximation of
the Butler–Volmer equation for activation overpotentials, and that operation remains well
below the limiting current, allowing simplification of concentration overpotential expressions
derived from the Nernst equation. These assumptions yield a compact parametric form for total

Project Activities
The project focused on developing innovative technologies to support the commercialization
and licensing of pyroprocessing for advanced nuclear fuel recycling. Major approaches included
the deployment of multimodal monitoring approaches (via the MSMU) for real-time material
accountancy, the development of the machine learning (ML) long short term memory (LSTM)
autoencoder for sensor fusion and anomaly detection, and digital twin (DT) models for
diagnostics and optimization. The MSMU demonstrated remarkable efficiency, completing its
full analysis suite in just 19 minutes, while the LSTM autoencoder achieved near-real-time data
analysis and anomaly detection in only 6 seconds. The digital twin played a critical role by
enabling explainable reasoning and diagnostics through the integration of measurement data
with physics-based models and determining the optimized sensor set for the target system
based on diagnostic needs. This project leveraged AI/ML to enhance safeguards, reduce false
positives, and optimize sensor configurations for efficient monitoring. High-level results
demonstrate the successful integration of MSMU with an engineering scale electrorefiner,
achieving near-real-time data analysis, improved anomaly detection through multimodal sensor
fusion, and validated DT models for process monitoring. These advancements collectively
enable economical recycling of fast reactor fuels while adhering to stringent regulatory
requirements, paving the way for sustainable nuclear energy deployment.
