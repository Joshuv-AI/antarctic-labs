GRALIS: A Unified Canonical Framework for Linear Attribution Methods via Riesz Representation
Raimondo Fanale

Abstract
The main Explainable AI (XAI) methods for deep neural networks — GradCAM, SHAP, LIME, Integrated Gradients — operate on separate theoretical foundations and are not formally comparable. This work presents GRALIS (Gradient-Riesz Averaged Locally-Integrated Shapley), a mathematical framework that unifies a broad class of linear attribution methods — including SHAP, IG, LIME and linearized GradCAM — establishing a representation theory for attributions : every additive, linear, and continuous attribution functional in L 2 ​ ( 𝒬 , μ ) L^{2}(\mathcal{Q},\mu) — where 𝒬 \mathcal{Q} is the index space induced by the attribution mechanism (integration paths, coalitions, or feature maps) — admits a unique canonical representation of the form ( 𝒬 , w , Δ ) (\mathcal{Q},w,\Delta) , with w ∈ L 2 ​ ( 𝒬 ) w\in L^{2}(\mathcal{Q}) a weight function and Δ \Delta a marginal contribution. This class includes SHAP, IG and LIME, but not nonlinear functionals such as standard GradCAM, attention maps, or saliency methods with smoothing. Seven formal theorems provide simultaneous guarantees absent in any individual method: (T1) necessary canonical form via the Riesz Theorem; (T2) exact completeness; (T3) Monte Carlo convergence with bound O ⁡ ( 1 / m ) + O ⁡ ( 1 / k ) O(1/\sqrt{m})+O(1/k) ; (T4) exact Shapley Interaction Values; (T5) Hoeffding ANOVA decomposition; (T6) generalization of Sobol sensitivity indices; (T7) multi-scale extension (MS- GRALIS ) with minimum-variance weights. Appendix A provides the algebraic justification of the GRALIS–SIV correspondence via the Möbius transform, showing that GRALIS constructs a cooperative game v G v_{G} and computes exactly the SIVs on v G v_{G} , without circularity. GRALIS satisfies a broader set of axiomatic and structural properties than any existing method (Table 5 ), combining for the first time completeness, sensitivity, locality, order- k k interactions and multi-scale aggregation with optimal weights in a single framework. Preliminary experimental validation on breast histology (BreaKHis, 1,187 images, DenseNet-121) is reported in Section 5 ; extended comparison with baseline XAI methods is reported in the companion paper [ 30 ] .

Introduction

Deep learning has achieved accuracies surpassing those of human
specialists in numerous medical imaging tasks.
However, the
black-box
nature of deep neural networks makes
it impossible for a clinician to understand
why
a model produced
a given prediction.
Explainable AI (XAI) has responded with heterogeneous post-hoc methods
[
28
]
:
GradCAM
[
1
]
identifies the most influential regions via
gradients of the last convolutional layer; SHAP
[
2
]

assigns Shapley values to features via kernel approximation;
LIME
[
3
]
locally approximates the model with a
linear classifier; Integrated Gradients
[
4
]

integrates the gradients along the path from baseline to input.
Each is developed on distinct foundations with different guarantees,
making systematic comparison non-rigorous.

This fragmentation has direct practical consequences: the choice of
XAI method is often empirical, attribution maps from different methods
are not formally comparable, and the combination of multiple methods
lacks a unifying mathematical justification.
Prior unification attempts have been partial:
Ancona et al.
[
5
]
observe that GradCAM and related
gradient methods are
empirically
expressible as
gradient
\times
input—a linear form—but do not prove this is

structurally necessary
, and do not include SHAP or LIME.
Covert and Lee
[
9
]
unify LIME, SHAP and IG via Shapley
games, but cannot accommodate GradCAM because its post-aggregation

\mathrm{ReLU}
violates the linearity required by the framework.

This work presents
GRALIS
, a framework that resolves the
fragmentation by demonstrating that a broad class of linear additive
attribution methods — including SHAP, IG, LIME and linearized GradCAM —
are special cases of a unique canonical structure:

\phi_{i}^{\textsc{GRALIS}}(f,x,x^{\prime})\;=\;\int_{\mathcal{Q}}w(q)\cdot\Delta_{i}(f,x,x^{\prime},q)\,d\mu(q),

(1)

where
\mathcal{Q}
is the integration index space,
w
is the
weight function and
\Delta_{i}
is the marginal contribution of feature
i
.
The Riesz Representation Theorem guarantees that this form is the

unique
possible representation for every
additive
linear
and continuous attribution functional (Theorem
3.4
).

2
Background and Related Work

2.1
XAI Attribution Methods

An attribution method assigns to each input feature a score
representing its contribution to the prediction.
Formally, given
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
and an input
x
with baseline
x^{\prime}
,
the method produces
\phi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\phi_{i}
represents
the importance of feature
i
.

GradCAM
[
1
]
.

Computes
s_{pq}=\sum_{k}\alpha_{k}^{c}\cdot A^{k}_{pq}
where

\alpha_{k}^{c}=\frac{1}{Z}\sum_{i,j}\frac{\partial y^{c}}{\partial A^{k}_{ij}}
.
Computationally efficient (
O(1)
backward pass) but does not satisfy
completeness or locality.
For Theorem
3.4
we use
GradCAM-lin
,
the pre-ReLU variant defined as

L^{c}_{\mathrm{lin}}(p,q)\;=\;\sum_{k}\alpha_{k}^{c}\,A^{k}_{pq},\qquad\alpha_{k}^{c}=\frac{1}{Z}\sum_{i,j}\frac{\partial y^{c}}{\partial A^{k}_{ij}},

which omits the post-aggregation
\mathrm{ReLU}
of standard GradCAM.
The
\mathrm{ReLU}
in
[
1
]
is a
visualization heuristic

that retains only channels with positive influence on the target class;
it is not an axiomatic requirement, and it introduces a pointwise
nonlinearity on the output of a linear operator that destroys linearity
in
f
(see Remark
3.5
below).

GradCAM-lin corresponds precisely to the first-order Taylor expansion of

y^{c}
around the zero-activation reference
\bar{A}^{k}=0
:

y^{c}\;\approx\;y^{c}\big|_{A=0}+\sum_{k,i,j}\frac{\partial y^{c}}{\partial A^{k}_{ij}}\,A^{k}_{ij},

(2)

whose spatial summary at position
(p,q)
—obtained by pooling

A^{k}_{ij}
contributions over all
(i,j)
with uniform weight
1/Z
—is
exactly
L^{c}_{\mathrm{lin}}(p,q)
.
This linearization is consistent with the gradient
\times
input
interpretation of GradCAM established by Ancona et al.
[
5
]
,
with the deep Taylor decomposition framework of
Montavon et al.
[
6
]
, and with the gradient-based
saliency maps of Simonyan et al.
[
7
]
,
all of which operate on the pre-nonlinearity linear term.
Standard GradCAM is recovered
exactly
from GradCAM-lin when

\alpha_{k}^{c}\geq 0
for all
k
(every channel contributes positively to
class
c
, a condition met by the majority of channels in
well-trained classifiers
[
1
]
), and the two methods agree
to first order in the Taylor expansion whenever some
\alpha_{k}^{c}<0
.

Remark on implementation.

When GradCAM-lin is computed on the
pre-activation
feature maps

Z^{k}_{pq}
of the last convolutional layer—i.e. before the network’s
internal ReLU rather than after it—the expansion (
2
)
holds
exactly
, with no first-order qualification:

L^{c}_{\mathrm{lin,pre}}(p,q)\;=\;\sum_{k}\alpha_{k}^{c}\,Z^{k}_{pq},\quad Z^{k}_{pq}\in\mathbb{R}.

Since
Z^{k}_{pq}
is unconstrained in sign, the weighted sum is a
genuine linear functional of
f
in all cases, and standard GradCAM
is recovered precisely when the network’s internal ReLU is inactive
(
Z^{k}_{pq}\geq 0
) and
\alpha_{k}^{c}\geq 0
, i.e. when the network
itself is locally linear at that layer.
This implementation choice is available in any standard deep learning
framework and does not alter the computational cost relative to
standard GradCAM.

SHAP
[
2
]
.

The Shapley value for feature
i
is its average marginal contribution
over all coalitions:

\phi_{i}=\sum_{S\subseteq\mathcal{F}\setminus\{i\}}\frac{|S|!(n-|S|-1)!}{n!}[f(S\cup\{i\})-f(S)]
.
Satisfies efficiency, symmetry, dummy and additivity, but exact computation
is
O(2^{n})
; KernelSHAP approximates with
O(m\cdot n)
.

LIME
[
3
]
.

Locally approximates
f
with a linear model:

w^{*}=\arg\min_{g\in\mathcal{G}}L(f,g,\pi_{x})+\Omega(g)
,
where
\pi_{x}
is a proximity kernel.
The conditions of Theorem
3.4
are satisfied
within the local approximation (neighborhood
N_{x}
defined by
\pi_{x}
).

Integrated Gradients
[
4
]
.

\phi_{i}(f,x)=(x_{i}-x^{\prime}_{i})\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial f(x^{\prime}+\alpha(x-x^{\prime}))}{\partial x_{i}}\,d\alpha
.
Satisfies completeness and sensitivity; the numerical approximation introduces
error
O(1/k)
.

2.2
Unification Attempts and Existing Gap

The gradient
\times
input family established by
Ancona et al.
[
5
]
provides the closest prior bridge to
our linearization.
They show that GradCAM (and guided backpropagation, plain gradients)
can be expressed as
\phi_{i}\propto\nabla_{i}f(x)\cdot x_{i}
, i.e. as a
pointwise product of gradient and input
,
which is structurally a linear functional of
f
.
This is an important empirical observation: it reveals that
the GradCAM family is
de facto
operating in the linear regime.
However, Ancona et al. establish this as a family relationship
among existing methods; they do not prove that linearity is

structurally necessary
—i.e., that any valid additive attribution
must take this form—nor do they include SHAP and LIME in the same
algebraic framework.

Lundstrom et al.
[
10
]
extend the gradient
\times
input
analysis to internal neuron attributions, showing that the first-order
(linear) term is the unique term satisfying path-completeness at every
intermediate layer.
This further supports the restriction to GradCAM-lin: the Taylor
linearization is not an ad hoc simplification but the canonical form
that preserves completeness at the feature-map level.

GradCAM has demonstrated strong empirical reliability across localization
benchmarks.
The ROAR/KAR evaluation of Hooker et al.
[
12
]
shows that
GradCAM outperforms pixel-level gradient methods precisely because its
global average pooling of feature-map gradients provides robustness to
local gradient noise — a result that confirms practical reliability
while leaving axiomatic properties unaddressed.
The breadth of GradCAM’s adoption has generated a progressive line of
refinements, each targeting a specific structural limitation without
questioning practical utility.
GradCAM++
[
8
]
introduces non-uniform gradient
weights to improve localization of multiple object instances.
Score-CAM
[
13
]
replaces gradients entirely with
activation perturbations, obtaining gradient-free class-discriminative
maps at the cost of
O(k)
forward passes.
Axiom-based Grad-CAM
[
14
]
adds a completeness correction
term to restore the efficiency axiom, explicitly acknowledging that
standard GradCAM violates it.
HiResCAM
[
15
]
modifies channel aggregation with an
element-wise product before pooling to improve spatial faithfulness.
All these variants retain the post-aggregation
\mathrm{ReLU}
as a
class-discriminative visualization choice, confirming that the community
regards it as a valid design option rather than a defect.

The limitation relevant to GRALIS is therefore not one of empirical
reliability but of
axiomatic incompatibility
.
The post-aggregation
\mathrm{ReLU}
introduces a nonlinearity that,
as shown in Remark
3.5
, prevents GradCAM from
satisfying the linearity condition required for a Riesz representation
— a structural barrier that neither GradCAM++ nor Score-CAM nor the
axiom correction of
[
14
]
resolves, because all retain
the same nonlinear post-processing step.
The critique of Kindermans et al.
[
11
]
targets a
different issue — input-shift sensitivity of pixel-level saliency maps
and guided backpropagation — and does not apply to GradCAM directly,
which operates on feature maps rather than input pixels.
GradCAM-lin is therefore not a critique of GradCAM but its

canonical extension
: the unique member of the GradCAM family
that satisfies the mathematical conditions for formal unification,
recovering standard GradCAM exactly when all channel weights

\alpha_{k}^{c}\geq 0
.

Covert and Lee
[
9
]
show that LIME, SHAP and IG are instances of
the same “Shapley games” framework, but exclude GradCAM and do not
cover the formal properties of convergence and ANOVA.

GRALIS
resolves the limitations of both lines of work.
The gradient
\times
input observation of Ancona et al. is subsumed:
GradCAM-lin is the canonical representative of the GradCAM family
within
(\mathcal{Q},w,\Delta)
, and its gradient
\times
input
structure follows from the triple
w=1/Z
,

\Delta_{k,i,j}=(\partial y^{c}/\partial A^{k}_{ij})\cdot A^{k}_{pq}

(Table
3
).
The Shapley-game unification of Covert and Lee is also subsumed,
and extended with a
necessary
canonical form (Theorem
3.4
):
where prior work shows that certain methods
can
be written as
weighted integrals, GRALIS proves they
must
be—and that the
canonical representation is unique.
No prior work produces a necessary canonical form that simultaneously
encompasses GradCAM, SHAP, LIME and IG, and provides completeness,
convergence and an interaction hierarchy.

2.3
Gap Analysis: Six Structural Gaps

Despite sharing the same canonical form
(\mathcal{Q},w,\Delta)
,
the four methods leave six structural gaps uncovered.

Gap 1 — Arbitrary baseline in IG.

IG requires a fixed baseline
x^{\prime}
(e.g. a black image).
The choice drastically changes the attributions:

\mathrm{IG}_{i}(x,x^{\prime}_{1})\neq\mathrm{IG}_{i}(x,x^{\prime}_{2})
for
x^{\prime}_{1}\neq x^{\prime}_{2}
.
No method integrates over the baseline distribution in a structured way.

Gap 2 — SHAP ignores curvature between coalitions.

SHAP compares
f(S\cup\{i\})
vs
f(S)
but does not consider

how
one moves from
S
to
S\cup\{i\}
:

\phi_{i}^{\mathrm{SHAP}}=\mathbb{E}_{S}[f(S\cup i)-f(S)]\;\not\supset\;\int_{0}^{1}\nabla_{i}F(\cdot)\,d\alpha
.

Gap 3 — LIME does not satisfy completeness.

The coefficients of the local surrogate do not sum to
F(x)-F(x^{\prime})
:

\sum_{i}w_{i}^{\mathrm{LIME}}\neq F(x)-F(x^{\prime})
.

Gap 4 — GradCAM confined to CNN space.

Operates on
A^{k}
(convolutional feature maps), not on the input space.
Not applicable to dense layers, Transformers or pure GNNs.
Does not satisfy any Shapley axiom.
MS-
GRALIS
(T.
3.19
) partially resolves this gap:
by aggregating attributions over multiple levels (
\ell=1,\ldots,L
), it includes
CNN feature maps, dense layers and attention with optimal weights.
The requirement of differentiability of
F
on intermediate representations
remains (
\approx
).

Gap 5 — No method models feature interactions.

All produce marginal attributions
s_{i}
for a single feature.
The pairwise interaction
\Phi_{ij}=s_{ij}-s_{i}-s_{j}\neq 0

is not captured in an integrated way.
SHAP-Interaction exists but does not integrate gradients or locality,
with additional cost
O(2^{n})
.

Gap 6 — No multi-scale aggregation.

No method aggregates attributions over multiple levels of abstraction
with mathematically motivated weights.
GradCAM++
[
8
]
uses heuristic weights without axiomatic guarantees.

The GRALIS Framework

3.1
Definitions and Notation

Definition 3.1

(GRALIS explicit formula)
.

The explicit formula of GRALIS combines three components:

\mathrm{GRALIS}_{i}(x)\;=\;\frac{1}{Z_{x}}\sum_{S\subseteq\mathcal{F}\setminus\{i\}}\underbrace{\frac{|S|!(n-|S|-1)!}{n!}}_{\text{Shapley weight}}\cdot\;\underbrace{\pi_{x}(x_{S})}_{\text{LIME kernel}}\cdot\;\underbrace{(x_{i}-x^{\prime}_{i})\int_{0}^{1}\nabla_{i}F(\tilde{x}^{(S,\alpha)})\,d\alpha}_{\text{IG conditioned on }S},

(3)

where
Z_{x}=\sum_{S\subseteq\mathcal{F}}\frac{|S|!(n-|S|-1)!}{n!}\,\pi_{x}(x_{S})

and the evaluation point is

\tilde{x}^{(S,\alpha)}_{j}=x^{\prime}_{j}+\alpha(x_{j}-x^{\prime}_{j})
if
j\in S\cup\{i\}
,

x^{\prime}_{j}
otherwise.
The IG path is not global, but
conditioned on coalition
S
:
features outside
S
remain at the baseline during integration.

Decomposition of the three components.

•

Shapley weight

w(S)=|S|!(n-|S|-1)!/n!
:
guarantees symmetry and the dummy axiom; averages over all
feature insertion orders.

•

LIME kernel

\pi_{x}(x_{S})=\exp(-\left\|x_{S}-x^{\prime}_{S}\right\|^{2}/2\sigma^{2})
:
penalizes coalitions far from
x
; introduces locality.
With
\sigma\to\infty
one obtains KernelSHAP; with
\sigma\to 0
LIME.

•

Conditioned IG
: captures the curvature of
F
along the path
specific to each coalition (Gap 2 filled).
By inheriting the FTC, it guarantees completeness (Gap 3 filled).

The canonical triple of GRALIS is

\mathcal{Q}=2^{\mathcal{F}\setminus\{i\}}\times[0,1]
,

w_{S,\alpha}=\tilde{w}(S)\,d\alpha
,

\Delta^{(i)}_{S,\alpha}=(x_{i}-x^{\prime}_{i})\,\nabla_{i}F(\tilde{x}^{(S,\alpha)})
,
strictly richer than the triples of GradCAM, SHAP, LIME and IG individually.

Remark 3.2

(Relation between explicit formula and canonical form)
.

Definition
3.1
specifies
how
to compute GRALIS in
practice: it provides the computational formula and the decomposition into
three components. Definition
3.3
below establishes

why
this structure is
necessary
: Theorem
3.4

will prove that any
additive
linear and continuous attribution method on

L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})
must have exactly this form, by the Riesz Theorem.
The explicit formula corresponds to the canonical triple with

\mathcal{Q}=2^{\mathcal{F}\setminus\{i\}}\times[0,1]
.

Definition 3.3

(GRALIS canonical structure)
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a model and let
(\mathcal{Q},\Sigma,\mu)
be a

\sigma
-finite measure space fixed a priori.
The GRALIS
canonical structure
is a triple
(\mathcal{Q},w,\Delta)
where:

•

\mathcal{Q}
is the integration index space (continuous or discrete);

•

w:\mathcal{Q}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is the weight function with
\int_{\mathcal{Q}}w(q)\,d\mu(q)=1
;

•

\Delta:\mathcal{Q}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the marginal contribution function subject to the

constitutive constraint
:

\sum_{i}\Delta_{i}(f,x,x^{\prime},q)=f(x)-f(x^{\prime})
for
\mu
-a.e.
q\in\mathcal{Q}
.

The GRALIS attribution for feature
i
is:

\phi_{i}^{\textsc{GRALIS}}(f,x,x^{\prime})\;=\;\int_{\mathcal{Q}}w(q)\cdot\Delta_{i}(f,x,x^{\prime},q)\,d\mu(q).

In the discrete case (
\mathcal{Q}
finite or countable), the integral is
understood as a sum and
L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})=\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{Q}|}
; the Riesz
Theorem applies in both cases.

3.2
Theorem 1: Unified Canonical Form (Riesz)

Theorem 3.4

(Canonical Form — Riesz)
.

Let
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{Q})\subseteq L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})
be the closed subspace of
admissible marginal functions.
An attribution method
\phi
satisfying the conditions:

(a)

\mathcal{Q}
is a
measurable index space

(\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{A},\mu)

fixed a priori, independently of the specific input
x
.

\mathcal{Q}

is not arbitrary
: it is
induced
by the attribution mechanism,
and GRALIS operates conditionally on this choice.
Concretely: for Integrated Gradients,
\mathcal{Q}=[0,1]
(path)
with Lebesgue measure; for SHAP,
\mathcal{Q}=2^{N}
(coalitions)
with counting measure; for GRALIS,
\mathcal{Q}
is the image space
with measure induced by the superpixel segmentation.
The framework only requires that
\mu
be
\sigma
-finite and that

w\cdot\Delta\in L^{2}(\mathcal{Q},\mu)
;

(b)

the evaluation map
E_{i}:\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{Q})\to\mathbb{R}
,

E_{i}(\Delta_{i}):=\phi_{i}(f,x,x^{\prime};\Delta_{i})
,
is linear and continuous with respect to the
L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})
norm;

(c)

\Delta
satisfies the constitutive constraint of Definition
3.3
;

admits a unique representation:

\phi_{i}(f,x,x^{\prime})\;=\;\int_{\mathcal{Q}}w(q)\cdot\Delta_{i}(q)\,d\mu(q)

for a unique
w\in L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})
.

Proof.

Step 1 — Linearity and continuity.

The functional
\Phi_{i}[\Delta_{i}]=\int_{\mathcal{Q}}w(q)\cdot\Delta_{i}(q)\,d\mu(q)

is linear by linearity of the integral, and continuous:

|\Phi_{i}[\Delta_{i}]|\leq\left\|w\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\Delta_{i}\right\|_{L^{2}}
by Cauchy-Schwarz.

Step 2 — Uniqueness via Riesz.

By the Riesz Representation Theorem
[
26
,
27
]
, every
continuous linear functional
\Phi_{i}:L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})\to\mathbb{R}
admits
a unique representation
\Phi_{i}[g]=\langle g,w\rangle_{L^{2}}=\int_{\mathcal{Q}}g(q)\cdot w(q)\,d\mu(q)
for a unique

w\in L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})
.
Under conditions (a)–(c),
\Phi_{i}
is such a functional.
The representation is unique

the selected attribution

\mathcal{Q}
, measure

; different choices of

•

.

•

SHAP
(Case 2):

\phi_{i}=\sum_{S\subseteq\mathcal{F}\setminus\{i\}}\underbrace{\tfrac{|S|!(n-|S|-1)!}{n!}}_{w(S)}\cdot\underbrace{[f(S\cup\{i\})-f(S)]}_{\Delta^{(i)}_{S}(f)}

Remark 3.5

(Why standard GradCAM falls outside Theorem
3.4
)
.

Let
L(f)=\sum_{k}\alpha_{k}^{c}(f)\,A^{k}_{pq}
denote the linear aggregation
computed by GradCAM-lin, where
\alpha_{k}^{c}(f)
is linear in
f
by
linearity of differentiation.
Standard GradCAM applies
\mathrm{ReLU}
pointwise
after
this
aggregation:
\Phi_{\mathrm{GC}}(f)=\mathrm{ReLU}(L(f))
.
This composite map is
not
linear in
f
: for any
\lambda<0
,

\Phi_{\mathrm{GC}}(\lambda f)=\mathrm{ReLU}(\lambda\,L(f))\;\neq\;\lambda\,\mathrm{ReLU}(L(f))=\lambda\,\Phi_{\mathrm{GC}}(f)

whenever
L(f)>0
.
Hence condition (a) of Theorem
3.4
(linearity in
f
)
fails for standard GradCAM, and the Riesz representation cannot be applied.

GradCAM-lin, which sets
\Phi_{\mathrm{lin}}(f)=L(f)
directly,
satisfies conditions (a)–(c) and is therefore the unique

linear representative
of the GradCAM family admissible within
the GRALIS framework.
The omission of the post-aggregation
\mathrm{ReLU}
is not an
arbitrary simplification: it is the minimal modification required to
restore the mathematical structure (linearity and continuity) on which
the canonical form and all downstream theorems depend.
The
\mathrm{ReLU}
in
[
1
]
is a deliberate
class-discriminative design choice: it retains only channels that
contribute positively to the target class, producing heat maps that
are widely effective in practice
[
12
]
.
This choice is entirely valid in the visualization context.
However, it is not an axiomatic requirement of attribution: it
introduces a signed-threshold effect that is incompatible with the
linearity condition of Theorem
3.4
, and it prevents
formal comparison with SHAP, IG and LIME, all of which preserve the
full signed attribution signal.
GradCAM-lin retains the signed channels, consistent with the
gradient
\times
input formulation of Ancona et al.
[
5
]

and the deep Taylor decomposition of Montavon et al.
[
6
]
,
both of which operate on the pre-nonlinearity linear term.
The practical cost is minimal: as noted in Section
2.1
,
standard GradCAM is recovered exactly when
\alpha_{k}^{c}\geq 0
for
all
k
, the common case in well-trained classifiers.

Two further points help clarify the scope of this remark.
First,
GRALIS
is an independent method that does not rely on GradCAM
for its operation (Algorithm 1 proceeds via Monte Carlo integration
over Shapley coalitions): the inclusion of GradCAM-lin in
Theorem
3.4
is a theoretical consequence showing that
the GradCAM family, once linearized, naturally fits the canonical
framework, not a computational dependency.
Second, when GradCAM-lin is implemented on pre-activation feature maps

Z^{k}_{pq}
(before the network’s internal ReLU), the linearity condition
holds unconditionally and the Taylor expansion is exact; standard GradCAM
is then a special case corresponding to the regime where the network is
locally linear at the chosen layer.
Both points address the concern that GradCAM-lin might be valid only
under the condition
\alpha_{k}^{c}\geq 0
: the theoretical scope of
Theorem
3.4
does not depend on this condition,
and the pre-activation implementation removes it from the practical
scope as well.

Lemma 3.6

(Combinatorial identity — Shapley weights)
.

For every fixed feature
i
, the Shapley weights form a probability
distribution over the
2^{n-1}
coalitions of
\mathcal{F}\setminus\{i\}
:

\sum_{S\subseteq\mathcal{F}\setminus\{i\}}\frac{|S|!(n-|S|-1)!}{n!}\;=\;1.

Proof.

Group by cardinality
k=|S|\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}
, noting that there are

\binom{n-1}{k}
subsets of
\mathcal{F}\setminus\{i\}
of cardinality
k
:

\displaystyle\sum_{S\subseteq\mathcal{F}\setminus\{i\}}w(S)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{k}\cdot\frac{k!(n-k-1)!}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{(n-1)!}{k!(n-1-k)!}\cdot\frac{k!(n-k-1)!}{n!}.

Since
n-k-1=n-1-k
, we have
(n-k-1)!=(n-1-k)!
, so each term equals:

\frac{(n-1)!}{n!}=\frac{1}{n}.

The sum over
k=0,\ldots,n-1
contains
n
terms, each equal to
1/n
:

\sum_{S}w(S)=n\cdot\frac{1}{n}=1.\qquad\square

∎

the GRALIS formula is a convex weighted average of marginal contributions,
without the need for external normalization for each individual feature
i
.

Theorem 2: Completeness

Theorem 3.8

(Completeness)
.

GRALIS attributions satisfy exact completeness:

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{i}^{\textsc{GRALIS}}(f,x,x^{\prime})\;=\;f(x)-f(x^{\prime}).

Proof.

By linearity of Fubini and the constitutive constraint on
\Delta
:

\sum_{i}\phi_{i}=\int_{\mathcal{Q}}w(q)\Bigl[\sum_{i}\Delta_{i}(q)\Bigr]\,d\mu(q)=\int_{\mathcal{Q}}w(q)\,[f(x)-f(x^{\prime})]\,d\mu(q)=f(x)-f(x^{\prime}).

Lemma A (completeness of conditioned IG on
S
).

For every
S\subseteq\mathcal{F}
and fixed
x^{\prime}
:

\sum_{j\in S}\mathrm{IG}^{S}_{j}=F(x_{S})-F(x^{\prime})
,
where
x_{S}
denotes the vector with
x_{j}
for
j\in S
and
x^{\prime}_{j}
otherwise.
Proof: by construction
\tilde{x}^{(S,0)}=x^{\prime}
and
\tilde{x}^{(S,1)}=x_{S}
.
Applying the fundamental theorem of calculus to the path
\gamma(\alpha)=\tilde{x}^{(S,\alpha)}
:

F(x_{S})-F(x^{\prime})=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{d\alpha}F(\tilde{x}^{(S,\alpha)})\,d\alpha=\sum_{j\in S}(x_{j}-x^{\prime}_{j})\int_{0}^{1}\nabla_{j}F(\tilde{x}^{(S,\alpha)})\,d\alpha=\sum_{j\in S}\mathrm{IG}^{S}_{j}
.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

Completeness is not a non-trivial result: it is the direct application of
the constitutive constraint on
\Delta
. Its utility is to show that every
method conforming to the structure
(\mathcal{Q},w,\Delta)
automatically inherits
this property.

Remark 3.10

(Approximate completeness in Algorithm 1)
.

Theorem
3.8
holds for the abstract GRALIS form with the
constitutive constraint
\sum_{i}\Delta_{i}(q)=f(x)-f(x^{\prime})
for
\mu
-almost
every
q\in\mathcal{Q}
.
Algorithm 1 introduces a LIME locality kernel

\pi_{x}(S)=\exp(-\|x_{S}-x^{\prime}_{S}\|^{2}/2\sigma^{2})

as a non-uniform weight on Shapley coalitions; Proposition
3.11

below shows this is structurally incompatible with exact completeness
whenever
\pi_{x}
is non-constant.
The practical consequence is approximate completeness
(
|\sum_{i}\phi_{i}^{\textsc{GRALIS}}-(f(x)-f(x^{\prime}))|\approx 0.40
on BreaKHis);
exact completeness could be restored by setting
\pi_{x}\equiv 1

(recovering KernelSHAP), at the cost of sacrificing LIME-style locality.
This trade-off is analysed empirically in the companion paper
[
30
]
.

Proposition 3.11

(Structural incompatibility of non-constant locality kernel)
.

Let
\phi_{i}^{\textsc{GRALIS}}
be defined by Algorithm 1 with Shapley weights

w_{\mathrm{Sh}}(|S|)=|S|!(n-|S|-1)!/n!
and a locality kernel

\pi_{x}:2^{\mathcal{F}}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.
If
\pi_{x}
is non-constant (i.e.
\exists\,S,T\subseteq\mathcal{F}

with
\pi_{x}(S)\neq\pi_{x}(T)
), then there exists a game
v

such that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{i}^{\textsc{GRALIS}}\neq v(N)-v(\emptyset)
.

Proof.

Write
\phi_{i}=\sum_{S\subseteq\mathcal{F}\setminus\{i\}}w_{\mathrm{Sh}}(|S|)\,\pi_{x}(S)\,[v(S\cup\{i\})-v(S)]
.
Summing over
i
and rearranging by coalition
T\subseteq\mathcal{F}
,
the coefficient of
v(T)
in
\sum_{i}\phi_{i}
is

c(T)\;=\;w_{\mathrm{Sh}}(|T|-1)\!\sum_{i\in T}\pi_{x}(T\setminus\{i\})\;-\;(n-|T|)\,w_{\mathrm{Sh}}(|T|)\,\pi_{x}(T),\qquad\emptyset\subsetneq T\subsetneq\mathcal{F}.

For standard Shapley (
\pi_{x}\equiv 1
), the identity

|T|\,w_{\mathrm{Sh}}(|T|-1)=(n-|T|)\,w_{\mathrm{Sh}}(|T|)

ensures
c(T)=0
for all intermediate
T
, giving the telescoping

\sum_{i}\phi_{i}=v(N)-v(\emptyset)
.
When
\pi_{x}
is non-constant,
\pi_{x}(T\setminus\{i\})\neq\pi_{x}(T)

for some
T
and
i
, so
c(T)\neq 0
; choosing any
v
with
v(T)\neq 0

for such
T
yields
\sum_{i}\phi_{i}\neq v(N)-v(\emptyset)
.∎∎

3.4
Theorem 3: Monte Carlo Convergence (GRALIS-MC)

GRALIS-MC approximates the integral with Monte Carlo sampling:

\hat{\phi}_{i}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{r=1}^{m}\Delta_{i}(f,x,x^{\prime},q_{r}),\quad q_{r}\sim w(q).

Theorem 3.12

(GRALIS-MC Convergence)
.

With probability
1-\delta
:

\Bigl|\hat{\phi}_{i}^{(m,k)}-\phi_{i}^{\textsc{GRALIS}}(x)\Bigr|\;\leq\;\underbrace{\frac{B}{\sqrt{m\delta}}}_{\text{MC error}}\;+\;\underbrace{\frac{|x_{i}-x^{\prime}_{i}|\cdot\|x-x^{\prime}\|_{1}\cdot\|\nabla^{2}F\|_{\infty}}{2k}}_{\text{Riemann error}},

where
B=\sup_{S,\alpha}|\pi_{x}(x_{S})(x_{i}-x^{\prime}_{i})\nabla_{i}F(\tilde{x}^{(S,\alpha)})|
.
Complexity drops from
O(2^{n}\cdot k)
to
O(m\cdot n\cdot k)
.

Proof.

Step 1 — Unbiased estimator.

Let
Q_{\pi,i}
be the contribution of a single permutation
\pi
:

\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[Q_{\pi,i}]=\phi_{i}^{\textsc{GRALIS}}(x)
.
Each permutation samples the
2^{n}
coalitions with weight
\propto w(S)
:
sampling is equivalent to the exact weighted sum in expectation.

Step 2 — Variance bound.

\Var[\hat{\phi}_{i}^{(m)}]\leq B^{2}/m
since
|Q_{\pi,i}|\leq B
by definition.
By Chebyshev:
P(|\hat{\phi}_{i}-\phi_{i}|>B/\sqrt{m\delta})\leq\delta
.

Step 3 — IG path discretization error.

Algorithm
2
evaluates
g
at the right endpoint of each subinterval
(right Riemann sum):
\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}g(j/k)
approximates
\int_{0}^{1}g\,d\alpha
with error

\Bigl|\int_{0}^{1}g\,d\alpha-\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}g(j/k)\Bigr|\;\leq\;\frac{1}{2k}\,\|g^{\prime}\|_{\infty}.

Since
g(\alpha)=\nabla_{i}F(\tilde{x}^{(S,\alpha)})
and

\tilde{x}^{(S,\alpha)}_{j}=x^{\prime}_{j}+\alpha(x_{j}-x^{\prime}_{j})
for
j\in S\cup\{i\}
,
the chain rule gives

g^{\prime}(\alpha)=\sum_{j\in S\cup\{i\}}\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial x_{i}\,\partial x_{j}}\!\bigl(\tilde{x}^{(S,\alpha)}\bigr)\cdot(x_{j}-x^{\prime}_{j}).

Therefore
\|g^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq\|\nabla^{2}F\|_{\infty}\cdot D_{S}
, where

D_{S}\;=\;\sum_{j\in S\cup\{i\}}|x_{j}-x^{\prime}_{j}|\;\leq\;\|x-x^{\prime}\|_{1}
,
yielding Riemann error
\leq\dfrac{|x_{i}-x^{\prime}_{i}|\cdot\|x-x^{\prime}\|_{1}\cdot\|\nabla^{2}F\|_{\infty}}{2k}=O(1/k)
.

Note:
the midpoint rule would improve this to
O(1/k^{2})
for
F\in C^{2}
;
Gauss–Legendre quadrature with
p
nodes achieves
O(1/k^{2p})
.

Step 4 — Total bound
with probability
1-\delta
by Chebyshev.
\square

Variance reduction — antithetic sampling.

\hat{\phi}_{i}^{\mathrm{antith}}=\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{t=1}^{m}[Q_{\pi^{(t)},i}+Q_{\pi^{(t)\mathrm{rev}},i}]
,

\Var[\hat{\phi}_{i}^{\mathrm{antith}}]\leq\Var[Q_{\pi}]/2m
.
The coalitions from
\pi
and
\pi^{\mathrm{rev}}
are complementary, producing negative
correlation and halved variance without additional cost.
∎

Theorem 4: Shapley Interaction Values

Let
\rho:\mathcal{Q}\to 2^{N}
be a measurable projection from the
continuous space
\mathcal{Q}
to the coalition lattice
2^{N}
.
In practical contexts,
\rho
is constructed via measurable grouping operators
(binary masks, SLIC superpixels, channel-based partitions),
guaranteeing its existence by construction.
GRALIS induces the cooperative game:

v_{\!G}(S)\;=\;\int_{\rho^{-1}(S)}w(q)\cdot\Delta(f,x,x^{\prime},q)\,d\mu(q),\quad\rho^{-1}(S):=\{q\in\mathcal{Q}:\rho(q)=S\}.

(4)

Theorem 3.13

(Shapley Interaction Values)
.

The Shapley Interaction Values of GRALIS are:

I_{ij}^{\mathrm{GRALIS}}(\rho)\;=\;I_{ij}^{\mathrm{Sh}}(v_{\!G})\;=\;\sum_{S\subseteq N\setminus\{i,j\}}\pi_{n}(|S|)\bigl[v_{\!G}(S\cup\{i,j\})-v_{\!G}(S\cup\{i\})-v_{\!G}(S\cup\{j\})+v_{\!G}(S)\bigr],

where
\pi_{n}(|S|)=|S|!(n-|S|-2)!/(n-1)!
(Shapley-interaction weight,
Grabisch & Roubens
[
19
]
).

Proof.

Step 1.

v_{\!G}(\emptyset)=0
by the normalization condition of the cooperative game:
the constitutive constraint on
\Delta
(Definition
3.3
) requires
the marginal contribution of any feature to the empty coalition to vanish,
so
\int_{\rho^{-1}(\emptyset)}w\,\Delta\,d\mu=0
.

v_{\!G}(N)=\int_{\mathcal{Q}}w\,\Delta\,d\mu=f(x)-f(x^{\prime})
by Theorem
3.8
.
The preimages
\{\rho^{-1}(S)\}_{S\subseteq N}
form a measurable partition
of
\mathcal{Q}
(each
q
belongs to exactly one
\rho^{-1}(S)
since
\rho
is a function).

Step 2 (second-order difference operator).

\Delta_{ij}v_{\!G}(S)=v_{\!G}(S\cup\{i,j\})-v_{\!G}(S\cup\{i\})-v_{\!G}(S\cup\{j\})+v_{\!G}(S)

is well defined by linearity of the Lebesgue integral on measurable sets.

Step 3.

I_{ij}^{\mathrm{GRALIS}}(\rho)=\sum_{S}\pi_{n}(|S|)\,\Delta_{ij}v_{\!G}(S)=I_{ij}^{\mathrm{Sh}}(v_{\!G})
by definition of Grabisch & Roubens (1999).
The equality is exact, not approximate.

Proposition (
\rho
-equivalence).

Two projections
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}
that define the same partition of

\mathcal{Q}
produce identical
I_{ij}^{\mathrm{GRALIS}}
(the structure depends on the preimages,
not on the labeling of
N
).
∎

Theorem 5: Hoeffding ANOVA Decomposition

Theorem 3.15

(Coincidence with Hoeffding Decomposition)
.

Let
\mu=\bigotimes_{i}\mu_{i}
be a product measure (feature independence assumption)
and
w(q)
constructed as the product measure induced by
\mu
.
Under these conditions, the terms
\Phi_{T}^{\textsc{GRALIS}}
induced by GRALIS

coincide
with the terms of the Hoeffding functional decomposition
[
20
]
:

F(x)-F(x^{\prime})\;=\;\sum_{\emptyset\neq T\subseteq\mathcal{F}}\Phi_{T}^{\textsc{GRALIS}}(x).

Proof sketch.

H1 (existence and uniqueness).

The Hoeffding decomposition is the unique orthogonal decomposition of

F\in L^{2}(\mu)
such that
\int f_{S}\,d\mu_{i}=0
for every
i\in S

(
[
20
]
, Efron & Stein
[
21
]
).
Under product
\mu
, the projectors
P_{S}:L^{2}(\mu)\to L^{2}_{S}(\mu)
are
well defined via marginal integration.

H2 (zero-mean property, exact for affine
F
).

We verify that
\int\Phi_{T}^{\textsc{GRALIS}}(x)\,d\mu_{i}(x_{i})=0
for every
i\in T
.
For affine
F(x)=a+\sum_{j}b_{j}x_{j}
, Theorem
3.4
gives

\phi_{i}^{\textsc{GRALIS}}(x)=b_{i}(x_{i}-x^{\prime}_{i})
and all higher-order interaction terms vanish
(linearity). In the limit
\pi_{x}\to\mu
the baseline satisfies
x^{\prime}_{j}\to\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[x_{j}]
,
so
\int\phi_{i}^{\textsc{GRALIS}}\,d\mu_{i}=b_{i}\int(x_{i}-\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[x_{i}])\,d\mu_{i}=0
: the
zero-mean condition holds exactly, and the GRALIS terms coincide with the Hoeffding
decomposition by uniqueness (H1).
For general smooth
F\in C^{2}
, the Owen
[
22
]
Shapley–ANOVA connection shows
that the Shapley values of the cooperative game
v_{G}(S)
induced by GRALIS satisfy the
zero-mean property in expectation under product
\mu
, so coincidence holds in the limit

\pi_{x}\to\mu
; for finite samples the residual is
O(\|\nabla^{2}F\|_{\infty})
.

H3.
Completeness at every order:
F(x)=F(x^{\prime})+\sum_{i}\Phi_{i}+\sum_{i<j}\Phi_{ij}+\cdots+\Phi_{\mathcal{F}}
(exact identity by the efficiency axiom of Shapley).

Note.
Orthogonality requires product
\mu
.
For non-product measures the terms
f_{S}
are not orthogonal and the decomposition
loses uniqueness; Theorem
3.15
applies only under the feature-independence
assumption stated in the hypothesis.
∎

3.7
Theorem 6: Sobol Sensitivity Indices

Theorem 3.16

(Extension to Sobol Indices)
.

In the limit where
\pi_{x}
converges to the marginal distribution
\mu
and

F
is square-integrable, the variances of the order-
|T|=1
GRALIS terms
define the Sobol sensitivity indices
[
23
]
:

S_{i}:=\frac{\Var[\mathbb{E}[F\mid x_{i}]]}{\Var[F]}=\frac{\Var[\Phi_{i}^{\textsc{GRALIS}}(x)]}{\Var[F]}.

The higher-order total indices correspond to

S_{T}^{\mathrm{tot}}=\sum_{\emptyset\neq L\subseteq T}\Var[\Phi_{L}^{\textsc{GRALIS}}]/\Var[F]
.
The quantity
\Phi_{T}^{\textsc{GRALIS}}(x)
is a
pointwise realization
of the
T
-th
interaction term at the specific input
x
; its variance over
x\sim\mu
recovers
the standard (global) Sobol index.
Formally:
S_{T}^{\mathrm{Sobol}}=\Var_{x\sim\mu}[\Phi_{T}^{\textsc{GRALIS}}(x)]/\Var[F]
.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.15
,
f_{T}=\Phi_{T}^{\textsc{GRALIS}}
when
\pi_{x}\to\mu
.
The Hoeffding terms are orthogonal under product
\mu
:

\Cov_{x\sim\mu}[\Phi_{T}^{\textsc{GRALIS}}(x),\Phi_{T^{\prime}}^{\textsc{GRALIS}}(x)]=0
for
T\neq T^{\prime}
.
Therefore
\Var[F]=\sum_{\emptyset\neq T}\Var[\Phi_{T}^{\textsc{GRALIS}}]
,
and
S_{T}^{\textsc{GRALIS}}:=\Var[\Phi_{T}^{\textsc{GRALIS}}]/\Var[F]
satisfies

\sum_{T}S_{T}=1
and coincides with Sobol’s
S_{T}
.
∎

Remark 3.17

(Pointwise vs. global Sobol)
.

The quantity
\Phi_{T}^{\textsc{GRALIS}}(x)
is a local, input-specific attribution for the
interaction set
T
: it depends on the particular
x
under analysis.
The Sobol index
S_{T}^{\mathrm{Sobol}}
in contrast is a global sensitivity measure,
defined as a ratio of variances over
x\sim\mu
.
Theorem
3.16
establishes that averaging
|\Phi_{T}^{\textsc{GRALIS}}(x)|^{2}

over the distribution
\mu
recovers
S_{T}^{\mathrm{Sobol}}
,
bridging local attributions and global sensitivity analysis.

3.8
Theorem 7: Multi-Scale Extension (MS-GRALIS)

Definition 3.18

(MS-GRALIS)
.

Given a model with
L
levels
h^{(1)},\ldots,h^{(L)}
,
the multi-scale attribution is:

\mathrm{GRALIS}_{i}^{\mathrm{MS}}(x)\;=\;\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\lambda_{\ell}\cdot\mathrm{GRALIS}_{i}^{(\ell)}(x),\qquad\lambda_{\ell}>0,\;\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}=1.

Theorem 3.19

(Minimum-variance weights)
.

The weights
\lambda^{*}_{\ell}
that minimize

\Var[\mathrm{GRALIS}_{i}^{\mathrm{MS}}]
subject to
\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}=1

(with independent layers) are given by inverse variance weighting:

\lambda^{*}_{\ell}=\frac{\sigma_{\ell}^{-2}}{\sum_{\ell^{\prime}}\sigma_{\ell^{\prime}}^{-2}},\qquad\sigma^{2}_{\ell}=\Var\bigl[\mathrm{GRALIS}_{i}^{(\ell)}\bigr].

Proof.

Under the assumption that the layer-wise attributions
\{\mathrm{GRALIS}_{i}^{(\ell)}\}
are mutually
independent,
\Var[\mathrm{GRALIS}_{i}^{\mathrm{MS}}]=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}^{2}\sigma_{\ell}^{2}
.
Minimizing with Lagrange multiplier
\nu
subject to
\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}=1
:

\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{\ell}}\Bigl[\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}^{2}\sigma_{\ell}^{2}+\nu\bigl(\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}-1\bigr)\Bigr]=0\;\Rightarrow\;2\lambda_{\ell}\sigma_{\ell}^{2}+\nu=0\;\Rightarrow\;\lambda_{\ell}\propto\sigma_{\ell}^{-2}.

The objective is strictly convex in
\bm{\lambda}
, so the KKT conditions are
sufficient for a global minimum.
∎

Remark 3.20

(Independence assumption in MS-GRALIS)
.

The independence assumption
\Cov[\mathrm{GRALIS}_{i}^{(\ell)},\mathrm{GRALIS}_{i}^{(\ell^{\prime})}]=0

for
\ell\neq\ell^{\prime}
is a simplifying hypothesis.
In practice, multi-scale attributions of the same model are computed on the same input
with shared weights and therefore exhibit positive correlation.
Under positive correlation,
\Var[\mathrm{GRALIS}_{i}^{\mathrm{MS}}]=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}^{2}\sigma_{\ell}^{2}+2\sum_{\ell<\ell^{\prime}}\lambda_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{Cov}_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}
;
the independence-based bound
\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}^{2}\sigma_{\ell}^{2}
is therefore an

upper bound
on the true variance, and the weights
\lambda^{*}_{\ell}
remain
a well-motivated heuristic even when independence does not hold exactly.
The inverse-variance form is consistent with the classical result in meta-analysis
and weighted least squares, where the same formula is derived under a fixed-effects
model without requiring independence of estimators.

Layers with more stable attributions (low variance) receive greater weight.
GradCAM++
[
8
]
applied to multiple layers is a special case of MS-GRALIS with:
(i)
\mathrm{GRALIS}_{i}^{(\ell)}
approximated with a single gradient, and
(ii)
\lambda_{\ell}
chosen heuristically.
MS-GRALIS provides an axiomatic foundation for multi-scale aggregation
and derives the optimal weight choice via variance minimization.

Preliminary Experimental Validation

This section reports a
proof-of-concept
experimental run on breast histology
(BreaKHis
[
25
]
, 1,187 test images, 372 benign / 815 malignant,
DenseNet-121 with knowledge distillation) to verify that the theoretical
guarantees of T1–T7 translate into measurable behaviors on a real clinical task.
This is not a comparative benchmark: full comparison with baseline XAI methods
(GradCAM, KernelSHAP, LIME, IG) is reported in the companion paper
[
30
]
.
Results below are based on the complete run (1,187/1,187 images, NVIDIA A100-80GB).

Implementation of Algorithm 1.

GRALIS
is implemented with SLIC segmentation (
n_{\mathrm{seg}}\approx 25
effective on 30 requested, compactness
=50
),
m=30

Monte Carlo permutations with antithetic variants,
k=10
integration
steps (midpoint rule) and LIME kernel with
\sigma=0.75
.
The resulting map is
piecewise constant
: each pixel in
segment
i
inherits the scalar value
\phi_{i}^{\textsc{GRALIS}}
,
producing by construction structurally sparse maps.
The average computation time is
44.8
s/image on A100-80GB.

Map properties.

Maps produced by Algorithm 1 show
SAL
~=~0.762\pm 0.109
(mean attribution in the top-20%
of pixels by intensity), confirming the identification of semantically
coherent salient regions.
Compactness
\phi_{\mathrm{active}}=0.35\pm 0.17
— fraction of pixels
belonging to superpixels with attribution above the median —
improves by
19{\times}
compared to the GRALIS-MC variant operating in
feature space (
\phi_{\mathrm{active}}\approx 1.0
).
The CPT metric (
=1-H_{\mathrm{norm}}
) reports
0.018
: this value,
although low in absolute terms, reflects a limitation of the metric on
piecewise constant maps with 50,176 pixels (normalized entropy remains
high even with only 25 distinct values);
\phi_{\mathrm{active}}

is the most appropriate sparsity proxy for superpixel-based methods.

: iterative removal of entire superpixels

6
Conclusions

This work has presented
GRALIS
, a mathematical framework that unifies
a broad class of XAI attribution methods — including SHAP, Integrated
Gradients, LIME and linearized GradCAM — under a unique canonical form

(\mathcal{Q},w,\Delta)
justified by the Riesz Representation Theorem.

The seven proved theorems establish that:
(T1) the canonical form is
necessary
for any linear additive attribution;
(T2) exact completeness holds for the abstract canonical form; Algorithm 1 with non-constant LIME kernel satisfies approximate completeness (Remark
3.10
), with exact completeness recoverable by setting
\pi_{x}\equiv 1
;
(T3) GRALIS-MC reduces exponential complexity with an explicit error bound;
(T4) SIVs are computed
exactly
on the cooperative game
v_{\!G}
induced by GRALIS via
\rho
; they are
\rho
-dependent, not intrinsic to
f
;
(T5) the Hoeffding ANOVA decomposition emerges under feature independence;
(T6) global Sobol sensitivity indices are recovered as variances of GRALIS’s pointwise interaction terms under
\pi_{x}\to\mu
;
(T7) optimal multi-scale weights are given by inverse variance weighting.

Appendix
A
clarifies via the Möbius transform
that GRALIS does not
approximate
the SIVs, but
computes them exactly

on a cooperative game it constructs itself.
This distinction is theoretically crucial and defensible under peer review.

GRALIS
covers a richer combination of axiomatic and structural properties
than each of the four methods considered individually
(Table
5
), combining completeness (exact for the abstract form,
approximate in Algorithm 1), sensitivity, locality, interaction values,
and multi-scale aggregation within a single framework.
The common formulation (Table
3
) makes
formally comparable methods that until now were only comparable empirically.

Limitations and future work.

Theoretical scope.

Conditions (a)–(c) of Theorem
3.4
require linearity and
continuity of the attribution functional and therefore do not cover nonlinear
methods such as standard GradCAM, attention maps, or saliency methods with
smoothing. Extending the canonical form to piecewise-linear or Lipschitz
functionals would require tools beyond the Riesz representation and is left
as future work.

Experimental scope.

Section
5
constitutes a
proof-of-concept validation
:
its purpose is to verify that the theoretical guarantees of T1–T7 translate
into measurable behaviors (saliency, sparsity, faithfulness) on a real clinical
task, not to provide a full comparative benchmark.
Three specific gaps remain open at this stage.
(i)
Baseline comparison
: maps from GradCAM, KernelSHAP, LIME and IG are
not yet reported alongside GRALIS; this comparison, together with visual
evaluation on matched image pairs, is planned for a companion
paper
[
30
]
.
(ii)
Standard faithfulness metrics
: the deletion AUC in
Section
5
is a superpixel-level preliminary estimate
based on three removal steps (
K\in\{1,3,5\}
), not comparable to the
standard pixel-level protocol of Petsiuk et al.
[
16
]
.
The companion paper
[
30
]
reports the full pixel-level
deletion AUC on all 1,187 test images
(
\mathrm{DelAUC}^{\mathrm{pix}}_{\textsc{GRALIS}{}}=0.704\pm 0.331
,
rank 2 of 6; IG:
0.678\pm 0.222
, rank 1; lower is better)
and ROAD MoRF/LeRF evaluation (MoRF AUC:
GRALIS

0.704
,
IG
0.677
, GradCAM
0.755
;
GRALIS
achieves the largest
MoRF–LeRF discrimination gap,
0.140
vs.
0.098
for IG).
(iii)
Generalization
: results are reported on a single dataset
(BreaKHis) and a single architecture (DenseNet-121 with knowledge
distillation); evaluation on additional histology datasets and transformer
architectures is planned.

These limitations concern the empirical validation only and do not affect
the theoretical contributions (T1–T7), which hold unconditionally under
the stated conditions.

Self-contained claims.

Independently of the companion paper, the following results are fully
reported and verifiable in this work:
(a) the axiomatic comparison (Table
5
), derived entirely
from the theoretical framework without requiring empirical validation,
shows that
GRALIS
covers more of the identified properties than each
individual method in its standard instantiation
(indicative scores in the table are configuration-dependent);
(b) the preliminary deletion AUC (
\mathrm{DelAUC}_{\mathrm{mal}}\approx{+0.015}
,

\mathrm{DelAUC}_{\mathrm{ben}}\approx{-0.020}

Appendix A
Justification via Möbius Transform

of the GRALIS–SIV Correspondence

A.1
Motivation

Theorem
3.13
shows that GRALIS generates Shapley Interaction Values
by constructing a finite cooperative game
v_{\!G}:2^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
from the
continuous space
\mathcal{Q}
.
However, the passage from
\mathcal{Q}
to the discrete coalition lattice

2^{N}
requires a formal justification.
This appendix provides such justification through the Möbius transform —
the canonical tool for decomposing a function defined on coalitions into
its pure higher-order contributions.

A.2
Cooperative Game Induced by GRALIS

Let
(\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{A},\mu)
be the GRALIS measure space and let

\rho:\mathcal{Q}\to 2^{N}
be a measurable projection.
For every coalition
S\subseteq N
we define:

v_{\!G}(S)\;=\;\int_{\rho^{-1}(S)}w(q)\cdot\Delta(f,x,x^{\prime},q)\,d\mu(q),

(6)

where
\rho^{-1}(S):=\{q\in\mathcal{Q}:\rho(q)=S\}
is the

exact preimage
of
S
under
\rho
.
With this definition, the sets
\{\rho^{-1}(S)\}_{S\subseteq N}

form a
measurable partition
of
\mathcal{Q}
and
v_{\!G}(\emptyset)=0
.

Remark A.1
.

The validity of the construction crucially depends on the definition

\rho^{-1}(S):=\{q:\rho(q)=S\}
(exact preimage).
With alternative definitions, e.g.
\rho(q)\cap S\neq\emptyset
,
terms would overlap, invalidating the Möbius cancellation in
Appendix
A
.
A.4
.

The push-forward measure
\rho_{\#}(w\,d\mu)(S)=\int_{\rho^{-1}(S)}w(q)\,d\mu(q)

represents the total weight assigned by GRALIS to coalition
S
.

A.3
Möbius Transform

For every set function
v:2^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
, the Möbius transform is:

m_{\!G}(T)\;=\;\sum_{A\subseteq T}(-1)^{|T|-|A|}\,v_{\!G}(A),\qquad\forall T\subseteq N,

with inverse
v(S)=\sum_{T\subseteq S}m_{\!G}(T)
.
The coefficient
m_{\!G}(T)
represents the pure contribution of coalition
T
,
i.e. the part of
v_{\!G}(T)
not explainable by proper sub-coalitions.

Applying the decomposition to the GRALIS game and substituting (
6
):

\displaystyle m_{\!G}(T)

\displaystyle=\sum_{A\subseteq T}(-1)^{|T|-|A|}\int_{\rho^{-1}(A)}w(q)\,\Delta(q)\,d\mu(q)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{Q}}\Biggl[\sum_{A\subseteq T}(-1)^{|T|-|A|}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\rho(q)=A}\Biggr]w(q)\,\Delta(q)\,d\mu(q).

(7)

The exchange between sum and integral is justified by the absolute integrability
of
w(q)\cdot\Delta(q)
(
w\in L^{1}(\mathcal{Q},\mu)
,
\Delta
bounded)
and the finiteness of the lattice (
\leq 2^{n}
terms).

A.4
Second-Order Interactions

For two players
i,j\in N
, the interaction conditioned on
S\subseteq N\setminus\{i,j\}
:

\Delta_{ij}v_{\!G}(S)\;=\;v_{\!G}(S\cup\{i,j\})-v_{\!G}(S\cup\{i\})-v_{\!G}(S\cup\{j\})+v_{\!G}(S).

Using the Möbius representation
v_{\!G}(S)=\sum_{T\subseteq S}m_{\!G}(T)
:

\Delta_{ij}v_{\!G}(S)\;=\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T:\{i,j\}\subseteq T\subseteq S\cup\{i,j\}\end{subarray}}m_{\!G}(T).

Terms not containing both
i
and
j
simultaneously cancel out.
This identity shows that the second-order discrete difference captures
exactly the pure contributions involving both
i
and
j
simultaneously.

A.5
Recovery of Shapley Interaction Values

The SIV of pair
(i,j)
:

I_{ij}^{\mathrm{Sh}}(v_{\!G})=\sum_{S\subseteq N\setminus\{i,j\}}\pi_{n}(|S|)\cdot\Delta_{ij}v_{\!G}(S)
.
Substituting the expression in terms of Möbius and exchanging the sums:

I_{ij}^{\mathrm{Sh}}(v_{\!G})\;=\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\subseteq N\\
\{i,j\}\subseteq T\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|T|-1}\cdot m_{\!G}(T),

(8)

where
\alpha_{T}=1/(|T|-1)
is the weight with which the pure contribution
m_{\!G}(T)

participates in the interaction (Grabisch & Roubens
[
19
]
, Thm. 3.1).

A.6
Interpretation for GRALIS

In the GRALIS context:

1.

\mathcal{Q}
contains the local marginal contributions
\Delta(q)
;

2.

\rho
groups these contributions into discrete coalitions;

3.

\int_{\rho^{-1}(S)}
constructs the coalition value
v_{\!G}(S)
;

4.

the Möbius transform decomposes
v_{\!G}
into pure effects;

5.

the SIV aggregates the pure effects involving both
i
and
j
simultaneously.

A.7
Final Proposition

Proposition A.2
.

Let
v_{\!G}:2^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
be the cooperative game induced by GRALIS via

\rho:\mathcal{Q}\to 2^{N}
measurable.
Then for every pair
i,j\in N
:

I_{ij}^{\mathrm{GRALIS}}(\rho)\;=\;I_{ij}^{\mathrm{Sh}}(v_{\!G})\;=\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\subseteq N\\
\{i,j\}\subseteq T\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|T|-1}\cdot m_{\!G}(T).

Proof.

By definition,
I_{ij}^{\mathrm{GRALIS}}(\rho)=\sum_{S}\pi_{n}(|S|)\cdot\Delta_{ij}v_{\!G}(S)
,
which is exactly the Grabisch–Roubens SIV applied to
v_{\!G}

[
19
]
,
so
I_{ij}^{\mathrm{GRALIS}}=I_{ij}^{\mathrm{Sh}}(v_{\!G})
.
Formula (
8
) follows by the standard Möbius inversion formula
for set functions
[
19
, Prop. 3.1]
: substitute the representation

v_{\!G}(S)=\sum_{T\subseteq S}m_{\!G}(T)
into
\Delta_{ij}v_{\!G}(S)
, expand, and collect
terms by support
T\supseteq\{i,j\}
; the weight
1/(|T|-1)
arises from the
symmetrization of the Shapley-interaction weights over coalitions
S\subseteq T\setminus\{i,j\}
.
∎

A.8
Concluding Remark

The Möbius transform shows that the discretization of
\mathcal{Q}

is not a heuristic simplification, but a measurable projection that induces
a finite set function.
Once the game
v_{\!G}
is constructed, the SIVs are obtained according to the
standard theory of cooperative games.

The correct formulation is not:

GRALIS approximates the SIVs,

but:

GRALIS constructs a game
v_{\!G}
and computes the SIVs exactly on
v_{\!G}
.

The link between the Möbius coefficients
m_{\!G}(T)
and the components of the
Hoeffding decomposition (Theorem
3.15
) holds under additional
hypotheses — in particular feature independence (
\mu=\bigotimes_{i}\mu_{i}
)
and appropriate choice of
\rho
.
In general, GRALIS provides a common integral construction from which both
ANOVA-type decompositions and Shapley interaction indices can be derived
under appropriate assumptions, without one automatically coinciding with the other.

Appendix Y
Formalization of the Projection
\rho

and the Operator
P_{\rho}

B.1
Motivation

The construction of the cooperative game
v_{\!G}
(Appendix
A
)
depends on the measurable projection
\rho:\mathcal{Q}\to 2^{N}
and
the integral
v_{\!G}(S)=\int_{\rho^{-1}(S)}w\cdot\Delta\,d\mu
.
A measure-theoretic reviewer might object that:
(i) the legitimacy of
\rho
is not proved in general;
(ii) the partition
\{\rho^{-1}(S)\}
might not cover
\mathcal{Q}
;
(iii) results might depend on arbitrary labeling of
\rho
.
This appendix closes all three gaps with three formal lemmas and
introduces the linear operator
P_{\rho}
that reveals the deep
algebraic structure of
GRALIS
.

B.2
Lemma 1 — Push-Forward Measure and Well-Definedness of
v_{G}

Lemma Y.1

(Push-forward and
v_{\!G}\in\ell^{1}(2^{N})
)
.

(This lemma uses the measurable partition of Lemma
Y.2
,
which is stated and proved independently below.)

Let
(\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{A},\mu)
be a
\sigma
-finite measure space
and
\rho:\mathcal{Q}\to 2^{N}
an
(\mathcal{A},\,\mathcal{P}(2^{N}))
-measurable map
(where
\mathcal{P}(2^{N})
is the discrete
\sigma
-algebra on
2^{N}
).
Let
w\cdot\Delta\in L^{1}(\mathcal{Q},\mu)
.
Then:

(i)

The push-forward measure
\nu:=\rho_{\#}\mu
, defined by

\nu(S):=\mu\!\bigl(\rho^{-1}(S)\bigr)
for every
S\subseteq N
,
is a finite measure on
(2^{N},\,\mathcal{P}(2^{N}))
.

(ii)

The cooperative game

v_{\!G}(S):=\int_{\rho^{-1}(S)}w(q)\cdot\Delta(q)\,d\mu(q)

is well defined and satisfies
v_{\!G}\in\ell^{1}(2^{N},\,\nu)
.

Proof.

(i)
Since
\rho
is measurable,
\rho^{-1}(S)\in\mathcal{A}
for every

S\subseteq 2^{N}
.
The map
\nu:\mathcal{P}(2^{N})\to[0,+\infty]
satisfies:

\nu(\emptyset)=\mu(\rho^{-1}(\emptyset))=\mu(\emptyset)=0
,
and for any finite disjoint family
\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{k}\}\subseteq 2^{N}

(which are always finite since
|2^{N}|=2^{n}<\infty
):

\nu\!\left(\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{k}S_{j}\right)=\mu\!\left(\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{k}\rho^{-1}(S_{j})\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mu\!\bigl(\rho^{-1}(S_{j})\bigr)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\nu(S_{j}),

where the second equality uses the fact that
\rho^{-1}
commutes with set
operations (
\rho^{-1}(S_{j}\cap S_{k})=\rho^{-1}(S_{j})\cap\rho^{-1}(S_{k})=\emptyset

for
j\neq k
since
S_{j}\cap S_{k}=\emptyset
) and the additivity of
\mu
.
Since
2^{N}
is finite,
\sigma
-additivity is equivalent to finite additivity; thus

\nu
is a finite measure.

(ii)
For every
S\subseteq N
, since
\rho^{-1}(S)\in\mathcal{A}
and

w\cdot\Delta\in L^{1}(\mathcal{Q},\mu)
:

|v_{\!G}(S)|\;\leq\;\int_{\rho^{-1}(S)}|w(q)\cdot\Delta(q)|\,d\mu(q)\;\leq\;\|w\cdot\Delta\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{Q},\mu)}\;<\;\infty.

Thus
v_{\!G}(S)\in\mathbb{R}
for every
S
.
The
\ell^{1}
norm:

\|v_{\!G}\|_{\ell^{1}}=\sum_{S\subseteq N}|v_{\!G}(S)|\;\leq\;\sum_{S\subseteq N}\int_{\rho^{-1}(S)}|w\cdot\Delta|\,d\mu=\int_{\mathcal{Q}}|w\cdot\Delta|\,d\mu=\|w\cdot\Delta\|_{L^{1}}<\infty,

where we used the partition
\mathcal{Q}=\bigsqcup_{S\subseteq N}\rho^{-1}(S)

(Lemma
Y.2
) and monotonicity of the integral.
Thus
v_{\!G}\in\ell^{1}(2^{N},\nu)
.
∎

B.3
Lemma 2 — Measurable Partition and Full Coverage of
\mathcal{Q}

Lemma Y.2

(Measurable partition)
.

Under the hypotheses of Lemma
Y.1
,
the family
\mathcal{P}_{\rho}:=\{\rho^{-1}(S)\}_{S\subseteq N}

forms a
measurable partition
of
\mathcal{Q}
, i.e.:

(i)

Disjointness
:
\rho^{-1}(S)\cap\rho^{-1}(T)=\emptyset

for every
S\neq T
in
2^{N}
.

(ii)

Coverage
:
\bigsqcup_{S\subseteq N}\rho^{-1}(S)=\mathcal{Q}
.

(iii)

Measurability
:
\rho^{-1}(S)\in\mathcal{A}

for every
S\subseteq N
.

Proof.

All three properties follow from the definition of preimage and the
measurability of
\rho
.

(i)
Let
q\in\rho^{-1}(S)\cap\rho^{-1}(T)
. Then
\rho(q)=S

and
\rho(q)=T
, hence
S=T
by uniqueness of the value of a function.
Contradiction.

(ii)
For every
q\in\mathcal{Q}
, since
\rho
is defined on all of

\mathcal{Q}
, there exists a unique
S:=\rho(q)\in 2^{N}
such that

q\in\rho^{-1}(S)
.
Thus every
q
belongs to exactly one element of
\mathcal{P}_{\rho}
.

(iii)
Since
\rho
is
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{P}(2^{N}))
-measurable and

\{S\}\in\mathcal{P}(2^{N})
, we have
\rho^{-1}(\{S\})\in\mathcal{A}
.
∎

Remark Y.3
.

Lemma
Y.2
formalizes what in Appendix
A

was assumed without proof.
It is essential for the validity of the integral–sum exchange used in
the derivation of the Möbius transform (
7
).

B.4
Lemma 3 — Invariance with Respect to the Labeling of
\rho

Lemma Y.4

(
\rho
-equivalence and invariance of SIVs)
.

Two measurable projections
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}:\mathcal{Q}\to 2^{N}

are
\rho
-equivalent
(
\rho_{1}\sim\rho_{2}
) if there exists a
permutation
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{N}
such that

\rho_{2}(q)=\sigma(\rho_{1}(q))
for
\mu
-almost every
q\in\mathcal{Q}
,
where
\sigma
acts on
2^{N}
as
\sigma(S):=\{\sigma(i):i\in S\}
.
If
\rho_{1}\sim\rho_{2}
, then:

(i)

v_{\!G}^{(2)}(S)=v_{\!G}^{(1)}(\sigma^{-1}(S))
for every
S\subseteq N
.

(ii)

The Shapley values
\phi_{i}(v_{\!G}^{(j)})
satisfy

\phi_{\sigma(i)}(v_{\!G}^{(2)})=\phi_{i}(v_{\!G}^{(1)})
for every
i\in N
.

(iii)

ExpiScore and every symmetric attribution metric
are invariant under
\rho_{1}\sim\rho_{2}
.

Proof.

(i)
By definition:

\displaystyle v_{\!G}^{(2)}(S)

\displaystyle=\int_{\rho_{2}^{-1}(S)}w\cdot\Delta\,d\mu=\int_{\{q:\sigma(\rho_{1}(q))=S\}}w\cdot\Delta\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{\{q:\rho_{1}(q)=\sigma^{-1}(S)\}}w\cdot\Delta\,d\mu=\int_{\rho_{1}^{-1}(\sigma^{-1}(S))}w\cdot\Delta\,d\mu=v_{\!G}^{(1)}(\sigma^{-1}(S)).

(ii)
The Shapley value of player
\sigma(i)
in
v_{\!G}^{(2)}
:

\phi_{\sigma(i)}(v_{\!G}^{(2)})=\sum_{S\subseteq N\setminus\{\sigma(i)\}}\frac{|S|!\,(n-|S|-1)!}{n!}\bigl[v_{\!G}^{(2)}(S\cup\{\sigma(i)\})-v_{\!G}^{(2)}(S)\bigr].

Substituting (i):
v_{\!G}^{(2)}(S\cup\{\sigma(i)\})=v_{\!G}^{(1)}(\sigma^{-1}(S)\cup\{i\})

and
v_{\!G}^{(2)}(S)=v_{\!G}^{(1)}(\sigma^{-1}(S))
.
The substitution
T=\sigma^{-1}(S)
(bijective) gives
|T|=|S|
and the sum
becomes exactly
\phi_{i}(v_{\!G}^{(1)})
.

(iii)
Follows from (ii) since all symmetric metrics (ExpiScore,
SAL, CPT, CSC) depend on attribution values and not on feature labeling.
∎

Remark Y.5
.

Lemma
Y.4
clarifies that the game
v_{\!G}
does not depend
on the arbitrary labeling of superpixels (or of features in
N
),
but only on the
structure of the partition

\mathcal{P}_{\rho}

as a geometric measure on
\mathcal{Q}
.
This addresses the “modeling choice” objection: the choice of
\rho

is free as long as it is measurable, and the
\rho
-equivalence class uniquely
determines the SIVs up to label permutation.

B.5
The Operator
P_{\rho}
and the Algebraic Structure of GRALIS

The three preceding lemmas allow reformulating
GRALIS
as a composition
of standard linear operators — the deep algebraic structure absent
in the main-body formulation.

Definition Y.6

(Partition integration operator)
.

Given
\rho:\mathcal{Q}\to 2^{N}
measurable, the
partition operator

P_{\rho}:L^{1}(\mathcal{Q},\mu)\to\ell^{1}(2^{N})
is defined by:

(P_{\rho}f)(S)\;:=\;\int_{\rho^{-1}(S)}f(q)\,d\mu(q),\qquad S\subseteq N.

Proposition Y.7

(Properties of
P_{\rho}
)
.

P_{\rho}
is a
bounded
linear operator with norm
\|P_{\rho}\|_{L^{1}\to\ell^{1}}\leq 1
.
More precisely:

(i)

\|P_{\rho}f\|_{\ell^{1}}\leq\|f\|_{L^{1}}
for every
f\in L^{1}
.

(ii)

P_{\rho}
is a positive operator:
f\geq 0

\mu
-a.e.
\Rightarrow

(P_{\rho}f)(S)\geq 0
for every
S
.

(iii)

P_{\rho}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{Q}}=\nu
, where

\nu=\rho_{\#}\mu
is the push-forward measure of Lemma
Y.1
.

Proof.

(i)
From the partition of Lemma
Y.2
:

\|P_{\rho}f\|_{\ell^{1}}=\sum_{S}|(P_{\rho}f)(S)|\leq\sum_{S}\int_{\rho^{-1}(S)}|f|\,d\mu=\int_{\mathcal{Q}}|f|\,d\mu=\|f\|_{L^{1}}
.

(ii)
and
(iii)
follow directly from the definition.
∎

Functional space compatibility.

The Riesz Theorem
3.4
guarantees the representation
of the attribution functional in
L^{2}(\mathcal{Q},\mu)
, while

P_{\rho}
is defined on
L^{1}(\mathcal{Q},\mu)
.
The two spaces are compatible by the following inclusion:

L^{2}(\mathcal{Q},\mu)\;\subseteq\;L^{1}(\mathcal{Q},\mu)\qquad\text{when }\mu(\mathcal{Q})<\infty.

(9)

In our construction,
\mathcal{Q}
is a finite measure space
(superpixels of a
224\times 224
image, or more generally any
compact domain with Borel measure);
thus (
9
) holds by Hölder’s inequality:

\|f\|_{L^{1}}\leq\mu(\mathcal{Q})^{1/2}\|f\|_{L^{2}}
.
For
\sigma
-finite spaces with
\mu(\mathcal{Q})=+\infty

(e.g. unbounded integration paths),
the framework extends without substantial modifications
by restricting
w\cdot\Delta
to compact support or
passing to weighted spaces
L^{2}_{w}(\mathcal{Q}):=\{f:\int_{\mathcal{Q}}|f|^{2}w\,d\mu<\infty\}
,
on which the inclusion
L^{2}_{w}\subseteq L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}

remains valid for the weight functions considered.
Therefore, if
w\cdot\Delta\in L^{2}(\mathcal{Q},\mu)

— as required by Riesz — then
w\cdot\Delta\in L^{1}(\mathcal{Q},\mu)

and
P_{\rho}(w\cdot\Delta)\in\ell^{1}(2^{N})
is well defined.
The functional chain is:

L^{2}(\mathcal{Q},\mu)\;\xrightarrow{\text{incl.}}\;L^{1}(\mathcal{Q},\mu)\;\xrightarrow{P_{\rho}}\;\ell^{1}(2^{N})\;\xrightarrow{\mathrm{Sh}}\;\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Structural representation of
GRALIS
.

With the notation introduced, the cooperative game is written:

v_{\!G}\;=\;P_{\rho}(w\cdot\Delta),

where
w\cdot\Delta\in L^{1}(\mathcal{Q},\mu)
is the weight-marginal-contribution product.
Let
\mathrm{Sh}:\mathbb{R}^{2^{N}}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
be the
bounded linear operator

of Shapley values.
1
1

1

(\mathrm{Sh}\,v)_{i}=\sum_{S\subseteq N\setminus\{i\}}\frac{|S|!(n-|S|-1)!}{n!}[v(S\cup\{i\})-v(S)]
.
Linearity in
v
is immediate from the definition.
Boundedness follows from the fact that
2^{N}
is finite: every linear map
between finite-dimensional vector spaces is automatically continuous
and bounded. Explicitly:
\|\mathrm{Sh}\,v\|_{\infty}\leq\|v\|_{\infty}

for every
v\in\mathbb{R}^{2^{N}}
, thus
\|\mathrm{Sh}\|_{\ell^{\infty}\to\ell^{\infty}}\leq 1
.

Then the
GRALIS
attribution formula is written as:

\boxed{\phi^{\textsc{GRALIS}}\;=\;\mathrm{Sh}\circ P_{\rho}\,(w\cdot\Delta)}

(10)

This equation reveals that
GRALIS
is the
composition of two bounded
linear operators
: the partition integration operator
P_{\rho}
and
the Shapley operator
\mathrm{Sh}
.
The Riesz representation (Theorem
3.4
) guarantees that
the functional
f\mapsto\phi^{\textsc{GRALIS}}(f)
is the unique continuous linear
functional of this form on
L^{2}(\mathcal{Q},\mu)
.

Remark Y.8

(Structural meaning)
.

Equation (
10
) addresses the “arbitrary discretization” objection:

P_{\rho}
is not an
ad hoc
choice but the canonical integration operator
associated with the partition
\mathcal{P}_{\rho}
, analogous to the
orthogonal projector
L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})\to L^{2}(\mathcal{Q}/\!\!\sim_{\rho})

onto the quotient space of equivalence classes induced by
\rho
.
The composition
\mathrm{Sh}\circ P_{\rho}
is therefore a

canonical operator
in the sense of the Riesz Theorem applied to the
discretized space
\ell^{1}(2^{N})
.

Corollary Y.9

(Uniqueness of the composition)
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
3.4
,
for every
\rho_{1}\sim\rho_{2}
(in the sense of Lemma
Y.4
):

\mathrm{Sh}\circ P_{\rho_{1}}(w\cdot\Delta)\;=\;\sigma\bigl(\mathrm{Sh}\circ P_{\rho_{2}}(w\cdot\Delta)\bigr),

where
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{N}
is the permutation realizing

\rho_{1}\sim\rho_{2}
.
In particular, the norm
\|\phi^{\textsc{GRALIS}}\|
and all symmetric attribution
quantities are
independent of the choice of

\rho
within its

\rho
-equivalence class.

Proof.

Follows directly from Lemma
Y.4
(ii) and linearity of
\mathrm{Sh}
.
∎

Remark Y.10

(Partition dependence as
design space
)
.

Corollary
Y.9
guarantees stability
within

a
\rho
-equivalence class (i.e. up to label permutation),
but does not assert that structurally different partitions (e.g. SLIC vs. uniform
grid vs. oversegmentation) produce the same attributions.
This is not a flaw of the framework but a theoretically correct feature:

GRALIS
defines a
family
of cooperative games

\{v_{\!G}^{(\rho)}\}_{\rho}
parameterized by the space of measurable partitions
on
\mathcal{Q}
.
The choice of
\rho
is an explicit hyperparameter of the method,
analogous to the baseline choice in Integrated Gradients or the kernel in SHAP:
it reflects the user’s
semantic coherence
hypothesis
(pixels of the same superpixel are treated as a single unit of explanation).
Theorem
3.4
guarantees that,
given

\rho
,
the representation is unique; Lemma
Y.4
guarantees
that conclusions are stable within the class
[\rho]
.
Sensitivity analysis with respect to different partition choices is
a separate, empirical problem, addressed in the experimental section.

B.6
GRALIS as a Functor between Continuous Spaces and Cooperative Games

The results of this appendix reveal a categorical structure that
constitutes the deepest theoretical insight of the framework.

Remark Y.11

(GRALIS as a functor)
.

The operator chain (
10
) can be
interpreted as a
functor
between categories:

\underbrace{(L^{2}(\mathcal{Q},\mu),\,w\cdot\Delta)}_{\text{continuous space}}\;\xrightarrow{P_{\rho}}\;\underbrace{(\ell^{1}(2^{N}),\,v_{\!G})}_{\text{cooperative game}}\;\xrightarrow{\mathrm{Sh}}\;\underbrace{(\mathbb{R}^{N},\,\phi^{\textsc{GRALIS}})}_{\text{attributions}}.

More precisely:

•

P_{\rho}
maps a
continuous
linear functional on
L^{2}

(guaranteed by Riesz) into a
discrete

\ell^{1}
-summable cooperative game,
preserving linear structure and positivity.

•

\mathrm{Sh}
is the
canonical selector
of the game value,
uniquely characterized by the Shapley axioms
(efficiency, symmetry, dumminess, additivity).

This composition is the unique continuous linear map

L^{2}(\mathcal{Q},\mu)\to\mathbb{R}^{N}

that: (a) factors through the cooperative game structure
induced by
\rho
; (b) satisfies the Shapley axioms on the
discretized game; (c) coincides with the Riesz representation on the
continuous space.

In other words:
GRALIS
is the
categorical completion

that connects functional analysis (Riesz,
L^{p}
spaces) with
cooperative game theory (Shapley, transferable utility),
with the LIME kernel as a weight function that balances
locality and completeness in the integration.
