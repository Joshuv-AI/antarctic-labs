Abstract
Median Arcuate Ligament Syndrome (MALS), also known as Dunbar syndrome, is a rare condition characterized by
extrinsic compression of the celiac artery by the median arcuate ligament. Its diagnosis represents a challenge due to
the nonspecific nature of its symptoms and the presence of anatomical celiac artery compression in asymptomatic
individuals. This article aims to present the case of a 47-year-old female patient with a six-month history of recurrent
abdominal pain, predominantly occurring before and after meals, associated with weight loss and inadequate response
to analgesic therapy. Abdominal computed tomography angiography revealed extrinsic compression of the celiac
artery, with approximately 50% stenosis. Considering the correlation between the clinical and radiological findings, a
diagnosis of MALS was established, and surgical treatment with laparoscopic release of the median arcuate ligament
was performed. The patient had a favorable clinical outcome following the procedure, with significant improvement
in symptoms. MALS should be considered in the differential diagnosis of patients with chronic abdominal pain,
particularly when symptoms are related to food intake and accompanied by weight loss, after excluding more common
causes. Appropriate patient selection based on clinical-radiological correlation is essential to achieve satisfactory
outcomes with surgical treatment.
Keywords: Median Arcuate Ligament Syndrome; Celiac artery; Laparoscopy; Abdominal pain; Weight loss.

1. Introduction
Median Arcuate Ligament Syndrome (MALS), also known as Dunbar syndrome, was initially described by Harjola in
1963 (Harjola, 1963) and was subsequently clinically characterized by Dunbar et al. in 1965, who reported a series of 15
patients undergoing surgical decompression of the celiac artery (Dunbar et al., 1965). It is a rare condition resulting from
extrinsic compression of the celiac artery by the median arcuate ligament, a fibrous band connecting the right and left
diaphragmatic crura, forming the anterior arch of the aortic hiatus and normally lying cranial to the origin of the celiac artery.
Anatomical variations in the position of the ligament or in the origin of the celiac artery may result in its extrinsic compression
(Horton, Talamini and Fishman, 2005). Since its original description, MALS has remained a controversial entity. Some authors
consider it primarily a vascular disorder secondary to reduced splanchnic blood flow, whereas others advocate for a
predominantly neurogenic mechanism associated with compression of the celiac plexus and ganglia adjacent to the ligament
(Weber et al., 2016).
Clinically, MALS is characterized by the classic triad of postprandial abdominal pain, weight loss, and, occasionally,
an epigastric bruit on auscultation, although its presentation is often nonspecific. Consequently, diagnosis may be delayed or
inaccurate. The estimated incidence is 2–5 cases per 100,000 inhabitants per year, varying according to the population studied
and the diagnostic method employed (Farooqui et al., 2025). In contrast, anatomical compression of the celiac artery by the
median arcuate ligament can be identified in 10%–24% of the general population using computed tomography angiography or
magnetic resonance angiography, with the vast majority of these individuals being asymptomatic (Horton, Talamini and
Fishman, 2005; Farooqui et al., 2025). This discrepancy reinforces that the diagnosis should not be based solely on the
radiological finding, but rather on clinical and radiological correlation, which is essential both for diagnosis and for the

3. Results and Discussion
Case Report
A 47-year-old female patient was admitted to the emergency department with a six-month history of abdominal pain
occurring during the preprandial and postprandial periods, refractory to analgesic therapy, associated with nausea, late
postprandial regurgitation, and weight loss. During the most recent episodes, she developed projectile vomiting associated with
arterial hypotension. Due to the persistence of symptoms, complementary imaging studies were requested. Abdominal
computed tomography angiography (Figure 1) demonstrated extrinsic compression of the proximal portion of the celiac artery
by the median arcuate ligament, resulting in approximately 50% luminal stenosis (Figure 2), with preserved flow in the
superior and inferior mesenteric arteries.
Given the correlation between the clinical presentation and radiological findings, surgical intervention was indicated.
The patient underwent laparoscopic release of the median arcuate ligament, with intraoperative confirmation of extrinsic
compression of the celiac artery and complete division of the ligament fibers (Figure 3), without complications. The
postoperative course was satisfactory, and the patient was discharged from the hospital 24 hours after the procedure.

Discussion
MALS remains a diagnostic challenge in surgical practice, given that celiac artery compression identified on imaging
studies does not, by itself, establish symptomatic disease (Horton, Talamini & Fishman, 2005; Farooqui et al., 2025). Since the
original publication by Harjola and the series reported by Dunbar et al. (Harjola, 1963; Dunbar et al., 1965), recognition of the
syndrome has relied on the association between the anatomical finding and a compatible clinical presentation, which explains
why a large proportion of individuals with radiological celiac artery compression remain asymptomatic throughout their lives.
The pathophysiology of MALS remains a matter of debate. One group of authors argues that symptoms primarily
result from reduced splanchnic blood flow secondary to celiac artery stenosis, representing the classical vascular mechanism,
whereas another proposes that the syndrome corresponds to a compressive neuropathy of the celiac plexus and ganglia adjacent
to the ligament rather than a true vascular disease (Weber et al., 2016). This second hypothesis is supported by the histological
identification of ganglionic tissue and activated lymph nodes in periarterial dissection specimens from surgically treated
patients (Butz et al., 2024), and may help explain why the degree of angiographic stenosis does not directly correlate with

From a clinical standpoint, MALS classically presents with postprandial abdominal pain, weight loss, and, in some
cases, an epigastric bruit on auscultation. This triad is not always complete, as observed in our patient. This presentation
overlaps with much more prevalent conditions, such as peptic disease, cholelithiasis, and functional gastrointestinal motility
disorders, requiring the systematic exclusion of these conditions before attributing symptoms to celiac artery compression
(Butz et al., 2024).
Mesenteric Doppler ultrasonography allows for an initial noninvasive assessment by demonstrating increased peak
systolic velocity during expiration; however, there is no consensus regarding the optimal cutoff value, with values ranging
from 200 to 350 cm/s depending on the study group (Butz et al., 2024). CT angiography and MR angiography allow for more
detailed characterization of celiac artery compression and possible post-stenotic dilatation, in addition to excluding
concomitant atherosclerotic disease, an important differential diagnosis in chronic mesenteric ischemia of atherosclerotic
etiology (Horton, Talamini & Fishman, 2005). Digital subtraction angiography, historically considered the reference standard
because it allows dynamic assessment during respiration, is currently reserved for equivocal cases or for planning endovascular
procedures (Farooqui et al., 2025). In our patient, preserved patency of the superior and inferior mesenteric arteries
corroborated that.
Definitive treatment of symptomatic MALS is surgical and consists of complete release of the median arcuate
ligament, with or without celiac ganglion neurolysis (Farooqui et al., 2025). Studies comparing open and laparoscopic
approaches have not demonstrated consistent differences in rates of symptomatic improvement between the two techniques;
however, the laparoscopic approach is associated with lower morbidity, shorter hospital stays, and faster recovery and has
become the preferred approach in most centers (Jimenez, Harlander-Locke & Dutson, 2012; El-Hayek et al., 2013). Technical
variations within the laparoscopic approach have also been described, including the antegrade approach, with safe and
effective results reported in both adolescents and young adults as well as older populations (Bahadir et al., 2025). Recent
Brazilian series and case reports corroborate these findings, describing favorable outcomes with low morbidity following
laparoscopic ligament release in this setting, further supporting the feasibility of this technique (Torres et al., 2017; Coelho et
al., 2020).
Regarding functional outcomes, the largest systematic review published to date on the treatment of MALS, which
included 38 studies and 880 adult patients, found symptomatic relief rates exceeding 70% in most series, with follow-up
periods of up to 228 months. The review also highlighted that the methodological quality of the available evidence is generally
low or uncertain, given the predominance of small retrospective series (Metz et al., 2022). Similar rates, with approximately
80% of patients achieving complete or partial symptom relief, have been reported in contemporary surgical cohorts undergoing
minimally invasive approaches (Butz et al., 2024). Patients who fail to achieve symptomatic relief may present with delayed
diagnosis, residual stenosis or respiratory re-compression of the celiac artery, concomitant functional gastrointestinal disorders
that were not initially excluded, or central sensitization secondary to chronic pain (Farooqui et al., 2025; Butz et al., 2024).
The importance of timely diagnosis and treatment is further supported by reports documenting complications of
uncorrected chronic celiac artery compression, including the development of visceral aneurysms, particularly involving the
pancreaticoduodenal arcade, secondary to sustained increases in collateral blood flow, with a potential risk of fatal hepatic or
mesenteric ischemia (Manchella, Wu & Dua, 2025). Although our patient did not. The present case includes several features

4. Conclusion
MALS is a rare and frequently underdiagnosed cause of chronic abdominal pain, with diagnosis relying on careful
correlation between clinical and radiological findings. Laparoscopic release of the median arcuate ligament represents a safe
and effective treatment for selected patients, associated with low morbidity and favorable symptomatic outcomes, as
demonstrated in the present case report. Appropriate clinical suspicion, together with the exclusion of more prevalent
differential diagnoses, remains essential for the appropriate management of this syndrome.
