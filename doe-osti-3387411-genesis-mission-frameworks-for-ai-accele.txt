 About SCAC
 The Office of Science Advisory Committee (SCAC) provides advice to the U.S. Department of Energy’s (DOE) Office of Science
 (SC) on complex scientific and technical issues that arise in the planning, management, and implementation of SC research
 programs. SCAC’s advice includes establishing research and facility priorities; determining program balance among disciplines;
 and identifying opportunities for interlaboratory collaboration, program integration, and industrial participation. SCAC primarily
 includes representatives of universities, DOE national laboratories, and industry.

 The National Return, in Three Numbers: The biology campaign targets a tenfold-larger U.S. bioeconomy ($1 trillion today to
 $10 trillion by 2050), a tenfold drop in the cost and time to solve a problem using biology, and a doubling of the nation’s scien-
 tific productivity (a central aim of the Genesis Mission). The precedent is real: The cost of sequencing a genome fell more than
 a millionfold in two decades, outpacing Moore’s Law (Wetterstrand 2022). What’s at Stake: The United States holds about a
 quarter of today’s $4 trillion to $5 trillion global bioeconomy; That market could grow to $30 trillion by 2050 (UNEP-CCC 2025).
 Without sustained investment, the U.S. share falls to 15–20% (NASEM 2020), or less than 10% if biomanufacturing also moves
 overseas the way drug-ingredient manufacturing did (Duman 2025). This AI-enabled biology campaign aims to increase the
 pace of U.S. bioeconomic growth, targeting a one-third share of the global bioeconomy by 2050. The potential loss to the
 U.S. bioeconomy with no sustained investment is estimated at $5 trillion to $7 trillion a year (McKinsey Global Institute 2020).

 AI Breaks Barriers in Understanding
 and Designing Proteins
 In 2024, the Nobel Prize in Chemistry recognized two AI-driven breakthroughs in biology (NobelPrize.org
 2024). David Baker was honored for using computational protein design to create a protein called Top7
 in 2003, the first protein with a structure unlike any found in nature. His group has since designed pro-
 teins that serve as medicines, vaccines, and molecular sensors. Demis Hassabis and John Jumper were
 honored for AlphaFold2, an AI model that largely solved a 50-year-old challenge: predicting a protein’s
 three-dimensional shape from its amino-acid sequence. AlphaFold2 has now predicted structures for
 over 200 million proteins, almost every protein known to science. Together, the power to predict pro-
 tein structure and to design new proteins from scratch shows how AI can break seemingly impenetra-
 ble barriers.

 DOE resources helped make this possible. AlphaFold2 was trained on the Protein Data Bank, the global
 archive of experimentally determined protein structures that DOE supports in part, alongside the vast
 public sequence databases DOE has long helped build, including through its Joint Genome Institute.
 Because protein structure informs protein function, these tools are already driving applications across
 biotechnology, manufacturing, energy, agriculture, and medicine.
