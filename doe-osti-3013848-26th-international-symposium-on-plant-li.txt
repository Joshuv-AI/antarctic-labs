Final Technical Report
 DOE Award No. DE-SC0025271
 BOARD OF REGENTS OF THE UNIVERSITY OF NEBRASKA
 26th International Symposium on Plant Lipids

The 2024 International Symposium on Plant Lipids (ISPL) was held July 14–19, 2024, at
the University of Nebraska–Lincoln. The symposium convened 220 participants from
academia, government, and industry to share recent advances and foster collaboration
across the broad field of plant lipid research. Attendees represented 15 countries across
four continents, highlighting the symposium’s international reach and impact.
The program featured ten scientific sessions that spanned the full spectrum of plant lipid
biology, from fundamental mechanisms to translational research and biotechnological
applications.’

Scientific themes included:
 • Fatty acid and glycerolipid metabolism: New insights into regulatory
 mechanisms such as SnRK1-mediated phosphorylation of lipid biosynthesis
 enzymes, functional genomics of ACCase in grasses, and turnover pathways
 during seed development.
 • Membrane lipid diversity and function: Presentations detailed sphingolipid
 roles in circadian regulation, cell death signaling, and lipid homeostasis, including
 comparative lipidomics across diverse plant species.
 • Plastidic lipids and chloroplast biogenesis: Research emphasized the roles of
 phosphatidylglycerol and monogalactosyldiacylglycerol in thylakoid formation and
 photosynthetic activation.
 • Lipid trafficking: Talks highlighted ER–plastid interactions, novel lipid transfer
 proteins, and lipid droplet dynamics in developmental and stress contexts.
 • Surface lipids and plant protection: Studies in sunflower, bryophytes, and
 Typha species illuminated the biosynthesis of wax esters and suberin, with
 implications for plant adaptation and carbon sequestration.
 • Lipid signaling: Speakers explored lipid-mediated regulation of autophagy,
 polyamine-triggered endocytosis, and systemic responses to biotic and abiotic
 stresses.
 • Biotechnological innovation: Topics included metabolic engineering of oil
 content and composition in crops and algae, synthetic biology platforms for gene
 containment, and strategies to improve cuticle function.

 • Emerging tools and methods: The program showcased advanced mass
 spectrometry, dynamic metabolic modeling, and community efforts to standardize
 lipidomics practices.
The symposium featured 66 oral presentations, including two keynote lectures—the
Stumpf and Galliard Lectures—and over 120 poster presentations spanning eleven topic
areas. Poster sessions and social events were designed to maximize interaction, promote
the exchange of ideas, and support early-career researcher engagement.
A highlight of the meeting was the conference gala dinner, which fostered informal
networking and strengthened connections across the plant lipid research community.
NSF support played a vital role in enabling the participation of graduate students,
postdoctoral researchers, and early-career scientists, contributing to the professional
development of the next generation of plant lipid researchers.
The 2024 International Symposium on Plant Lipids (ISPL) provided extensive
opportunities for training and professional development, particularly for early-career
scientists. Support from NSF helped enable the participation of graduate students,
postdoctoral researchers, and junior investigators by covering registration and partial
travel and lodging costs. The conference program was designed to promote professional
growth through multiple activities. Early-career researchers had the opportunity to present
their work through 66 oral talks and over 120 poster presentations, with additional
exposure through lightning talks selected from poster abstracts. These formats
encouraged development of scientific communication skills and fostered peer-to-peer and
mentor-mentee interactions. The symposium sessions featured invited keynote
presentations by leading scientists alongside talks selected from submitted abstracts,
offering early-career participants a platform to engage directly with the international plant
lipid research community. Informal networking opportunities, including poster sessions,
coffee breaks, and a conference gala dinner, facilitated professional connections and
collaboration-building. Further, the symposium included a session on emerging research
tools and technologies, aimed at broadening participants’ technical expertise. Selected
talks were recorded and made available online to extend learning opportunities beyond
the in-person meeting. Overall, the ISPL contributed significantly to advancing the
training, communication skills, professional networks, and research development of the
next generation of plant lipid scientists.
The results of the 2024 International Symposium on Plant Lipids (ISPL) have been
disseminated to the plant lipid research community and related scientific fields. An online
abstract book containing all oral and poster presentation abstracts was made available to
participants and the broader research community through the conference website. In
addition, recordings of selected keynote lectures were provided in a shared folder
accessible to conference participants after the meeting. Scientific findings and ideas
presented at ISPL have also been shared informally through professional networks,
follow-up communications, and collaborative discussions initiated at the symposium.
These dissemination efforts have helped ensure that advances presented at ISPL
