Abstract.

We consider Gaussian free field (GFF) on simply connected domains with piecewise constant Dirichlet boundary data. We show that the crossing probabilities for its level lines are determined by conformal blocks of primary fields in a conformal field theory (CFT) with central charge
c=1
which are degenerate at each insertion. Alternatively, the crossing probabilities are ratios of explicit partition functions of fused multiple
\mathrm{SLE}_{4}
curves, which can be written in terms of fused Specht polynomials introduced recently by Lafay, Peltola & Roussillon in a representation-theoretic context.

We also prove that for the metric graph GFF introduced by Lupu, with appropriate boundary conditions, the crossing probabilities for its level sets converge in the scaling limit to our formulas. In particular, the geometry of the level-set percolation for both the continuum GFF and the metric graph GFF has a CFT description in terms of the aforementioned
c=1
conformal blocks, which are linearly independent and solve the Belavin-Polyakov-Zamolodchikov (BPZ) PDEs of arbitrary orders.

Interestingly, not all combinatorial boundary conditions are amenable for the GFF models — while the CFT contains conformal blocks of primary fields labeled by generalized Dyck paths (i.e., semi-standard Young tableaux), the ones appearing in the above models satisfy specific monotonicity constraints.

2.1.1.

A
(generalized) Dyck path
of
p

\displaystyle\beta\colon\{0,1,\ldots,p\}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\qquad\textnormal{with }\beta_{0}=\beta_{p}=0.

We shall consistently visualize Dyck paths by joining the points
(j,\beta_{j})
and
(j+1,\beta_{j+1})
with a line segment for each
j\in\{0,1,\ldots,p-1\}

|\beta_{j}-\beta_{j-1}|\geq 1

1\leq j\leq p

p

1.3

To such a Dyck path is associated a multiindex (valence vector)
\varsigma=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{p})\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{p}
given by
\varsigma_{j}=|\beta_{j}-\beta_{j-1}|

which reveals the number of GFF level lines adjacent to the
j
:th boundary jump point, for all
1\leq j\leq p
.
The collection of boundary conditions
\beta
generating given valences
\varsigma
is denoted
\mathsf{MLP}_{\varsigma}
; see Definition
2.2
below.

It is natural to index the topological connectivities of GFF level lines by general
\varsigma
-valenced link patterns
. Given an integer

and

\varsigma=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{p})\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{p}

(2.1)

\displaystyle s_{0}\coloneqq 0,\qquad N=\frac{|\varsigma|}{2}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{p}s_{j},\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad q_{k}\coloneqq s_{0}+\cdots+s_{k}.

A

\displaystyle\alpha=\big\{\{a_{1},b_{1}\},\ldots,\{a_{N},b_{N}\}\big\}\in\mathsf{LP}_{\varsigma}.

Proof of Lemma

.

The proof is based on an extension of the bijection between Dyck paths and link patterns (see Figure

(left)): for
\beta\in\mathsf{MLP}_{\varsigma}
, we see that

•

the heights of the Dyck path
\beta

between the link endpoints (dotted lines in Figure

•

the links of the valenced link pattern
\beta

Now, suppose first that
\imath(\beta)\smash{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptsize{()}}}{{\leftarrow}}}\imath(\alpha)
.
By
[
KKP20
, Lemma 2.5]

\imath(\beta)
on the lower half-plane with all links oriented from right to left,
\imath(\alpha)
can be drawn in the upper half-plane with some orientation of its links so that

consists of oriented loops (see Figure

(left)). Define now a function on

as zero on the infinite component, and upon moving inward across the loops of

By the above observations (as this inward exploration can be performed in the lower half-plane), this function coincides with

on
\mathbb{R}
, whence
\imath(\alpha)
is a level-line pattern for the boundary condition
\imath(\beta)
.
The analogous claim for
\alpha
and
\beta
follows by fusion.

Suppose now that
\alpha\in\mathsf{LP}_{\varsigma}
is a level-line pattern for the boundary condition
\beta
. Note that
\mathbb{H}\setminus\alpha
may contain connected components that do not intersect

, so their height is not directly determined by
\beta
(see Figure

, bottom-right panel). We however claim that, by adjusting such undetermined heights, it is possible to achieve a situation where all neighboring components of

.
Assuming this claim for a moment, since
\beta\in\mathsf{MLP}_{\varsigma}
,
``

shows that

is a combinatorial level line pattern for the boundary condition

\imath(\beta)

\imath(\alpha)
on the upper half-plane and
\imath(\beta)
on the lower half-plane and extend

We now prove the postponed claim by induction on

2.3.

We next introduce

,
SW05
,
Sch06
]

These are random curves which arise in the context of statistical mechanics, and their geometry and regularity have been studied extensively.

Consider the Loewner differential equation

(2.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}g_{0}(z)=\;&z,\\
\partial_{t}g_{t}(z)=\;&\frac{2}{g_{t}(z)-W(t)},\qquad\;t>0,\end{split}

For each

\mathbb{H}

The family of conformal maps

We say that the Loewner chain

is the complement of the unbounded connected component of
\mathbb{H}\setminus\gamma[0,t]
, for each
t\geq 0
.

To construct chordal
\mathrm{SLE}_{\kappa}
, one solves the ODE (
2.9
) with

, where
B
is a standard Brownian motion and
\kappa\geq 0

7
7

7

The geometry of
\eta
depends heavily on

and the curves exhibit three phases: almost surely,

and space-filling if

\kappa=4

.
Then, one obtains a family of compact
\mathbb{H}
-hulls

]
.
This curve is an

from

to

in

\mathrm{SLE}_{\kappa}

Indeed, if

Moreover,

For the special case of

For more details on

[

Fix

Gaussian free field and local sets

Fix a domain
D\subsetneq\mathbb{C}

Let
C_{0}^{\infty}(D)
denote the space of smooth functions on

The
Dirichlet inner product

is defined by

8

8

We note that for functions that vanish on the boundary

\displaystyle(f,g)_{\nabla}\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{C}}\nabla f(z)\cdot\nabla g(z)\,\mathrm{d}z,\qquad f,g\in C_{0}^{\infty}(D),

is the two-dimensional Lebesgue measure.
We denote by

with respect to

(e_{n})_{n\geq 1}

standard normal random variables. Then, the zero-boundary

\displaystyle\Phi\coloneqq\sum_{n\geq 1}\zeta_{n}e_{n}.

The partial sums of (

for each

is realized as a random distribution. Its law is independent of the choice of the basis.

It follows immediately from the conformal invariance of

that if

Another important property of the zero-boundary GFF is the

. That is, if we fix some open

\Phi_{D\setminus O}
is a distribution which agrees with
\Phi

Lastly, consider a real-valued function

For more details on the GFF, see, e.g.,

2.4.3.
First passage sets

Another important class of local sets is that of the first passage sets of
\Phi

Intuitively, for

\mathbb{A}_{-a}^{u}(\Phi)=\mathbb{A}_{-a}(\Phi+u)
of
\Phi
comprises

take values at least

Definition 2.18
.

The (upper) first passage set (FPS) of level
-a<0
of
\Phi+u
is the local set
\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{A}_{-a}^{u}(\Phi)=\mathbb{A}_{-a}(\Phi+u)
which contains

and satisfies the following properties.

(i)

Inside each component

of

, the harmonic function

equals
-a
on
\partial O\setminus\partial D
and
u
on
\partial O\cap\partial D
, in such a way that

on

.

(ii)

\Phi_{\mathbb{A}}-h_{\mathbb{A}}\geq 0
, i.e., for any positive test function

, we have
(\Phi_{\mathbb{A}}-h_{\mathbb{A}},f)\geq 0
.

(iii)

For any connected component
O
of
D\setminus\mathbb{A}
, for any
\epsilon>0
, and for any
z\in\partial O\cap\partial D
and all sufficiently small

, almost surely, we have

\displaystyle h_{\mathbb{A}}(z)+u(z)\geq\min\Big\{\!\!-a,\inf_{w\in B(z,\delta)\cap\overline{O}}u(z)\Big\}-\epsilon.

(iv)

Almost surely,
\mathbb{A}
contains no isolated points and each connected component of
\mathbb{A}
that does not intersect
\partial D
has a neighborhood that does not intersect any other connected component of
\mathbb{A}
.
∎

Definition 2.19
.

Let

be the connected components of

sharing a boundary segment with
\partial D
. Then, we say that the

of the FPS

is the set

\displaystyle\bigcup_{j\geq 1}\partial O_{j}\setminus\partial D.

∎

Theorem 2.20

(
[
ALS20a
, Theorem 4.3]
)

The FPS

of level

]

Proposition 3.1
.

Fix valences
\varsigma\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{p}
and a valenced link pattern

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{\beta}^{\mathrm{GFF}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{p})=\frac{\mathcal{U}_{\beta}(x_{1},\dots,x_{p})}{(\det_{\zeta}(-\Delta))^{1/2}}.

Proof.

The proof boils down to computing

\displaystyle{{\bm{\colon}}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{H}}|\nabla u_{\beta}|^{2}\mathrm{d}z{\bm{\colon}}}=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{H}\setminus(B(x_{1},\epsilon)\cup\cdots\cup B(x_{p},\epsilon))}|\nabla u_{\beta}(z)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}z+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\frac{(2\lambda\mathrm{dir}\beta_{j})^{2}}{\pi}\log\epsilon\bigg),

where
2\lambda\mathrm{dir}\beta_{j}
is the jump in the boundary values of
u_{\beta}
at
x_{j}
.
In particular, according to Equation (
1.1
),
we have to prove that
(u_{\beta},u_{\beta})_{\nabla}\coloneqq{{\bm{\colon}}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{H}}|\nabla u_{\beta}|^{2}\mathrm{d}z{\bm{\colon}}}=-2\log\mathcal{U}_{\beta}(x_{1},\dots,x_{p})
.

Since
u_{\beta}
is a bounded harmonic function on
\mathbb{H}
with piecewise constant boundary data,
we can conveniently write it as the following sum of the bounded harmonic functions:

\displaystyle u_{\beta}(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{p}v_{j}(z),\quad\textnormal{where}\quad v_{j}(z)\coloneqq 2\lambda\mathrm{dir}\beta_{j}\Big(1-\tfrac{1}{\pi}\,\text{Im}(\log(z-x_{j}))\Big).

Let us consider the integral

\displaystyle I_{\epsilon}\coloneqq\;

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}\setminus(B(x_{1},\epsilon)\cup\cdots\cup B(x_{p},\epsilon))}|\nabla u_{\beta}(z)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{p}\int_{\mathbb{H}\setminus(B(x_{1},\epsilon)\cup\cdots\cup B(x_{p},\epsilon))}\nabla u_{\beta}(z)\cdot\nabla v_{j}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{p}\int_{\mathbb{H}\setminus B(x_{j},\epsilon)}\nabla u_{\beta}(z)\cdot\nabla v_{j}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z+o(1),\qquad\epsilon\rightarrow 0.

Using the identity
\nabla\cdot(u_{\beta}(z)\nabla v_{j}(z))=\nabla u_{\beta}(z)\cdot\nabla v_{j}(z)+u_{\beta}(z)\Delta v_{j}(z)=\nabla u_{\beta}(z)\cdot\nabla v_{j}(z)
and the divergence theorem
10
10

10

Strictly speaking, the divergence theorem addresses finite domains; integrals over suitable approximating domains can be seen to converge, e.g., by Equation (
3.2
).
,
we find that

\displaystyle I_{\epsilon}=\;

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{p}\int_{\partial(\mathbb{H}\setminus B(x_{j},\epsilon))}u_{\beta}(z)(\nabla v_{j}(z)\cdot\mathrm{n}_{z})\,|\mathrm{d}z|+o(1),\qquad\epsilon\rightarrow 0,

where
\mathrm{n}_{z}
is the outward normal vector and
|\mathrm{d}z|
the length measure.
A computation yields

\displaystyle\nabla v_{j}(z)=\frac{2\lambda\mathrm{dir}\beta_{j}}{\pi}\bigg(\frac{y}{(x-x_{j})^{2}+y^{2}},-\frac{x-x_{j}}{(x-x_{j})^{2}+y^{2}}\bigg),\quad\textnormal{for}\quad z=x+\mathfrak{i}y.

In particular, we note that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\nabla v_{j}(z)\cdot\mathrm{n}_{z}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{2\lambda\mathrm{dir}\beta_{j}}{\pi}\frac{1}{x-x_{j}},&x\in(-\infty,x_{j}-\epsilon)\cup(x_{j}+\epsilon,\infty)\\
0,&x\in\mathbb{H}\cap\partial B(x_{j},\epsilon),\textnormal{for any }\epsilon>0.\end{cases}

Moreover, since
\mathrm{dir}\beta_{k}=\beta_{k}-\beta_{k-1}
and
u_{\beta}(x)=2\lambda\beta_{k}
for

, we have

\displaystyle I_{\epsilon}=\;

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{p}\frac{4\lambda^{2}\mathrm{dir}\beta_{j}}{\pi}\bigg[\sum_{k=1}^{j-2}\beta_{k}\int_{x_{k}}^{x_{k+1}}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{x-x_{j}}+\beta_{j-1}\int_{x_{j-1}}^{x_{j}-\epsilon}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{x-x_{j}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\beta_{j}\int_{x_{j}+\epsilon}^{x_{j+1}}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{x-x_{j}}+\sum_{k=j+1}^{p-1}\beta_{k}\int_{x_{k}}^{x_{k+1}}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{x-x_{j}}\bigg]+o(1)

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{p}\frac{4\lambda^{2}\mathrm{dir}\beta_{j}}{\pi}\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{j-2}\beta_{k}\log\Big|\frac{x_{k+1}-x_{j}}{x_{k}-x_{j}}\Big|+\beta_{j-1}\log\Big|\frac{\epsilon}{x_{j-1}-x_{j}}\Big|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\beta_{j}\log\Big|\frac{x_{j+1}-x_{j}}{\epsilon}\Big|+\sum_{k=j+1}^{p-1}\beta_{k}\log\Big|\frac{x_{k+1}-x_{j}}{x_{k}-x_{j}}\Big|\bigg)+o(1)

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{p}\frac{4\lambda^{2}\mathrm{dir}\beta_{j}}{\pi}\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{j-1}\mathrm{dir}\beta_{k}\log|x_{k}-x_{j}|+\mathrm{dir}\beta_{j}\log\epsilon+\sum_{k=j+1}^{p}\mathrm{dir}\beta_{k}\log|x_{k}-x_{j}|\bigg)+o(1)

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{p}\frac{4\lambda^{2}(\mathrm{dir}\beta_{j})^{2}}{\pi}\log\epsilon-\sum_{j=1}^{p}\frac{4\lambda^{2}\mathrm{dir}\beta_{j}}{\pi}\bigg(\sum_{k\neq j}\mathrm{dir}\beta_{k}\log|x_{k}-x_{j}|\bigg)+o(1),\qquad\textnormal{as}\quad\epsilon\rightarrow 0.

Thus, since the logarithmic blowup in

is canceled by the regularization in the Dirichlet norm

, we find (using the expression in Equation (

\displaystyle(u_{\beta},u_{\beta})_{\nabla}=\;

\displaystyle{{\bm{\colon}}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{H}}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}z{\bm{\colon}}}=\;\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\bigg(I_{\epsilon}+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\frac{(2\lambda\mathrm{dir}\beta_{j})^{2}}{\pi}\log\epsilon\bigg)

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{p}\frac{4\lambda^{2}\mathrm{dir}\beta_{j}}{\pi}\bigg(\sum_{k\neq j}\mathrm{dir}\beta_{k}\log|x_{k}-x_{j}|\bigg)

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle-\log\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq p}(x_{k}-x_{j})^{\mathrm{dir}\beta_{j}\mathrm{dir}\beta_{k}}=\;-2\log\mathcal{U}_{\beta}(x_{1},\dots,x_{p}),

as was to be shown.

[-C,C]^{2}
with zero boundary values on
\partial[-C,C]^{2}
.
We can also extend
\widetilde{\phi}^{n}
this way and denote the extension by
\widehat{\phi}^{n}
. We will make use of the following result.

Proposition 5.2

(
[
ALS20b
, Proposition 4.7]
)
.

Let
\Phi
be a zero-boundary GFF on
\Omega
, let
u
be a bounded harmonic function with piecewise constant boundary values.
Let
(u^{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a collection of bounded harmonic functions in
\widetilde{\Omega}^{n}
, which converge uniformly on compact subsets to
u
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
Then, we can couple
(\widehat{\phi}^{n})_{n\geq 1}
and
\Phi
such that
\widehat{\phi}^{n}
converge to
\Phi
in probability as generalized functions, and upon doing so,

(\widehat{\phi}^{n},\widetilde{\mathbb{A}}_{a}^{u^{n}}(\widetilde{\phi}^{n})\cup\partial\Omega)\;\smash{\overset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow}}\;(\Phi,\mathbb{A}_{a}^{u}(\Phi))
in probability, where the convergence in the second coordinate is with respect to the Hausdorff metric (
2.13
).

5.4.
Connection probabilities

Similarly to the continuum case, we have the corresponding connection probability result for the frontiers of the metric graph GFF.

Consider a boundary condition
u=u_{\beta}
analogous to Equation (
1.3
), where
\mathbb{H}
is replaced by
\Omega
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{p}
by
y_{1},\ldots,y_{p}
.
We let
\Omega^{n}
be a polygon approximating
\Omega
as in Section
5.3
,
and we let
u^{n}\colon\partial\Omega^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be the boundary data such that
u^{n}|_{(y_{j}^{n},y_{j+1}^{n})}=u|_{(y_{j},y_{j+1})}
for
j\in\{1,\dots,N\}
(with the convention that
y_{N+1}=y_{1}
).
Let
\vartheta_{\mathrm{GFF}}^{n}
denote the random valenced link pattern formed by the frontiers of the first passage sets
\widetilde{\mathbb{A}}_{0}^{u^{n}}(\widetilde{\phi}^{n}),\ldots,\widetilde{\mathbb{A}}_{2(\Lambda-1)\lambda}^{u^{n}}(\widetilde{\phi}^{n})
. Then, the following holds (see
[
LW21
, Lemma 5.5]
for a similar statement).

Theorem E
.

In the convergence setup of Section
5.3
, we have
(
denoting the coupling measure
\mathbb{P}_{\beta}
)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[\vartheta_{\mathrm{GFF}}^{n}=\alpha]=\mathbb{P}_{\beta}[\vartheta_{\mathrm{GFF}}=\alpha],\qquad\alpha\in\mathsf{PLP}_{\varsigma}.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that for every subsequence of
(n_{k})_{k}
, there is a further subsequence
(n_{k_{\ell}})_{\ell}
along which the claimed convergence holds. We may, and will, choose
k_{\ell}
so that the convergence in probability of Proposition
5.2
also holds in the almost sure sense along
(n_{k_{\ell}})_{\ell}
.
We now claim that along this subsequence denoted by
\ell
, the convergence
\smash{\vartheta_{\mathrm{GFF}}^{\ell}\rightarrow\vartheta_{\mathrm{GFF}}}
as
\ell\rightarrow\infty
holds almost surely.
To this end, note that
\vartheta_{\mathrm{GFF}}
is uniquely determined by which segments
(y_{j},y_{j+1})
, for
1\leq j\leq p
, are connected to each other within the FPS complements
\Omega\setminus(\mathbb{A}_{2m\lambda}^{u}\setminus\partial\Omega)
for
m\in\{0,1,\ldots,\Lambda-1\}
. A similar property holds for
\smash{\vartheta_{\mathrm{GFF}}^{\ell}}
and the metric graph FPSs.
Observe that, if some segments
(y_{j},y_{j+1})
and
(y_{i},y_{i+1})
are connected by a path
P
in
\Omega\setminus(\mathbb{A}_{2m\lambda}^{u}\setminus\partial\Omega)
,
then, since this FPS was cut out by a chordal curve inside
\Omega
, there exists
\delta>0
such that a
\delta
-neighborhood of
P
lies inside
\Omega\setminus(\mathbb{A}_{2m\lambda}^{u}\setminus\partial\Omega)
.
Now, by the almost sure Hausdorff convergence, the corresponding boundary arcs
\smash{(y^{\ell}_{j},y^{\ell}_{j+1})}
and
\smash{(y^{\ell}_{i},y^{\ell}_{i+1})}

are also connected in
\Omega^{\ell}\setminus\mathbb{A}_{2m\lambda}^{u^{\ell}}(\smash{\widetilde{\phi}^{n_{k_{\ell}}}})
, for all
\ell
large enough.
Since there are finitely many possible values for
j,i
, and
m
, we conclude that taking
\ell
large enough,
we have
\smash{\vartheta_{\mathrm{GFF}}^{\ell}}=\vartheta_{\mathrm{GFF}}
. This proves the desired convergence along the subsequence, which implies the result.
∎
