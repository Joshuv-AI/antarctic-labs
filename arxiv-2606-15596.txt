Interplay of insurance and financial risks in a non Lévy-Renewal environment
Dimitrios G. Konstantinides, Charalampos D. Passalidis

Abstract
. In this paper we consider a multivariate risk model, with common counting process and common process of logarithmic returns for the investment portfolio. We assume that the claim-vectors, the counting process and the logarithmic returns of the investment portfolio satisfy a weak dependence structure. Further, we consider that the counting process represents an inhomogeneous renewal process, and the logarithmic returns represent a cádlág process with independent but not necessarily stationary increments. Under these conditions we provide an asymptotic expression for the infinite-time entrance probability of the discounted aggregate claims into some rare set x ​ A x\,A , where A A denotes a set from a general set family, crucial for the actuarial practice, when the common distribution of the claim vectors belong to a multivariate heavy-tailed distribution class, denoted by ( 𝒟 ∩ 𝒜 ) A (\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{A})_{A} . This result, is derived under a moment condition for the financial risks, and underlines the multivariate linear single big jump principle. When we restrict the distribution class of the claim-vectors to multivariate regular variation, we find more explicit asymptotic expressions, weakening the moment conditions on the financial risks. The asymptotic formulas, derived through double ’dependence solution’, become more direct and practical in applications. With respect to the technical part, due to non Lévy-Renewal framework, the classical Kesten-Goldie theorems are not applicable, nor their extensions. The way we make the discretization of the process of the discounted aggregate claims permits to derive uniform asymptotics with respect to the number of summands, that facilitate the approximation of the infinite sums of the main results.



Keywords: Multivariate risk model; Dependent insurance and financial risks;
Heavy tailed random vectors; Infinite time horizon; Uniformity

1.
Introduction and motivation

In this paper we consider an insurer, who operates
d
-lines of business, with
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
, that share a common counting process. Namely, the claim vectors of the
d
-lines of business, are described by a sequence of non-negative random vectors
\{{\bf X}^{(i)}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
, which arrive at the corresponding moments
\{\tau_{i}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
, with
\tau_{0}=0
. We notice that to avoid trivial cases, each vector
{\bf X}^{(i)}=(X_{1}^{(i)},\,\ldots,\,X_{d}^{(i)})^{\top}
, can contain zero components, but not all of the components to be zero.

Further, the arrival times
\{\tau_{i}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
represent a counting process
\{N(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
, which is defined as

\displaystyle N(t)=\sup\{i\in{\mathbb{N}}\;:\;\tau_{i}\leq t\}\,,

for any
t\geq 0
, with
\sup\emptyset=0
conventionally, and has finite mean

\displaystyle\lambda(t)=\mathbf{E}[N(t)]=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}(\tau_{i}\leq t)\,,

for any fixed
t\geq 0
. We also define the interval
\Lambda=\{t\;:\;\mathbf{P}(\theta_{1}\leq t)>0\}
, where the sequence
\{\theta_{i}:=\tau_{i}-\tau_{i-1}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
represents the inter-arrival times between the successive claim vector arrivals. We also assume that the insurer invests his surplus into risk-free or risky investments, or both. The logarithmic returns of the investment portfolio are depicted by a cádlág stochastic process
\{\xi(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
, with initial value
\xi(0)=0
. Then, the discounted aggregate claims up to time
t\geq 0
, are given through the relation

\displaystyle{\bf D}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{N(t)}{\bf X}^{(i)}\,e^{-\xi(\tau_{i})}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N(t)}X_{1}^{(i)}\,e^{-\xi(\tau_{i})},\,\ldots,\,\sum_{i=1}^{N(t)}X_{d}^{(i)}\,e^{-\xi(\tau_{i})}\right)^{\top}\,,

for any
t\geq 0
(where
{\bf y}^{T}
denotes the transpose of vector
{\bf y}
), while the corresponding discounted aggregate claims over infinite time horizon, are given by the relation

\displaystyle{\bf D}(\infty)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}{\bf X}^{(i)}\,e^{-\xi(\tau_{i})}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}X_{1}^{(i)}\,e^{-\xi(\tau_{i})},\,\ldots,\,\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}X_{d}^{(i)}\,e^{-\xi(\tau_{i})}\right)^{\top}\,.

(1.1)

We focus our attention on the asymptotic behavior of the probability

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left({\bf D}(\infty)\in x\,A\right)\,,

(1.2)

as
x\to\infty
, where
A
denotes a set from a general family of sets, and it can take several interesting forms for the actuarial practice (see the set family
\mathscr{R}
, and related discussions in subsection 2.1). This set is immediately connected with the multivariate distribution classes, which is assumed for the common distribution

Before proceeding to more details about the assumptions of our model, it is expedient to overview the recent literature about the estimation of the probability (

), and of the corresponding asymptotic relations for

, or of the asymptotic relations of the ruin probabilities, under different forms of

Distribution classes and dependence structures

For two real numbers
a,\,b
, we define

,

. All the random vectors are of

, or for a finite quantity

, the scalar product becomes
k\,{\bf x}=(k\,x_{1},\,\ldots,\,k\,x_{d})^{\top}
. For any set
{\mathbb{B}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:=(-\infty,\,\infty)^{d}
, we write as
{\mathbb{B}}^{c}
for its complement set, as
\overline{{\mathbb{B}}}
for its closed hull, as
\partial{\mathbb{B}}
its border, and as

for its indicator function.

For any random variable (or, vector)
Y
, we write
Y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\sim}}V
, when

follows distribution
V
, and in this case we depict by
s(V)
or by
s(Y)
the support of the distribution
V
. If distribution
V
is one-dimensional, we denote by
\overline{V}(x)=1-V(x)
, for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, its distribution tail.

Hereafter, all the limit relations hold as
x\to\infty
, except otherwise stated. For two positive, uni-variate functions
f(\cdot)
,
g(\cdot)
, we denote by
f(x)\sim c\,g(x)
, for some
0<c<\infty
, if

\displaystyle\lim\dfrac{f(x)}{g(x)}=c\,,

by
f(x)=o[g(x)]
, if

\displaystyle\lim\dfrac{f(x)}{g(x)}=0\,,

by
f(x)=O[g(x)]
, if

\displaystyle\limsup\dfrac{f(x)}{g(x)}<\infty\,,

and
f(x)\asymp g(x)
, if both
f(x)=O[g(x)]
and
g(x)=O[f(x)]
are true.
The previous asymptotic notation remains intact for multidimensional set up, for example, for
{\bf f}
,
{\bf g}
two
d
-variate, positive functions, and
{\mathbb{B}}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{d}
, with
{\bf 0}\notin\overline{{\mathbb{B}}}
. We write
f(x\,{\mathbb{B}})=O[g(x\,{\mathbb{B}})]

\displaystyle\limsup\dfrac{f(x\,{\mathbb{B}})}{g(x\,{\mathbb{B}})}<\infty\,.

2.1.
Multivariate heavy tailed distributions

For sake of compactness of the text, the following definitions are provided for distributions with support on the non-negative quadrant, while all the one-dimensional distributions, have infinite right endpoint (namely it holds
\overline{V}(x)>0
, or
\overline{G}_{A}(x)>0
, for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
).

To define the most of the following multivariate distribution classes, we need the following wide set family

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}=\{A\subsetneq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\;:\;A\;\text{open, increasing}\,,\;A^{c}\;\text{convex}\,,\;{\bf 0}\notin\overline{A}\}\,,

(2.1)

where
{\bf 0}
is the origin of the axes, and a set
A
is called increasing if for any
{\bf x}\in A
,
{\bf y}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{d}
, we have
{\bf x}+{\bf y}\in A
.

Remark 2.1
.

We observe that the set
\mathscr{R}
in (
2.1
) represent a cone with respect to positive scalar multiplication, namely if
A\in\mathscr{R}
and
k>0
, then we find
k\,A\in\mathscr{R}
. Further, some sets from the family
\mathscr{R}
, that are useful in actuarial practice, are as follows

\displaystyle A_{1}=\left\{{\bf y}\;:\;\sum_{j=1}^{d}l_{j}\,y_{j}>b\right\}\,,

with
b>0
and
l_{1},\,\ldots,\,l_{d}\geq 0
, with
l_{1}+\cdots+l_{d}=1
, and

\displaystyle A_{2}=\left\{{\bf y}\;:\;y_{j}>b_{j}\,,\;\exists\;j=1,\,\ldots,\,d\right\}\,.

Hence, the probability in (
1.2
), when
A=A_{i}
, with
i=1,\,2
, corresponds to some interesting situations about the solvency of the insurer. More specifically, when
A=A_{1}
, we find that (
1.2
) represents the probability that the sum of discounted aggregate claims of the
d
-lines of business, exceeds a threshold
b\,x
, while when
A=A_{2}
, we obtain that (
1.2
) represents the probability that in some of the
d
-lines of business, the discounted aggregate claims exceeds a threshold
b_{j}\,x
. Even more, in the special sub-case of
A_{2}
with
d=1
, we have
A_{3}=(b,\,\infty)
, for some
b>0
, with the case
A=(1,\,\infty)
to show the tail distribution of the discounted aggregate claims in one-dimensional set up. For more details about the family
\mathscr{R}
, see
[
40
, Sec. 4]
.

Based on the set
\mathscr{R}
, in
[
40
]
was introduced the class of multivariate subexponential distributions. For this purpose, was proved that for any random vector
{\bf Z}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\sim}}G
, with support on the non-negative quadrant, the random variable

\displaystyle Z_{A}=\sup\left\{u\;:\;{\bf Z}\in u\,A\right\}\,,

has proper distribution
G_{A}
, whose tail is presented by the relation

\displaystyle\overline{G}_{A}(x)=\mathbf{P}\left({\bf Z}\in x\,A\right)=\mathbf{P}\left(\sup_{{\bf p}\in I_{A}}{\bf p}^{\top}\,{\bf Z}>x\right)\,,

(2.2)

for any
x>0
, for some index set
I_{A}\subsetneq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, see further
[
40

, for full argumentation.

Let

be a fixed set. We say that the distribution
G
belongs to the class of multivariate subexponential distributions on
A
, symbolically

\displaystyle\lim\dfrac{\overline{G^{n*}_{A}}(x)}{\overline{G}_{A}(x)}=n\,,

where

with itself.

By similar way, in
[
29
]
were introduced the classes
\mathcal{L}_{A}
and
\mathcal{D}_{A}
. Concretely, we say that the distribution

belongs to the class of multivariate dominatedly varying distributions on

, symbolically

, if

, namely for any (or, equivalently, for some)

\displaystyle\limsup\dfrac{\overline{G}_{A}(b\,x)}{\overline{G}_{A}(x)}<\infty\,.

And similarly, we say that the distribution

belongs to the class of multivariate long tailed distributions on

, symbolically

, if

, namely for any (or, equivalently, for some)

\displaystyle\lim\dfrac{\overline{G}_{A}(x-a)}{\overline{G}_{A}(x)}=1\,.

In this way, we write

, while from the one-dimensional properties of heavy-tailed distributions, we obtain that

In

, symbolically

G\in(\mathcal{P_{D}})_{A}

, namely for any (or, equivalently, for some)

\displaystyle\limsup\dfrac{\overline{G}_{A}(v\,x)}{\overline{G}_{A}(x)}<1\,,

as also the class of multivariate subexponential and positively decreasing distributions on

, symbolically

. We say that

, if

Now, we recall some definitions of the regularly varying distributions and the Matuszewska indexes. We say that an one-dimensional distribution
V
belongs to class of regularly varying distributions, symbolically

, with
\alpha\in(0,\,\infty)

\displaystyle\lim\dfrac{\overline{V}(t\,x)}{\overline{V}(x)}=t^{-\alpha}\,,

The lower and upper Matuszewska indexes are defined as

\displaystyle J_{V}^{-}=-\lim_{u\to\infty}\dfrac{\log\overline{V}^{*}(u)}{\log u}\,,\qquad J_{V}^{+}=-\lim_{u\to\infty}\dfrac{\log\overline{V}_{*}(u)}{\log u}\,,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\overline{V}_{*}(u)=\liminf\dfrac{\overline{V}(u\,x)}{\overline{V}(x)}\,,\qquad\overline{V}^{*}(u)=\limsup\dfrac{\overline{V}(u\,x)}{\overline{V}(x)}\,.

The Matuszewska indexes in (

, while in case of

. For more informations about Matuszewska indexes see
[

, Sec. 2.1.2]

, in its standard from. We say that

, with

, such that

. In this case we write

. The previous distribution class was introduced in
[

]
, see also
[

]
for various treatments about

, and
[

]
,
[

]
,
[

]
,

]
,
[

]
,

]

\displaystyle MRV\subsetneq(\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{A})_{\mathscr{R}}\subsetneq(\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L})_{\mathscr{R}}\subsetneq\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{R}}\subsetneq\mathcal{L}_{\mathscr{R}}\,,

2.2.
Assumptions of the model

Now we present the main assumptions with respect to risk model from (
1.1
). The first assumption is related with the increments of the processes
\{N(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
and
\{\xi(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
, that are independent but not necessary identically distributed, and thus the renewal and Lévy processes are not mandatory, respectively.

Assumption 2.1
.

We suppose that the
\{\theta_{i}=\tau_{i}-\tau_{i-1}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
are independent, positive, random variables. The
\{\xi(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
is a cádlág process with independent increments, and
\xi(0)=0
.

The following assumption describes a weak dependence structure among
\{N(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
,
\{{\bf X}^{(i)}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
and
\{\xi(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
, and represents a non-identical extension of
[
31
, Ass. 2.2]
. In fact, this assumption in its static form and in one-dimensional set up, is inspired by
[
1
]
, that was extended later to continuous time models, see
[
36
]
,
[
22
]
,
[
53
]
, among others.

Assumption 2.2
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
be a fixed set. We suppose that the
\{({\bf X}^{(i)},\,e^{\xi(\tau_{i-1})-\xi(\tau_{i})})\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
are independent, with respect to
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, with
{\bf X}^{(i)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\sim}}F
and
e^{\xi(\tau_{i-1})-\xi(\tau_{i})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\sim}}Q_{i}
, for any
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Further, for any
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we assume that there exists some measurable function
h_{i}\;:\;[0,\,\infty)\to(0,\,\infty)
such that it holds

\displaystyle 0<\inf_{y\in\Delta_{i}}h_{i}(y)\,,

(2.5)

where
\Delta_{i}
denotes a left area of support
s(Q_{i})
, and for which it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\;\big|\;e^{\xi(\tau_{i-1})-\xi(\tau_{i})}=y\right)\sim h_{i}(y)\,\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right)\,,

(2.6)

uniformly for
y\in s(Q_{i})
.

Before the analysis of the assumption above, we should make some technical clarifications.

Remark 2.2
.

At first, the uniformity of (
2.6
) can be understood as follows

\displaystyle\lim\sup_{y\in s(Q_{i})}\left|\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\;\big|\;e^{\xi(\tau_{i-1})-\xi(\tau_{i})}=y\right)}{h_{i}(y)\,\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right)}-1\right|=0\,.

Further, the equality to the conditional terms present a usual technique to simplify the writing, and the left member of (

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\;\big|\;e^{\xi(\tau_{i-1})-\xi(\tau_{i})}\in[y-\varepsilon,\;y+\varepsilon]\right)\,,

If
y\in E

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(e^{\xi(\tau_{i-1})-\xi(\tau_{i})}\in dy\right)=0\,,

then the probability at the left member of (

, for any

is bounded from above, namely there exists

for all

, see

. Obviously, if

, then it holds

according to the above.

Remark 2.3
.

The Assumption
2.2
includes a dependence structure among the insurance risks, the financial risks and the number of claims. We can see from (

) that if
\{{\bf X}^{(i)}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
is independent of
\{\xi(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
and of
\{N(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
(but the

not necessarily independent of

), then the (

) holds with

, for any
y\in(0,\,\infty)
. However, the opposite is not true, namely if
h(y)=1
, for any
y\in(0,\,\infty)
, this does not implies that the
\{{\bf X}^{(i)}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
is independent of
\{\xi(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
and of
\{N(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
(see
[
15
, Prop. 2.6]
for a counterexample). Having in mind that

,

, the dependence at (

[

,
[

[

), where the

takes concrete forms.

Hence, Assumption

provides a weak dependence structure for the increments of the processes
\{{\bf X}^{(i)}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
,
\{\xi(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
and

, which includes the independence as special case.

The assumption that

are independent, implies that the dependence of these three sources of randomness happens only in the time interval

, which is plausible enough from practical point of view. Finally, if

, be a Lévy process, then the (

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\;\big|\;e^{-\xi(\theta_{i})}=y\right)\sim h_{i}(y)\,\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right)\,,

(2.8)

uniformly for

. If additionally we have a renewal

) holds with the same

and the

Remark 2.4
.

The dependence structure in relation (

), can be ’solved’. This follows from the fact that for any

, it holds

The dependence of Assumption

Example 2.1
.

Let for any

the

be such that (

) is valid. If it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\;|\;e^{\xi(s_{i-1})-\xi(s_{i})}=y\,,\;\tau_{i=1}=s_{i-1}\,,\;\tau_{i}=s_{i}\right)\sim h_{i}(y)\,\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right)\,,

, then for any

we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\;\big|\;e^{\xi(\tau_{i-1})-\xi(\tau_{i})}=y\right)=\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\,,\;e^{\xi(\tau_{i-1})-\xi(\tau_{i})}=y\right)}{\mathbf{P}\left(e^{\xi(\tau_{i-1})-\xi(\tau_{i})}=y\right)}

3.
Asymptotics for discounted aggregate claims

Here we present the main results, that are oriented to the estimation of probability in (

), as

, as also their proofs.

3.1.
Main results

The following statement is the main result of the paper. We recall that if
F_{A}\in\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{A}\subsetneq\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{P_{D}}
, hence it holds
0<J_{F_{A}}^{-}\leq J_{F_{A}}^{+}<\infty

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
be a fixed set, and the discounted aggregate claims be of the (
1.1
). We suppose that the Assumptions
2.1
and
2.2
hold. If
F\in(\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{A})_{A}
and there exist
0<p_{1}<J_{F_{A}}^{-}\leq J_{F_{A}}^{+}<p_{2}<\infty
, such that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[e^{p_{1}(\xi(\tau_{i-1})-\xi(\tau_{i}))}\bigvee e^{p_{2}(\xi(\tau_{i-1})-\xi(\tau_{i}))}\right]<1\,,

(3.1)

for any

, then we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left({\bf D}(\infty)\in x\,A\right)\sim\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\,e^{-\xi(\tau_{i})}\in x\,A\right)

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\,e^{\xi_{h_{i}}(\tau_{i-1}^{*})-\xi_{h_{i}}(\tau_{i}^{*})}\,e^{-\xi(\tau_{i-1})}\in x\,A\right)\,.

(3.2)

Remark 3.1
.

We notice that if the assumption of Theorem
3.1
, are valid for any
A\in\mathscr{R}
, that means

, and further Assumption
2.2
holds for any

, and the moment condition (
3.1

for which

, then relation (
3.1
) is true for any
A\in\mathscr{R}
. The conditions of Theorem
3.1
are general enough, to permit simultaneous dependence of the claim vectors, of the number of claims, and of the logarithmic returns of the investment portfolio. According to the best of our knowledge, such models have not been studied before, even in one-dimensional set up.

Remark 3.2
.

Theorem
3.1
has two ’good’ properties with respect to its applicability. The first one is that in the last sum of (
3.1
),
each probability contains products of independent factors, which makes its application much easier.

The second one is that equation (
3.1
) holds in fact uniformly for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, see Lemma
4.5
, and therefore the infinite sum at the last term of (
3.1
) can be approximated by some finite sum with ’sufficiently large

’. This is important for the use of monte-carlo simulations for the approximations of probability (
1.2
). In literature, such kind of problems (of infinite time) were faced via some change probability measure techniques, see for example
[
2
]
, however such approximations have been used only in more conservative risk models without interest force. So, in more complex risk models, the uniformity with respect to all time horizons (or, here, the uniformity with respect to
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
) functions as antidote for the practitioners (see,
[
41
]
for more discussions on uniformity).

Remark 3.3
.

The products of insurance and financial risks, at each arrival epoch,

\displaystyle{\bf X}^{(i)}\,e^{\xi(\tau_{i-1})-\xi(\tau_{i})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\sim}}H_{i}\,,

with
i\in{\mathbb{N}}

. To show this, we just check that
X_{A}^{(i)}\,e^{\xi(\tau_{i-1})-\xi(\tau_{i})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\sim}}H_{A}^{(i)}

\displaystyle X_{A}^{(i)}=\sup\{u\;:\;X_{A}^{(i)}\in u\,A\}\,,

\displaystyle W_{i}=e^{\xi(\tau_{i-1})-\xi(\tau_{i})}\,,\qquad W_{h,i}=e^{\xi_{h_{i}}(\tau_{i-1}^{*})-\xi_{h_{i}}(\tau_{i}^{*})}\,,

(recall relation (

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(X_{A}^{(i)}\,e^{\xi(\tau_{i-1})-\xi(\tau_{i})}>x\right)\sim\mathbf{P}\left(X_{A}^{(i)}\,e^{\xi_{h_{i}}(\tau_{i-1}^{*})-\xi_{h_{i}}(\tau_{i}^{*})}>x\right)\,.

The financial factor

4.
Proofs of main results

Our technique of proof is based on discretization of process
{\bf D}(\infty)
, and in the presence of multivariate linear single big jump principle. Hence, we need some preliminary lemmas, mostly on the randomly weighted sums on discrete time, and for this we employ the Assumption
4.1
. In some sense this approach reminds the usual Kesten-Goldie theorems (see
[
24
]
,
[
17
]
) on stochastic recurrence equations, but firstly is not restricted to
MRV
, secondly has not independent and identical innovations (that used on Lévy-Renewal models), while simultaneously contains exact asymptotic expressions. See,
[
23
]
,
[
37
]
for some recent approaches of infinite-time ruin probability through stochastic recurrence equations, in one-dimensional Lévy-Renewal risk models with independent insurance and financial risks.
Let us note that for
\{{\bf X}^{(i)}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
, we denote
X_{A}^{(i)}=\sup\{u\;:\;{\bf X}^{(i)}\in u\,A\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\sim}}F_{A}^{(i)}
, for any
A\in\mathscr{R}
, and
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Assumption 4.1
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
be a fixed set. We suppose that the
\{({\bf X}^{(i)},\,W_{i}),\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
are independent random vectors (with respect to
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
), with
{\bf X}^{(i)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\sim}}F
and
W_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\sim}}G_{i}
, for any
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Further, for any
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we assume that there exists some measurable function
h_{i}\;:\;[0,\,\infty)\to(0,\,\infty)
such that it holds

\displaystyle 0<\inf_{y\in E_{i}}h_{i}(y)\,,

(4.1)

where
E_{i}
denotes a left area of the right endpoint for the support
s(G_{i})
, and for which it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\;\big|\;W_{i}=y\right)\sim h_{i}(y)\,\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right)\,,

(4.2)

uniformly for
y\in s(G_{i})
.

Recall that the sequence
\{{\bf X}^{(i)},\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
is called tail asymptotic independent on A, symbolically
TAI_{A}
, if the sequence
\{X_{A}^{(i)},\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
is
TAI
, namely for any
i,\,j\,\in{\mathbb{N}}
, with
i\neq j
it holds

\displaystyle\lim_{x_{i}\wedge x_{j}\to\infty}\mathbf{P}(X_{A}^{(i)}>x_{i}\;\big|\;X_{A}^{(j)}>x_{j})=0\,,

where
TAI
, was introduced by
[
16
]
. Hereafter, for any
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
we denote

\displaystyle\Pi_{i}=\prod_{j=1}^{i}W_{j}\,,

where conventionally
\prod_{j=1}^{0}=1
.

We also introduce
W_{h,i}
, with
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, that represent random variables independent from all the other sources of randomness (and independent each other), with
W_{h,i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\sim}}G_{h,i}
, and
G_{h,i}
given by the relation

\displaystyle G_{h,i}(dy)=h_{i}(y)\,G_{i}(dy)\,,

(4.3)

which presents a proper distribution, since
\mathbf{E}[h_{i}(W_{i})]=1
(as follows by integration of both
members of (
4.2
), with respect to
W_{i}
, over the entire
s(W_{i})
).

In the first lemma we show that under Assumption
4.1
, the sequence
\{{\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Pi_{i},\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
, with
F\in\mathcal{D}_{A}
, is
TAI_{A}
, with distributions in class
\mathcal{D}_{A}
, while with
F\in(\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L})_{A}
, then the random variables
\{{\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Pi_{i},\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
have distributions again in
(\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L})_{A}
. Additionally, we prove a type of weak tail equivalence on terms of the sequence.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
be a fixed set. We suppose that the
\{({\bf X}^{(i)},\,W_{i}),\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
satisfy Assumption
4.1
, with
F\in\mathcal{D}_{A}
and it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[W_{i}^{p}\right]<\infty\,,

for any
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and for some
p>J_{F_{A}}^{+}
. Then:

(i)

The terms of
\{{\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Pi_{i},\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
have distributions in class
\mathcal{D}_{A}
, and it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Pi_{i}\in x\,A\right)\asymp\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right)\,,

(4.4)

for any
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

(ii)

It holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Pi_{i}\in x\,A\right)\sim\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\,W_{h,i}\,\Pi_{i-1}\in x\,A\right)\,,

(4.5)

(iii)

The sequence
\{{\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Pi_{i},\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
is
TAI_{A}
.

(iv)

If additionally

In the next lemma, we reformulate

Lemma 4.4
.

Let

, such that

\displaystyle\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left(\xi\,Z>x\right)}{\mathbf{P}\left(Z>x\right)}\leq C\,\mathbf{E}\left[\xi^{p_{1}}\vee\xi^{p_{2}}\right]\,,

for any

In the following lemma we find out that relation (
4.14
) holds uniformly with respect to
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, under some stricter conditions, in comparison to these of Lemma

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left|\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left({\bf S}_{n}\in x\,A\right)}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Pi_{i}\in x\,A\right)}-1\right|=\lim_{x\to\infty}\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left|\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left({\bf S}_{n}\in x\,A\right)}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\,W_{h,i}\,\Pi_{i-1}\in x\,A\right)}-1\right|

\displaystyle=0\,.

(4.15)

, instead of

Lemma 4.5

(ii).

Further, we need to prove two intermediate auxiliary relations. The first one is

\displaystyle\lim_{M\to\infty}\limsup\dfrac{\sum_{i=M+1}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Pi_{i}\in x\,A\right)}{\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(1)}\,W_{1}\in x\,A\right)}=0\,.

Initially, by
[

4.4
, for any

there exist

4.12

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[\Pi_{i-1}^{p_{k}}\right]=\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}\mathbf{E}\left[W_{j}^{p_{k}}\right]\leq\left(\mathbf{E}\left[\check{W}_{i}^{p_{k}}\right]\right)^{i-1}\,,

\varepsilon>0
, there exists some
M=M(\varepsilon)\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that it holds

\displaystyle\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left(\eta\,\Pi_{M}>x\right)}{\mathbf{P}\left(X_{A}^{(1)}\,W_{1}>x\right)}\sim\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left(\eta\,\Pi_{M}>x\right)}{\mathbf{P}\left(\eta>x\right)/c}\leq c\,C\,\mathbf{E}\left[\Pi_{M}^{p_{1}}\vee\Pi_{M}^{p_{2}}\right]

(4.26)

\displaystyle\leq c\,C\,\left(\prod_{j=1}^{M}\left\{\mathbf{E}\left[W_{j}^{p_{1}}\right]+\mathbf{E}\left[W_{j}^{p_{2}}\right]\right\}\right)\leq c\,C\,\left[\left(\mathbf{E}\left[\check{W}_{M+1}^{p_{1}}\right]\right)^{M}+\left(\mathbf{E}\left[\check{W}_{M+1}^{p_{2}}\right]\right)^{M}\right]\leq\varepsilon\,.

From relations (
4
) and (
4.26
), and for all
n>M
we find

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left({\bf S}_{n}\in x\,A\right)\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{M}\mathbf{P}\left(X_{A}^{(i)}\,\Pi_{i}>x\right)+\varepsilon\,\mathbf{P}\left(X_{A}^{(1)}\,W_{1}>x\right)

\displaystyle\leq(1+\varepsilon)\,\sum_{i=1}^{M}\mathbf{P}\left(X_{A}^{(i)}\,\Pi_{i}>x\right)\leq(1+\varepsilon)\,\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Pi_{i}\in x\,A\right)\,.

(4.27)

From the other hand side, since set
x\,A
is increasing, and the summands of the
{\bf S}_{n}
are non-negative, we obtain, for all
n>M

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left({\bf S}_{n}\in x\,A\right)\geq\mathbf{P}\left({\bf S}_{M}\in x\,A\right)\sim\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}-\sum_{i=M+1}^{n}\right)\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Pi_{i}\in x\,A\right)

(4.28)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Pi_{i}\in x\,A\right)-\varepsilon\,\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(1)}\,W_{1}\in x\,A\right)\geq(1-\varepsilon)\,\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Pi_{i}\in x\,A\right)\,,

where at the second we apply Lemma
4.3
, and at the third step we use relation (
4.16
).

From relations (
4
), (
4.28
) and the arbitrary choice of
\varepsilon>0
, follows that the first relation of (
4
) tends to zero for all
n>M
, namely we have that (
4.14
) holds uniformly for
n>M
.
∎

The following lemma plays crucial role for the proof of Corollary
3.1
and in fact provides more explicit expressions than (
4
) that was proved in Lemma
4.5
, when we restrict
F
in class
MRV
, under some slightly weaker moment conditions on
\{W_{i}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
some fixed set. Let assume that Assumption
4.1
is satisfied for
\{({\bf X}^{(i)},\,W_{i})\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
, with
F\in MRV(\alpha,\,\mu)
, and
\alpha\in(0,\,\infty)
. If for any
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
the inequalities

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}[W_{i}^{\alpha}]<1\,,\qquad\mathbf{E}[W_{i}^{p_{2}}]<\infty\,,

(4.29)

are true, for some
p_{2}>\alpha
, then it holds

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left|\dfrac{\mathbf{P}[{\bf S}_{n}\in x\,A]}{\mu(A)\,\overline{V}(x)\,\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{E}[W_{h,i}^{\alpha}]\,\mathbf{E}[\Pi_{i-1}^{\alpha}]}-1\right|=0\,.

(4.30)

Proof.

Firstly, we note that since
F\in MRV(\alpha,\,\mu)
, then for any
A\in\mathscr{R}
, it holds
F_{A}\in\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}
(see proof of
[
40
, Prop. 4.14]
), and therefore we obtain
J_{F_{A}}^{-}=J_{F_{A}}^{+}=\alpha
.

From (
4.29
), via monotone convergence theorem we find

\displaystyle\lim_{\gamma\to 0}\mathbf{E}[W_{i}^{\alpha-\gamma}\vee W_{i}^{\alpha+\gamma}]=\lim_{\gamma\to 0}\int_{0}^{\infty}(y^{\alpha-\gamma}\vee y^{\alpha+\gamma})\,\mathbf{P}(W_{i}\in dy)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\lim_{\gamma\to 0}(y^{\alpha-\gamma}\vee y^{\alpha+\gamma})\,\mathbf{P}(W_{i}\in dy)=\mathbf{E}[W_{i}^{\alpha}]<1\,.

Hence, there exists some
\delta>0
such that
\mathbf{E}[W_{i}^{\alpha-\delta}\vee W_{i}^{\alpha+\delta}]<1
, and so by Lemma
4.5
follows that relation (
4
) is valid.

For any
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, from the second relation in (
4.29
) and relation (
2.7
), via the application of Breiman’s theorem (see,
[
32
, Prop. 5.2 (iv)]
) we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}({\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Pi_{i}\in x\,A)\sim\mathbf{P}({\bf X}^{(i)}\,W_{h,i}\,\Pi_{i-1}\in x\,A)=\mathbf{P}(X_{A}^{(i)}\,W_{h,i}\,\Pi_{i-1}>x)

\displaystyle\sim\mathbf{P}(X_{A}^{(i)}>x)\mathbf{E}[W_{h,i}^{\alpha}]\,\mathbf{E}[\Pi_{i-1}^{\alpha}]\sim\mu(A)\,\overline{V}(x)\,\mathbf{E}[W_{h,i}^{\alpha}]\,\mathbf{E}[\Pi_{i-1}^{\alpha}]\,,

(4.31)

where at the last step we used that
F\in MRV(\alpha,\,\mu)
.

From (
4
) and (
4.14
), we find that for any fixed
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}({\bf S}_{n}\in x\,A)\sim\mu(A)\,\overline{V}(x)\,\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{E}[W_{h,i}^{\alpha}]\,\mathbf{E}[\Pi_{i-1}^{\alpha}]\,.

Now it remains to show that (
4.30
) holds. From the proof of Lemma
4.5
(recall (
4
), (
4.28
)), for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\,1)
there exists some large enough
M\in{\mathbb{N}}
, such that for any
n>M
it holds

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon)\,\sum_{i=1}^{M}\mathbf{P}({\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Pi_{i}\in x\,A)\lesssim\mathbf{P}({\bf S}_{n}\in x\,A)

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\varepsilon)\,\sum_{i=1}^{M}\mathbf{P}({\bf X}^{(i)}\,\Pi_{i}\in x\,A)\,.

From the last relation, in combination with (
4
) we get

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon)\,\mu(A)\,\overline{V}(x)\,\sum_{i=1}^{M}\mathbf{E}[W_{h,i}^{\alpha}]\,\mathbf{E}[\Pi_{i-1}^{\alpha}]\lesssim\mathbf{P}({\bf S}_{n}\in x\,A)

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\varepsilon)\,\mu(A)\,\overline{V}(x)\,\sum_{i=1}^{M}\mathbf{E}[W_{h,i}^{\alpha}]\,\mathbf{E}[\Pi_{i-1}^{\alpha}]\,.

(4.32)

From (
4
) we immediately obtain that for all
n>M
from right hand side, it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}({\bf S}_{n}\in x\,A)\lesssim(1+\varepsilon)\,\mu(A)\,\overline{V}(x)\,\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{E}[W_{h,i}^{\alpha}]\,\mathbf{E}[\Pi_{i-1}^{\alpha}]\,.

(4.33)

From the left hand side, due to (
2.7
), (
4.29
) and the fact that the
\{W_{i}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
are independent, we find that the series
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{E}[W_{h,i}^{\alpha}]\,\mathbf{E}[\Pi_{i-1}^{\alpha}]
is convergent, hence by (
4
) we conclude that for all
n>M
it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}({\bf S}_{n}\in x\,A)\gtrsim(1-\varepsilon)\,\mu(A)\,\overline{V}(x)\,\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}-\sum_{i=M+1}^{\infty}\right)\mathbf{E}[W_{h,i}^{\alpha}]\,\mathbf{E}[\Pi_{i-1}^{\alpha}]

\displaystyle\gtrsim(1-\varepsilon)^{2}\,\mu(A)\,\overline{V}(x)\,\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{E}[W_{h,i}^{\alpha}]\,\mathbf{E}[\Pi_{i-1}^{\alpha}]\,.

(4.34)

From relations (
4.33
), (
4
) and the arbitrariness in the choice of
\varepsilon>0
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\sup_{N<n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left|\dfrac{\mathbf{P}({\bf S}_{n}\in x\,A)}{\mu(A)\,\overline{V}(x)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{E}[W_{h,i}^{\alpha}]\,\mathbf{E}[\Pi_{i-1}^{\alpha}]}-1\right|=0\,.

Further, from (
4.14
) and (
4
) follows immediately

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\sup_{N\geq n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left|\dfrac{\mathbf{P}({\bf S}_{n}\in x\,A)}{\mu(A)\,\overline{V}(x)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{E}[W_{h,i}^{\alpha}]\,\mathbf{E}[\Pi_{i-1}^{\alpha}]}-1\right|=0\,.

From these two last relations we obtain that (
4.30
) is valid.
∎

Now, we are ready to prove Theorem
3.1
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1

For any
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
we define

\displaystyle W_{i}=e^{\xi(\tau_{i-1})-\xi(\tau{i})}\,.

Firstly, from Assumption
2.1
the
\{W_{i}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
are independent each other. Further, by relation (
3.1
) there exists a pair
p_{1},\,p_{2}
, with
0<p_{1}<J_{F_{A}}^{-}\leq J_{F_{A}}^{+}<p_{2}<\infty
such that the inequality

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[W_{i}^{p_{1}}\vee W_{i}^{p_{2}}\right]<1\,,

is satisfied for any
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Hence, for any
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\;\big|\;e^{\xi(\tau_{i-1})-\xi(\tau_{i})}=y\right)\sim h_{i}(y)\,\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right)\,,

(4.35)

uniformly with respect to
y\in s(Q_{i})
, with function
h_{i}
to satisfy relation (
4.1
). Further, by Assumption
2.2
, due to relation (
2.5
), we get that (
4.1
) is true. We also denote the sequence of independent (from other sources of randomness)
\{W_{h,i}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
by

\displaystyle W_{h,i}=e^{\xi_{h_{i}}(\tau_{i-1}^{*})-\xi_{h_{i}}(\tau{i}^{*})}\,.

Furthermore, since

\displaystyle\Pi_{i}:=e^{-\xi(\tau_{i})}=\prod_{j=1}^{i}e^{\xi(\tau_{j-1})-\xi(\tau_{j})}\,,

and because of (
4.35
) and the moment condition for the
\{W_{i}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
, we can apply Lemma
4.5
with
n=\infty
to find that

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left({\bf D}(\infty)\in x\,A\right)=\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}{\bf X}^{(i)}\,e^{-\xi(\tau_{i})}\in x\,A\right)\sim\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\,e^{-\xi(\tau_{i})}\in x\,A\right)

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\,e^{\xi_{h_{i}}(\tau_{i-1}^{*})-\xi_{h_{i}}(\tau_{i}^{*})}\,e^{-\xi(\tau_{i-1})}\in x\,A\right)\,,

which provides relation (
3.1
).
∎

Proof of Corollary
3.1

It follows by similar way as the proof of Theorem
3.1
, with the only difference that we apply Lemma
4.6
instead of Lemma
4.5
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
3.2

(i)

Because of relation (
3.1
), and since the process
\{N(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
is independent of any other source of randomness, we obtain by Theorem
3.1

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left({\bf D}(\infty)\in x\,A\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\,e^{-\xi(\tau_{i})}\in x\,A\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\,e^{-\xi(\tau_{i})}\in x\,A\,,\;\tau_{i}<\infty\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\,e^{-\xi(s)}\in x\,A\right)\,\lambda(ds)\,,

which provides the first relation in (
3.7
).

For the second relation in (
3.7
) we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left({\bf D}(\infty)\in x\,A\right)\sim\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\,e^{\xi_{h_{i}}(\tau_{i-1}^{*})-\xi_{h_{i}}(\tau_{i}^{*})}\,e^{-\xi(\tau_{i-1})}\in x\,A\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{K}\int_{\Lambda}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\,e^{\xi_{h_{i}}(s_{i-1})-\xi_{h_{i}}(s_{i})}\,e^{-\xi(t)}\in x\,A\right)\,\mathbf{P}(\tau_{i-1}^{*}\in ds_{i-1}\,,\;\tau_{i}^{*}\in ds_{i})\,\mathbf{P}(\tau_{i-1}\in dt)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{K}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\,e^{\xi_{h_{i}}(s_{i-1})-\xi_{h_{i}}(s_{i})}\,e^{-\xi(t)}\in x\,A\right)\,\mathbf{P}(\tau_{i-1}^{*}\in ds_{i-1}\,,\;\tau_{i}^{*}\in ds_{i})\,\lambda(dt)\,.

(4.36)

(ii)

Relation (
3.5
) follows through Corollary
3.1
, via a similar way as with (
(i)
).

∎
