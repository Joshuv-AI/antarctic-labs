Toric rings of signed posets and conic divisorial ideals via matroid theory
Koji Matsushita

Abstract
. We study conic divisorial ideals from the viewpoint of matroid theory and apply the resulting framework to toric rings arising from signed posets. For a toric ring, we describe the polytope representing divisor classes corresponding to conic divisorial ideals in terms of matroids. We then turn to the toric ring R P R_{P} associated with a signed poset P P . We compute the divisor class group and characterize the ( ℚ \mathbb{Q} -)Gorenstein property of R P R_{P} in terms of P P . Moreover, we also construct a polytope characterizing the conic divisorial ideals of R P R_{P} . This recovers and extends previous results on Hibi rings to the setting of signed posets.

1.
Introduction

Throughout this paper, let
\Bbbk
be a field.
Let
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a
d
-dimensional rational polyhedral cone.
We define the toric ring

R:=\Bbbk[C\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}]=\Bbbk[\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{m}}:=t_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots t_{d}^{m_{d}}\;:\;\mathbf{m}:=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{d})\in C\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}].

Toric rings are objects of interest in various areas such as commutative algebra, combinatorics, algebraic geometry, and representation theory, and have been studied from many different viewpoints (see, for example,
[
8
,
10
,
18
]
).
They enjoy several favorable ring-theoretic properties, and many of their invariants can be described explicitly.
For instance, toric rings are normal affine semigroup rings and hence Cohen–Macaulay by Hochster’s theorem
[
23
]
.
Moreover, their canonical modules and divisor class groups admit concrete descriptions in terms of the underlying cones (see Section
2
).

For a positive integer
k
, let

R^{1/k}:=\Bbbk\bigl[C\cap\tfrac{1}{k}\mathbb{Z}^{d}\bigr],

regarded as an
R
-module.
Then
R^{1/k}
has a natural decomposition as an
R
-module:

R^{1/k}=\bigoplus_{L\in\frac{1}{k}\mathbb{Z}^{d}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\Bbbk\{\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{m}}:\mathbf{m}\in C\cap L\}.

The modules appearing in this decomposition are called the conic modules, or conic divisorial ideals, of
R
.
Conic modules form a distinguished class of divisorial ideals, that is, rank 1 reflexive modules, on toric rings, and their applications have been extensively studied (see, e.g.,
[
9
,
7
]
).
It is known that the number of isomorphism classes of conic modules of
R
is finite, and that every conic module appears as a direct summand of
R^{1/k}
for all sufficiently large
k
(
[
7
, Proposition 3.6]
,
[
32
, Proposition 3.2.3]
).
Moreover, since
R^{1/k}
is a maximal Cohen–Macaulay (MCM, for short)
R
-module, every conic module is also an MCM
R
-module.

Conic divisorial ideals play important roles in non-commutative algebraic geometry and commutative algebra with positive characteristic.
For example, non-commutative (crepant) resolutions (NC(C)Rs, for short) of toric rings can be constructed by considering endomorphism rings of direct sums of conic divisorial ideals (see, e.g.,
[
6
,
13
,
20
,
21
,
24
,
28
,
33
]
).
Moreover, conic divisorial ideals are used to analyze the structure of the Frobenius push-forward of
R
.
In particular, invariants such as the (generalized, dual)
F
-signatures and the Hilbert–Kunz multiplicity can be computed using information on conic divisorial ideals (see, e.g.,
[
9
,
22
,
26
]
).

From the viewpoint of divisor class groups, conic modules admit a convenient description.
Indeed, every toric ring
R
can be realized as the degree
{\bf 0}
component
S_{\bf 0}
of a
\operatorname{Cl}(R)
-graded polynomial ring
S:=\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, where
\operatorname{Cl}(R)
denotes the divisor class group of
R
, and that every conic module is isomorphic to
S_{\mathbf{a}}
for some
\mathbf{a}\in\operatorname{Cl}(R)
.
Let
\beta_{i}:=\operatorname{deg}(x_{i})
for
i=1,\ldots,n
, and call it a weight of
R
.
Then the conic modules of
R
are characterized by a polytope in
\operatorname{Cl}(R)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
determined by the weights of
R
(see Section
2
).

In light of the above observations and current situation, it is important to determine the weights of
R
and the polytope that characterizes its conic modules.
For several classes of toric rings arising from combinatorial objects, both the weights and the corresponding polytope have been explicitly determined:

•

Hibi rings (
[
21
,
25
]
),

•

edge rings of complete multipartite graphs (
[
20
]
),

•

stable set rings of perfect graphs (
[
25
]
).

On the other hand, for general toric rings, the problem of determining these data is quite difficult, and, outside the classes listed above, it has not been studied in much detail.

In a previous paper
[
25
]
, the author suggested that the theory of oriented matroids provides an effective framework for studying the weights and the polytope characterizing conic modules of general toric rings.
In this paper, we develop this idea in a more precise and systematic way.
As an application, we determine these data for a class of toric rings arising from signed posets.

Let
\Phi\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a root system.
A signed poset
P
is a subset of
\Phi
satisfying certain conditions.
We then define the toric ring of
P
, denoted by
R_{P}
, to be the toric ring associated with the dual cone of the cone generated by
P
(see Section
4.1
for the precise definitions).

In
[
11
]
, this toric ring
R_{P}
is called the
weight semigroup ring
of
P
.
In this paper, however, we will simply refer to it as the toric ring of
P
.
Some of its properties, such as defining ideals, Gröbner bases, and complete intersectionness, have already been studied (see
[
5
,
11
,
14
]
).

The toric ring
R_{P}
may be regarded as a generalization of Hibi rings, which are toric rings arising from posets introduced by Hibi (
[
19
]
).
For Hibi rings, various properties have been investigated, including divisor class groups (
[
17
]
), Gorensteinness (
[
19
]
), and conic divisorial ideals (
[
21
,
25
,
31
]
).

In this paper, we extend these results on Hibi rings to toric rings of signed posets.

We summarize our main results as follows:

•

We describe the facet-defining inequalities of the polytope characterizing conic divisorial ideals of a toric ring in terms of matroid theory (Theorem
3.2
).
Moreover, we provide a formula for the number of isomorphism classes of conic divisorial ideals of toric rings (Corollary
3.5
).

•

We compute the divisor class groups and the weights of toric rings of signed posets (Proposition
4.4
and Theorem
4.5
).
Using this description of the weights, we characterize when toric rings of signed posets are (
\mathbb{Q}
-)Gorenstein (Theorem
4.7
).
In addition, we adapt the result of Theorem
3.2
associated with conic divisorial ideals to toric rings of signed posets (Theorems
4.9
).

•

We further specialize the above results to balanced signed posets.
In particular, in this case we show that the isomorphism classes of conic divisorial ideals are in bijection with suitable acyclic orientations of the graph associated with the signed poset (Theorems
5.3
and
5.4
).
These results may be regarded as generalizations of known facts for Hibi rings.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we recall the definitions and notation concerning toric rings and their conic divisorial ideals.
In Section
3
, we review the necessary notation from matroid theory and state our main results for general toric rings. We study the polytope characterizing conic divisorial ideals and the number of their isomorphism classes.
In Section
4
, we turn to toric rings of signed posets. After recalling the necessary definitions and notation for signed posets and signed graphs, we compute their divisor class groups and weights. We also characterize when these rings are (
\mathbb{Q}
-)Gorenstein and describe their conic divisorial ideals.
In Section
5
, we focus on balanced signed posets. We explain the notion of balanced signed posets and establish a bijection between the isomorphism classes of conic divisorial ideals and certain acyclic orientations of an associated graph.

Notation and Conventions

For a set
S
and its subset
S\subseteq X
, let
\chi_{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{X}
denote its characteristic vector. In particular, for
s\in X
, we write
\chi_{s}
for the characteristic vector of
\{s\}
, that is, the corresponding unit vector.
We naturally regard
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
as
\mathbb{R}^{[d]}
and
\mathbb{Z}^{[d]}
, respectively, where
[d]=\{1,\ldots,d\}
.
Unless an explicit representation is given, all vectors are understood to be column vectors.
For a finite subset
V
of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
\#V=n
,
we denote by
A_{V}
the
d\times n
-matrix whose columns are the vectors in
V
.
We regard
A_{V}
as the
\mathbb{Z}
-linear map from
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
to
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
given by matrix multiplication.
For a vector
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we write
|\mathbf{a}|
for the sum of the components of
\mathbf{a}
, that is,

|\mathbf{a}|:=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathbf{a}(i)
.
Moreover, we define the vectors
\mathbf{a}^{+}
and
\mathbf{a}^{-}
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
as follows:

\mathbf{a}^{+}(i):=\begin{cases}\mathbf{a}(i)&\text{ if $\mathbf{a}(i)\geq 0$},\\
0&\text{ if $\mathbf{a}(i)<0$},\end{cases}\qquad\mathbf{a}^{-}(i):=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if $\mathbf{a}(i)\geq 0$},\\
-\mathbf{a}(i)&\text{ if $\mathbf{a}(i)<0$}.\end{cases}

2.
Preliminaries on toric rings

First, we recall toric rings and their conic divisorial ideals.

Let
V:=\{\mathbf{v}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{v}_{n}\}
be a finite subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
We consider the rational polyhedral cone

C(V):=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}V=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\mathbf{v}_{i}:a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\right\}

generated by
\mathbf{v}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{v}_{n}
.
We assume that this system of generators is minimal, the generators are primitive, i.e.,
\epsilon\mathbf{v}_{i}\notin\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for any
0<\epsilon<1
, and
C(V)
is strongly convex.
We also consider the dual cone
C^{\vee}(V)
of
C(V)
:

C^{\vee}(V):=\{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:A^{T}_{V}\mathbf{x}(i)\geq 0\text{ for all }i\in[n]\}.

We now define the toric ring

\displaystyle R=\Bbbk[C^{\vee}(V)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}]=\Bbbk[t_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots t_{d}^{m_{d}}:(m_{1},\ldots,m_{d})\in C^{\vee}(V)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}].

(2.1)

Note that
R
is a
d
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay normal domain.
In addition, for each
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we set

T(\mathbf{a})=\{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:A^{T}_{V}\mathbf{x}(i)\geq\mathbf{a}(i)\text{ for all }i\in[n]\}.

Then, we define the module
\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{a})
generated by all monomials whose exponent vector is in
T(\mathbf{a})
.
By the definition, we have
T(0)=C^{\vee}(V)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
\mathcal{T}(0)=R
.
Moreover, we note some facts associated with the module
\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{a})
(see, e.g.,
[
8
, Section 4.F]
):

•

Since
A^{T}_{V}\mathbf{x}(i)\in\mathbb{Z}
for any
i\in[n]
and any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we can see that
\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{a})=\mathcal{T}(\lceil\mathbf{a}\rceil)
, where
\lceil\;\rceil
means the round up
and
\lceil\mathbf{a}\rceil=(\lceil\mathbf{a}(1)\rceil,\cdots,\lceil\mathbf{a}(n)\rceil)
.

•

The module
\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{a})
is a divisorial ideal and any divisorial ideal of
R
takes this form. Therefore, we can identify each
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with the divisorial ideal
\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{a})
.
In particular,
\mathcal{T}(\chi_{[n]})
is isomorphic to the canonical module of
R
(
[
34
]
).

•

It is known that the isomorphic classes of divisorial ideals of
R
one-to-one correspond to the elements of the divisor class group
\operatorname{Cl}(R)
of
R
.
We see that for
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{a}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
,
\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{a})\cong\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{a}^{\prime})
if and only if there exists
{\bf y}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
such that
\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{a}^{\prime}+A^{T}_{V}\mathbf{y}
.
Thus, we have
\operatorname{Cl}(R)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\operatorname{Im}(A^{T}_{V})
.
In particular, we have

\operatorname{Cl}(R)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n-r}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/s_{1}\mathbb{Z}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Z}/s_{r}\mathbb{Z},

where
r:=\operatorname{rank}A_{V}
and
s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}
are positive integers appearing in the diagonal of the Smith normal form of
A_{V}
.

The integers
s_{1},\ldots,s_{m}
are called the
invariant factors
of
A_{V}
.
It is known that
s_{i}=g_{i}(A_{V})/g_{i-1}(A_{V})
(see, e.g.,
[
29
]
).
Here, for a matrix with integer entries
M
and
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
g_{i}(M)
denotes the greatest common divisor of all
i\times i
minors of
M
.
We let
g_{0}(M)=1
, and
g_{i}(M)=0
if
i>\operatorname{rank}M
.

We are interested in a divisorial ideal called
conic
.

Definition 2.1

(see, e.g.,
[
7
, Section 3]
)
.

We say that a divisorial ideal
\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{a})
is
conic
if there exists
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\mathbf{a}=\lceil A^{T}_{V}\mathbf{x}\rceil
.
Let
\mathscr{C}(R)
be the set of isomorphic classes of conic divisorial ideals of
R
.

We give a description of a region in
\operatorname{Cl}(R)
corresponding to the classes of conic divisorial ideals of
R
.
The following argument already appears in
[
7
, Section 1]
.

Since
\operatorname{Cl}(R)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\operatorname{Im}(A_{V}^{T})
, there is an exact sequence of
\mathbb{Z}
-modules:

0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Im}(A^{T}_{V})\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{n}\xlongrightarrow{\pi}\operatorname{Cl}(R)\longrightarrow 0.

(2.2)

Let
\operatorname{Cl}(R)_{\mathbb{R}}:=\operatorname{Cl}(R)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
and
\pi_{\mathbb{R}}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\operatorname{Cl}(R)_{\mathbb{R}}
be the
\mathbb{R}
-linear extension of
\pi
.
Let
\operatorname{Cl}(R)_{\mathrm{tor}}
(resp.
\operatorname{Cl}(R)_{\mathrm{fre}}
) be the torsion subgroup (resp. the free subgroup) of
\operatorname{Cl}(R)
.
Note that
\operatorname{Cl}(R)_{\mathrm{fre}}
is naturally regarded as a lattice in
\operatorname{Cl}(R)_{\mathbb{R}}
.
In addition, for each
i=1,\ldots,n
, let
\beta_{i}:=\pi(\chi_{i})
and
\widetilde{\beta_{i}}:=\pi_{\mathbb{R}}(\chi_{i})\in\operatorname{Cl}(R)_{\mathrm{fre}}
.
We refer to the elements
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n}
as
weights
of
R
. Note that these weights depend on the choice of
\pi
.

We set
\mathcal{B}:=\{\widetilde{\beta_{1}},\ldots,\widetilde{\beta_{n}}\}
and

\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{B}):=\pi_{\mathbb{R}}([0,1)^{n})=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\alpha_{i}\widetilde{\beta_{i}}:0\leq\alpha_{i}<1\;\forall i\in[n]\right\}\subset\operatorname{Cl}(R)_{\mathbb{R}}.

For
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
such that there exists
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\mathbf{a}=\left\lceil A^{T}_{V}\mathbf{y}\right\rceil
, we have
\mathbf{a}-A^{T}_{V}\mathbf{y}=\left\lceil A^{T}_{V}\mathbf{y}\right\rceil-A^{T}_{V}\mathbf{y}\in[0,1)^{n}
.
Therefore, the divisorial ideal
\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{a})
is conic if and only if
(\mathbf{a}-\operatorname{Im}(A^{T}_{V}))\cap[0,1)^{n}\neq\emptyset
, and hence we get the following proposition:

Proposition 2.2

(cf.
[
9
, Corollary 1.5 (a)]
)
.

Under the above notation, there exists a bijection between
\mathscr{C}(R)
and
(\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{B})\cap\operatorname{Cl}(R)_{\mathrm{fre}})\times\operatorname{Cl}(R)_{\mathrm{tor}}
.

3.
Conic divisorial ideals via matroid theory

In this section, we introduce the necessary notation from matroid theory and establish our results on conic divisorial ideals of toric rings.

3.1.
Preliminaries on matroid theory

This subsection is devoted to recalling matroid theory.
We refer the reader to e.g.,
[
3
,
30
]
for the introduction to (oriented) matroid theory.

Let
M
be a matroid on the ground set
E
with the set of independent sets
\mathfrak{I}
, the set of bases
\mathfrak{B}
and the rank function
\operatorname{rk}:2^{E}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
We collect some fundamental objects associated with matroids:

•

For a subset
A\subset E
, let
\mathrm{cl}(A)
be the closure of
A
, that is,
\mathrm{cl}(A):=\{e\in E:\operatorname{rk}(A\cup\left\{e\right\})=\operatorname{rk}(A)\}
. We say that a subset
F\subset E
is a
flat
if
\mathrm{cl}(F)=F
.

•

A subset
S\subset E
is called
spanning
if it contains a basis of
M
.

•

A subset
C\subset E
is called a
circuit
if it is a minimal dependent set of
M
. We denote the set of circuits of
M
by
\mathfrak{C}(M)
.

•

The
dual matroid

M^{*}:=(E,\mathfrak{I}^{*})
of
M
is
defined via

\displaystyle\mathfrak{I}^{*}\coloneqq\left\{J\subseteq E:E\setminus J\ \text{is a
spanning set of }M\right\}

with the rank function
\operatorname{rk}^{*}(S):=\operatorname{rk}(E\setminus S)+\#S-\operatorname{rk}(S)
for
S\subset E
.

•

A
hyperplane
of
M
is a flat of rank
\operatorname{rk}(M)-1
. It is known that a subset
S\subset E
is a circuit of
M
if and only if
E\setminus S
is a hyperplane of
M^{*}
(
[
30
, Proposition 2.1.6]
).

Throughout this paper, we consider only matroids that are representable over
\mathbb{R}
.
Given a finite set of vectors
V:=\{\mathbf{v}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{v}_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we write
M(V)
for the matroid on
V
defined by linear independence over
\mathbb{R}
.
Then we have
\operatorname{rk}(M(V))=r:=\operatorname{rank}A_{V}
.

Fix a basis
B=\{\mathbf{b}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{b}_{n-r}\}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}
of
\operatorname{Ker}(A_{V})
and let
\overline{V}:=\{\overline{\mathbf{v}}_{1},\ldots,\overline{\mathbf{v}}_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n-r}
denote the set of column vectors of the matrix
A^{T}_{B}
.
Then
M^{*}(V)
is isomorphic to
M(\overline{V})
as matroids via the correspondence
\mathbf{v}_{i}\mapsto\overline{\mathbf{v}}_{i}
for each
i
.

Let
\mathcal{Z}(V):=A_{V}[0,1]^{n}=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\mathbf{v}_{i}:0\leq a_{i}\leq 1\;\forall i\in[n]\right\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be the zonotope generated by
\mathbf{v}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{v}_{n}
.
According to oriented matroid theory, there is a bijection between the flats of
M(V)
and the pairs of parallel opposite faces of
Z(V)
.
Under this correspondence, hyperplanes of
M(V)
correspond exactly to pairs of parallel opposite facets of
Z(V)
.
More precisely, we have the following lemma:

Lemma 3.1

(cf.
[
3
, Proposition 2.2.2]
)
.

Let
H\subset V
be a hyperplane of
M(V)
.
Then there exists
\mathbf{w}^{*}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{Z})
with
\{\mathbf{v}_{i}\in V:\mathbf{w}^{*}(\mathbf{v}_{i})=0\}=H
, and

\displaystyle F^{+}_{H}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{v}_{i}\in V\\
\mathbf{w}^{*}(\mathbf{v}_{i})>0\end{subarray}}\mathbf{v}_{i}+\sum_{\mathbf{v}_{j}\in H}a_{j}\mathbf{v}_{j}:a_{j}\in[0,1]\right\}\quad\text{ and }

\displaystyle F^{-}_{H}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{v}_{i}\in V\\
\mathbf{w}^{*}(\mathbf{v}_{i})<0\end{subarray}}\mathbf{v}_{i}+\sum_{\mathbf{v}_{j}\in H}a_{j}\mathbf{v}_{j}:a_{j}\in[0,1]\right\}

are facets of
\mathcal{Z}(V)
.
Conversely, all facets of
\mathcal{Z}(V)
are obtained in this way.

3.2.
Description of conic divisorial ideals of toric rings

In this section, we use the notation introduced in Section
2
: recall that
V:=\{\mathbf{v}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{v}_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is a finite set of primitive integer vectors and assume that the rational polyhedral cone
C(V):=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}V
is strongly convex.
We set
R:=\Bbbk[C^{\vee}(V)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}]
.
Take an exact sequence (
2.2
) and let
\beta_{i}:=\pi(\chi_{i})
and
\widetilde{\beta_{i}}:=\pi_{\mathbb{R}}(\chi_{i})\in\operatorname{Cl}(R)_{\mathrm{fre}}
for each
i=1,\ldots,n
.
In addition, we set
\mathcal{B}:=\{\widetilde{\beta_{1}},\ldots,\widetilde{\beta_{n}}\}
.

Applying
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(-,\mathbb{Z})
to the exact sequence (
2.2
), we obtain the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\operatorname{Cl}(R),\mathbb{Z})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},\mathbb{Z})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\operatorname{Im}(A^{T}_{V}),\mathbb{Z}).

The last term is regarded as a subgroup of
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{Z})
via the surjection
A_{V}^{T}:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\operatorname{Im}(A_{V}^{T})
.
Under the standard identifications
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{i},\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{i}
,
the composition

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},\mathbb{Z})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\operatorname{Im}(A^{T}_{V}),\mathbb{Z})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{Z})

is identified with
A_{V}
. Therefore, we have
\operatorname{Ker}(A_{V})\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\operatorname{Cl}(R),\mathbb{Z})\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\operatorname{Cl}(R)_{\mathrm{fre}},\mathbb{Z})
.
In particular, the matroid
M(\mathcal{B})
is isomorphic to
M^{*}(V)
under the correspondence
\mathbf{v}_{i}\mapsto\widetilde{\beta_{i}}
.

For a circuit
C:=\{\mathbf{v}_{i_{1}},\ldots,\mathbf{v}_{i_{k}}\}
of
M(V)
, there exists a primitive vector
\mathbf{a}_{C}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
satisfying
A_{V}\mathbf{a}_{C}={\bf 0}
(that is,
\mathbf{a}_{C}\in\operatorname{Ker}(A_{V})
) such that
\mathbf{a}_{C}(j)\neq 0
for
j\in\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}
and
\mathbf{a}_{C}(j)=0
otherwise.
Such a vector
\mathbf{a}_{C}
is unique up to sign.
Since
\operatorname{Ker}(A_{V})\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\operatorname{Cl}(R)_{\mathrm{fre}},\mathbb{Z})
, the vector
\mathbf{a}_{C}
corresponds to an element of
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\operatorname{Cl}(R)_{\mathrm{fre}},\mathbb{Z})
, denoted by
\mathbf{a}_{C}^{*}
.
Then we can see that
\mathbf{a}_{C}^{*}(\widetilde{\beta_{i}})=\mathbf{a}_{C}(i)
for each
i=1,\ldots,n
.

From this observation, we can give an inequality description of
\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{B})
:

Theorem 3.2
.

We have

\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{B})=\bigcap_{C\in\mathfrak{C}(M(V))}\left\{\mathbf{z}\in\operatorname{Cl}(R)_{\mathbb{R}}:-|\mathbf{a}_{C}^{-}|<\mathbf{a}^{*}_{C}(\mathbf{z})<|\mathbf{a}^{+}_{C}|\right\}.

Remark 3.6
.

In fact, the above corollary also admits another proof using the multiplicity Tutte polynomial.

For
\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we denote
\mathbf{y}\sim\mathbf{y}^{\prime}
if
\mathcal{T}(\left\lceil A_{V}^{T}\mathbf{y}\right\rceil)=\mathcal{T}(\left\lceil A_{V}^{T}\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\right\rceil)
.
This defines an equivalence relation on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and hence induces a partition
\mathscr{S}
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
An equivalence class (a cell of
\mathscr{S}
) containing
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is of the form

\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{y}):=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\left\lceil A_{V}^{T}\mathbf{y}\right\rceil(i)-1<A_{V}^{T}\mathbf{x}(i)\leq\left\lceil A_{V}^{T}\mathbf{y}\right\rceil(i)\text{ for any }i\in[n]\},

and it is full-dimensional since
C(V)
is strongly convex.
Moreover, two conic divisorial ideals
\mathcal{T}(\left\lceil\nu(\mathbf{y})\right\rceil)
and
\mathcal{T}(\left\lceil\nu(\mathbf{y}^{\prime})\right\rceil)
are isomorphic if and only if
\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{y})=\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{y}^{\prime})+\mathbf{z}
for some
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
Thus, a full dimensional cell of the decomposition
\mathscr{T}:=\mathscr{S}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
of the
d
-th dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}:=\mathbb{R}^{d}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
one-to-one corresponds to an isomorphic class of conic divisorial ideals.
This argument already appears in
[
9
]
.
In particular, the number of isomorphic classes of conic divisorial ideals of
R
is equal to that of connected components of
\mathbb{T}\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\overline{H}_{\mathbf{v}_{i}}
, where
\overline{H}_{\mathbf{v}_{i}}
denotes the image of
H_{\mathbf{v}_{i}}:=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\langle\mathbf{v}_{i},\mathbf{x}\rangle=0\}
under the quotient map
\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{T}^{d}
.
The finite set
\{\overline{H}_{\mathbf{v}_{1}},\ldots,\overline{H}_{\mathbf{v}_{n}}\}
is called a
toric hyperplane arrangement
.

On the other hand, the matroid
M(V)
carries the structure of a multiplicity (arithmetic) matroid, and its multiplicity Tutte polynomial is given as follows:

M_{V}(x,y):=\sum_{S\subseteq V}g_{\operatorname{rk}(S)}(A_{S})(x-1)^{\operatorname{rk}(V)-\operatorname{rk}(S)}(y-1)^{\#S-\operatorname{rk}(S)}.

For background on multiplicity matroids and multiplicity Tutte polynomials, see, e.g.,
[
12
,
27
]
.
It is known from
[

, Corollary 5.16]
that the number of connected components of

is given by
M_{V}(1,0)=\sum_{S\subset V}(-1)^{\#S-r}g_{r}(A_{S})

4.
Applications to toric rings of signed posets

In this section, we study the toric rings of signed posets.

4.1.
Preliminaries on signed posets and their toric rings

First, we recall the necessary definitions and notation for signed posets and signed graphs.

Let
\Phi\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a root system.
For
P\subset\Phi
, the
positive linear closure
,

\overline{P}
, is the set of
\alpha\in\Phi
which are non-negative linear combinations of elements of
P
, i.e.
\overline{P}=C(P)\cap\Phi
.
A
\Phi
-poset
is a subset
P\subset\Phi
such that (i) if
\alpha\in P
, then
-\alpha\notin P
, and (ii)
P=\overline{P}
.

For a
\Phi
-poset
P\subset\Phi
, we are interested only in the primitive ray structure of the cone
C(P)
.
Accordingly, we define
\widetilde{P}
as the set of primitive generators of the extremal rays of
C(P)
.
In other words, one first removes all redundant generators from
P
, and then replaces each remaining generator by the primitive lattice vector on the same ray.
Although
\widetilde{P}
may fail to be a subset of
\Phi
, this is precisely the level of generality relevant to the semigroup rings studied here.

We define the toric ring
R_{P}
of a
\Phi
-poset
P
as the toric ring determined by
\widetilde{P}
, that is,

R_{P}:=\Bbbk[C^{\vee}(\widetilde{P})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}].

Let
\Phi_{A_{d-1}}
and
\Phi_{B_{d}}
be the root systems of type
A
and
B
, respectively. More precisely, we set

\displaystyle\Phi_{A_{d-1}}

\displaystyle:=\{\chi_{i}-\chi_{j}:i,j\in[d]\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ and }

\displaystyle\Phi_{B_{d}}

\displaystyle:=\{\pm\chi_{i}\pm\chi_{j}:i,j\in[d]\}\cup\{\pm\chi_{i}:i\in[d]\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}.

In this paper, we mainly consider the root system of type
B
.
When
\Phi=\Phi_{B_{d}}
, a
\Phi
-poset
P
is usually called a
signed poset
.
In this case,
P
admits a convenient combinatorial description by means of an oriented signed graph, which we regard as its Hasse diagram.
To make this correspondence precise, we briefly recall the notion of an oriented signed graph.
We refer the reader to
[
35
,
36
]
for an introduction to signed graphs.

Graphs are not assumed to be simple and may contain loops (edges whose two endpoints coincide), multiple edges, halfedges (edges with exactly one endpoint) and loose edges (edges with no endpoints).
In this paper, however, we restrict attention to graphs with neither loops nor loose edges.
This is sufficient for our purposes, since signed graphs corresponding to signed posets of
\Phi_{B_{d}}
have only (multiple) edges and halfedges.

A
signed graph

\Sigma
is a pair
(\Gamma,\sigma)
consisting of a graph
\Gamma
on vertex set
V(\Gamma)
with edge set
E(\Gamma)
and a map
\sigma
assigning a sign to each edge of
\Gamma
except for halfedges.

An
orientation
of a signed graph
\Sigma=(\Gamma,\sigma)
is a map
\tau
from the set of vertex-edge incidences to
\{\pm\}
such that
\sigma(e)=-\tau(e,u)\tau(e,v)
for every edge
e=\{u,v\}
.
Equivalently, one may begin with an unsigned graph
\Gamma=(V(\Gamma),E(\Gamma))
and choose a
bidirection

\tau
on
\Gamma
, that is, a map from the set of vertex-edge incidences to
\{\pm\}
. Then, for each edge
e=\{u,v\}
, setting

\sigma(e)=-\tau(e,u)\tau(e,v)
produces a signed graph
\Sigma=(\Gamma,\sigma)
together with an orientation
\tau
. Thus, oriented signed graphs and bidirected graphs may be viewed as equivalent objects.
One may interpret
\tau
as follows. If
\tau(e,v)=+
(resp.
\tau(e,v)=-
), then the edge
e
is directed into (resp. away from) the vertex
v
, that is, the arrow at the incidence
(e,v)
points toward (resp. away from)
v
.

For an oriented signed graph
\Sigma
with an orientation
\tau
, we define the
incidence matrix

by

\displaystyle A_{\Sigma}(v,e):=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if $v$ and $e$ are not incident,}\\
+1&\text{ if $e$ enters $v$, that is, }\tau(v,e)=+\,,\\
-1&\text{ if $e$ exits $v$, that is, }\tau(v,e)=-.\end{cases}

We now define the

of a signed poset

.
More precisely,

with the edge set

, where each

is regarded as an edge as follows:

•

if

for some

is a halfedge incident to

•

for some

The bidirection of an edge

Thus, we have

).

5.
Toric rings of balanced signed posets

In this section, we focus on the toric rings of balanced signed posets.

5.1.
Balanced signed posets

First, we recall balanced signed posets.
Let
\Sigma=(\Gamma,\sigma)
be a signed graph with orientation
\tau
.
Recall that
\Sigma
has no loops and no loose edges as in Section
4
.

We say that
\Sigma
is
balanced
if any circle on
\Sigma
is positive.
It is known that
\Sigma
is balanced if and only if
\Sigma
can be converted into a signed graph with only positive edges except for halfedges by
switching
operations, that is, for a fixed vertex
v
flipping the sign of
\tau(v,e)
for all node-edge-incidences involving
v
(
[
35
, Corollary 3.3]
).
Moreover,
\Sigma
is balanced if and only if
A_{\Sigma}
is
totally unimodular
, that is, every minor of
A_{\Sigma}
is
0
or
\pm 1
(
[
35
, Theorem 8A.5]
).

We say that a signed poset
P\subset\Phi_{B_{d}}
is
balanced
if its Hasse diagram
\mathcal{H}_{P}
is balanced.
We now explain that the study of the toric ring of a balanced signed poset reduces to that of a signed poset in a root system of type
A
.

Let
P\subset\Phi_{B_{d}}
be a balanced signed poset whose Hasse diagram is connected.
We construct from
\mathcal{H}_{P}
a new signed poset in
\Phi_{A_{d}}
as follows.
Since
\mathcal{H}_{P}
is balanced, we can obtain a signed graph
\Sigma
such that every ordinary edge of
\Sigma
is positive by applying a suitable sequence of switchings to
\mathcal{H}_{P}
.

If
\Sigma
has a halfedge, adjoin a new vertex
d+1
to the underlying graph of
\Sigma
. For each halfedge
e
, replace it with an ordinary edge
e^{\prime}
joining its original endpoint
v
to
d+1
, and choose the local direction at
v
to be the same as that of the halfedge
e
. The local direction at
d+1
is then determined so that the resulting edge is positive. If
\Sigma
has no halfedges, we do nothing.

Let
P^{b}
be the signed poset whose Hasse diagram coincides with this new oriented signed graph.
The Hasse diagram
\mathcal{H}_{P^{b}}
has no halfedges and its all edges are positive, and hence
P^{b}\subset\Phi_{A_{d}}
.
Moreover, its bidirection defines an ordinary orientation, and therefore
\mathcal{H}_{P^{b}}
may be viewed as a directed graph.
We denote this orientation of
\Gamma_{P^{b}}
by
\mathscr{O}_{P^{b}}
.

with

Let

.
For a subset

In this paper, however, we use filters in place of poset ideals, in order to make our notation compatible with the orientation conventions for Hasse diagrams.

Set

,

For each edge

5.2.
Conic divisorial ideals of toric rings of balanced posets

In this subsection, we characterize the conic divisorial ideals of the toric ring of a balanced signed poset.
In the previous subsection, we showed that the case of balanced signed posets can be reduced to type
A
.
Therefore, from now on, we always consider signed posets in
\Phi_{A_{d-1}}
.

Throughout this subsection, let
P\subset\Phi_{A_{d-1}}
be a signed poset whose Hasse diagram is connected.
As mentioned in the previous section,
\mathcal{H}_{P}
is regarded as a directed graph with underlying graph
\Gamma_{P}
and orientation
\mathscr{O}_{P}
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a subset of
E(\Gamma_{P})
such that
\mathcal{H}_{P}(\mathcal{F})
is a signed tree.
Moreover, let
\epsilon_{1},\ldots,\epsilon_{t}
be the edges in
E(\Gamma_{P})\setminus\mathcal{F}
and let
\mathcal{C}_{i}
be the fundamental circuit with respect to
\mathcal{F}
for each
i\in[t]
.
Furthermore, let
\mathcal{B}:=\{\beta_{e}:e\in E(\Gamma_{P})\}
be the set of weights of
R_{P}
defined as in Section
4.2
, and let
\phi:\mathbb{Z}^{E(\Gamma_{P})}\to\mathcal{G}_{P}=\mathbb{Z}^{t}
be the morphism induced by
\chi_{e}\mapsto\beta_{e}
.

Since
\mathcal{H}_{P}
has no negative circles and no halfedges, a circuit of
M(\widetilde{P})
one-to-one corresponds to a cycle of
\Gamma_{P}
.
For a cycle
\mathcal{C}:=(i_{0},i_{1},\ldots,i_{\ell-1},i_{\ell}=i_{0})
of
\Gamma_{P}
, where
i_{j}\in V(\Gamma_{P})
and
\{i_{j-1},i_{j}\}\in E(\Gamma_{P})
, we set

\mathcal{C}^{\uparrow}:=\left\{\{i_{j-1},i_{j}\}:\chi_{i_{j}}-\chi_{i_{j-1}}\in\widetilde{P}\right\},\quad\mathcal{C}^{\downarrow}:=\left\{\{i_{j-1},i_{j}\}:\chi_{i_{j-1}}-\chi_{i_{j}}\in\widetilde{P}\right\}.

Note that we can take a canonical walk
W_{\mathcal{C}}
on
\mathcal{C}
with
W^{+}_{\mathcal{C}}=\mathcal{C}^{\uparrow}
and
W^{-}_{\mathcal{C}}=\mathcal{C}^{\downarrow}
.
In particular, we may assume that
\epsilon_{i}\in\mathcal{C}^{\uparrow}_{i}
for each
i\in[t]
.

From Theorem
4.9
, we have the following theorem, which generalizes the result of Hibi rings (
[
21
, Theorem 1.3]
,
[
25
, Theorem 1.2]
):

Theorem 5.3
.

Work with the above notation and assumptions.
Then we have

\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{B})=\bigcap_{\mathcal{C}}\left\{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{t}:-\#\mathcal{C}^{\downarrow}<\sum_{\epsilon_{i}\in\mathcal{C}^{\uparrow}}\mathbf{z}(i)-\sum_{\epsilon_{j}\in\mathcal{C}^{\downarrow}}\mathbf{z}(j)<\#\mathcal{C}^{\uparrow}\right\},

where
\mathcal{C}
runs over all cycles in
\Gamma_{P}
.

Moreover, the conic divisorial ideals of the toric ring of a balanced signed poset can be characterized in terms of certain acyclic orientations of a graph.

Theorem 5.4
.

There is a bijection among the following three sets:

(i)

the isomorphism classes of conic divisorial ideals of
R_{P}
;

(ii)

the lattice points in
\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{B})
;

(iii)

the acyclic orientations of
\Gamma_{P}
with a chosen vertex as the unique source.

This theorem generalizes the result of
[
31
, Theorem 2.3.13]
for Hibi rings.
Although the proof follows a similar idea, we give a simpler and shorter argument.

We need some notation and lemmas.
Let
\mathscr{A}(\Gamma_{P})
be the set of acyclic orientations of
\Gamma_{P}
.
Note that
\mathscr{O}_{P}\in\mathscr{A}(\Gamma_{P})
.
In addition, for a vertex
v\in V(\Gamma_{P})
, let
\mathscr{A}(\Gamma_{P},v)
be the set of acyclic orientations of
\Gamma_{P}
with a unique source at vertex
v
.

Since
P
is a balanced signed poset,
A_{\widetilde{P}}
is totally unimodular, and hence the multiplicity Tutte polynomial
M_{\widetilde{P}}(x,y)
coincides with the Tutte polynomial of
\Gamma_{P}
.
Here, the
Tutte polynomial
of a simple graph
G=(V,E)
is defined by

T_{G}(x,y):=\sum_{A\subset E}(x-1)^{c(A)-c(E)}(y-1)^{c(A)+\#A-\#V},

where
c(A)
denotes the number of connected components of the subgraph induced by
A
.
It is known that
T_{\Gamma_{P}}(1,0)=\#\mathscr{A}(\Gamma_{P},v)
for any

(
[
16

For two orientations
\mathscr{O}_{1}
and

of
\Gamma_{P}

\Gamma_{P}
that are oriented in the same direction in both
\mathscr{O}_{1}

For any

Assume that

By Theorem

, there exists a cycle
\mathcal{C}
of
\Gamma_{P}
with

or
\mathbf{a}^{*}_{C}(\mathbf{b})\leq-\#\mathcal{C}^{\downarrow}

is the circuit of

proof of Theorem
