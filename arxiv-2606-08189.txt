Every Rank-Two Entangled State is Projectively Steerable
Yu-Xuan Zhang, Jing-Ling Chen

Abstract
Pure entangled states are already steerable by suitable projective measurements within their Schmidt supports, whereas rank two is the first genuinely mixed rank at which entanglement and Einstein–Podolsky–Rosen steering could bifurcate. We prove that this bifurcation does not occur even under the restricted measurement class of projective measurements: every rank-two bipartite entangled state in arbitrary finite local dimensions is projectively steerable in at least one direction, and is two-way projectively steerable when the effective local dimensions are equal. The proof is boundary-geometric rather than a steering-inequality optimization or a two-qubit reduction. A dimension–rank obstruction forces a projective outcome on the larger effective party to hit a pure boundary point of the trusted state cone. At such a contact, a nonzero support–kernel tangent block is simultaneously an NPT minor and a projective-steering certificate; if the contact is degenerate, a Schur-complement peel removes one product layer and preserves the same rank-forcing mechanism on the entangled residual. This gives a directional low-rank strengthening: if an effective m ⊗ n m\otimes n entangled state satisfies rank ⁡ ρ ≤ 1 + ⌊ ( m − 1 ) / ( n − 1 ) ⌋ \operatorname{rank}\rho\leq 1+\lfloor(m-1)/(n-1)\rfloor , then it is projectively steerable from A A to B B , with the symmetric statement after exchanging the parties. Thus rank two is the first complete mixed stratum of a genuine rank hierarchy for steering, and the proof identifies the boundary contact that certifies the nonclassicality.



Introduction.–
The EPR paper exposed the possibility of remotely assigning quantum states, and Schrödinger named this asymmetric phenomenon “steering”
Einstein1935
;
Schrodinger1935
. Bell nonlocality later separated classical locality from quantum correlations
Bell1964
, while the modern formulation of Wiseman, Jones, and Doherty made steering an operational resource: an untrusted party steers a trusted party precisely when the observed assemblage has no local-hidden-state (LHS) model
Wiseman2007
;
Jones2007
. The hierarchy is strict: Werner-type constructions and one-way steering examples show that entanglement alone is not a steering certificate
Werner1989
;
Bowles2014
;
Uola2020
;
Cavalcanti2017
. Operationally, steering also connects to semidefinite programs, steering monotones, and joint-measurability formulations
Skrzypczyk2014
;
Quintino2014
;
UolaMoroder2014
;
UolaBudroni2015
. Pure entangled states, however, already steer under suitable projective measurements within their Schmidt supports. The first place where a genuine mixed-state separation could appear is therefore rank two. The sharp structural question is not whether all entangled states steer, but whether this first mixed rank is already split by the hierarchy or remains an exact stratum where the entanglement–steering gap closes.

This paper closes that first genuinely mixed stratum. Rank one is the pure-state boundary; rank two is the first essential mixed-state test of the hierarchy. We prove that every rank-two bipartite entangled state, in arbitrary finite local dimensions after removing null local supports, is steerable already with projective measurements in at least one direction. If the two effective local dimensions are equal, the conclusion is two-way. Thus there is no exceptional rank-two entangled family hidden behind a projective LHS model. The theorem is not a two-qubit reduction and it is not obtained by optimizing a steering inequality. It is a support–kernel theorem: the density operator itself contains the steering certificate.

The local boundary criterion used below is proved self-containedly in the Supplemental Material; a broader version is developed in Ref.
ZhangChenBoundary2026
. It is complementary to geometric pictures of steering based on steering ellipsoids and EPR-correlation bodies
Jevtic2014
;
Jevtic2015
;
Nguyen2019
. When a trusted conditional state lies on a boundary face, positivity fills a kernel direction only to second order. If a neighboring projective outcome produces a first-order support–kernel tangent block, no LHS model can reproduce the resulting linear-versus-quadratic scaling. The same matrix element is an NPT minor, in line with the close relation between negativity and steering
Pusey2013
. Thus one boundary contact converts a local coherence into both projective steering and partial-transpose negativity.

The global step is a rank obstruction. A rank-
r
state on
\mathbb{C}^{m}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n}
defines an
m
-dimensional contraction space in
M_{n\times r}(\mathbb{C})
. If this space is too large to avoid the rank-one determinantal variety, then some projective outcome on the
m
-dimensional party prepares a pure conditional state on the
n
-dimensional party. Rank two is the first mixed case of this mechanism, but the Schur-complement branch is in fact recursive. If a contact is locally degenerate, one product layer can be peeled off; the entangled residual has rank and effective untrusted dimension lowered by one, so the same rank-forcing inequality remains valid. Thus the rank-two theorem is the first complete mixed stratum of a broader rank stratification of steering: high local dimension on the measuring side forces many mixed ranks to be steerable, while the reverse orientation may force only pure states.

This formulation ties the EPR steering hierarchy to low-rank PPT separability, determinantal algebraic geometry, and support–kernel certificates. Its practical significance is that the support and kernel of a low-rank state are often the most accessible and stable data: they can come directly from source constraints, state reconstruction, variational or tensor-network descriptions, dissipative dark-state manifolds, or a chosen bipartite cut of a larger system. Such structural information is available before, and often more robust than, an optimized steering inequality or a full SDP search. The theorem turns rank/support data from a reconstruction or model into a steering decision throughout the rank-two sector; beyond rank two, the same support–kernel scalar remains a directly checkable certificate.

We use the following terminology. Projective measurements on the untrusted party are taken to mean complete rank-one projective measurements; their rank-one outcomes generate the unnormalized projective assemblage. A state is
projectively steerable
in a given direction if this projective assemblage has no local-hidden-state representation. A
pure contact
for the direction
A\to B
is a projective outcome on Alice whose unnormalized Bob state is nonzero and rank one. Such a contact is
active
if, along some tangent direction of Alice’s projective space, the first-order Bob block connects the support of the rank-one conditional state to its kernel; otherwise it is
degenerate
. This is exactly the support–kernel condition used in the boundary criterion; all new work below lies in forcing, organizing, or reducing the contact. Throughout,
|x\rangle\langle x|
denotes the unnormalized rank-one operator associated with
x
; no normalization of
x
is assumed unless explicitly stated. If
S
is a subspace,
P_{S}
denotes the orthogonal projection onto
S
; for a nonzero vector
x
, we write
P_{x}:=P_{\mathbb{C}x}
. Vectors that label actual projective outcomes are taken to be unit representatives.

Let
\rho
be a density operator on
\mathbb{C}^{m}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n}
with rank
r
, and consider the steering direction
A\to B
. We take
m
and
n
to be the effective local support dimensions of
\rho
. Thus no nonzero vector
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
satisfies
(\langle\alpha|\otimes I)\psi=0
for every
\psi\in\operatorname{Ran}\rho
.

This convention removes a harmless nuisance. If, for example,
\rho_{A}
has a null vector, then every steering statement should first be restricted to
\operatorname{supp}\rho_{A}
, since that null local direction never appears as a projective outcome with nonzero probability. The dimension count below is applied only after this reduction. Under this convention the contraction map used in the next section is injective, so the dimension of the family of conditional vectors is exactly the effective dimension of the untrusted side.

The main result can now be stated without any reference to the proof mechanism.

Theorem 1
(Complete rank-two theorem)

Let
\rho
be a rank-two entangled state with effective local dimensions
m
and
n
. If
m\geq n
, then
\rho
is projectively steerable from
A
to
B
. If
n\geq m
, then
\rho
is projectively steerable from
B
to
A
. For
m=n
, both directions hold.

The rest of the paper proves Theorem
1
. The supporting low-rank statement is more general than rank two; here it serves to locate the boundary contact from which the rank-two conclusion follows.

Rank-forced pure contacts.–
Choose a square-root decomposition
\rho=\sum_{j=1}^{r}|\psi_{j}\rangle\langle\psi_{j}|
, where the
\psi_{j}
are linearly independent but need not be normalized. For a covector
x\in(\mathbb{C}^{m})^{*}
, set
V(x)=[(x\otimes I)\psi_{1},\ldots,(x\otimes I)\psi_{r}]
, an
n\times r
matrix; for a unit representative of a projective outcome
\alpha
we use
x=\langle\alpha|
. Then the unnormalized Bob state is
\sigma_{\alpha}=\operatorname{Tr}_{A}[(P_{\alpha}\otimes I)\rho]=V(\langle\alpha|)V(\langle\alpha|)^{\dagger}
. Hence
\sigma_{\alpha}
is a nonzero pure conditional state exactly when
V(\langle\alpha|)
has rank one. Equivalently, for some line
\mathbb{C}\phi\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
,

|\alpha\rangle\otimes\phi^{\perp}\subseteq\operatorname{Ker}\rho.

(1)

The covector map
x\mapsto V(x)
is linear; the passage from a ket
\alpha
to
\langle\alpha|
is only the usual antilinear Riesz identification. The vectors orthogonal to
\phi
are precisely the zero directions of
\sigma_{\alpha}
, and positivity converts zero expectation values into kernel vectors.

On the effective Alice support this covector map is injective. Its image is therefore an
m
-dimensional linear subspace
L\subset M_{n\times r}(\mathbb{C})
. In projective space
\mathbb{P}(M_{n\times r})
, the nonzero rank-one matrices form the Segre variety
\Sigma_{1}=\mathbb{P}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{P}^{r-1}
of dimension
n+r-2
. The projectivization
\mathbb{P}L
has dimension
m-1
, while the ambient projective dimension is
nr-1
. Over
\mathbb{C}
, the projective dimension theorem for projective varieties
Harris1992
implies that avoiding
\Sigma_{1}
would require
(m-1)+(n+r-2)<nr-1
, equivalently
m\leq(n-1)(r-1)
. Therefore the strict reverse inequality,
m>(n-1)(r-1)
, forces a nonzero rank-one matrix in
L
. Equivalently,

r\leq 1+\left\lfloor\frac{m-1}{n-1}\right\rfloor,

(2)

forces the tensor-zero slice in Eq. (
1
) for the direction
A\to B
. Exchanging
m
and
n
gives the corresponding condition for
B\to A
.

Equivalently, we have proved the following auxiliary lemma.

Lemma 1
(Rank-forced pure contact)

For the steering direction
A\to B
, let
m
and
n
be the effective local dimensions and let
r=\operatorname{rank}\rho
. If

r\leq 1+\left\lfloor\frac{m-1}{n-1}\right\rfloor,

(3)

then there exists a projective outcome on Alice, with unit representative
\alpha
, such that
\langle\alpha|\rho|\alpha\rangle
is nonzero and rank one. Equivalently, for some Bob line
\mathbb{C}\phi
, one has
|\alpha\rangle\otimes\phi^{\perp}\subseteq\operatorname{Ker}\rho
, meaning
|\alpha\rangle\otimes|\beta\rangle\in\operatorname{Ker}\rho
for every
\beta\perp\phi
.

This count is the rank-avoidance form of the determinantal bound, closely related to the classical Flanders–Atkinson theory of spaces of matrices with restricted rank
Flanders1962
;
AtkinsonLloyd1980
. A linear subspace of
M_{n\times r}
with no nonzero rank-one matrix has dimension at most
(n-1)(r-1)
. The Supplemental Material gives an explicit rank-one-free construction showing that this threshold is sharp as a universal pure-contact guarantee on each nonempty full-effective exact-rank layer. In the present setting, avoiding rank-one matrices would mean that every nonzero projective outcome on the untrusted side prepares a Bob state of rank at least two. Once the untrusted effective dimension exceeds this avoidance bound, some outcome must prepare a pure Bob state.

The asymmetry of Eq. (
2
) is explicit. For a fixed steering direction
A\to B
, the untrusted dimension
m
helps, whereas the trusted dimension
n
makes rank-one conditional states harder to force. Thus, in
d\otimes 2
, steering from the
d
-dimensional side to the qubit side has the large window
r\leq d
, whereas the reverse direction is forced only in the trivial rank-one case when
d>2
. For equal dimensions
m=n=d
, the forced window begins with
r=2
and, for
d>2
, does not include
r=3
. This asymmetry reflects the directional nature of steering.

Steering consequence.–
Assume Eq. (
2
), and choose unit representatives
\alpha_{0}
and
\phi
such that
A:=\langle\alpha_{0}|\rho|\alpha_{0}\rangle=aP_{\phi}
with
a>0
. For a unit tangent direction
\alpha_{1}\perp\alpha_{0}
, write the associated two-dimensional Alice block as
\rho_{01}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&B\\
B^{\dagger}&D\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, where

B=(\langle\alpha_{0}|\otimes I)\rho(|\alpha_{1}\rangle\otimes I)
and
D=\langle\alpha_{1}|\rho|\alpha_{1}\rangle
. The projective vector
|\xi_{t}\rangle=(|\alpha_{0}\rangle+t|\alpha_{1}\rangle)/\sqrt{1+t^{2}}
prepares
\sigma_{t}=[A+t(B+B^{\dagger})+t^{2}D]/(1+t^{2})
. Let
\beta\in\operatorname{Ker}A=\phi^{\perp}
be unit. Positivity gives
B^{\dagger}\beta=0
, because
\beta
is a zero direction of the positive corner
A
. Hence, in the Bob slice
\operatorname{span}\{\phi,\beta\}
, the
\phi
–
\beta
entry of
\sigma_{t}
is
t\langle\phi|B|\beta\rangle+O(t^{2})
, whereas the kernel population is
O(t^{2})
. Thus a nonzero matrix element
\langle\phi|B|\beta\rangle
is precisely the first-order tangential motion accompanied only by a second-order inward defect.

The steering input is the following boundary-contact certificate, proved in the Supplemental Material and stated here in the form used below; it is the support–kernel form of the boundary theorem in Ref.
ZhangChenBoundary2026
.

Lemma 2
(Boundary-contact certificate)

Let
A=aP_{\phi}
,
a>0
, be a pure contact in the direction
A\to B
, with
\alpha_{0}
and
\phi
chosen unit. Let
B=(\langle\alpha_{0}|\otimes I)\rho(|\alpha_{1}\rangle\otimes I)
be the first-order block along a unit tangent direction
\alpha_{1}\perp\alpha_{0}
. If

P_{\operatorname{supp}A}BP_{\operatorname{Ker}A}\neq 0,

(4)

then, with partial transpose on Bob’s trusted Hilbert space,

\rho^{\Gamma_{B}}\not\geq 0,\qquad\rho\text{ is projectively steerable from }A\text{ to }B.

(5)

The NPT conclusion comes from the same two-dimensional compression: in the partial transpose on Bob, the matrix element
\langle\phi|B|\beta\rangle
becomes the off-diagonal entry of a principal block with a zero diagonal entry, and hence gives a negative determinant. The steering conclusion is the cap-scaling obstruction proved in the Supplemental Material: an LHS model cannot reproduce the required linear tangential displacement while the trusted-state weight in the kernel direction remains only quadratic.

Thus Lemma
1
supplies the pure boundary contact, while Lemma
2
turns a nonzero support–kernel block into both NPT entanglement and projective steering.

The two ingredients should be kept separate. The rank bound alone forces Eq. (
1
); it does not, by itself, force activity. If the forced contact is active, the boundary certificate gives NPT and projective steering. If it is degenerate, this is not a failure of the rank method: a Schur complement peels off one product layer and transfers the same question to an entangled residual. The proof therefore follows the dichotomy

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\text{active contact}&\Rightarrow&\text{NPT and projective steering},\\
\text{degenerate contact}&\Rightarrow&\text{Schur peel and recurse}.\end{array}

(6)

In rank two the first degenerate peel leaves a pure residual, recovering the original rank-two branch; in higher rank each degenerate peel lowers the rank and keeps the recursion finite.

A useful summary is the following. In the rank window, the implication
\text{entangled}\Longrightarrow\text{contact}
is dimension-theoretic, while
\text{contact}+\text{nondegeneracy}\Longrightarrow\text{projective steering}
is local and boundary-geometric. The first implication is insensitive to partial transposition and does not use steering. The second is insensitive to the rest of the Hilbert space, because it only uses the two-dimensional trusted slice generated by the support vector and one kernel vector. This separation is why the argument extends beyond two qubits without relying on a Bloch-ellipsoid picture.

Schur-complement recursion and proof.–
We now prove a slightly stronger statement and then read Theorem
1
from it. The only new point beyond the boundary certificate is that degenerate contacts do not stop the argument: they peel off one product layer and leave the same rank-forcing problem on a smaller entangled residual.

Lemma 3
(Degenerate-contact peel)

Let
\rho
be supported effectively on
\mathbb{C}^{m}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and suppose that, in the direction
A\to B
, there is a pure contact

A:=\langle\alpha_{0}|\rho|\alpha_{0}\rangle=aP_{\phi},\qquad a>0.

For
\eta\perp\alpha_{0}
, set

B_{\eta}=(\langle\alpha_{0}|\otimes I)\rho(|\eta\rangle\otimes I).

Assume that every tangent block at this contact is degenerate, i.e.

P_{\operatorname{supp}A}B_{\eta}P_{\operatorname{Ker}A}=0\quad\text{for all }\eta\perp\alpha_{0}.

Then, after choosing
\alpha_{0}
unit and writing
\mathbb{C}^{m}=\mathbb{C}\alpha_{0}\oplus\mathcal{K}_{A}
with
\mathcal{K}_{A}=\alpha_{0}^{\perp}
, one has a Schur-complement decomposition

\rho=a|\chi\rangle\langle\chi|\otimes P_{\phi}+\rho^{(1)},\qquad\chi=\alpha_{0}+\gamma,

(7)

where
\gamma\in\mathcal{K}_{A}
and
\rho^{(1)}\geq 0
is supported on
\mathcal{K}_{A}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Moreover,

\operatorname{rank}\rho^{(1)}=\operatorname{rank}\rho-1,\qquad\dim\operatorname{supp}\operatorname{Tr}_{B}\rho^{(1)}=m-1.

(8)

If
\rho
is entangled, then
\rho^{(1)}
is entangled.

Sketch of proof.
Positivity of the two-by-two block determined by
\alpha_{0}
and a tangent vector
\eta
first gives
B_{\eta}^{\dagger}\beta=0
for every
\beta\perp\phi
, hence
\operatorname{Ran}B_{\eta}\subseteq\mathbb{C}\phi
. The degeneracy assumption also gives
B_{\eta}P_{\phi^{\perp}}=0
, so
B_{\eta}
is a scalar multiple of
P_{\phi}
. Thus the whole off-diagonal coupling from the contact corner lies in the product line
\mathcal{K}_{A}\otimes\mathbb{C}\phi
. Completing the square in the one-dimensional corner
aP_{\alpha_{0}\otimes\phi}
gives Eq. (
7
). The first term is a positive rank-one product operator, and the Schur complement is positive and supported on
\mathcal{K}_{A}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n}
. The two ranges intersect trivially. Since the peeled product vector has a nonzero
\alpha_{0}
component, the Alice support decomposes as

\operatorname{supp}\operatorname{Tr}_{B}\rho=\mathbb{C}\chi\dotplus\operatorname{supp}\operatorname{Tr}_{B}\rho^{(1)},

so effective support gives Eq. (
8
). Finally, if
\rho^{(1)}
were separable, then Eq. (
7
) would be a separable decomposition of
\rho
. Details, including the lifting of active contacts back through the peel, are given in the Supplemental Material.
\square

The rank-forcing condition is stable under this peel. If

m-1\geq(n-1)(r-1),\qquad r=\operatorname{rank}\rho,

(9)

then after a degenerate peel the residual has
r_{1}=r-1
,
m_{1}=m-1
, and trusted effective dimension
n_{1}\leq n
. If the residual is not pure, its entanglement gives
m_{1},n_{1}\geq 2
, so the rank-forcing count may be reapplied. Hence

\displaystyle m_{1}-1-(n_{1}-1)(r_{1}-1)

\displaystyle\geq[m-1-(n-1)(r-1)]

\displaystyle\quad+(n-2)

\displaystyle\quad+(n-n_{1})(r-2)\geq 0.

(10)

Thus a new pure contact is again forced unless the residual has already become pure.

Theorem 2
(Directional rank-forced steering)

Let
\rho
be an entangled state on effective local supports
\mathbb{C}^{m}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n}
, with
m,n\geq 2
and rank
r
. If

r\leq 1+\left\lfloor\frac{m-1}{n-1}\right\rfloor,

(11)

then
\rho
is projectively steerable from
A
to
B
. Exchanging the parties gives the symmetric statement: if

r\leq 1+\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{m-1}\right\rfloor,

(12)

then
\rho
is projectively steerable from
B
to
A
.

Proof.
We prove the first direction. If
r=1
, entanglement means that
\rho
is a pure entangled state, and a pair of nonzero Schmidt coefficients gives a pure contact with an active tangent direction. Lemma
2
then gives projective steering. If
r>1
, Lemma
1
gives a pure contact. If this contact is active, Lemma
2
again applies. If it is degenerate, Lemma
3
peels off one product layer and leaves an entangled residual satisfying the same rank-forcing inequality. Iterating, the rank strictly decreases at every degenerate step. The process therefore terminates after finitely many steps at either an active contact or a pure entangled residual; in the latter case, a Schmidt-pair tangent supplies an active contact. This active contact of the residual lifts through all previous Schur peels: the affine lifts of the form
Jx=x-\langle\gamma|x\rangle\alpha_{0}
, one for each peel, annihilate the corresponding peeled product layers, so the support–kernel matrix element is unchanged. Hence the original state is projectively steerable.
\square

The title theorem is the first mixed-rank corollary. If
\operatorname{rank}\rho=2
and
m\geq n
, then Eq. (
11
) holds and Theorem
2
gives steering from
A
to
B
. If
n\geq m
, the exchanged statement gives steering from
B
to
A
. For equal effective dimensions both conditions hold, proving Theorem
1
. Thus rank two is not only a special case: it is the lowest mixed stratum in which the rank hierarchy already gives a complete projective-steering statement.

Certificate and rank window.–
The proof leaves a compact certificate beyond the rank-two theorem. A forced pure contact gives unit vectors
\alpha_{0}
and
\phi
with
\langle\alpha_{0}|\rho|\alpha_{0}\rangle=aP_{\phi}
. A tangent direction
\alpha_{1}
is active precisely when some scalar
c_{\beta}=\langle\phi|B_{\alpha_{1}}|\beta\rangle
, with
\beta
a unit vector orthogonal to
\phi
, is nonzero. The two-dimensional Bob compression onto
\operatorname{span}\{\phi,\beta\}
already contains the boundary-steering obstruction, and the same scalar gives a negative
2\times 2
partial-transpose minor. Thus the certificate consists only of a contact vector, a tangent vector, and a kernel direction; no auxiliary ensemble or steering inequality is needed. The full proof is given in the Supplemental Material.

The rank-forced window also has no PPT entanglement. The low-rank PPT separability theorem says that a PPT state of rank at most
\max\{m,n\}
is separable

;
Horodecki1996
;
HorodeckiRMP2009
;
HorodeckiLowRank2000
. Since the directional window satisfies

r\leq 1+\left\lfloor\frac{m-1}{n-1}\right\rfloor\leq m\leq\max\{m,n\},

(13)

every entangled state in this window is NPT. This PPT comparison is separate from the steering proof: projective steering is certified by the nonzero support–kernel tangent block above. For
d\otimes 2
the direction
d\to 2
gives
r\leq d
; for
d\otimes d
the two-sided rank-two case is exactly the complete sector of Theorem
1
.

Discussion and outlook.–
Theorem
1
identifies rank two as the first genuine mixed-state test of the entanglement–steering hierarchy and closes it. Once the steering orientation is chosen according to the effective local dimensions, there is no exceptional rank-two entangled family with a projective LHS model. This is a sector theorem, not a witness for a selected ansatz: the first possible essential separation between entanglement and projective steering is pushed beyond rank two, and measurement optimization disappears from the first mixed-state rank.

Beyond the rank-two theorem, the proof also leaves a usable certificate. First pass to effective local supports and choose the steering direction. Second, check the rank window and solve the rank-one contraction problem for
L=\{V(x):x\in(\mathbb{C}^{m})^{*}\}
. This identifies a product-zero slice
\alpha\otimes\phi^{\perp}\subseteq\operatorname{Ker}\rho
and hence a pure trusted conditional state. Third, for a unit tangent direction
\alpha_{1}\perp\alpha_{0}
, inspect the neighboring block
B_{\alpha_{1}}:=(\langle\alpha_{0}|\otimes I)\rho(|\alpha_{1}\rangle\otimes I)
for a nonzero scalar
\langle\phi|B_{\alpha_{1}}|\beta\rangle
. Such a scalar is simultaneously an NPT minor and a projective-steering certificate. Low-rank linear algebra is thereby converted directly into steering, without constructing an independent inequality.

The reach is not restricted to two qubits or to specially parametrized examples. The argument uses only the support and kernel of the density operator, the parts of a low-rank state that are often fixed by preparation, reconstruction, variational structure, dissipative dark-state manifolds, or the chosen bipartition of a larger system. In such settings structural data are typically more robust than an optimized steering inequality and cheaper to use than a full SDP search. The present theorem shows that, at rank two, this robust information already fixes the projective-steering status of every entangled state.

The conceptual message is that steering can be forced by how a state meets the boundary of the trusted positive cone. At an active contact, positivity supplies only a quadratic inward filling while quantum mechanics supplies a linear tangential coherence; an LHS model would have to concentrate hidden-state weight in caps shrinking to a point. Rank two is the first mixed-state sector where the global rank constraint and the local boundary obstruction lock together completely, turning entanglement itself into projective steering.
