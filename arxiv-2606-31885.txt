Halo Semantics for Modal Logic
Yoàv Montacute

Abstract
In nonstandard analysis the halo of a point in a topological space is the intersection of the nonstandard extensions of all its open neighbourhoods. We define a parametric family of modal operators from the halo by varying which elements of the nonstandard extension are admitted as witnesses, and identify four canonical instances. Two recover well-known modalities: the topological closure and the Cantor derivative. A third reduces to Kripke semantics over the specialisation preorder. The fourth, purely nonstandard instance admits only nonstandard witnesses. The Transfer Principle forces it to coincide with the ω \omega -accumulation point operator, a classical topological notion not previously studied in modal logic. Unlike the Cantor derivative, the ω \omega -accumulation operator maps arbitrary sets to closed sets without any separation axiom, yielding an ω \omega -Cantor–Bendixson decomposition on all topological spaces. Axiom 4 holds universally, again without separation conditions. We prove that 𝖪𝟦 \mathsf{K4} is the complete logic over infinite spaces, and 𝖦𝖫 \mathsf{GL} over infinite ω \omega -scattered spaces.



1
Introduction

In Robinson’s nonstandard analysis, every point
x
in a topological space acquires an infinitesimal neighbourhood: the intersection of the nonstandard extensions of all its open sets, called the
halo
.
It replaces the infinitary quantification over all open neighbourhoods that defines topological operations with a single membership test against one set.
The halo encodes the local topology of the space in a single object.
This invites the following inquiry:
If a formula
\varphi
is true at various points of the space, then what is the relationship between the halo of a given point and the nonstandard extension
{{}^{*}\!}[\![\varphi]\!]
of the truth set of such a formula?

We investigate this by defining a family of modal operators which specify for each point
x
, a set
E(x)
of witnesses which are excluded from the nonstandard truth set
{{}^{*}\!}[\![\varphi]\!]
.
The resulting family has four canonical members, on which we focus.
The purely standard case collapses to Kripke semantics over the specialisation preorder.
Two more turn out to coincide with well-known modalities: when nothing is excluded the operator equates to topological closure, and when the point itself is excluded it equates to the Cantor derivative.
Hence, the classical semantics of McKinsey-Tarski
[
9
]
and
Esakia
[
6
]
arise naturally from our framework.

The truly novel operator is the one obtained by excluding all standard points, so that witnesses must be nonstandard hyperpoints.
The
Transfer Principle
of nonstandard analysis forces this condition to be equivalent to a well-known but modally unstudied topological notion:
\omega
-accumulation.
Unlike the Cantor derivative, the
\omega
-accumulation operator maps arbitrary sets to closed sets without any separation axiom.
This yields a Cantor–Bendixson-style decomposition on all topological spaces, paralleling the classical theorem.
In addition, Axiom 4 holds on all topological spaces, again with no separation condition required.
We show that the logic
\mathsf{K4}
is complete for the nonstandard semantics over all infinite topological spaces; and that
\mathsf{GL}
is complete over all infinite
\omega
-scattered spaces.

\mathsf{K4}
and
\mathsf{GL}
.
We conclude with some remarks.

2
Nonstandard Framework

We recall the axiomatic framework for nonstandard analysis following Salbany and Todorov
[
11
]
, who work within the superstructure of an infinite set.
This framework specifies the conditions required for halos to have the appropriate topological properties.

2.1
Superstructures and the star map

Let
S
be an infinite set. In our applications
S=X\cup\mathbb{R}
, where
(X,\tau)
is a topological space.
The
superstructure
over
S
is the hierarchy

V(S)=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}V_{k}(S),

built inductively as

V_{0}(S)=S,\qquad V_{k+1}(S)=V_{k}(S)\cup\mathcal{P}(V_{k}(S)).

Each level adds all subsets of the previous level. The idea is that
V_{0}(S)
contains the base objects,
V_{1}(S)
adds all sets of base objects,
V_{2}(S)
adds all sets of those, and so on. Concretely:

•

V_{0}(S)=S
: the points of
X
and the real numbers.

•

V_{1}(S)=S\cup\mathcal{P}(S)
: includes all subsets of
S
,
in particular every open set
U\subseteq X
, since
U\subseteq X\subseteq S
and hence
U\in\mathcal{P}(S)\subseteq V_{1}(S)
.

•

V_{2}(S)
: includes all subsets of
V_{1}(S)
, in particular the topology
\tau
, since
\tau
is a collection of open sets and each open set is in
V_{1}(S)
, so
\tau\subseteq V_{1}(S)
and hence
\tau\in\mathcal{P}(V_{1}(S))\subseteq V_{2}(S)
.

Functions are identified with their graphs (sets of ordered pairs), and ordered pairs are encoded as sets in the usual way (
\langle a,b\rangle=\{\{a\},\{a,b\}\}
), so functions also appear at a finite level of
V(S)
.
Every mathematical object used in this paper, such as points, open sets, the topology, valuations, and the neighbourhood filter, is an element of
V(S)
at some finite level.

The formal language
\mathcal{L}(V(S))
consists of
bounded quantifier formulas
: formulas built from
=
,
\in
, the logical connectives, and quantifiers restricted to the form
\forall x\in A
or
\exists x\in A
for some
A\in V(S)
.
Restricting to bounded quantifiers ensures that the formula does not quantify over the entire (proper-class-sized) universe, only over specific sets already in
V(S)
.
This language is expressive enough to state all standard topological properties of
(X,\tau)

(see
[
11
]
).

Definition 2.1

(nonstandard model)
.

A
nonstandard model
of
V(S)
is a superstructure
V({{}^{*}\!}S)
together with a
star map

{{}^{*}\!}:V(S)\to V({{}^{*}\!}S)
satisfying the axioms
\mathrm{NSA1}
,
\mathrm{NSA2}
and
\mathrm{NSA3}
below.

A set
B\in V({{}^{*}\!}S)
is called
internal
if
B\in{{}^{*}\!}C
for some
C\in V(S)
, i.e. if
B
is an element of the nonstandard extension of some standard set. A set that is not internal is called
external
.

(NSA1)

Extension Principle.

{{}^{*}\!}s=s
for all
s\in S
; equivalently,
S\subseteq{{}^{*}\!}S
.

(NSA2)

Transfer Principle.

A bounded quantifier formula
\Phi(A_{1},\ldots,A_{n})
is true in
V(S)
if and only if
\Phi({{}^{*}\!}A_{1},\ldots,{{}^{*}\!}A_{n})
is true in
V({{}^{*}\!}S)
.

(NSA3)

Saturation Principle.

Every family of internal sets of cardinality at most
\kappa\geq|V(S)|
with the finite intersection property has non-empty intersection.

We call a model satisfying the axioms above

polysaturated
.
Such models exist for any infinite
S
, as shown by the “ultrapower construction”
[
11
]
.
We fix one from this point onward.

The distinction between internal and external sets is fundamental to nonstandard analysis.
Any set explicitly constructed by Transfer from a standard formula, e.g.
{{}^{*}\!}(U\cap A)
,
{{}^{*}\!}U\setminus{{}^{*}\!}A
, and so on is internal.
The Transfer Principle applies only to internal sets as bounded quantifier formulas can be transferred between
V(S)
and
V({{}^{*}\!}S)
only when all sets quantified over are internal.
Similarly, the Saturation Principle produces intersections only for families of internal sets.
Important examples of external sets used in this paper are the standard universe
X
inside
{{}^{*}\!}X
, the halo
\mu(x)
and the set
{{}^{*}\!}S\setminus X
for infinite
S
.

For us this distinction is important when applying Saturation to a family such as
\{{{}^{*}\!}(U\cap A)\setminus G:U\in\mathcal{N}(x),\,\text{finite }G\subseteq X\}
, where
\mathcal{N}(x)=\{U\in\tau:x\in U\}
is the open neighbourhood filter of
x
, because each individual member
{{}^{*}\!}(U\cap A)\setminus G
is internal;
the intersection of this family, however, need not be internal, and indeed, it is a subset of
{{}^{*}\!}A\setminus X
, which is external.

The Transfer Principle immediately yields the following structural properties of
{{}^{*}\!}
, which we use throughout without further comment.

Lemma 2.2

(properties of the star map)
.

For all
A,B\subseteq X
and
x\in X
:

(i)

{{}^{*}\!}(A\cap B)={{}^{*}\!}A\cap{{}^{*}\!}B
;

(ii)

{{}^{*}\!}(A\cup B)={{}^{*}\!}A\cup{{}^{*}\!}B
;

(iii)

{{}^{*}\!}(X\setminus A)={{}^{*}\!}X\setminus{{}^{*}\!}A
;

(iv)

A\subseteq B\Rightarrow{{}^{*}\!}A\subseteq{{}^{*}\!}B

(monotonicity)
;

(v)

{{}^{*}\!}\emptyset=\emptyset
and
{{}^{*}\!}X\supseteq X
;

(vi)

x\in A
if and only if
x\in{{}^{*}\!}A
, for
x\in X
.

Proof.

Each statement is a direct application of the Transfer Principle to the corresponding bounded quantifier formula expressing set operations and membership.
For instance, (i) follows by transferring

\forall z\in X\,(z\in A\cap B\leftrightarrow z\in A\wedge z\in B)
,
which yields

\forall z\in{{}^{*}\!}X\,(z\in{{}^{*}\!}(A\cap B)\leftrightarrow z\in{{}^{*}\!}A\wedge z\in{{}^{*}\!}B)
.
In part (v),
{{}^{*}\!}\emptyset=\emptyset
follows by Transfer and
{{}^{*}\!}X\supseteq X
from Extension.
Part (vi) holds because
x\in S\subseteq{{}^{*}\!}S
by Extension and the formula
x\in A
is quantifier-free.
It readily follows by transfer that
x\in A
if and only if
x\in{{}^{*}\!}A
.
∎

2.2
The halo and nonstandard characterisations

Fix a topological space
(X,\tau)
.

Definition 2.3

(halo)
.

For
x\in X
, the
halo
of
x
is defined as

\mu(x)=\bigcap_{U\in\mathcal{N}(x)}{{}^{*}\!}U.

This set is also called the
monad
of
x
in the
literature
[
10
,
11
]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

For all
x\in X
,
x\in\mu(x)
.

Proof.

For any
U\in\mathcal{N}(x)
we have
x\in U
, hence
x\in{{}^{*}\!}U
by Lemma
2.2
(vi).
Since this holds for every
U\in\mathcal{N}(x)
,
x\in\bigcap_{U\in\mathcal{N}(x)}{{}^{*}\!}U=\mu(x)
.
∎

The following is immediate from the definition of a halo.

Lemma 2.5
.

If
\tau^{\prime}\subseteq\tau
, then
\mu_{\tau}(x)\subseteq\mu_{\tau^{\prime}}(x)
.

Definition 2.6

(beacon)
.

For
B\subseteq{{}^{*}\!}X
, the
beacon
of
B
is defined as

\mathrm{bcn}(B)=\{x\in X\mid\mu(x)\cap B\neq\emptyset\}.

Each standard point
x
casts a halo
\mu(x)
into
{{}^{*}\!}X
.
The beacon of
B
is the set of standard points whose halos reach
B
.
As we show below, the beacon of
{{}^{*}\!}A
is precisely the topological closure of
A
.

The following proposition collects the fundamental nonstandard equivalences.
Parts (i) and (ii) are due to Robinson
[
10
]

(see also
[
8
]
); the remaining parts are standard consequences recorded, e.g. in

[
11
]
.

Proposition 2.7

(nonstandard characterisations)
.

Let
(X,\tau)
be a topological space with polysaturated nonstandard extension. For
A\subseteq X
and
x\in X
:

(i)

Closure:

x\in\mathrm{cl}(A)
iff
\mu(x)\cap{{}^{*}\!}A\neq\emptyset
.
Equivalently,
\mathrm{cl}(A)=\mathrm{bcn}({{}^{*}\!}A)
.
So
A
is closed iff
A=\mathrm{bcn}({{}^{*}\!}A)
.

(ii)

Open set:

A\in\tau
iff
\mu(x)\subseteq{{}^{*}\!}A
for every
x\in A
.

(iii)

Limit point:

x
is a limit point of
A
iff
\mu(x)\cap({{}^{*}\!}A\setminus\{x\})\neq\emptyset
.

(iv)

Isolated point:

x\in A
is isolated in
A
iff
\mu(x)\cap{{}^{*}\!}A=\{x\}
.

(v)

T_{1}
space:

(X,\tau)
is
T_{1}
iff
\mu(x)\cap X=\{x\}
for every
x\in X
.

(vi)

Hausdorff space:

(X,\tau)
is Hausdorff iff
\mu(x)\cap\mu(y)=\emptyset
for all distinct
x,y\in X
.

3
The Halo Operator

3.1
Logic

Let
\mathbf{Prop}=\{p,q,r,\ldots\}
denote the set of
propositional variables.
The modal language has a single diamond primitive

\Diamond
.
The language is defined inductively as follows:

\varphi\;::=\;p\;\mid\;\bot\;\mid\;\neg\varphi\;\mid\;\varphi\wedge\psi\;\mid\;\Diamond\varphi.

Boolean connectives
\top,\vee,\to,\leftrightarrow
are standard abbreviations. The box is always the De Morgan dual:

\Box\varphi:=\neg\Diamond\neg\varphi
.
The axiomatic systems used in this paper are all normal modal logics, i.e. include the Axiom K.
We recall the relevant axiom schemas:

(K)

\Box(\varphi\to\psi)\to(\Box\varphi\to\Box\psi)

(T)

\Box\varphi\to\varphi

(4)

\Box\varphi\to\Box\Box\varphi

(w4)

\Diamond\Diamond\varphi\to\Diamond\varphi\vee\varphi

(L)

\square(\square\varphi\to\varphi)\to\square\varphi

All of the axiomatic systems discussed in this paper include classical tautologies, Modus Ponens and Necessitation.
The axiomatic systems we consider are:

\mathsf{S4}
= K + T + 4;
\;\mathsf{K4}
= K + 4;
\;\mathsf{wK4}
= K + w4;
\;\mathsf{GL}
= K + L.

Definition 3.1

(topological model)
.

A
topological model
is a pair
\mathcal{M}=((X,\tau),\nu)
where
(X,\tau)
is a topological space and
\nu:\mathbf{Prop}\to\mathcal{P}(X)
is a valuation.
We work inside a fixed polysaturated nonstandard extension
of
X
.

The
extension

[\![\varphi]\!]^{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq X
is defined inductively in the obvious way on Boolean connectives, with the modal clause determined by the choice of
exclusion function
.

Definition 3.2

(halo operator)
.

Given a topological model
\mathcal{M}
and an exclusion function
E:X\to\mathcal{P}({{}^{*}\!}X)
, the
halo
operator

\Diamond_{E}
is defined by the semantic clause:

[\![\Diamond_{E}\varphi]\!]^{\mathcal{M}}=\bigl\{x\in X\;\bigm|\;\mu(x)\cap\bigl({{}^{*}\!}[\![\varphi]\!]^{\mathcal{M}}\setminus E(x)\bigr)\neq\emptyset\bigr\}.

We write
x\models\varphi
for
x\in[\![\varphi]\!]^{\mathcal{M}}
under the semantics determined by
E
.
We omit the superscript
\mathcal{M}
when clear from context.

We focus on four choices for
E
in this paper: the purely standard case, where
E(x)={{}^{*}\!}X\setminus X
; the closure case, where
E(x)=\varnothing
; the Cantor derivative case, where
E(x)=\{x\}
; and the purely nonstandard case, where
E(x)=X
.
We denote the resulting modalities by
\lozenge_{\rm st}
,
\lozenge_{c}
,
\lozenge_{d}
and
\Diamond_{\mathrm{ns}}
, respectively.
The names will be justified in Section
5
, where we show that the two operators
\lozenge_{c}
and
\lozenge_{d}
coincide with the closure and the Cantor derivative, respectively.

We establish the relationship between the four modalities.

Proposition 3.3
.

For every topological model
\mathcal{M}
and formula
\varphi
:

[\![\Diamond_{\mathrm{ns}}\varphi]\!]\;\subseteq\;[\![\Diamond_{d}\varphi]\!]\;\subseteq\;[\![\Diamond_{c}\varphi]\!].

The purely standard operator satisfies
[\![\Diamond_{\mathrm{st}}\varphi]\!]\subseteq[\![\Diamond_{c}\varphi]\!]
, but is incomparable with
\Diamond_{d}
.

Proof.

The inclusions
[\![\Diamond_{\mathrm{ns}}\varphi]\!]\subseteq[\![\Diamond_{d}\varphi]\!]\subseteq[\![\Diamond_{c}\varphi]\!]
follow from

{{}^{*}\!}A\setminus X\subseteq{{}^{*}\!}A\setminus\{x\}\subseteq{{}^{*}\!}A
.
For
[\![\Diamond_{\mathrm{st}}\varphi]\!]\subseteq[\![\Diamond_{c}\varphi]\!]
, every standard witness in

\mu(x)\cap[\![\varphi]\!]
is also a witness for
\Diamond_{c}
, so
[\![\Diamond_{\mathrm{st}}\varphi]\!]\subseteq[\![\Diamond_{c}\varphi]\!]
.

For incomparability, consider an arbitrary topological space
(X,\tau)
, a point
x\in X
, and set
\nu(p)=\{x\}
.
Since
x\in\mu(x)
always, we have
\mu(x)\cap[\![p]\!]=\{x\}\neq\emptyset
, so

x\models\Diamond_{\mathrm{st}}p
. But since

\mu(x)\cap(\{x\}\setminus\{x\})=\emptyset
then

x\not\models\Diamond_{d}p
.
Hence
[\![\lozenge_{\mathrm{st}}\varphi]\!]\not\subseteq[\![\lozenge_{d}\varphi]\!]
in general.

For the other direction, consider
\mathbb{R}
with the usual topology, let
x=0
and
\nu(p)=\{1/n:n\geq 1\}
.
Then since every neighbourhood of
0
contains points of
\nu(p)\setminus\{0\}
,
0\models\Diamond_{d}p
.
But
\mathbb{R}
is
T_{1}
, so

\mu(0)\cap X=\{0\}
by Proposition
2.7
(v).
Since
0\notin\nu(p)
, it follows that
\mu(0)\cap[\![p]\!]=\emptyset
, and so

0\not\models\Diamond_{\mathrm{st}}p
.
Hence
[\![\lozenge_{d}\varphi]\!]\not\subseteq[\![\lozenge_{\mathrm{st}}\varphi]\!]
in general.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

For every topological model
\mathcal{M}
and formula
\varphi
:

[\![\Diamond_{c}\varphi]\!]=[\![\Diamond_{\mathrm{st}}\varphi]\!]\cup[\![\Diamond_{\mathrm{ns}}\varphi]\!].

Proof.

The inclusion
\supseteq
follows from Proposition
3.3
.
For
\subseteq
, suppose
x\in[\![\Diamond_{c}\varphi]\!]
, so there exists
y\in\mu(x)\cap{{}^{*}\!}[\![\varphi]\!]
.
Either
y\in X
or
y\notin X
.
In the first case,
y\in\mu(x)\cap[\![\varphi]\!]
by Lemma
2.2
(vi), so
x\in[\![\Diamond_{\mathrm{st}}\varphi]\!]
.
In the second case
y\in\mu(x)\cap({{}^{*}\!}[\![\varphi]\!]\setminus X)
, so
x\in[\![\Diamond_{\mathrm{ns}}\varphi]\!]
.
∎

3.2
Examples

We illustrate all four operators on two examples. The first is a
T_{1}
space
(
\mathbb{R}
), which demonstrates the collapse
\Diamond_{\mathrm{ns}}=\Diamond_{d}

and the incomparability of
\Diamond_{\mathrm{st}}
with
\Diamond_{d}
. The second is
a non-
T_{1}
space (the Sierpiński space), chosen specifically to separate
\Diamond_{d}
from
\Diamond_{\mathrm{ns}}
.

Example 3.5

(the real line)
.

Let
X=\mathbb{R}
with the standard topology and
\nu(p)=(0,1)\cup\{2\}
.
Since
\mathbb{R}
is
T_{1}
, Proposition
2.7
(v) implies
\mu(x)\cap\mathbb{R}=\{x\}
for every
x
.
The halo
\mu(x)=\{y\in{{}^{*}\!}\mathbb{R}:|y-x|<r\text{ for all standard }r>0\}
consists of all hyperreals infinitesimally close to
x
.
By Transfer,
{{}^{*}\!}(0,1)=\{y\in{{}^{*}\!}\mathbb{R}:0<y<1\}
and
{{}^{*}\!}\{c\}=\{c\}
, for each
c\in\mathbb{R}
.

At
x=0
, each positive infinitesimal
\varepsilon
satisfies
\varepsilon\in\mu(0)
,

\varepsilon\in{{}^{*}\!}(0,1)\subset{{}^{*}\!}\nu(p)
, and
\varepsilon\notin\mathbb{R}
.
Hence
0\in[\![\Diamond_{c}p]\!]
; since
\varepsilon\neq 0
,
0\in[\![\Diamond_{d}p]\!]
; since
\varepsilon\notin\mathbb{R}
,
0\in[\![\Diamond_{\mathrm{ns}}p]\!]
.
But
0\notin\nu(p)
, so
0\notin[\![\Diamond_{\mathrm{st}}p]\!]
.

At
x=2
, the point

is isolated in
\nu(p)
.
For each
y\in\mu(2)
with
y\neq 2
,
|y-2|
is a positive infinitesimal, so
y\notin{{}^{*}\!}(0,1)
.
Thus
\mu(2)\cap({{}^{*}\!}\nu(p)\setminus\{2\})=\emptyset
,
so
2\notin[\![\Diamond_{d}p]\!]
and
2\notin[\![\Diamond_{\mathrm{ns}}p]\!]
.
However
2\in\nu(p)
, so
2\in[\![\Diamond_{\mathrm{st}}p]\!]
; and
2\in\mathrm{cl}(\nu(p))
, so
2\in[\![\Diamond_{c}p]\!]
.

The example above does not separate
\lozenge_{d}
from
\lozenge_{ns}
.
We show that the two can be separated even on a two-point topological space.

Example 3.6

(the Sierpiński space)
.

Let
X=\{a,b\}
with topology
\tau=\{\emptyset,\{a\},\{a,b\}\}
.
The open sets containing
a
are
\{a\}
and

; the only open set containing

is

. This space is not

: the singleton

is open, hence

is closed, but

is not closed since
\{b\}
is not open.
Let
\nu(p)=\{a\}
.
Since
\{a\}
and

are the open sets containing

, we obtain

\mu(a)={{}^{*}\!}\{a\}\cap{{}^{*}\!}X={{}^{*}\!}\{a\}
.
Since
X
is the only open set containing

,
\mu(b)={{}^{*}\!}X
.
Recall that by Transfer finite sets are fixed by
{{}^{*}\!}
.
In particular,
{{}^{*}\!}\nu(p)={{}^{*}\!}\{a\}=\{a\}
.
So

is in
[\![\lozenge_{\mathrm{st}}p]\!]
and
[\![\lozenge_{c}p]\!]
but not in
[\![\lozenge_{d}p]\!]
or
[\![\Diamond_{\mathrm{ns}}p]\!]
.

At
x=b
, we have
\mu(b)\cap\{a\}=\{a\}\neq\emptyset
and

\mu(b)\cap{{}^{*}\!}\{a\}=\{a\}\neq\emptyset
, so

b\models\Diamond_{\mathrm{st}}p
and
b\models\Diamond_{c}p
.
Also
\mu(b)\cap(\{a\}\setminus\{b\})=\{a\}\neq\emptyset
,
so
b\models\Diamond_{d}p
.
But
\mu(b)\cap(\{a\}\setminus X)=\{a\}\setminus\{a,b\}=\emptyset
,
so
b\not\models\Diamond_{\mathrm{ns}}p
.

4
The purely standard operator

The purely standard modality
\Diamond_{\mathrm{st}}
is obtained by setting

E(x)={{}^{*}\!}X\setminus X
, so that only standard points are admitted as witnesses.

Proposition 4.1
.

On arbitrary topological spaces
(X,\tau)
, the operator
\Diamond_{\mathrm{st}}
computes exactly the Kripke diamond over the
specialisation preorder

\leq_{\tau}
defined by
x\leq_{\tau}y
iff
x\in\mathrm{cl}(\{y\})
, i.e.

x\models\Diamond_{\mathrm{st}}\varphi\iff\exists\,y\in X\;:\;x\leq_{\tau}y\text{ and }y\models\varphi.

Proof.

Using
{{}^{*}\!}[\![\varphi]\!]\subseteq{{}^{*}\!}X
and
{{}^{*}\!}A\cap X=A
for all
A\subseteq X
(Lemma
2.2
(vi)):

x\models\Diamond_{\mathrm{st}}\varphi\iff\mu(x)\cap\bigl({{}^{*}\!}[\![\varphi]\!]\setminus({{}^{*}\!}X\setminus X)\bigr)\neq\emptyset\iff\mu(x)\cap[\![\varphi]\!]\neq\emptyset.

For
y\in X
,
y\in\mu(x)
iff
y\in{{}^{*}\!}U
for every
U\in\mathcal{N}(x)
, and by Lemma
2.2
(vi),
y\in{{}^{*}\!}U
iff
y\in U
for standard
y

U\in\mathcal{N}(x)
be arbitrary.
By definition,
|U\cap\omega(A)|\geq\aleph_{0}
; pick a point
y\in U\cap\omega(A)
.
Since
U
is an open neighbourhood of
y
and
y\in\omega(A)
,

|U\cap A|\geq\aleph_{0}
.
Since
U
is arbitrary,
x\in\omega(A)=[\![\Diamond_{\mathrm{ns}}\varphi]\!]
by Theorem
6.4
.
∎

Theorem 6.16

(topological completeness for K4)
.

\mathsf{K4}
is sound for
\Diamond_{\mathrm{ns}}
over all topological spaces.
It is complete over all infinite topological spaces.

Proof.

Soundness follows from the results above.
For completeness, suppose
\varphi\notin\mathsf{K4}
.
By the Kripke completeness of
\mathsf{K4}

[
5
]
,
\varphi
is
falsified on some finite transitive Kripke frame.
By Bezhanishvili, Esakia, and Gabelaia
[
3
,
4
]
, every such frame
is the image of a d-map from a metrizable, hence infinite and
T_{1}
,
space
(X,\tau)
, and

on

.
Since

is

, Lemma
6.6

, so

Lemma 6.17
.

The
L
axiom

\Box(\Box\varphi\to\varphi)\to\Box\varphi

is valid for the nonstandard semantics over all
\omega
-scattered topological spaces.

Proof.

Fix a model
\mathcal{M}
and set
A=[\![\neg\varphi]\!]^{\mathcal{M}}
.
By the
\omega
-accumulation characterisation,

[\![\Box_{\mathrm{ns}}\varphi]\!]^{\mathcal{M}}=X\setminus\omega(A)
.
From this we obtain
[\![\Box_{\mathrm{ns}}(\Box_{\mathrm{ns}}\varphi\to\varphi)]\!]=X\setminus\omega(A\setminus\omega(A))
.
Hence L is false at
x\in X
if and only if
x
belongs to the set
D:=\omega(A)\setminus\omega(A\setminus\omega(A))
.
In particular, L is valid in
\mathcal{M}
if and only if
D=\emptyset
.

(\Leftarrow)

Suppose
(X,\tau)
is
\omega
-scattered. We show
D=\emptyset
by proving that
D
is
\omega
-perfect;
\omega
-scatteredness then forces
D=\emptyset
.
Let
x\in D
.
By definition, since
x\in\omega(A)
, every open
U\ni x
satisfies

|U\cap A|\geq\aleph_{0}
; since
x\notin\omega(A\setminus\omega(A))
, there is an open
neighbourhood
U_{x}
of
x
with

\bigl|U_{x}\cap(A\setminus\omega(A))\bigr|<\aleph_{0}
.
Let
V
be an arbitrary open neighbourhood of
x
; replacing it with
V\cap U_{x}
if necessary, we may assume
V\subseteq U_{x}
.
Since
x\in\omega(A)
, the set
V\cap A
is infinite.
Since
V\subseteq U_{x}
, the set
V\cap(A\setminus\omega(A))
is finite.
Therefore

V\cap A\cap\omega(A)=(V\cap A)\setminus(A\setminus\omega(A))

is infinite.
We claim that every
y\in V\cap A\cap\omega(A)
belongs to
D
, i.e. satisfies
y\notin\omega(A\setminus\omega(A))
.
Suppose for contradiction that
y\in\omega(A\setminus\omega(A))
.
Since
y\in V
and
V
is open,
\bigl|V\cap(A\setminus\omega(A))\bigr|\geq\aleph_{0}
.
But
V\subseteq U_{x}
implies

V\cap(A\setminus\omega(A))\subseteq U_{x}\cap(A\setminus\omega(A))
,
which is finite, a contradiction.

Therefore
V\cap D\supseteq V\cap A\cap\omega(A)
, which is infinite.
Since
V
was an arbitrary open neighbourhood of
x
, we have
x\in\omega(D)
.
As
x\in D
was arbitrary,
D\subseteq\omega(D)
, so
\omega(D)
is a closed
\omega
-perfect subset of
X
.
Since
(X,\tau)
is
\omega
-scattered,
\omega(D)=\emptyset
, hence

, so L is valid in
\mathcal{M}
.

(
\Rightarrow
)
Suppose

is a non-empty
\omega
-perfect set, so

\omega(P)\supseteq P
. Define a model

such that
\nu(p)=X\setminus P
,
and set

.
Since
\omega(P)\supseteq P
,

[\![\Box_{\mathrm{ns}}p]\!]=X\setminus\omega(P)\subseteq X\setminus P=[\![p]\!],

so
\Box_{\mathrm{ns}}p\to p
is valid on
\mathcal{M}
.
Hence
[\![\neg(\Box_{\mathrm{ns}}p\to p)]\!]=\emptyset
and so

\omega(\emptyset)=\emptyset
implies

[\![\Box_{\mathrm{ns}}(\Box_{\mathrm{ns}}p\to p)]\!]=X\setminus\omega(\emptyset)=X
.
On the other hand, for each
x\in P\subseteq\omega(P)=\omega(A)
, we have
x\notin X\setminus\omega(A)=[\![\Box_{\mathrm{ns}}p]\!]
.
Therefore, L is not valid on
(X,\tau)
.
∎

(topological completeness for GL)
.

\mathsf{GL}
is sound for
\Diamond_{\mathrm{ns}}
over all
\omega
-scattered topological spaces.
It is complete over all infinite
\omega
-scattered topological spaces.

Proof.

Soundness follows from the results above.
For completeness, suppose
\varphi\notin\mathsf{GL}
.
By the Kripke completeness of
\mathsf{GL}

[
12
]
,
\varphi

is falsified on some finite irreflexive transitive Kripke frame.
By Bezhanishvili, Esakia, and Gabelaia
[
3
,
4
]
, every such frame
is the image of a d-map from a scattered metrizable, hence infinite and

T_{1}
, space
(X,\tau)
, and
\varphi
is falsified under
\Diamond_{d}

on
(X,\tau)
.

Since
(X,\tau)
is
T_{1}
, Lemma
6.6
gives

\Diamond_{\mathrm{ns}}=\Diamond_{d}
, so
\varphi
is falsified under the

It remains to show
(X,\tau)
is
\omega
-scattered.
If
P
is a non-empty
\omega
-perfect subset, then
P\subseteq\omega(P)=d(P)
by Lemma
6.6
, so
P
is a non-empty perfect set, contradicting scatteredness.

(X,\tau)
is
\omega
-scattered, and
\varphi
is falsified under

∎

The argument above shows in particular that every scattered space is

7
Final remarks

We have introduced the halo operator, giving a uniform nonstandard-analytic framework from which the classical closure and derived-set semantics arise as instances.
It yields a new operator which turns out to be the modal counterpart of the
\omega
-accumulation point operator, with
\mathsf{K4}
and
\mathsf{GL}
as its complete logics over infinite spaces and infinite
\omega
-scattered spaces, respectively.

The four exclusion functions studied here are the canonical extremes, but intermediate choices are a natural next step.
For instance,
E(x)=\{x\}\cup(\mu(x)\cap X)
excludes the point itself together with all standard points in its halo.
More generally, one could require witnesses to avoid any prescribed internal subset of
\mu(x)
, producing a rich family of operators interpolating between
\Diamond_{d}
and
\Diamond_{\mathrm{ns}}
.

Another interesting direction for future work is the logic of
\Diamond_{\mathrm{ns}}
over a single fixed infinite space such as
\mathbb{R}
.
The natural candidate is
\mathsf{K4}
extended by the seriality axiom
\Diamond_{\mathrm{ns}}\top
, since
\mathbb{R}
is dense and every point is an
\omega
-accumulation point of any infinite set, but a precise
axiomatisation and proof remain to be established.
