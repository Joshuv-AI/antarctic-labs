 Executive Summary
 As part of the Better Buildings Summit 2025, a workshop was developed to simulate the decision
 process for implementing a set of energy savings measures on an existing building. As part of the
 workshop a “game” was created around energy savings strategies, and workshop participants
 selected renovation cards that provided data around the impacts of that renovation choice.
 Participants then were able to calculate overall savings and payback from the selected retrofit
 opportunities.

 This publication has been produced to package the resources used in the activity in an organized
 document so that the materials can be distributed and reused more broadly. It provides a
 comprehensive framework for replicating the workshop, including step-by-step instructions,
 retrofit “game cards” with data points, and facilitation guidance. By using these materials,
 readers can conduct their own version of this interactive workshop with stakeholders in their
 networks, such as building owners, energy managers, or policy makers. The goal is to empower
 users to simulate the decision-making processes for energy projects, foster collaboration, and
 drive meaningful discussions around cost-effective retrofit opportunities and goal-oriented
 outcomes.

 Setting the Stage
 As part of the Innovative Solutions Lab Workshop at the 2025 Better Buildings Summit 1, an
 activity was developed to engage attendees in an interactive educational experience. Through
 collaborative problem-solving and creative thinking, attendees gained valuable insights and
 practical strategies for saving energy in commercial buildings. Participants worked together to
 devise innovative solutions to the challenges faced in implementing energy initiatives in various
 sectors.

 The effort was designed to provide Better Buildings Partners, who are typically building owners
 and operators, a platform to learn how to make decisions about energy upgrades that could be
 implemented in existing buildings to help them meet their Better Buildings goals 2. The workshop
 attendees were divided into groups of 6 to 8 participants. Each group pretended to be an owner
 team that was considering strategies for retrofitting an existing building. The current condition of
 this hypothetical building was known, and a handout was provided with this building assessment
 (1.1 Activity Overview). After review, the workshop teams completed the Overview Table (1.3
 Overview Table). Based on the discussion, the workshop team evaluated options for improving
 the building (1.1 Game Cards). Once options were selected, the team completed the Energy
 Savings Table (1.5).

 Technical Philosophy
 Better Buildings Partners often are faced with how to make decisions as to what retrofits to
 undertake to save energy. A key objective of this activity is to help participants improve their
 conceptual understanding of energy retrofits and to observe the sensitivities of different decision-
 making tradeoffs as they make the business case for saving energy.

 The activity has been designed without a specific problem statement, and as such there is no
 predetermined solution or “correct answer”. Participants begin the activity by determining and
 stating the goal(s) they want the sample building to meet. The challenge posed by the activity is
 then for participants to identify the best combination of retrofit measures to meet their goals.
 Retrofit measures have been grouped into the following categories for the activity:

 - Envelope
 - Lighting
 - Plug Loads
 - Ventilation
 - Domestic Hot Water
 - Heating/Cooling
 - Solar PV

 Each category includes a range of options that is representative of the current state of the market,
 including a “do nothing” option. The retrofit options are not presented with energy savings, but
 rather as additive components of the building’s energy use that are combined to build an
 alternate retrofit scenario that is compared to the baseline scenario to determine savings. Because
 of the interdependence of building components on overall energy use, individual measures are
 difficult to evaluate without knowing other details about the building. For example, replacing the
 heating system with a more efficient alternative saves energy on its own but those savings are
 amplified if the building envelope is also upgraded to reduce heat loss. Therefore, this activity
 has been designed to evaluate savings after all building systems have been considered.

 Figure 1 helps to visualize this, where each category of building energy consumption stacks to an
 overall energy use intensity (EUI). The sample retrofit package shown here achieves around 30%
 energy savings overall, once the components are summed together.

 Expanding this visualization further, Figure 2 shows the composition of each category of energy
 loads in the Baseline Scenario. The colors represent the building systems, and the patterns
 represent the energy load categories. Together, they all add to the Baseline EUI of 81 kBtu/ft2 –
 which is the same as choosing the “do nothing” option for each building system.

 Details of the scenario and retrofit measures in the activity have been developed using sample
 data that is representative of industry norms.

 The outcomes of the activity can vary widely based on how the participants’ interests and
 priorities influence the package of retrofit options they select. Depending on the business case
 participants intend to make, the activity will produce different solutions that are economically
 viable.

 1.1 Activity Overview
 Workshop Activity
 The hypothetical building is a 60,000 ft2 office building built in 1980. The building has had
 minimal energy improvements implemented over the years, and many systems are at end of life.
 The building is located in climate zone 4A, which is characterized by warm humid summers and
 cold winters (such as Washington, DC). Cooling is provided by an electric chiller and natural gas
 heating is provided by the original boiler that makes 180°F hot water. Hot and cold water is
 circulated to the building zones to a conventional variable air volume (VAV) system with reheat.
 Lighting is provided by T8 fluorescent lamps that were installed in the late 1990’s. The roof has
 been replaced once in 2005.

 Utility data has been collected from the building and logged over several years (an important
 first step in reducing loads) and it has been determined that the site EUI is 81 kBtu/ft2 annually.
 This is similar to buildings of this vintage and approximately twice the energy use of a building
 built to current practices. Please note that office buildings designed to achieve stringent energy
 goals can often operate around 20 kBtu/ft2.

 An ASHRAE level 2 audit 3 was done and from the audit the following EUI end-uses were
 determined for the current building’s systems:

 Activity

 The audit identified 34 options for improving the building’s energy use as well as calculated
 energy impacts and capital costs for the retrofit options. Each of these measures is identified on
 the color-coded cards. Your task, as a building owner team, is to select a card from each category
 (i.e., one card of each color) and determine a total retrofit “package” for the building. Note that
 each category has a card that represents no change to the building.

 Each card contains information about the measure’s contribution to building energy loads, the
 capital cost of implementing that measure, and the savings achieved by that measure (expressed
 in terms of payback period). Some cards also include special multipliers or other considerations.
 Please note that the energy metrics on the cards do not represent savings, but rather contributions
 to the building’s energy use in each category. For each color family, the “do nothing” card is the
 base. Savings are the difference between a retrofit card and the “do nothing” card.

 6

 Step 1: Consider Options and Goals
 Using the Overview Table (1.3), write down your goals. Below are examples of potential goals
 for this activity:

 - Is your goal to have the best payback (i.e. best return based on the money
 invested)?
 - Are you aiming to achieve the lowest possible EUI?
 - Do you want to determine a set energy savings level, such as a 50% reduction
 from the baseline?
 - How disruptive will the retrofit be to the building occupants? Is that an important
 consideration to your team?

 Step 2: Examine Activity Cards
 Review the activity Game Cards (1.4) and discuss the retrofit measures with your team. Each
 card indicates a particular strategy to be applied to the building and provides information on how
 it will contribute to energy loads, how much it will cost, and how long it will take to pay back the
 investment with savings. EUI values provided on each card specify how much that measure will
 contribute to building loads in each category.

 Example within the Lighting category:

 - Card 16 (“No Changes to Lighting”) specifies that the baseline conditions of the
 building consist of 10 kBtu/ft2 for the lighting load. And because the T8 lights
 generate heat, there is a +3 kBtu/ft2 contribution to the cooling load and a -3
 kBtu/ft2 credit toward the heating load.

 - Card 11 (“LED Upgrade”) contributes only 5 kBtu/ft2 to the building’s lighting
 load. And because LEDs generate less heat compared to T8s, the magnitude of
 this measure’s impact on heating and cooling loads is reduced (+1.5 kBtu/ft2 to
 cooling and -2 kBtu/ft2 to heating).

 Once your team has reviewed the options and considered the goals you set in Step 1, select one
 card from each color category to assemble your retrofit package for the building.

 Step 3: Calculate Retrofit EUI and Costs
 Enter the relevant details from your selected cards into the Energy Savings Table (1.5) to
 determine the EUI and capital cost of the retrofit package you create. Then, proceed to the
 bottom of this table to calculate the overall savings and payback period. If you have time, make
 changes to the retrofit options selected to better align with the team’s goals for the project. Be
 prepared to discuss the reasons for making any changes.

 7

 Step 4: Summarize Findings
 Return to the Overview Table (1.3), where you initially documented your team’s goals for the
 project, to summarize your findings. Be prepared to explain how your choices addressed your
 stated goals, how the selected retrofit package did/didn’t meet these goals, and what else may
 result from the choices you made. It may also be important to consider not just the energy and
 costs, but also the impact of these retrofits on building occupants (e.g., disruption to daily
 operations, long-term impacts on occupant comfort and productivity, etc.).

 Key Steps for the Team
 Please note that key roles and responsibilities for the SME facilitator are bolded. However, all of
 the information included can be helpful and important for the SME and their team to consider.

 Goal Setting
 Teams should relate their goals to their own organizational goals and/or the Better Buildings
 goals they have set. Additionally, the goals should be clear and measurable.

 For example:
 - Achieve a 20% energy reduction
 - Achieve a 20% energy reduction with a payback of less than X years
 - Create a building that has reduced consumption to the point that on-site renewables can
 balance the load
 o In this case, these can all be translated to energy (EUI) targets, if the group desires
 (i.e., retrofit the building to use less than 30 kBtu/ft2)

 They may also want to set some other goals that are a bit softer—but these should be in addition
 to some energy/environmental goals (e.g., execute the renovation without displacing staff).
 These goals are hard to quantify but might help the team prioritize their card selections.

 Card Selection
 Setting clear goals provides a framework for deciding which cards to select. Tables should look
 at the cards by color family and discuss which card to select. As cards are selected, someone can
 start to fill in the worksheet.

 Please feel free to add some color to the discussion about this being the result of an energy audit
 of the building and that owners should have an expectation that audits will provide ideas that
 include energy savings and up-front costs.

 Some questions the team might consider discussing are below—feel free to add to this list:

 - How disruptive will the measure be to building operations and occupants?
 - How long will the measure take to implement?

 16

 - Will the measure(s) help achieve other goals, such as occupant comfort or facility
 modernization?
 - Weigh the importance of payback and energy savings. What is the balance here?
 o Ideally, they would think about this in the context of how they implement and
 select projects.

 After cards are selected, they should calculate the payback and the new energy performance level
 (EUI). Push the teams along such that they can discuss their results.

 They may want to cycle back and investigate picking different cards, especially if they did not
 meet their goals. A key question could be – what are the consequences of an organization not
 achieving their goals?

 Ultimately, this exercise is foundational to creating an overall plan to meet organizational goals.
 Challenge them to decide how they will meet their goals, especially if this building retrofit
 does not. Individual buildings that don’t hit the goals put an additional burden on the rest of the
 portfolio.

 Report Out
 Ask the table what they have learned from this process. They should appoint a spokesperson
 to report out at the end of the workshop.
