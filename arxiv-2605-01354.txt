The proximal point method and its two variants for monotone vector fields in Hadamard spaces
Parin Chaipunya, Fumiaki Kohsaka

Abstract
. We prove existence and convergence of sequences generated by the proximal point method and its two variants for monotone vector fields in Hadamard spaces. Before obtaining our results, we investigate some fundamental properties of tangent spaces, resolvents, and monotone vector fields in such spaces.



1.
Introduction

Many nonlinear problems arising in the fields of nonlinear analysis and
convex analysis can be formulated as the problem of finding a solution
to the equation

(1.1)

\displaystyle 0\in Au

for a maximal monotone operator
A\colon H\to 2^{H}
defined in a real Hilbert space
H
.
A typical problem of this type is the minimization problem for
a proper lower semicontinuous convex function

f\colon H\to(-\infty,\infty]
.
In this case, the equation (
1.1
) for
A=\partial f

is reduced to

(1.2)

\displaystyle f(u)=\inf f(H).

The proximal point method was first introduced by Martinet
[
19
]
.
Rockafellar
[
21
]
studied it more generally for maximal monotone
operators in Hilbert spaces. This is a well-known method for approximating
a solution to (
1.1
) which generates a sequence in
H
by

x_{1}\in H
and

(1.3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\bigl(x_{n}-x_{n+1}\bigr)\in Ax_{n+1}\quad(n=1,2,\dots),

where
\{\lambda_{n}\}
is a sequence of positive real numbers.
It is known that for a given point
x_{n}
in the space
H
and

\lambda_{n}>0
, there
exists a unique
x_{n+1}
in
H
satisfying (
1.3
).
Rockafellar
[
21
]
proved that if
\inf_{n}\lambda_{n}>0
, then
the following hold.

(i)

\{x_{n}\}
is bounded if and only if (
1.1
) has a solution;

(ii)

if (
1.1
) has a solution, then
\{x_{n}\}
is weakly convergent
to a solution to (
1.1
).

The asymptotic behavior of the proximal point method
is deeply related to the firm nonexpansiveness
of the resolvent
J_{\lambda}
of
A
defined by

\displaystyle J_{\lambda}x=\left\{z\in H:\frac{1}{\lambda}(x-z)\in Az\right\}

for all
x\in H
and
\lambda>0
.
It is also known that for each
\lambda>0
,
the equation (
1.1
)
is equivalent to
J_{\lambda}u=u
.
The mapping
J_{\lambda}
is a single-valued and
firmly nonexpansive mapping of
H
into itself, that is,

\displaystyle\left\lVert J_{\lambda}x-J_{\lambda}y\right\rVert^{2}\leq\left\langle J_{\lambda}x-J_{\lambda}y,x-y\right\rangle

for all
x,y\in H
.

Since 1976 when Rockafellar
[
21
]
studied
the proximal point method
there had been various kinds of researches of the method
in the framework of Hilbert spaces, Banach spaces, and
\textup{CAT}(\kappa)
spaces
for a real
\kappa
. In particular, Aoyama, Kohsaka, and Takahashi
[
3
]

obtained existence and convergence theorems on two variants of the proximal point
method for monotone operators in Banach spaces. On the other hand,
Bačák
[
5
]

obtained a
\Delta
-convergence theorem for a sequence generated by
the proximal point method for a proper lower semicontinuous convex
function defined in
\textup{CAT}(0)
spaces; see also
[
6
]
.
The theorem by Bačák
[
5
]
were based on
some fundamental results on convex analysis in Hadamard spaces
obtained by Jost
[
13
]
and Mayer
[
20
]
.

Later, Chaipunya and Kumam
[
12
]

and Khatibzadeh and Ranjbar
[
14
]
studied
the asymptotic behavior of the proximal point method
for monotone vector fields and monotone operators
in
\textup{CAT}(0)
spaces, respectively.
It should be noted that they applied the concept of
dual spaces of
\textup{CAT}(0)
spaces introduced by
Ahmadi Kakavandi and Amini
[
1
]

to their studies on the proximal point method.
Further, Kimura and Kohsaka
[
15
]
obtained
existence and convergence theorems related to
two modified proximal point methods for convex functions
in such spaces.

In 2021, Chaipunya, Kohsaka, and Kumam
[
11
]

introduced a concept of tangent spaces in
\textup{CAT}(0)
spaces
and defined the concepts of monotone vector fields and their resolvents
in such spaces. Further, they also obtained a generation
theorem of a nonexpansive semigroup by monotone vector fields
in
\textup{CAT}(0)
spaces.

The aim of this paper is to obtain existence and convergence theorems
for sequences generated by the proximal point method and its two variants
for monotone vector fields in
\textup{CAT}(0)
spaces. To obtain our results
we also study some fundamental properties of tangent spaces and
resolvents of such vector fields in the space.
The definition of tangent spaces of
\textup{CAT}(0)
spaces in this paper
is slightly different from the one in our former work given in
[
11
]
.

The following is a slight modification of
Alexandrov’s lemma in
[
9
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
x,y,z
be nonzero points in
\mathbb{R}^{2}

and let

\theta=\arccos\frac{\left\langle x,y\right\rangle}{\left\lvert x\right\rvert\left\lvert y\right\rvert}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\theta^{\prime}=\arccos\frac{\left\langle z,y\right\rangle}{\left\lvert z\right\rvert\left\lvert y\right\rvert}.

If
\theta+\theta^{\prime}\geq\pi
,
then

\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert+\left\lvert y-z\right\rvert\geq\left\lvert x\right\rvert+\left\lvert z\right\rvert.

Proof.

Setting
z^{\prime}=-(\left\lvert z\right\rvert/\left\lvert x\right\rvert)x
, we have

\left\lvert z^{\prime}\right\rvert=\left\lvert z\right\rvert
and

\arccos\frac{\left\langle y,z^{\prime}\right\rangle}{\left\lvert y\right\rvert\left\lvert z^{\prime}\right\rvert}=\arccos\left(-\frac{\left\langle y,x\right\rangle}{\left\lvert y\right\rvert\left\lvert x\right\rvert}\right)=\pi-\arccos\frac{\left\langle y,x\right\rangle}{\left\lvert y\right\rvert\left\lvert x\right\rvert}=\pi-\theta.

This implies that

\cos(\pi-\theta)=\frac{\left\langle y,z^{\prime}\right\rangle}{\left\lvert y\right\rvert\left\lvert z^{\prime}\right\rvert}=\frac{\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert z^{\prime}\right\rvert^{2}-\left\lvert y-z^{\prime}\right\rvert^{2}}{2\left\lvert y\right\rvert\left\lvert z^{\prime}\right\rvert}=\frac{\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert z\right\rvert^{2}-\left\lvert y-z^{\prime}\right\rvert^{2}}{2\left\lvert y\right\rvert\left\lvert z\right\rvert}.

We also have

\cos\theta^{\prime}=\frac{\left\langle y,z\right\rangle}{\left\lvert y\right\rvert\left\lvert z\right\rvert}=\frac{\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert z\right\rvert^{2}-\left\lvert y-z\right\rvert^{2}}{2\left\lvert y\right\rvert\left\lvert z\right\rvert}.

Since
0\leq\pi-\theta\leq\theta^{\prime}\leq\pi
, we obtain

\frac{\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert z\right\rvert^{2}-\left\lvert y-z^{\prime}\right\rvert^{2}}{2\left\lvert y\right\rvert\left\lvert z\right\rvert}=\cos(\pi-\theta)\geq\cos\theta^{\prime}=\frac{\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert z\right\rvert^{2}-\left\lvert y-z\right\rvert^{2}}{2\left\lvert y\right\rvert\left\lvert z\right\rvert}

and hence

\left\lvert y-z^{\prime}\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert y-z\right\rvert.

This implies that

\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert+\left\lvert y-z\right\rvert\geq\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert+\left\lvert y-z^{\prime}\right\rvert\geq\left\lvert x-z^{\prime}\right\rvert=\left\lvert\left(1+\frac{\left\lvert z\right\rvert}{\left\lvert x\right\rvert}\right)x\right\rvert=\left\lvert x\right\rvert+\left\lvert z\right\rvert.

This completes the proof.
∎

Let
(X,\rho)
be a metric space.
We denote by
X^{2}
the set
X\times X
.
Each element
(x,y)
in
X^{2}
is denoted by
\overrightarrow{xy}
.
The quasilinearization
[
7
]
on
X

is defined by

\left\langle\overrightarrow{xy},\overrightarrow{zw}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\rho(x,w)^{2}+\rho(y,z)^{2}-\rho(x,z)^{2}-\rho(y,w)^{2}\bigr)

for all
\overrightarrow{xy},\overrightarrow{zw}\in X^{2}
.
If
X
is a nonempty subset of a real Hilbert space,
then

\left\langle\overrightarrow{xy},\overrightarrow{zw}\right\rangle=\left\langle x-y,z-w\right\rangle

for all
x,y,z,w\in X
. We can prove the following simple lemma.

Lemma 2.4
.

If
(X,\rho)
is a metric space, then

\left\lvert\left\langle\overrightarrow{xy},\overrightarrow{xz}\right\rangle\right\rvert\leq\rho(x,y)\rho(x,z)

for all
x,y,z\in X
.

Proof.

If
x,y,z\in X
, then the triangle inequality
implies that

\displaystyle\begin{split}2\left\langle\overrightarrow{xy},\overrightarrow{xz}\right\rangle&=\rho(x,z)^{2}+\rho(y,x)^{2}-\rho(y,z)^{2}\\
&\leq\rho(x,z)^{2}+\rho(x,y)^{2}-\bigl(\rho(x,y)-\rho(x,z)\bigr)^{2}=2\rho(x,y)\rho(x,z)\end{split}

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}2\left\langle\overrightarrow{xy},\overrightarrow{xz}\right\rangle&=\rho(x,z)^{2}+\rho(y,x)^{2}-\rho(y,z)^{2}\\
&\geq\rho(x,z)^{2}+\rho(y,x)^{2}-\bigl(\rho(y,x)+\rho(x,z)\bigr)^{2}=-2\rho(x,y)\rho(x,z).\end{split}

Thus the result follows.
∎

Let
(X,\rho)
be a metric space,
p
an element in
X
,
and
l
a nonnegative real number.
Then a mapping
\gamma\colon[0,l]\to X
is said to be
a geodesic from

if
\gamma(0)=p
and

\rho(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))=\left\lvert s-t\right\rvert

for all
s,t\in[0,l]
.
Such mapping
\gamma
is also called
a geodesic from
p
to
q
, where
q=\gamma(l)
.
In this case, the mapping
\gamma

is called a geodesic from
p
to
q
.
The mapping
\gamma
is called nonzero if
p\neq q
.

A metric space
(X,\rho)
is said to be
uniquely geodesic if for each
p,q\in X
,
there exists a unique geodesic
\gamma
from
p
to
q
.
The unique geodesic from
p
to
q

is also denoted by
\gamma_{p,q}
.
We can define the convex combination between
p
and
q
by

(1-\alpha)p\oplus\alpha q=\gamma_{p,q}\bigl(\alpha\rho(p,q)\bigr)

for all
\alpha\in[0,1]
.
The geodesic segment
[p,q]
between
p
and
q
is defined by

[p,q]=\bigl\{(1-\alpha)p\oplus\alpha q:0\leq\alpha\leq 1\bigr\}.

Let
(X,\rho)
be a metric space.
A mapping
T\colon X\to X
is said to be

•

nonexpansive if
\rho(Tx,Ty)\leq\rho(x,y)
for all
x,y\in X
;

•

quasinonexpansive if
\Fix(T)
is nonempty and

\rho(Tx,y)\leq\rho(x,y)
for all
x\in X
and
y\in\Fix(T)
;

•

firmly metrically nonspreading if

\rho(Tx,Ty)^{2}\leq\left\langle\overrightarrow{(Tx)(Ty)},\overrightarrow{xy}\right\rangle

for all
x,y\in X
;

•

metrically nonspreading if

2\rho(Tx,Ty)^{2}\leq\rho(Tx,y)^{2}+\rho(Ty,x)^{2}

for all
x,y\in X
.

It is obvious that
T
is firmly metrically nonspreading
if and only if

2\rho(Tx,Ty)^{2}+\rho(Tx,x)^{2}+\rho(Ty,y)^{2}\leq\rho(Tx,y)^{2}+\rho(Ty,x)^{2}

for all
x,y\in X
.
This implies that every firmly spherically
nonspreading mapping is metrically nonspreading.
If
T
is a firmly metrically nonspreading and

\Fix(T)
is nonempty, then

(2.2)

\displaystyle\rho(Tx,y)^{2}+\rho(Tx,x)^{2}\leq\rho(x,y)^{2}

for all
x\in X
and
y\in\Fix(T)
.
Firmly metrically nonspreading mappings are also called
firmly nonexpansive mappings in
[
14
]
.
See also
[
18
]
on some results for
metrically nonspreading mappings.
A mapping
T
of a uniquely geodesic metric space

(X,\rho)
into itself is said to be
firmly nonexpansive
[
4
]
if

\rho(Tx,Ty)\leq\rho\bigl((1-\alpha)Tx\oplus\alpha x,(1-\alpha)Ty\oplus\alpha y\bigr)

for all
\alpha\in[0,1]
and
x,y\in X
.

A metric space
(X,\rho)
is said to be a
\textup{CAT}(0)
space
if it is uniquely geodesic and

\rho\bigl((1-\alpha)x\oplus\alpha y,(1-\beta)x\oplus\beta z\bigr)\leq\left\lvert(1-\alpha)\bar{x}+\alpha\bar{y}-\bigl((1-\beta)\bar{x}+\beta\bar{z}\bigr)\right\rvert

whenever
\alpha,\beta\in[0,1]
,

x,y,z\in X
,
\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

\rho(x,y)=\left\lvert\bar{x}-\bar{y}\right\rvert,\quad\rho(y,z)=\left\lvert\bar{y}-\bar{z}\right\rvert,\quad\textrm{and}\quad\rho(z,x)=\left\lvert\bar{z}-\bar{x}\right\rvert.

The sets
\Delta
and
\overline{\Delta}
defined by

\Delta=[x,y]\cup[y,z]\cup[z,x]\quad\textrm{and}\quad\overline{\Delta}=[\bar{x},\bar{y}]\cup[\bar{y},\bar{z}]\cup[\bar{z},\bar{x}]

are called a geodesic triangle with vertices
x,y,z
and
a comparison triangle for
\Delta
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Every complete
\textup{CAT}(0)
space is called an Hadamard space.
Typical examples of Hadamard spaces are
nonempty closed convex subsets of real Hilbert spaces,
open unit balls of complex Hilbert spaces with hyperbolic metrics,
complete
\mathbb{R}
-trees,
and simply connected complete Riemannian manifolds with

[
6
,
9
,
10
]
for more details

\textup{CAT}(0)

If
(X,\rho)
is a
\textup{CAT}(0)

(2.3)

\displaystyle\rho\bigl((1-\alpha)x\oplus\alpha y,z\bigr)\leq(1-\alpha)\rho(x,z)+\alpha\rho(y,z)

(2.4)

\displaystyle\rho\bigl((1-\alpha)x\oplus\alpha y,z\bigr)^{2}\leq(1-\alpha)\rho(x,z)^{2}+\alpha\rho(y,z)^{2}-\alpha(1-\alpha)\rho(x,y)^{2}

for all

, and

3.
Tangent spaces

We next propose a concept of a tangent space

\textup{CAT}(0)

\displaystyle g_{p}(\alpha\gamma_{p,x},\beta\gamma_{p,y})

\displaystyle=\alpha\rho(p,x)\beta\rho(p,y)\cos\angle_{p}(\gamma_{p,x},\gamma_{p,y})

\displaystyle\geq\alpha\rho(p,x)\beta\rho(p,y)\cos\overline{\angle}_{p}(\gamma_{p,x},\gamma_{p,y})

\displaystyle=\alpha\rho(p,x)\beta\rho(p,y)\cdot\frac{\rho(p,x)^{2}+\rho(p,y)^{2}-\rho(x,y)^{2}}{2\rho(p,x)\rho(p,y)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha\beta}{2}\bigl(\rho(p,x)^{2}+\rho(p,y)^{2}-\rho(x,y)^{2}\bigr)=\alpha\beta\left\langle\overrightarrow{px},\overrightarrow{py}\right\rangle.

Thus the result holds.
∎

We know the following lemma.

Lemma 3.5

)
.

Using Lemma

Lemma 3.6
.

Let

Proof.

\displaystyle g_{p}\bigl(\alpha\gamma_{p,x},\beta\gamma_{p,y}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\alpha\rho(p,x)\beta\rho(p,y)\cos\angle_{p}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\alpha\rho(p,x)\beta\rho(p,y)\cos\left(\lim_{t\to+0}\overline{\angle}_{p}\bigl(x,\gamma_{p,y}(t)\bigr)\right)

\displaystyle\rho(p,x)\bigl(p,\gamma_{p,y}(t)\bigr)\cos\overline{\angle}_{p}(x,\gamma_{p,y}(t))

\displaystyle=\rho(p,x)\rho\bigl(p,\gamma_{p,y}(t)\bigr)\frac{\rho(p,x)^{2}+\rho\bigl(p,\gamma_{p,y}(t)\bigr)^{2}-\rho\bigl(x,\gamma_{p,y}(t)\bigr)^{2}}{2\rho(p,x)\rho\bigl(p,\gamma_{p,y}(t)\bigr)}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\overrightarrow{px},\overrightarrow{p\bigl(\gamma_{p,y}(t)\bigr)}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\overrightarrow{px},\overrightarrow{p\left(\left(1-\frac{t}{\rho(p,y)}\right)p\oplus\frac{t}{\rho(p,y)}y\right)}\right\rangle

for all

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to+0}\frac{\alpha\beta\rho(p,y)}{t}\left\langle\overrightarrow{px},\overrightarrow{p\left(\left(1-\frac{t}{\rho(p,y)}\right)p\oplus\frac{t}{\rho(p,y)}y\right)}\right\rangle

Lemma 3.7
.

Let

,

(ii)

We prove the part (i).

\tilde{d}_{p}\bigl((\alpha,\gamma_{p,x}),(\beta,\gamma_{p,y})\bigr)=\alpha\left\lVert x-p\right\rVert=\left\lVert\alpha(x-p)-\beta(y-p)\right\rVert.

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\bigl(\alpha\left\lVert x-p\right\rVert\bigr)^{2}+\bigl(\beta\left\lVert y-p\right\rVert\bigr)^{2}-2\left\langle\alpha(x-p),\beta(y-p)\right\rangle}

4.
Monotone vector fields and subdifferentials

In this section, we study some fundamental properties of
monotone vector fields and subdifferential mappings in
\textup{CAT}(0)
spaces.

Let
(X,\rho)
be a
\textup{CAT}(0)
space.
The tangent bundle
TX
of
X
is defined by

TX=\bigcup_{x\in X}T_{x}X
.
The domain
\D(A)
of a mapping

A\colon X\to 2^{TX}
is defined by

\D(A)=\{x\in X:Ax\neq\emptyset\}.

A mapping
A\colon X\to 2^{TX}
is said to be
a vector field on
X
if

Ax\subset T_{x}X

for all
x\in X
. A vector field
A\colon X\to 2^{TX}

is said to be monotone if

g_{x}(x^{*},\gamma_{x,y})+g_{y}(y^{*},\gamma_{y,x})\leq 0

whenever
(x,x^{*}),(y,y^{*})\in\Gra(A)
, where

\Gra(A)
is the graph of
A
defined by

\Gra(A)=\bigl\{(x,x^{*})\in X\times TX:x^{*}\in Ax\bigr\}.

A monotone vector field
A\colon X\to 2^{TX}
is said to satisfy
the surjectivity condition if for each
\lambda>0
and
x\in X
,
there exists
z\in X
such that

\frac{1}{\lambda}\gamma_{z,x}\in Az.

In this case, the resolvent
J_{\lambda}
of
A

with respect to
\lambda>0

is defined by

J_{\lambda}x=\left\{z\in X:\frac{1}{\lambda}\gamma_{z,x}\in Az\right\}

for all
x\in X
. We denote by
\Zer(A)
the set defined by

\Zer(A)=\{u\in X:0_{u}\in Au\}.

Each point in
\Zer(A)
is called a zero point of
A
.

If
X
is particularly a real inner product space, then

T_{p}X
is isomorpic to
X
by Corollary
3.9

for all
p\in X
.
Let
\tau_{p}
be the isometry of
T_{p}X
onto
X
given by

\tau_{p}\bigl(\alpha\gamma_{p,x}\bigr)=\alpha(x-p)

for all
p\in X
and
\alpha\gamma_{p,x}\in T_{p}X
.
Let
A\colon X\to 2^{TX}
be a vector field.
Then we define
\tilde{A}\colon X\to 2^{X}
by

\tilde{A}x=\{\tau_{x}(x^{*}):x^{*}\in Ax\}

for all
x\in X
. Then we can prove that

A
is monotone if and only if
\tilde{A}
is monotone.
In fact, suppose that
\tilde{A}
is monotone and let

(x,x^{*}),(y,y^{*})\in\Gra(A)
be given.
Since
x^{*}\in T_{x}X
and
y^{*}\in T_{y}X
, we have

\alpha,\beta\geq 0
and
u,v\in X
such that

x^{*}=\alpha\gamma_{x,u}
and
y^{*}=\beta\gamma_{y,v}
.
Then it follows from Lemma
3.8

and the monotonicity of
\tilde{A}
that

\displaystyle g_{x}(x^{*},\gamma_{x,y})+g_{y}(y^{*},\gamma_{y,x})

\displaystyle=g_{x}(\alpha\gamma_{x,u},\gamma_{x,y})+g_{y}(\beta\gamma_{y,v},\gamma_{y,x})

\displaystyle=\left\langle\alpha(u-x),y-x\right\rangle+\left\langle\beta(v-y),x-y\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\tau_{x}(x^{*}),y-x\right\rangle+\left\langle\tau_{y}(y^{*}),x-y\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle x-y,\tau_{x}(x^{*})-\tau_{y}(y^{*})\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq 0.

Thus
A
is monotone. On the other hand,
suppose that
A
is monotone and
let
(x,z),(y,w)\in\Gra(\tilde{A})
be given.
Then we have
x^{*}=\alpha\gamma_{x,u}\in Ax

and
y^{*}=\beta\gamma_{y,v}\in Ay
such that

\tau_{x}(x^{*})=z
and
\tau_{y}(y^{*})=w
.
By Lemma
3.8

and the monotonicity of
A
, we have

\displaystyle\left\langle x-y,z-w\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle x-y,\tau_{x}(x^{*})-\tau_{y}(y^{*})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle x-y,\alpha(u-x)-\beta(v-y)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle y-x,\alpha(u-x)\right\rangle+\left\langle y-x,\beta(v-y)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\bigl(g_{x}(x^{*},\gamma_{x,y})+g_{y}(y^{*},\gamma_{y,x})\bigr)\geq 0.

Thus
\tilde{A}
is monotone.

Suppose that
X
is a real inner product space and

A\colon X\to 2^{TX}
is a monotone vector field.
Then
A
satisfies the surjectivity condition
if and only if for each
\lambda>0
and
x\in X
,
there exists
z\in X
such that

x\in z+\lambda\tilde{A}z.

In fact, if
\lambda>0
and
x,z\in X
, then we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda}\gamma_{z,x}\in Az\Longleftrightarrow\tau_{z}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}\gamma_{z,x}\right)\in\tilde{A}z\Longleftrightarrow\frac{1}{\lambda}(x-z)\in\tilde{A}z\Longleftrightarrow x\in z+\lambda\tilde{A}z.

In this case, we can also prove that

J_{\lambda}x=\left\{z\in X:x\in z+\lambda\tilde{A}z\right\}

for all
\lambda>0
and
x\in X
.

We prove some fundamental properties
of resolvents of monotone vector fields.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(X,\rho)
be a
\textup{CAT}(0)
space,

A\colon X\to 2^{TX}
a monotone vector field
satisfying the surjectivity condition, and

J_{\lambda}
the resolvent of
A

with respect to
\lambda>0
.
Then the following hold.

(i)

J_{\lambda}\colon X\to X

is a single-valued mapping for all
\lambda>0
;

(ii)

\Fix(J_{\lambda})=\Zer(A)
for all
\lambda>0
.

(iii)

if
0<\mu\leq\lambda
and
x\in X
, then

J_{\mu}\Bigl(\left(1-\frac{\mu}{\lambda}\right)J_{\lambda}x\oplus\frac{\mu}{\lambda}x\Bigr)=J_{\lambda}x;

(iv)

J_{\lambda}
is firmly metrically nonspreading
for all
\lambda>0
;

(v)

J_{\lambda}
is firmly nonexpansive for all
\lambda>0
.

Proof.

We first prove the part (i).
Let
x,z_{1},z_{2}\in X
and
\lambda>0
satisfy

\frac{1}{\lambda}\gamma_{z_{1},x}\in Az_{1}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\frac{1}{\lambda}\gamma_{z_{2},x}\in Az_{2}.

Since
A
is monotone, we have from Lemma
3.4
that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq g_{z_{1}}\left(\lambda^{-1}\gamma_{z_{1},x},\gamma_{z_{1},z_{2}}\right)+g_{z_{2}}\left(\lambda^{-1}\gamma_{z_{2},x},\gamma_{z_{2},z_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda}\bigl(g_{z_{1}}\left(\gamma_{z_{1},x},\gamma_{z_{1},z_{2}}\right)+g_{z_{2}}\left(\gamma_{z_{2},x},\gamma_{z_{2},z_{1}}\right)\bigr)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\lambda}\bigl(\left\langle\overrightarrow{z_{1}x},\overrightarrow{z_{1}z_{2}}\right\rangle+\left\langle\overrightarrow{z_{2}x},\overrightarrow{z_{2}z_{1}}\right\rangle\bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda}\bigl(\left\langle\overrightarrow{z_{1}x},\overrightarrow{z_{1}z_{2}}\right\rangle+\left\langle\overrightarrow{xz_{2}},\overrightarrow{z_{1}z_{2}}\right\rangle\bigr)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\left\langle\overrightarrow{z_{1}z_{2}},\overrightarrow{z_{1}z_{2}}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{\lambda}\rho(z_{1},z_{2})^{2}

and hence
z_{1}=z_{2}
. Thus the part (i) holds.

If
\lambda>0
and
u\in X
, then

u\in\Fix(J_{\lambda})\Longleftrightarrow J_{\lambda}u=u\Longleftrightarrow\frac{1}{\lambda}\gamma_{u,u}\in Au\Longleftrightarrow 0_{u}\in Au\Longleftrightarrow u\in\Zer(A).

Thus the part (ii) follows.

We next prove the part (iii).
Let
x\in X
be given and suppose that

0<\mu\leq\lambda
. Since the case where

\mu=\lambda
is trivial, we may suppose that
\mu<\lambda
.
Set

z=\left(1-\frac{\mu}{\lambda}\right)J_{\lambda}x\oplus\frac{\mu}{\lambda}x.

If
J_{\lambda}x=x
, then
z=x\in\Zer(A)
and hence
J_{\mu}z=z=x=J_{\lambda}x
.
If
J_{\lambda}x\neq x
, then

\frac{1}{\mu}\rho(z,J_{\lambda}x)=\frac{1}{\mu}\cdot\frac{\mu}{\lambda}\rho(x,J_{\lambda}x)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\rho(x,J_{\lambda}x)>0.

It follows from (
3.1
) that

\displaystyle\overline{\angle}_{J_{\lambda}x}(\gamma_{J_{\lambda}x,x},\gamma_{J_{\lambda}x,z})

\displaystyle=\arccos\frac{\rho(J_{\lambda}x,x)^{2}+\rho(J_{\lambda}x,z)^{2}-\rho(x,z)^{2}}{2\rho(J_{\lambda}x,x)\rho(J_{\lambda}x,z)}

\displaystyle=\arccos\frac{\rho(J_{\lambda}x,x)^{2}+\frac{\mu^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\rho(J_{\lambda}x,x)^{2}-\left(1-\frac{\mu}{\lambda}\right)^{2}\rho(x,J_{\lambda}x)^{2}}{\frac{2\mu}{\lambda}\rho(x,J_{\lambda}x)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\arccos\frac{\bigl(1+\frac{\mu^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}-(1-\frac{\mu}{\lambda})^{2}\bigr)\rho(x,J_{\lambda}x)^{2}}{\frac{2\mu}{\lambda}\rho(x,J_{\lambda}x)^{2}}=\arccos 1=0.

Thus we have

0\leq\angle_{J_{\lambda}x}(\gamma_{J_{\lambda}x,x},\gamma_{J_{\lambda}x,z})\leq\overline{\angle}_{J_{\lambda}x}(\gamma_{J_{\lambda}x,x},\gamma_{J_{\lambda}x,z})=0

and hence
\angle_{J_{\lambda}x}(\gamma_{J_{\lambda}x,x},\gamma_{J_{\lambda}x,z})=0
.
It then follows from Lemma
3.3
that

\frac{1}{\mu}\gamma_{J_{\lambda}x,z}=\frac{1}{\lambda}\gamma_{J_{\lambda}x,x}

and hence

\frac{1}{\mu}\gamma_{J_{\lambda}x,z}\in AJ_{\lambda}x.

Consequently, the part (i) implies that
J_{\mu}z=J_{\lambda}x
.

We next prove the part (iv). Let
\lambda>0
and
x,y\in X
be given.
By the definition of
J_{\lambda}
, we have

\frac{1}{\lambda}\gamma_{J_{\lambda}x,x}\in AJ_{\lambda}x\quad\textrm{and}\quad\frac{1}{\lambda}\gamma_{J_{\lambda}y,y}\in AJ_{\lambda}y.

The monotonicity of
A
and Lemma
3.4
imply that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq g_{J_{\lambda}x}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}\gamma_{J_{\lambda}x,x},\gamma_{J_{\lambda}x,J_{\lambda}y}\right)+g_{J_{\lambda}y}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}\gamma_{J_{\lambda}y,y},\gamma_{J_{\lambda}y,J_{\lambda}x}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda}\bigl(g_{J_{\lambda}x}\left(\gamma_{J_{\lambda}x,x},\gamma_{J_{\lambda}x,J_{\lambda}y}\right)+g_{J_{\lambda}y}\left(\gamma_{J_{\lambda}y,y},\gamma_{J_{\lambda}y,J_{\lambda}x}\right)\bigr)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(\left\langle\overrightarrow{(J_{\lambda}x)x},\overrightarrow{(J_{\lambda}x)(J_{\lambda}y)}\right\rangle+\left\langle\overrightarrow{(J_{\lambda}y)y},\overrightarrow{(J_{\lambda}y)(J_{\lambda}x)}\right\rangle\right)

and hence

\left\langle\overrightarrow{(J_{\lambda}x)x},\overrightarrow{(J_{\lambda}x)(J_{\lambda}y)}\right\rangle\leq\left\langle\overrightarrow{(J_{\lambda}y)y},\overrightarrow{(J_{\lambda}x)(J_{\lambda}y)}\right\rangle.

This implies that

\rho(J_{\lambda}x,J_{\lambda}y)^{2}+\rho(x,J_{\lambda}x)^{2}-\rho(x,J_{\lambda}y)^{2}\leq\rho(y,J_{\lambda}x)^{2}-\rho(J_{\lambda}y,J_{\lambda}x)^{2}-\rho(y,J_{\lambda}y)^{2}

and hence the part (iv) holds.

We finally prove the part (v). Let
\lambda>0
and
x,y\in X
be given.
It follows from Lemma
2.5
and the part (iv)
that

\rho(J_{\lambda}x,J_{\lambda}y)^{2}\leq\left\langle\overrightarrow{(J_{\lambda}x)(J_{\lambda}y)},\overrightarrow{xy}\right\rangle\leq\rho(J_{\lambda}x,J_{\lambda}y)\rho(x,y).

Thus
J_{\lambda}
is nonexpansive.
We next prove that
J_{\lambda}
is firmly nonexpansive.
Let
x,y\in X
and
\alpha\in[0,1]
be given.
We may assume that
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
Setting
\mu=\alpha\lambda
, we have
from the part (iii) and the nonexpansiveness of
J_{\lambda}
that

\displaystyle\rho(J_{\lambda}x,J_{\lambda}y)

\displaystyle=\rho\Bigl(J_{\mu}\left(\left(1-\frac{\mu}{\lambda}\right)J_{\lambda}x\oplus\frac{\mu}{\lambda}x\right),J_{\mu}\left(\left(1-\frac{\mu}{\lambda}\right)J_{\lambda}y\oplus\frac{\mu}{\lambda}y\right)\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\rho\Bigl(\left(1-\frac{\mu}{\lambda}\right)J_{\lambda}x\oplus\frac{\mu}{\lambda}x,\left(1-\frac{\mu}{\lambda}\right)J_{\lambda}y\oplus\frac{\mu}{\lambda}y\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\rho\bigl((1-\alpha)J_{\lambda}x\oplus\alpha x,(1-\alpha)J_{\lambda}y\oplus\alpha y\bigr).

Thus
J_{\lambda}
is firmly nonexpansive.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(X,\rho)
be a
\textup{CAT}(0)
space,

A\colon X\to 2^{TX}
a monotone vector field
satisfying the surjectivity condition,
and
J_{\lambda}
the resolvent of
A
with respect to
\lambda>0
.
Then the following hold.

(i)

The inequality

\displaystyle(\lambda+\mu)\rho(J_{\lambda}x,J_{\mu}y)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\rho(J_{\lambda}x,y)^{2}+\mu\rho(J_{\mu}y,x)^{2}-\mu\rho(J_{\lambda}x,x)^{2}-\lambda\rho(J_{\mu}y,y)^{2}

holds for all
\lambda,\mu>0
and
x,y\in X
;

(ii)

the inequality

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu}\rho(J_{\mu}J_{\lambda}x,J_{\lambda}x)\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\rho(J_{\lambda}x,x)

holds for all
\lambda,\mu>0
and
x\in X
.

Proof.

We first prove the part (i). Let
\lambda,\mu>0
and
x,y\in X

be given. Then we have

\frac{1}{\lambda}\gamma_{J_{\lambda}x,x}\in AJ_{\lambda}x\quad\textrm{and}\quad\frac{1}{\mu}\gamma_{J_{\mu}y,y}\in AJ_{\mu}y.

The monotonicity of
A
and Lemma
3.4
imply that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq g_{J_{\lambda}x}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}\gamma_{J_{\lambda}x,x},\gamma_{J_{\lambda}x,J_{\mu}y}\right)+g_{J_{\mu}y}\left(\frac{1}{\mu}\gamma_{J_{\mu}y,y},\gamma_{J_{\mu}y,J_{\lambda}x}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda\mu}\bigl(\mu g_{J_{\lambda}x}\left(\gamma_{J_{\lambda}x,x},\gamma_{J_{\lambda}x,J_{\mu}y}\right)+\lambda g_{J_{\mu}y}\left(\gamma_{J_{\mu}y,y},\gamma_{J_{\mu}y,J_{\lambda}x}\right)\bigr)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\lambda\mu}\left(\mu\left\langle\overrightarrow{(J_{\lambda}x)x},\overrightarrow{(J_{\lambda}x)(J_{\mu}y)}\right\rangle+\lambda\left\langle\overrightarrow{(J_{\mu}y)y},\overrightarrow{(J_{\mu}y)(J_{\lambda}x)}\right\rangle\right)

and hence

\mu\left\langle\overrightarrow{(J_{\lambda}x)x},\overrightarrow{(J_{\lambda}x)(J_{\mu}y)}\right\rangle\leq\lambda\left\langle\overrightarrow{(J_{\mu}y)y},\overrightarrow{(J_{\lambda}x)(J_{\mu}y)}\right\rangle.

This implies that

\displaystyle\mu\bigl(\rho(J_{\lambda}x,J_{\mu}y)^{2}+\rho(x,J_{\lambda}x)^{2}-\rho(x,J_{\mu}y)^{2}\bigr)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\lambda\bigl(\rho(y,J_{\lambda}x)^{2}-\rho(J_{\mu}y,J_{\lambda}x)^{2}-\rho(y,J_{\mu}y)^{2}\bigr)

and hence we obtain the conclusion.

We next prove the part (ii). Let
\lambda,\mu>0
and
x\in X
be given.
Setting
y=J_{\lambda}x
in the part (i), we have

\displaystyle(\lambda+\mu)\rho(J_{\lambda}x,J_{\mu}J_{\lambda}x)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\lambda\rho(J_{\lambda}x,J_{\lambda}x)^{2}+\mu\rho(J_{\mu}J_{\lambda}x,x)^{2}-\mu\rho(J_{\lambda}x,x)^{2}-\lambda\rho(J_{\mu}J_{\lambda}x,J_{\lambda}x)^{2}.

Hence it follows from Lemma
2.5
that

\displaystyle\lambda\rho(J_{\lambda}x,J_{\mu}J_{\lambda}x)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mu}{2}\bigl(\rho(J_{\mu}J_{\lambda}x,x)^{2}-\rho(J_{\lambda}x,x)^{2}-\rho(J_{\lambda}x,J_{\mu}J_{\lambda}x)^{2}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\mu\left\langle\overrightarrow{(J_{\mu}J_{\lambda}x)(J_{\lambda}x)},\overrightarrow{(J_{\lambda}x)x}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\mu\rho(J_{\mu}J_{\lambda}x,J_{\lambda}x)\rho(J_{\lambda}x,x).

Therefore we obtain the desired inequality.
∎

We next prove the following demiclosedness principle.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
(X,\rho)
be a
\textup{CAT}(0)
space,

A\colon X\to 2^{TX}
a monotone vector field
satisfying the surjectivity condition,

J_{\lambda}
the resolvent of
A
with respect to
\lambda>0
.

\{\lambda_{n}\}
a sequence of positive real numbers,

\{z_{n}\}
a sequence in
X
, and
p
an element in
X
.
If

\AC\bigl(\{J_{\lambda_{n}}z_{n}\}\bigr)=\{p\}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\rho(J_{\lambda_{n}}z_{n},z_{n})\to 0,

then
p\in\Zer(A)
.

Proof.

Let
\mu>0
be given.
It follows from the part (ii) in Lemma
4.2
that

0\leq\rho(J_{\mu}J_{\lambda_{n}}z_{n},J_{\lambda_{n}}z_{n})\leq\frac{\mu}{\lambda_{n}}\rho(J_{\lambda_{n}}z_{n},z_{n})

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and hence it follows from assumption that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\rho(J_{\mu}J_{\lambda_{n}}z_{n},J_{\lambda_{n}}z_{n})=0.

Since
J_{\mu}
is nonexpansive by Lemma
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle\rho(J_{\lambda_{n}}z_{n},J_{\mu}p)

\displaystyle\leq\rho(J_{\lambda_{n}}z_{n},J_{\mu}J_{\lambda_{n}}z_{n})+\rho(J_{\mu}J_{\lambda_{n}}z_{n},J_{\mu}p)

\displaystyle\leq\rho(J_{\lambda_{n}}z_{n},J_{\mu}J_{\lambda_{n}}z_{n})+\rho(J_{\lambda_{n}}z_{n},p)

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Taking the upper limit, we obtain

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\rho(J_{\lambda_{n}}z_{n},J_{\mu}p)\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\rho(J_{\lambda_{n}}z_{n},p).

Since
\AC\bigl(\{J_{\lambda_{n}}z_{n}\}\bigr)=\{p\}
,
we know that
J_{\mu}p=p
. Consequently,
the part (ii) in Lemma
4.1
implies that

p\in\Zer(A)
.
∎

Let
(X,\rho)
be a
\textup{CAT}(0)
space and

f\colon X\to(-\infty,\infty]
a proper lower semicontinuous
convex function.
For a fixed element
x\in X
, a point
x^{*}\in T_{x}X
is said to be
a subgradient of
f
at
x
if

f(x)+g_{x}(x^{*},\gamma_{x,y})\leq f(y)

for all
y\in X
. The set of all subgradients of
f
at
x\in X

is denoted by
\partial f(x)
. This set is called
the subdifferential of
f
at
x\in X
.
The vector field
\partial f\colon X\to 2^{TX}
is called
the subdifferential mapping of
f
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
(X,\rho)
be a
\textup{CAT}(0)
space and

f\colon X\to(-\infty,\infty]
a proper lower semicontinuous
convex function.
Then the following hold.

(i)

\partial f\colon X\to 2^{TX}
is a monotone vector field;

(ii)

\Zer(\partial f)=\Argmin_{X}f
;

(iii)

if
X
is complete,
then
\partial f
satisfies the surjectivity condition.
Further, if
\lambda>0
, then
the resolvent
J_{\lambda}
of
\partial f
with respect to

\lambda
coincides with
the proximal mapping
\Prox_{\lambda f}
of
\lambda f
.

Proof.

We first prove the part (i).
The definition of
\partial f
implies that
\partial f

is a vector field on
X
.
Let
(x,x^{*}),(y,y^{*})\in\Gra(\partial f)
be given.
Then we have

f(x)+g_{x}(x^{*},\gamma_{x,z})\leq f(z)

for all
z\in X
. Since
f
is proper, we have
a\in X
such that

f(a)\in\mathbb{R}
. This implies that

f(x)\leq f(a)-g_{x}(x^{*},\gamma_{x,a})<\infty

and hence
x\in\Dom f
. Similarly, we have
y\in\Dom f
.
Since

f(x)+g_{x}(x^{*},\gamma_{x,y})\leq f(y)\quad\textrm{and}\quad f(y)+g_{y}(y^{*},\gamma_{y,x})\leq f(x),

we have

g_{x}(x^{*},\gamma_{x,y})+g_{y}(y^{*},\gamma_{y,x})\leq 0,

which shows that
\partial f
is monotone.

We next prove the part (ii). Let
u\in X
be given. Then we have

\displaystyle u\in\Zer(\partial f)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow 0_{u}\in\partial f(u)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow f(u)+g_{x}(0_{u},\gamma_{x,y})\leq f(y)\quad(\forall y\in X)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow f(u)\leq f(y)\quad(\forall y\in X)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow u\in\Argmin_{X}f.

Thus we have the equality.

We finally prove the part (iii).
Suppose that
X
is complete and let
\lambda>0
be given.
Then Lemma
2.9

ensures that the proximal mapping
\Prox_{\lambda f}
of
\lambda f

is well defined.
Let
x\in X
be given and set
z=\Prox_{\lambda f}x
.
Fix
y\in X
and set

u_{n}=\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)z\oplus\frac{1}{n}y

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then it follows from the convexity of
f

and (
2.4
) that

\displaystyle f(z)+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\rho(z,x)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq f(u_{n})+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\rho(u_{n},x)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)f(z)+\frac{1}{n}f(y)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\left(\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)\rho(z,x)^{2}+\frac{1}{n}\rho(y,x)^{2}-\frac{1}{n}\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)\rho(z,y)^{2}\right)

and hence

\displaystyle f(z)+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\left(\rho(z,x)^{2}+\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)\rho(z,y)^{2}-\rho(y,x)^{2}\right)\leq f(y)

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Taking the limit with respect to
n
, we have

\displaystyle f(y)\geq f(z)+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\bigl(\rho(z,x)^{2}+\rho(z,y)^{2}-\rho(y,x)^{2}\bigr)=f(z)+\frac{1}{\lambda}\left\langle\overrightarrow{zx},\overrightarrow{zy}\right\rangle.

Thus we obtain

\displaystyle f(z)+\frac{1}{\lambda}\left\langle\overrightarrow{zx},\overrightarrow{zy}\right\rangle\leq f(y).

If
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, then we have

\displaystyle f(z)+\frac{1}{\lambda}\left\langle\overrightarrow{zx},\overrightarrow{z\bigl((1-\varepsilon)z\oplus\varepsilon y\bigr)}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq f\bigl((1-\varepsilon)z\oplus\varepsilon y\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq(1-\varepsilon)f(z)+\varepsilon f(y)

and hence

\displaystyle f(z)+\frac{1}{\varepsilon\lambda}\left\langle\overrightarrow{zx},\overrightarrow{z\bigl((1-\varepsilon)z\oplus\varepsilon y\bigr)}\right\rangle\leq f(y).

It then follows from Lemma
3.6
that

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to+0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon\lambda}\left\langle\overrightarrow{zx},\overrightarrow{z\bigl((1-\varepsilon)z\oplus\varepsilon y\bigr)}\right\rangle=g_{z}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}\gamma_{z,x},\gamma_{z,y}\right).

Consequently, we obtain

\displaystyle f(z)+g_{z}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}\gamma_{z,x},\gamma_{z,y}\right)\leq f(y)

for all
y\in X
. Thus we have

\lambda^{-1}\gamma_{z,x}\in\partial f(z)
.
Therefore,
\partial f
satisfies the surjectivity condition.
It also holds that

J_{\lambda}x=z=\Prox_{\lambda f}(x)

for all
x\in X
. Consequently,
we have
J_{\lambda}=\Prox_{\lambda f}
.
∎

5.
The proximal point method

In this section, we study the asymptotic behavior of
sequences generated by the proximal point method
for monotone vector fields in Hadamard spaces.

The part (i) and the part (ii) of the following result generalize
the corresponding theorems in Hilbert spaces by
Rockafellar
[
21
]
and
Brézis and Lions
[
8
]
, respectively.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
X
be an Hadamard space,

A\colon X\to 2^{TX}
a monotone vector field
satisfying the surjectivity condition,

J_{\lambda}
the resolvent of
A
with respect to
\lambda>0
,
and
\{x_{n}\}
a sequence defined by
x_{1}\in X
and

x_{n+1}=J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n}\quad(n=1,2,\dots),

where
\{\lambda_{n}\}
is a sequence of positive real numbers.
Then the following hold.

(i)

If
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}=\infty
,
then
\{x_{n}\}
is bounded if and only if
\Zer(A)
is nonempty;

(ii)

if
\Zer(A)
is nonempty and

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{2}=\infty
,
then
\{x_{n}\}
is
\Delta
-convergent to
a point in
\Zer(A)
.

Proof.

We first prove the part (i).
Suppose that
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}=\infty
.
If
\Zer(A)
is nonempty, then we can fix
z\in\Zer(A)
.
Since
z\in\Fix(J_{\lambda})
and
J_{\lambda}
is nonexpansive
for each
\lambda>0
, we have

\displaystyle\rho(z,x_{n+1})=\rho(J_{\lambda_{n}}z,J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n})\leq\rho(z,x_{n})

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. This implies that

\bigl\{d(z,x_{n})\bigr\}
is convergent and hence

\{x_{n}\}
is bounded.
On the other hand, we suppose that
\{x_{n}\}
is bounded.
Let
\{\sigma_{n}\}
be the real sequence given by

\sigma_{n}=\sum_{l=1}^{n}\lambda_{l}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
g
be the real function defined by

\displaystyle g(y)=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sigma_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}\rho(y,x_{k+1})^{2}

for all
y\in X
.
It follows from Theorem
2.10
that
there exists
p\in X
such that

\Argmin_{X}g=\{p\}.

Let
\mu>0
be given.
Using the part (i) in Lemma
4.2
, we have

\displaystyle(\lambda_{k}+\mu)\rho(x_{k+1},J_{\mu}p)^{2}\leq\lambda_{k}\rho(x_{k+1},p)^{2}+\mu\rho(J_{\mu}p,x_{k})^{2}

and hence

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}\rho(x_{k+1},J_{\mu}p)^{2}\leq\lambda_{k}\rho(x_{k+1},p)^{2}+\mu\bigl(\rho(J_{\mu}p,x_{k})^{2}-\rho(J_{\mu}p,x_{k+1})^{2}\bigr)

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. This implies that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}\rho(x_{k+1},J_{\mu}p)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\sigma_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}\rho(x_{k+1},p)^{2}+\frac{\mu}{\sigma_{n}}\rho(J_{\mu}p,x_{1})^{2}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Taking the upper limit, we have

\displaystyle g(J_{\mu}p)\leq g(p).

Since
p
is the unique minimizer of
g
, we obtain

J_{\mu}p=p
and hence
p\in\Zer(A)
.
Thus
\Zer(A)
is nonempty.

We next prove the part (ii).
Suppose that
\Zer(A)
is nonempty and
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{2}=\infty
.
Let
z\in\Zer(A)
be given. It then follows from (ii) and (iv)
in Lemma
4.1
that

\displaystyle\rho(z,x_{n+1})^{2}

\displaystyle=\rho(z,J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\rho(z,x_{n})^{2}-\rho(J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n},x_{n})^{2}

\displaystyle=\rho(z,x_{n})^{2}-\rho(J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n},x_{n})^{2}\leq\rho(z,x_{n})^{2}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence
\bigl\{\rho(z,x_{n})^{2}\bigr\}
is convergent
and
\{x_{n}\}
is bounded. We also have

(5.1)

\displaystyle\rho(J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n},x_{n})^{2}\leq\rho(z,x_{n})^{2}-\rho(z,x_{n+1})^{2}

and hence we obtain

(5.2)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\rho(J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n},x_{n})=0.

It follows from (
5.1
) that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\rho(J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n},x_{n})\right)^{2}\leq\rho(z,x_{1})^{2}-\lim_{n\to\infty}\rho(z,x_{n})^{2}<\infty.

Since
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{2}=\infty
, we have

(5.3)

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\rho(J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n},x_{n})=0.

In fact, if this does not hold,
then we have
\delta>0
and
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\rho(J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n},x_{n})>\delta

for all
n\geq n_{0}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\rho(J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n},x_{n})\right)^{2}\geq\delta^{2}\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{2}=\infty.

This is a contradiction.
It then follows from the part (ii) in Lemma
4.2
that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{n+1}}\rho(J_{\lambda_{n+1}}x_{n+1},x_{n+1})=\frac{1}{\lambda_{n+1}}\rho(J_{\lambda_{n+1}}J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n},J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n})\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\rho(J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n},x_{n})

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and hence the sequence

\left\{\lambda_{n}^{-1}\rho(J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n},x_{n})\right\}

is convergent. Thus it follows from (
5.3
) that

(5.4)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\rho(J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n},x_{n})=0.

Let
w\in\omega_{\Delta}\bigl(\{x_{n}\}\bigr)
be given.
Then we have a subsequence
\{x_{n_{i}}\}
of
\{x_{n}\}
which is

\Delta
-convergent to
w
.
It follows from (
5.2
) that

\displaystyle\limsup_{i\to\infty}\rho(y,x_{n_{i}})=\limsup_{i\to\infty}\rho(y,J_{\lambda_{n_{i}}}x_{n_{i}})

for all
y\in X
and hence

(5.5)

\displaystyle\AC\bigl(\{J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n_{i}}\}\bigr)=\AC\bigl(\{x_{n_{i}}\}\bigr)=\{w\}.

It then follows from (
5.4
), (
5.5
),
and Lemma
4.3
that
w\in\Zer(A)
.
This implies that
\{\rho(w,x_{n})\}
is convergent.
Consequently,
\{\rho(w,x_{n})\}
is convergent
for all
w\in\omega_{\Delta}\bigl(\{x_{n}\}\bigr)
.
Then Lemma
2.8
implies that

\{x_{n}\}
is
\Delta
-convergent to some point
x_{\infty}

in
X
. Since

\displaystyle\{x_{\infty}\}=\omega_{\Delta}\bigl(\{x_{n}\}\bigr)\subset\Zer(A),

we conclude that
\{x_{n}\}
is
\Delta
-convergent to
the point
x_{\infty}
in
\Zer(A)
.
This completes the proof.
∎

As a direct consequence of Lemma
4.4

and Theorem
5.1
, we obtain the following.
Note that Bačák
[
5
]
proved that
the conclusion of the part (ii) holds whenever

\Argmin_{X}f
is nonempty and
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}=\infty
;
see also Kimura and Kohsaka
[
15
]
.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
X
be an Hadamard space,

f\colon X\to(-\infty,\infty]
a proper lower semicontinuous convex function,
and
\{x_{n}\}
a sequence defined by
x_{1}\in X
and

x_{n+1}=\Prox_{\lambda_{n}f}x_{n},

where
\{\lambda_{n}\}
is a sequence of positive real numbers.
Then the following hold.

(i)

If
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}=\infty
,
then
\{x_{n}\}
is bounded if and only if the set
\Argmin_{X}f
is nonempty;

(ii)

if
\Argmin_{X}f
is nonempty and

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{2}=\infty
,
then
\{x_{n}\}
is
\Delta
-convergent to
a point in
\Argmin_{X}f
.

6.
Two modified proximal point methods

In this section, we study the asymptotic behavior of
two variants of the proximal point method for
monotone vector fields in Hadamard spaces.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
(X,\rho)
be an Hadamard space,

A\colon X\to 2^{TX}
a monotone vector field
satisfying the surjectivity condition,

J_{\lambda}
the resolvent of
A
with respect to

\lambda>0
, and

\{x_{n}\}
a sequence defined by
x_{1}\in X
and

(6.1)

\displaystyle x_{n+1}=\alpha_{n}x_{n}\oplus(1-\alpha_{n})J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n}\quad(n=1,2,\dots),

where
\{\alpha_{n}\}
is a sequence in
[0,1)

and
\{\lambda_{n}\}
is a sequence of positive real numbers such that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-\alpha_{n})\lambda_{n}=\infty.

Then the following hold.

(i)

\{J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n}\}
is bounded
if and only if
\Zer(A)
is nonempty;

(ii)

if
\Zer(A)
is nonempty,
\sup_{n}\alpha_{n}<1
, and

\inf_{n}\lambda_{n}>0
, then

\{x_{n}\}
and
\{J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n}\}
are
\Delta
-convergent to a point
x_{\infty}
in
\Zer(A)
.

Proof.

We first prove the part (i). If
\Zer(A)
is nonempty, then we can fix
z\in\Zer(A)
.
Since
z\in\Fix(J_{\lambda})
and
J_{\lambda}
is nonexpansive
for each
\lambda>0
, we have

\displaystyle\rho(z,x_{n+1})

\displaystyle=\rho(z,\alpha_{n}x_{n}\oplus(1-\alpha_{n})J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{n}\rho(z,x_{n})+(1-\alpha_{n})\rho(z,J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n})

\displaystyle=\alpha_{n}\rho(z,x_{n})+(1-\alpha_{n})\rho(J_{\lambda_{n}}z,J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n})\leq\rho(z,x_{n})

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. This implies that
\{\rho(z,x_{n})\}
is convergent and
\{x_{n}\}
is bounded.
Since

\displaystyle\rho(z,J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n})\leq\rho(z,x_{n})

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the sequence
\{J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n}\}
is also bounded.
Conversely, we suppose that
\{J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n}\}
is bounded.
Let
\{\sigma_{n}\}
be the real sequence given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}=\sum_{l=1}^{n}(1-\alpha_{l})\lambda_{l}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
g
be the real function defined by

\displaystyle g(y)=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sigma_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(1-\alpha_{k})\lambda_{k}\rho(y,J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n})^{2}

for all
y\in X
. Theorem
2.10
ensures that

\displaystyle\Argmin_{X}g=\{p\}

for some
p\in X
. Let
\mu>0
be given.
By the part (i) in Lemma
4.2
, we have

\displaystyle(\lambda_{k}+\mu)\rho(J_{\lambda_{k}}x_{k},J_{\mu}p)^{2}\leq\lambda_{k}\rho(J_{\lambda_{k}}x_{k},p)^{2}+\mu\rho(J_{\mu}p,x_{k})^{2}

and hence

(6.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lambda_{k}\rho(J_{\lambda_{k}}x_{k},J_{\mu}p)^{2}\leq\lambda_{k}\rho(J_{\lambda_{k}}x_{k},p)^{2}+\mu\bigl(\rho(x_{k},J_{\mu}p)^{2}-\rho(J_{\lambda_{k}}x_{k},J_{\mu}p)^{2}\bigr)\end{split}

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
On the other hand, it follows from the definition of
\{x_{n}\}
that

\displaystyle\rho(x_{k+1},J_{\mu}p)^{2}

\displaystyle=\rho\bigl(\alpha_{k}x_{k}\oplus(1-\alpha_{k})J_{\lambda_{k}}x_{k},J_{\mu}p\bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{k}\rho(x_{k},J_{\mu}p)^{2}+(1-\alpha_{k})\rho(J_{\lambda_{k}}x_{k},J_{\mu}p)^{2}

and hence

(6.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}(1-\alpha_{k})\bigl(\rho(x_{k},J_{\mu}p)^{2}-\rho(J_{\lambda_{k}}x_{k},J_{\mu}p)^{2}\bigr)\leq\rho(x_{k},J_{\mu}p)^{2}-\rho(x_{k+1},J_{\mu}p)^{2}\end{split}

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. It then follows from (
6.2
)
and (
6.3
) that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&(1-\alpha_{k})\lambda_{k}\rho(J_{\lambda_{k}}x_{k},J_{\mu}p)^{2}\\
&\leq(1-\alpha_{k})\lambda_{k}\rho(J_{\lambda_{k}}x_{k},p)^{2}+(1-\alpha_{k})\mu\bigl(\rho(x_{k},J_{\mu}p)^{2}-\rho(J_{\lambda_{k}}x_{k},J_{\mu}p)^{2}\bigr)\\
&\leq(1-\alpha_{k})\lambda_{k}\rho(J_{\lambda_{k}}x_{k},p)^{2}+\mu\bigl(\rho(x_{k},J_{\mu}p)^{2}-\rho(x_{k+1},J_{\mu}p)^{2}\bigr)\end{split}

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{1}{\sigma_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(1-\alpha_{k})\lambda_{k}\rho(J_{\lambda_{k}}x_{k},J_{\mu}p)^{2}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\sigma_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(1-\alpha_{k})\lambda_{k}\rho(J_{\lambda_{k}}x_{k},p)^{2}+\frac{\mu}{\sigma_{n}}\bigl(\rho(x_{1},J_{\mu}p)^{2}-\rho(x_{n+1},J_{\mu}p)^{2}\bigr)\end{split}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Taking the upper limit, we have from
\lim_{n}\sigma_{n}=\infty
that

\displaystyle g(J_{\mu}p)\leq g(p).

Since
p
is the unique minimizer of
g
, we know that
J_{\mu}p=p
.
This implies that
p\in\Zer(A)
and hence
\Zer(A)
is nonempty.

We next prove the part (ii). Suppose that
\Zer(A)
is nonempty,
\sup_{n}\alpha_{n}<1
,
and
\inf_{n}\lambda_{n}>0
. Let
z\in\Zer(A)
be given.
It follows from (
2.2
) and the part (iv) in Lemma
4.1
that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\rho(z,J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n})^{2}\leq\rho(z,x_{n})^{2}-\rho(J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n},x_{n})^{2}\end{split}

and hence

(6.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\rho(z,x_{n+1})^{2}&=\rho\bigl(z,\alpha_{n}x_{n}\oplus(1-\alpha_{n})J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n}\bigr)^{2}\\
&\leq\alpha_{n}\rho(z,x_{n})^{2}+(1-\alpha_{n})\rho(z,J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n})^{2}\\
&\leq\rho(z,x_{n})^{2}-(1-\alpha_{n})\rho(J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n},x_{n})^{2}\end{split}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus
\{\rho(z,x_{n})\}
is convergent and
\{x_{n}\}
is bounded.
Thus
\{J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n}\}
is bounded since

\displaystyle\rho(z,J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n})\leq\rho(z,x_{n}),

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
It then follows from (
6.4
) that

\displaystyle(1-\alpha_{n})\rho(J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n},x_{n})^{2}\leq\rho(z,x_{n})^{2}-\rho(z,x_{n+1})^{2}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence we have from
\sup_{n}\alpha_{n}<1
that

(6.5)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\rho(J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n},x_{n})=0.

Let
w\in\omega_{\Delta}\bigl(\{x_{n}\}\bigr)
be given.
Then we have a subsequence
\{x_{n_{i}}\}
of
\{x_{n}\}
which
is
\Delta
-convergent to
w
.
The equality (
6.5
) implies that

\displaystyle\limsup_{i\to\infty}\rho(y,x_{n_{i}})=\limsup_{i\to\infty}\rho(y,J_{\lambda_{n_{i}}}x_{n_{i}})

for all
y\in X
and hence

\displaystyle\AC\bigl(\{J_{\lambda_{n_{i}}}x_{n_{i}}\}\bigr)=\AC\bigl(\{x_{n_{i}}\}\bigr)=\{w\}.

It also follows from (
6.5
) and
\inf_{n}\lambda_{n}>0
that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\rho(J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n},x_{n})=0.

Hence Lemma
4.3
implies that

w\in\Zer(A)
. Thus
\{\rho(w,x_{n})\}
is convergent for each
w\in\omega_{\Delta}\bigl(\{x_{n}\}\bigr)
.
It then follows from Lemma
2.8
that

\{x_{n}\}
is
\Delta
-convergent to an element
x_{\infty}
in
X
.
Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\{x_{\infty}\}=\omega_{\Delta}\bigl(\{x_{n}\}\bigr)\subset\Zer(A).

It follows from (
6.5
) that

\{J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n}\}
is also
\Delta
-convergent to
x_{\infty}
.
Therefore we conclude that
\{x_{n}\}
and
\{J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n}\}
are

\Delta
-convergent to an element
x_{\infty}
in
\Zer(A)
.
∎

4.4

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
(X,\rho)
be an Hadamard space,

A\colon X\to 2^{TX}
a monotone vector field
satisfying the surjectivity condition,

J_{\lambda}
the resolvent of
A
with respect to

\lambda>0
,
v
an element in
X
, and

\{x_{n}\}
a sequence defined by
x_{1}\in X
and

(6.7)

\displaystyle x_{n+1}=\alpha_{n}v\oplus(1-\alpha_{n})J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n}\quad(n=1,2,\dots),

where
\{\alpha_{n}\}
is a sequence in
[0,1]

and
\{\lambda_{n}\}
is a sequence of positive real numbers such that

\lim_{n}\lambda_{n}=\infty
.
Then the following hold.

(i)

\{J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n}\}
is bounded
if and only if
\Zer(A)
is nonempty;

(ii)

if
\Zer(A)
is nonempty,
\lim_{n}\alpha_{n}=0
, and

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}=\infty
, then

\{x_{n}\}
and
\{J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n}\}
are convergent to
Pv
, where

P
denotes the metric projection of
X
onto
\Zer(A)
.

Proof.

We first prove the part (i). If
\Zer(A)
is nonempty, then
we can fix
z\in\Zer(A)
. By the definition of
\{x_{n}\}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\rho(z,x_{n+1})&\leq\alpha_{n}\rho(z,v)+(1-\alpha_{n})\rho(z,J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n})\\
&\leq\alpha_{n}\rho(z,v)+(1-\alpha_{n})\rho(z,x_{n})\end{split}

and hence

(6.8)

\displaystyle\rho(z,x_{n})\leq\max\{\rho(z,v),\rho(z,x_{1})\}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We also know that

(6.9)

\displaystyle\rho(z,J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n})\leq\rho(z,x_{n})

and hence
\{x_{n}\}
and
\{J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n}\}
are bounded.
Conversely, if
\{J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n}\}
is bounded, then Lemma
2.7

implies that

(6.10)

\displaystyle\AC\bigl(\{J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n}\}\bigr)=\{p\}

for some
p\in X
. Since

\displaystyle\begin{split}\rho(y,x_{n+1})&\leq\alpha_{n}\rho(y,v)+(1-\alpha_{n})\rho(y,J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n})\\
&\leq\rho(y,v)+\rho(y,J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n})\end{split}

for all
y\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the sequence
\{x_{n}\}
is also bounded.
Moreover, since
\lim_{n}\lambda_{n}=\infty
and both
\{x_{n}\}
and
\{J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n}\}
are
bounded, we have

(6.11)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\rho(J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n},x_{n}).

By (
6.10
), (
6.11
),
and Lemma
4.3
, we have

p\in\Zer(A)
and hence
\Zer(A)
is nonempty.

We next prove the part (ii). Suppose that
\Zer(A)
is nonempty,

\lim_{n}\alpha_{n}=0
, and
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}=\infty
.
Set
y_{n}=J_{\lambda_{n}}x_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
z\in\Zer(A)
be fixed.
Then we know that (
6.8
) and (
6.9
) hold
and hence
\{x_{n}\}
and
\{y_{n}\}
are bounded.
Let
P
be the metric projection of
X
onto
\Zer(A)
. Then we have

(6.12)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\rho(Pv,x_{n+1})^{2}\\
&=\rho\bigl(Pv,\alpha_{n}v\oplus(1-\alpha_{n})y_{n}\bigr)^{2}\\
&\leq\alpha_{n}\rho(Pv,v)^{2}+(1-\alpha_{n})\rho(Pv,y_{n})^{2}-\alpha_{n}(1-\alpha_{n})\rho(v,y_{n})^{2}\\
&\leq(1-\alpha_{n})\rho(Pv,x_{n})^{2}+\alpha_{n}\bigl(\rho(Pv,v)^{2}-(1-\alpha_{n})\rho(v,y_{n})^{2}\bigr)\end{split}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Setting

\displaystyle s_{n}=\rho(Pv,x_{n})^{2}\quad\textrm{and}\quad t_{n}=\rho(Pv,v)^{2}-(1-\alpha_{n})\rho(v,y_{n})^{2},

we have

(6.13)

\displaystyle s_{n+1}\leq(1-\alpha_{n})s_{n}+\alpha_{n}t_{n}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
\{y_{n}\}
is bounded, we have a subsequence
\{y_{n_{i}}\}

of
\{y_{n}\}
which is
\Delta
-convergent to
q\in X
and

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\rho(v,y_{n_{i}})^{2}=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\rho(v,y_{n})^{2}.

Since

\displaystyle\AC\bigl(\{y_{n_{i}}\}\bigr)=\{q\}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda_{n_{i}}}\rho(y_{n_{i}},x_{n_{i}})=0,

it follows from Lemma
4.3
that
q\in\Zer(A)
.
Then the
\Delta
-lower semicontinuity of
\rho(v,\cdot)^{2}
implies that

\displaystyle\rho(v,q)^{2}\leq\liminf_{i\to\infty}\rho(v,y_{n_{i}})^{2}=\lim_{i\to\infty}\rho(v,y_{n_{i}})^{2}=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\rho(v,y_{n})^{2}.

Since
\rho(Pv,v)\leq\rho(q,v)
and
\alpha_{n}\to 0
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\limsup_{n\to\infty}t_{n}&=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\bigl(\rho(Pv,v)^{2}-\rho(v,y_{n})^{2}+\alpha_{n}\rho(v,y_{n})^{2}\bigr)\\
&=\rho(Pv,v)^{2}-\liminf_{n\to\infty}\rho(v,y_{n})^{2}\leq\rho(Pv,v)^{2}-\rho(v,q)^{2}\leq 0.\end{split}

Thus it follows from Lemma
2.1
that

\{s_{n}\}
converges to
0
. Thus
\{x_{n}\}
is convergent to
Pv
. Since

\displaystyle\rho(Pv,y_{n})\leq\rho(Pv,x_{n})

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the sequence
\{y_{n}\}
is also convergent to
Pv
.
