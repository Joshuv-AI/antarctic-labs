1.0 Summary
 Campaign purpose and scope (abridged from SHIRE application):

 An extended-range atmospheric emitted radiance interferometer (AERI) instrument to be deployed to
 Scott Base (78 S, 166 E) in McMurdo Sound, Antarctica for the purpose of synergizing with the National
 Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA) Polar Radiant Energy in the Far-InfraRed Experiment
 (PREFIRE) satellite mission (https://prefire.ssec.wisc.edu/), examining the closure of atmospheric
 thermal emission, and providing validation for PREFIRE. The PREFIRE mission is the first satellite
 mission to map Earth’s spectrally resolved thermal emission, from 5 to 50 microns, over seasonal to
 annual timescales. These new observations represent the beginning of an intensive examination of the
 nature of thermal emission from Earth (emission from both the surface and atmosphere). A primary
 requirement of the PREFIRE mission is to provide validation of the retrieved variables. Numerous field
 sites in the Arctic (such as the U.S. Department of Energy Atmospheric Radiation Measurement (ARM)
 North Slope of Alaska observatory and Summit Station, Greenland) have existing instrumentation to
 facilitate validation, leaving an observational gap in Antarctica. Through SHIRE, we propose to establish
 a temporary, intensive measurement site in Antarctica to ensure that both poles are suitably represented,
 and benefit from this state-of-the art science. This will also allow Arctic and Antarctic atmospheric
 radiative states to be compared and contrasted. For SHIRE specifically, we also propose further objectives
 to examine the spectrally resolved thermal radiative closure of the polar atmosphere and the historical
 changes in Antarctic radiative forcing, using previous Antarctic field campaigns, such as the ARM West
 Antarctic Radiation Experiment (AWARE).

 1.1 Science Objectives
 SHIRE has three distinct scientific objectives:
 1. Provide ancillary data for PREFIRE retrieval algorithm development and validation, specifically
 through retrieved vertical profiles of temperature and water from the AERI. The timeseries as
 retrieved by an AERI will also provide additional contextualization for PREFIRE overpasses,
 characterizing the observed nature of the atmosphere from a credible instrument. From initial
 estimates, one PREFIRE cubesat overpasses Scott Base every two to three days, with a total of about
 14 overpasses per month. This revisit time is certainly expected to capture the variable synoptic
 weather conditions observed at Scott Base, especially variations in temperature and moisture that may
 be impactful to the local cryosphere.
 2. Investigate the radiative closure of the atmospheric spectrally resolved thermal emission in Antarctica
 using PREFIRE top-of-the-atmosphere measurements in conjunction with coincident AERI
 ground-based measurements. With measurements of the spectrally resolved atmospheric thermal
 emission downwelling towards the surface and upwelling at the top of the atmosphere, it may be
 possible to, for the first time, calculate the closure of atmospheric spectral infrared radiation. Along
 with the mean state, the combined measurements from the proposed AERI and PREFIRE can assess
 this closure under variable atmospheric states, across seasons.
 3. Longitudinal study of changes in Antarctica surface radiative forcing due to increasing greenhouse
 gasses. We plan to use observations collected in this campaign, along with measurements made as
 part of the ARM AWARE campaign in 2016 and other past campaigns, to quantify the direct change
 in surface radiative forcing due to changing greenhouse gas concentrations.

 1.2 Summary to Date
 The campaign plan was to initially deploy the AERI instrument at NIWA’s atmospheric research
 laboratory located in Lauder, Central Otago, New Zealand over the period October 15, 2024-February 1,
 2025. Then the AERI will be deployed at Scott Base, Ross Island, Antarctica for 1 year (February 1,
 2025-February 1, 2026).

 The AERI required unplanned servicing by the manufacturer, delaying deployment to Lauder until
 August 2025 (10 months later than planned). This made deployment to Scott Base not possible until
 October 2025 at the earliest. After a one-month deployment at Lauder the AERI was freighted to Scott
 Base in September 2025 and installed in the first week of November 2025; nine months later than
 planned.

 The compressed time at Lauder still produced valuable data and training experience on the instrument.
 We still would like/need to collect at least a full season’s worth (one year) of data at Scott Base (allowing
 measurement throughout the polar night) and have secured logistical support from Antarctica New
 Zealand to operate the AERI out to February 2027 (giving ~15 months of data).

 We are just now (start of 2026) starting analysis of the data.

2.0 Results
 We are still in the preliminary stage of data collection and analysis. The focus is still on instrument
 operation and data archiving along with collection and processing of ancillary data sets needed for
 analysis (collocated sonde flights, global navigation satellite system (GNSS) precipitable water vapor
 (PWV), ceilometer, and surface temperature data sets).

 The AERI installation and observations made at Lauder, New Zealand (August-September 2025) were
 successful. Data is archived at the University of Wisconsin, Madison.
