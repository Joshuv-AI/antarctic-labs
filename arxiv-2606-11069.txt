Adjacent comparison bounds and extremal sets for Ruzsa numbers
Yuchen Ding, Huixi Li, Junfeng Li, Wei Niu and Xiamiao Zhao

Abstract
. Let m m be a positive integer and ℤ m \mathbb{Z}_{m} the residue class ring modulo m m . The Ruzsa number R m R_{m} is defined to be the least integer r r such that there is a subset 𝒜 \mathcal{A} of ℤ m \mathbb{Z}_{m} satisfying 1 ≤ σ 𝒜 ​ ( n ) ≤ r 1\leq\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)\leq r for any n ∈ ℤ m n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m} , where σ 𝒜 ​ ( n ) = # ⁡ { ( a , a ′ ) ∈ 𝒜 2 : a + a ′ ≡ n ( mod m ) } . \sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)=\#\big\{(a,a^{\prime})\in\mathcal{A}^{2}:a+a^{\prime}\equiv n\pmod{m}\big\}. Motivated by a 2024 conjecture of Ding and Zhao, we prove | R m + 1 − R m | ≤ 144 . |R_{m+1}-R_{m}|\leq 144. Let 𝒜 \mathcal{A} be a subset of ℤ m \mathbb{Z}_{m} satisfying 1 ≤ σ 𝒜 ​ ( n ) ≤ R m 1\leq\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)\leq R_{m} for any n ∈ ℤ m n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m} . We also give nontrivial bounds for the size of 𝒜 \mathcal{A} . Additionally, we provide exact values of R m R_{m} for all m ≤ 100 m\leq 100 , which substantially extends the table of values given by Sándor and Yang in 2017. Finally, we pose several related problems and prove some partial results.

1.
Introduction

Let
m
be a positive integer and
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
the residue class ring modulo
m
. For any

n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
and
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{m}
, let

\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)=\#\big\{(a,a^{\prime})\in\mathcal{A}^{2}:a+a^{\prime}\equiv n\pmod{m}\big\}.

The Ruzsa number
R_{m}
is defined to be the least integer
r
such that there is a subset
\mathcal{A}
of

\mathbb{Z}_{m}
satisfying

1\leq\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)\leq r~(\forall~n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}).

Motivated by the Erdős-Turán conjecture on additive bases
[
ET41
]
, Ruzsa
[
Ru90
]
proved that there is an absolute constant
C>0
such that
R_{m}\leq C
for all positive integers
m
. Later, Tang and Chen
[
TC06
]
showed that
R_{m}\leq 768
provided that
m
is sufficiently large. In a subsequent article
[
TC07
]
, Tang and Chen proved that
R_{m}\leq 5120
for all positive integers
m
. In
[
Ch08
]
Chen improved the upper bound, and proved
R_{m}\leq 288
for all positive integers
m
.
The proof of Chen’s bound
R_{m}\leq 288
consists of two ingredients. First, using Ruzsa’s constructions with refinements he proved
R_{2p^{2}}\leq 48
, where
p
is a prime number. Second, he established the following comparison lemma.

Proposition 1.1

(Chen’s comparison lemma)
.

Let
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
be two positive integers with
m_{1}<m_{2}<\frac{3}{2}m_{1}
. Then we have
R_{m_{1}}\leq 6R_{m_{2}}
.

Using
R_{2p^{2}}\leq 48
and his comparison lemma, Chen obtained the bound
R_{m}\leq 288
by the explicit prime number theorem. Motivated by Chen’s comparison lemma, recently Ding and Zhao
[
DZ24
]
proved the following refined comparison lemma.

Proposition 1.2

(Ding-Zhao’s comparison lemma)
.

Let
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
be two positive integers with
\frac{3}{2}m_{1}\leq m_{2}<2m_{1}
. Then we have
R_{m_{2}}\leq 4R_{m_{1}}
.

As a corollary, Ding and Zhao
[
DZ24
]
further improved Chen’s bound which asserts

\displaystyle R_{m}\leq 192

(1.1)

for all
m
. On the other hand, Chen
[
Ch08
]
pointed out
R_{m}\geq 3
if
m\neq 1,2,3
. Interestingly, Sándor and Yang
[
SY17
]
claimed the lower bound
R_{m}\geq 6
for
m\geq 36
. In addition, Sándor and Yang gave a list of values of
R_{m}
up to
m\leq 35
. Based on their numerical values, Ding and Zhao
[
DZ24
]
made the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.1

(Ding-Zhao)
.

For any positive integer
m
, we have
\big|R_{m+1}-R_{m}\big|\leq 1
.

However, small inaccuracies crept into the calculations of Sándor and Yang. It can be computed that
R_{36}=6
while
R_{37}=4
, thus giving a counterexample to
Conjecture
1.1
for
m=36
. Nevertheless, the lower bound of Sándor and Yang has no fundamental issues.
We compute the exact values of
R_{m}
up to
36\leq m\leq 100
, and find that
R_{37}=4
and
R_{39}=5
are the only two exceptions. Our computation is certificate-based. For
m\leq 45
, lower bounds are verified by finite certificate verification; for
m>45
, Proposition
2.3

gives
R_{m}\geq 6
, while the displayed sets give
R_{m}\leq 6
.
We will give an extended list of the values of
R_{m}
in the next section, and furthermore formulate the corrected eventual picture. Based on the numerical calculations, we are confident of the following revised conjecture.

Conjecture 1.2

(Ding-Zhao, revised)
.

For all sufficiently large
m
, we have

\big|R_{m+1}-R_{m}\big|\leq 1.

Probably, Conjecture
1.1
is true except for
m=36
and
37
.
One notes easily that the algebraic structures of
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{m+1}
could be significantly different due to the differences between the number of prime factors of
m
and
m+1
.
Conjecture
1.2
if true would be a little surprising since it provides an alternative example where combinatorial randomness eventually defeats the rigidity of algebraic structure.
At present, we are not able to attack Conjecture
1.2
. Clearly, the lower bound
R_{m}\geq 6
and the upper bound
R_{m}\leq 192
indicate that for all sufficiently large
m
we have

\big|R_{m+1}-R_{m}\big|\leq 192-6=186.

In this note, we give an improvement of the above somewhat trivial bound.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any positive integer
m
we have
\big|R_{m+1}-R_{m}\big|\leq 144
.

For any positive integer
m
, let
\mathscr{A}_{m}
be the set of all subsets
\mathcal{A}\subset[0,m-1]
such that for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
we have

1\leq\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)\leq R_{m}.

In
[
DZ24
, Conjecture 3.4]
, the authors asked for estimates for the cardinalities of the sets
\mathcal{A}\in\mathscr{A}_{m}
. Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathscr{A}_{m}
be a given set. Note that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}|^{2}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n).

(1.2)

Then, from (
1.2
) and Sándor-Yang
[
SY17
, Lemmas 2.2]
we know

\Big(\sqrt{2m}-\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{2}\leq|\mathcal{A}|^{2}\leq mR_{m}.

Our second theorem gives nontrivial bounds on the size of
|\mathcal{A}|
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For any fixed
\varepsilon>0
, let
m
be sufficiently large and
\mathcal{A}\in\mathscr{A}_{m}
a given set. Then we have

\displaystyle\Bigg(\left\lfloor\frac{R_{m}}{2}\right\rfloor+\frac{1}{2}-

\displaystyle\sqrt{\left\lfloor\frac{R_{m}}{2}\right\rfloor^{2}-3\left\lfloor\frac{R_{m}}{2}\right\rfloor+\frac{1}{4}}-\varepsilon\Bigg)m

\displaystyle\leq|\mathcal{A}|^{2}\leq\Bigg(\left\lfloor\frac{R_{m}}{2}\right\rfloor+\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{\left\lfloor\frac{R_{m}}{2}\right\rfloor^{2}-3\left\lfloor\frac{R_{m}}{2}\right\rfloor+\frac{1}{4}}+\varepsilon\Bigg)m.

Since
m
is sufficiently large,
R_{m}\geq 6
by Proposition
2.3
. So, we have

\left\lfloor\frac{R_{m}}{2}\right\rfloor^{2}-3\left\lfloor\frac{R_{m}}{2}\right\rfloor+\frac{1}{4}>0.

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
m
be sufficiently large and
\mathcal{A}\in\mathscr{A}_{m}
a given set. Then we have

|\mathcal{A}|\leq\sqrt{191m}.

Proof.

Since
R_{m}\leq 192
, we will get the upper bound by making
\varepsilon
sufficiently small in Theorem
1.2
.
∎

Corollary 1.2
.

For any fixed
\varepsilon>0
, let
m
be sufficiently large and
\mathcal{A}\in\mathscr{A}_{m}
a given set. If
R_{m}=6
, then

\big(\sqrt{3}-\varepsilon\big)\sqrt{m}\leq|\mathcal{A}|\leq(2+\varepsilon)\sqrt{m}.

We will see later that the assumption
R_{m}=6
in Corollary
1.2
is natural due
to Conjecture
2.3
.

2.
Extended values of
R_{m}

2.1.
The lower bound of Sándor and Yang revisited

Sándor and Yang
[
SY17
]
used the mean-square lower bound of
Lev and Sárközy
[
LS11
]
together with several finite
reductions to prove that the case
R_{m}\leq 5
cannot persist for large

m
. Their argument actually gave the following proposition.

Proposition 2.3

(Sándor-Yang)
.

If
m>45
, then
R_{m}\geq 6
.

Before the proof of Proposition
2.3
, we first introduce the inequality of Lev and Sárközy
[
LS11
]
.

Lemma 2.1

(Lev-Sárközy)
.

If
A
is a subset of a finite
nontrivial abelian group
G
, then for any real number
c
we have

\sum_{g\in G}\big(r_{A}(g)-c\big)^{2}\geq\frac{1}{|G|-1}\left(\frac{|A|^{4}}{|G|}-2|A|^{3}+|A|^{2}|G|\right),

where
r_{A}(g)=\#\big\{(a_{1},a_{2})\in A^{2}:g=a_{1}+a_{2}\big\}
.

Proof of Proposition
2.3
.

We sketch the proof of Sándor and Yang
[
SY17
, Theorem 1.2]
.
Suppose, for contradiction, that
R_{m}\leq 5
. Then there is a set

\mathcal{A}\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}_{m}
,
\left|\mathcal{A}\right|=k
, such that

1\leq\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)\leq 5

for any
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{m}
.
Lev and Sárközy’s lower bound says that for every real
c
,

\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}\big(\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)-c\big)^{2}\geq\frac{1}{m-1}\left(\frac{k^{4}}{m}-2k^{3}+k^{2}m\right).

Taking
c=3
, and then rearranging the right-hand side above gives

\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}\big(\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)-3\big)^{2}\geq\frac{k^{2}(m-k)^{2}}{m(m-1)}.

(2.1)

On the other hand, if
\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)
is odd, then one of the representations
of
n
is diagonal, say
n=2a
with
a\in\mathcal{A}
, since the
off-diagonal pairs occur in symmetric pairs. Since
|\mathcal{A}|=k
, the number of
residue classes with odd
\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)
is at most
k
. Recall that

1\leq\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)\leq 5
. It then follows that

\big(\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)-3\big)^{2}\leq\begin{cases}4,&\text{if }\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)\text{ is odd},\\
1,&\text{if }\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)\text{ is even}.\end{cases}

Therefore, by assuming that there are
\ell\leq k
odd
\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)
we get

\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}\big(\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)-3\big)^{2}\leq 4\ell+(m-\ell)=m+3\ell\leq m+3k.

(2.2)

Combining (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) yields the necessary
condition

k^{2}(m-k)^{2}\leq(m+3k)m(m-1).

(2.3)

Additionally, from Sándor and Yang
[
SY17
, Lemmas 2.2 and 2.3]
we also have the following elementary bounds

\sqrt{2m}-\frac{1}{2}<k\leq\sqrt{5m}.

(2.4)

For
m>91
, the inequalities (
2.3
) and
(
2.4
) are incompatible. This already proves

R_{m}\geq 6
for
m>91
.

For
1\leq i\leq 5
, let

k_{i}=\#\big\{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{m}:\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)=i\big\}.

Since these
k_{i}
’s partition the
m
residue classes, any
counterexample must satisfy

\displaystyle k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}+k_{4}+k_{5}

\displaystyle=m,

(2.5)

\displaystyle k_{1}+2k_{2}+3k_{3}+4k_{4}+5k_{5}

\displaystyle=k^{2},

(2.6)

\displaystyle k_{1}+k_{3}+k_{5}

\displaystyle\leq k,

(2.7)

with equality in (
2.7
) when
m
is odd. Taking

c=k^{2}/m
in the Lev–Sárközy bound gives the additional
necessary condition

\sum_{i=1}^{5}\left(i-\frac{k^{2}}{m}\right)^{2}k_{i}\geq\frac{k^{2}(m-k)^{2}}{m(m-1)}.

(2.8)

Checking the integer solutions of (
2.4
),
(
2.3
), and (
2.5
)–(
2.8
)
already reduces the possible pairs
(m,k)
to a range whose largest
member is
(50,12)
.

The decisive improvement comes from the additive energy identity

\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)^{2}=\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}\sigma_{\mathcal{A},-\mathcal{A}}(n)^{2}.

Here

\sigma_{\mathcal{A},-\mathcal{A}}(t)=\#\big\{(a,a^{\prime})\in\mathcal{A}^{2}:a-a^{\prime}\equiv t\pmod{m}\big\}.

Note that

\sigma_{\mathcal{A},-\mathcal{A}}(0)=k
and

\sum_{t=1}^{m-1}\sigma_{\mathcal{A},-\mathcal{A}}(t)=k^{2}-k.

We write

k^{2}-k=q(m-1)+r,\qquad 0\leq r<m-1,

where

q=\left\lfloor(k^{2}-k)/(m-1)\right\rfloor

The sum of squares

\sum_{t=1}^{m-1}\sigma_{\mathcal{A},-\mathcal{A}}(t)^{2}

is minimized when the nonzero
difference counts are as equal as possible. Hence

\sum_{t=1}^{m-1}\sigma_{\mathcal{A},-\mathcal{A}}(t)^{2}\geq q^{2}(m-1)+(2q+1)r.

Consequently every counterexample must satisfy the sharper inequality

\sum_{i=1}^{5}\left(i-\frac{k^{2}}{m}\right)^{2}k_{i}\geq k^{2}+q^{2}(m-1)+(2q+1)r-\frac{k^{4}}{m}.

(2.9)

A direct finite check of the integer conditions
(
2.4
), (
2.3
),
(
2.5
)–(
2.7
), and (
2.9
)
forces
m\leq 45
.
Therefore no counterexample can
exist for
m>45
, and hence
R_{m}\geq 6
for
m>45
.
∎

2.2.
Extended values of
R_{m}

The computation is certificate-based. For a proposed row

(m,\mathcal{A},R)
, we recompute all ordered sums
a+a^{\prime}\pmod{m}
, form the vector

\bigl(\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(0),\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(1),\ldots,\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(m-1)\bigr),

and check

\min_{n}\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)\geq 1,\qquad\max_{n}\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)\leq R.

For
m>45
, Proposition
2.3
gives
R_{m}\geq 6
. Hence a
certificate satisfying
\max_{n}\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)\leq 6
proves
R_{m}=6
.
For
2\leq m\leq 45
, exactness is verified separately: after checking the displayed
upper certificate for
R_{m}
, the verification searches for a certificate with bound

R_{m}-1
and proves that none exists.

The computation was carried out in two stages. First, for
2\leq m\leq 45
, we
verified the displayed upper certificates and then proved that no certificate exists
with bound
R_{m}-1
. This exact verification took about five minutes. Second, for
larger
m
, we searched for certificates with maximal representation count at most

6
, and then checked each certificate by recomputing all ordered sums.

The computations were carried out on the server
lthpc-G2232-G2V2
, equipped with two Intel Xeon Platinum 8360Y processors at 2.40 GHz (72 physical cores, 144 hardware threads) and 1.0 TiB of RAM.
The programs were compiled with
g++ 11.4.0
using

-O3 -std=c++17 -pthread
, and run under Ubuntu 22.04 with Linux kernel 6.8.0-40-generic. No GPU acceleration was used.

The full search for the table took about
69
hours in total, including unsuccessful
first passes and later deeper searches. For each tested cardinality
k
, we used
three increasing budgets: first
5000
attempts, allowing at most
10^{5}
changes in
each attempt; then
50000
attempts, allowing at most
3\cdot 10^{5}
changes in each
attempt; and finally
500000
attempts, allowing at most
10^{6}
changes in each
attempt. The first pass did not find certificates for
m=94,98,100
; the
certificates displayed below for these three moduli were found after deeper searches.
The displayed sets are used only as certificates for the claimed upper bounds. In
particular, for a fixed
m
, the table records one successful basis; it is not meant
to solve the separate optimization problem of minimizing
\left|\mathcal{A}\right|
.

Procedure: certificate search and exact verification.

Input:
a modulus
m
, a target bound
R
for exact verification when

m\leq 45
, the target bound
6
for larger moduli, a range of admissible set sizes,
and optional seed sets from nearby moduli.

(1)

Normalize candidate sets by translation so that
0\in\mathcal{A}
, and compute
the full ordered representation vector
\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}
.

(2)

If
m\leq 45
, first verify the displayed upper certificate for
R_{m}
. Then try
to build a set with all representation numbers at most
R_{m}-1
. The construction
is exhaustive: at each step one chooses an uncovered residue class and adds a pair
which could represent it. A partial construction is discarded as soon as some
representation number becomes larger than
R_{m}-1
, or when the elementary size
bound
\lfloor\sqrt{(R_{m}-1)m}\rfloor
is exceeded. If all possibilities are
discarded, no such set exists, and the displayed value of
R_{m}
is exact.

(3)

If
m>45
, use Proposition
2.3
for the lower bound

R_{m}\geq 6
, and search for a certificate with
\max_{n}\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)\leq 6
.
The search tries set sizes in the range suggested by the elementary constraints,
using successful sets for nearby moduli as initial candidates.

(4)

For a candidate
\mathcal{A}
, measure its defect by first counting uncovered
residue classes and then counting the excess over the bound
6
. Thus covering all
residue classes is treated as the first priority, and reducing large representation
numbers is the second priority.

(5)

Improve the candidate by replacing one element
a\in\mathcal{A}
with one
value
b\notin\mathcal{A}
. The choice is biased toward removing elements that
contribute to large representation numbers and adding elements that cover missing
residue classes. Most changes are accepted only when they improve the candidate, but
early in the search some non-improving changes are also allowed.

(6)

Repeat until either exact nonexistence has been proved, a valid certificate has
been found, or the chosen budget has been exhausted. Every displayed
certificate is finally verified by recomputing all ordered sums.

Lemma 3.2
.

For any positive integer
m
we have both

R_{m}\leq 4R_{m+1}\quad\text{and}\quad R_{m+1}\leq 4R_{m}.

Proof.

We first prove the assertion
R_{m+1}\leq 4R_{m}
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{A}
is a subset of
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
such that for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
we have

1\leq\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)\leq R_{m}.

Without loss of generality, we may assume
0\leq a\leq m-1
for any
a\in\mathcal{A}
. Define

\mathcal{A}_{1}=\big\{a\in\mathcal{A}:a\geq\left\lfloor m/2\right\rfloor\big\}

and

\mathcal{A}_{+}=\mathcal{A}_{1}+1:=\{a+1:a\in\mathcal{A}_{1}\}.

Now, we let

\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{A}_{+},

and regard
\mathcal{B}
as a subset of
\mathbb{Z}_{m+1}
.

First, we show that
\mathcal{B}
is an additive basis of
\mathbb{Z}_{m+1}
, i.e.,
\sigma_{\mathcal{B}}(n)\geq 1
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m+1}
. For any
0\leq n\leq m-1
, there are two elements
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}
such that

n\equiv a+a^{\prime}\pmod{m},

which implies
n=a+a^{\prime}
or
n=a+a^{\prime}-m
since
0\leq a+a^{\prime}\leq 2m-2
. If
n=a+a^{\prime}
, then

n\equiv a+a^{\prime}\pmod{m+1}.

Now, suppose that
n=a+a^{\prime}-m
. Then
a+a^{\prime}\geq m
, and hence at least one of
a
and
a^{\prime}
is
\geq\left\lfloor m/2\right\rfloor
. We may assume without loss of generality that
a\geq\left\lfloor m/2\right\rfloor
. Then we have

n=(a+1)+a^{\prime}-(m+1)\equiv(a+1)+a^{\prime}\pmod{m+1},

which means that
\sigma_{\mathcal{B}}(n)\geq 1
for any
0\leq n\leq m-1
. It remains to show
\sigma_{\mathcal{B}}(m)\geq 1
. Note that
there are two elements
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
of
\mathcal{A}
such that

m-1\equiv a_{1}+a_{2}\pmod{m}.

Since
0\leq a_{1}+a_{2}\leq 2m-2
, we must have
m-1=a_{1}+a_{2}
. It follows that at least one of
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
is
\geq\left\lfloor m/2\right\rfloor
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
a_{1}\geq\left\lfloor m/2\right\rfloor
. Hence,

m=(a_{1}+1)+a_{2}\equiv(a_{1}+1)+a_{2}\pmod{m+1},

completing the proof of
\sigma_{\mathcal{B}}(n)\geq 1
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m+1}
.

Next, we will prove
\sigma_{\mathcal{B}}(n)\leq 4R_{m}
for any
0\leq n\leq m
. Let

\mathcal{A}_{2}=\mathcal{A}\setminus\mathcal{A}_{1}=\big\{a\in\mathcal{A}:a<\left\lfloor m/2\right\rfloor\big\}.

By this symbol, we have

\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{A}_{1}\cup\mathcal{A}_{2}\cup\mathcal{A}_{+}.

For any subsets
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{2}
of integers, define

f_{\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{2}}(n)=\#\big\{(s_{1},s_{2})\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}:n=s_{1}+s_{2}\big\}.

We first consider the range
0\leq n\leq m-1
. Suppose that

\displaystyle n\equiv b_{1}+b_{2}\pmod{m+1},

where
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
are two elements of
\mathcal{B}
.
Then
n=b_{1}+b_{2}
or
n=b_{1}+b_{2}-(m+1)
since
0\leq b_{1}+b_{2}\leq 2m
. In the case
n=b_{1}+b_{2}
we have

\displaystyle\#\big\{(b_{1},b_{2})

\displaystyle\in\mathcal{B}^{2}:n=b_{1}+b_{2}\big\}\leq f_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1}}(n)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}}(n)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathcal{A}_{1}}(n)

\displaystyle+f_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{+}}(n)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{+},\mathcal{A}_{1}}(n)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathcal{A}_{2}}(n)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathcal{A}_{+}}(n)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{+},\mathcal{A}_{2}}(n)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{+},\mathcal{A}_{+}}(n).

Note that

f_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{+}}(n)=f_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1}}(n-1)=0,\quad f_{\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathcal{A}_{+}}(n)=f_{\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathcal{A}_{1}}(n-1),

f_{\mathcal{A}_{+},\mathcal{A}_{1}}(n)=f_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1}}(n-1)=0,\quad f_{\mathcal{A}_{+},\mathcal{A}_{2}}(n)=f_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}}(n-1),

and

f_{\mathcal{A}_{+},\mathcal{A}_{+}}(n)=f_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1}}(n-2)=0

since
a+a^{\prime}\geq 2\left\lfloor m/2\right\rfloor\geq m-1>n-1
for any
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
,
from which we obtain

\displaystyle\#\big\{(b_{1},b_{2})\in\mathcal{B}^{2}

\displaystyle:n=b_{1}+b_{2}\big\}\leq f_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1}}(n)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}}(n)

\displaystyle+f_{\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathcal{A}_{1}}(n)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathcal{A}_{2}}(n)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathcal{A}_{1}}(n-1)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}}(n-1).

(3.1)

Similarly, in the case
n=b_{1}+b_{2}-(m+1)
we have

\displaystyle\#\big\{(b_{1},b_{2}

\displaystyle)\in\mathcal{B}^{2}:n+(m+1)=b_{1}+b_{2}\big\}

\displaystyle\leq f_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1}}(n+1+m)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}}(n+1+m)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathcal{A}_{1}}(n+1+m)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+f_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{+}}(n+1+m)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{+},\mathcal{A}_{1}}(n+1+m)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathcal{A}_{2}}(n+1+m)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+f_{\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathcal{A}_{+}}(n+1+m)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{+},\mathcal{A}_{2}}(n+1+m)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{+},\mathcal{A}_{+}}(n+1+m)

\displaystyle=f_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1}}(n+1+m)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}}(n+1+m)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathcal{A}_{1}}(n+m+1)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+2f_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1}}(n+m)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathcal{A}_{2}}(n+1+m)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+f_{\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathcal{A}_{1}}(n+m)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}}(n+m)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1}}(n-1+m).

(3.2)

Hence, we conclude from (
3
) and (
3
),

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathcal{B}}(n)

\displaystyle\leq f_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}}(n)+2f_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}}(n+m)+f_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}}(n-1)+f_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}}(n-1+m)+f_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}}(n+1+m)

\displaystyle\leq 2\#\big\{(a_{1},a_{2})\in\mathcal{A}^{2}:n\equiv a_{1}+a_{2}\pmod{m}\big\}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\#\big\{(a_{1},a_{2})\in\mathcal{A}^{2}:n-1\equiv a_{1}+a_{2}\pmod{m}\big\}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\#\big\{(a_{1},a_{2})\in\mathcal{A}^{2}:n+1\equiv a_{1}+a_{2}\pmod{m}\big\}

\displaystyle\leq 4R_{m}.

(3.3)

It remains to estimate
\sigma_{\mathcal{B}}(m)
. Suppose that

m\equiv b_{1}+b_{2}\pmod{m+1},

where
b_{1},b_{2}\in\mathcal{B}
. Since
0\leq b_{1}+b_{2}\leq 2m
, we must have
b_{1}+b_{2}=m
. Each
b_{i}
can be written in the form
b_{i}=a_{i}+\varepsilon_{i}
, where
a_{i}\in\mathcal{A}
and
\varepsilon_{i}\in\{0,1\}
; here
\varepsilon_{i}=1
can occur only when
a_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
. Counting all such choices gives an upper bound, and hence

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathcal{B}}(m)

\displaystyle\leq f_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}}(m)+2f_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}}(m-1)+f_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}}(m-2)

\displaystyle\leq\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(0)+2\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(-1)+\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(-2)

\displaystyle\leq 4R_{m}.

Therefore, we have
R_{m+1}\leq 4R_{m}
.

Proof of the assertion
R_{m}\leq 4R_{m+1}
.

The cases
m=1
and
2
are trivial. Now, we assume
m\geq 3
.
Suppose that
\mathcal{A}
is a subset of
\mathbb{Z}_{m+1}
such that for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m+1}
we have

1\leq\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)\leq R_{m+1}.

Without loss of generality, we may assume
0\leq a\leq m
for any
a\in\mathcal{A}
. Define

\mathcal{A}_{1}=\big\{a\in\mathcal{A}:a\geq m/2+1\big\}

and

\mathcal{A}_{-}=\mathcal{A}_{1}-1:=\{a-1:a\in\mathcal{A}_{1}\}.

Now, we let

\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{A}_{-},

and regard
\mathcal{B}
as a subset of
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
.

First, we show that
\mathcal{B}
is an additive basis of
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
, i.e.,
\sigma_{\mathcal{B}}(n)\geq 1
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
. We first consider
n=0
. Since
\mathcal{A}
is an additive basis of
\mathbb{Z}_{m+1}
, there exist
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}
such that

m\equiv a+a^{\prime}\pmod{m+1}.

Since
0\leq a+a^{\prime}\leq 2m
, we must have
a+a^{\prime}=m
. Hence
0\equiv a+a^{\prime}\pmod{m}
, and so
\sigma_{\mathcal{B}}(0)\geq 1
.

Now let
1\leq n\leq m-1
. There are two elements
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}
such that

n\equiv a+a^{\prime}\pmod{m+1},

which implies
n=a+a^{\prime}
or
n=a+a^{\prime}-(m+1)
since
0\leq a+a^{\prime}\leq 2m
. If
n=a+a^{\prime}
, then

n\equiv a+a^{\prime}\pmod{m}.

Now, suppose that
n=a+a^{\prime}-(m+1)
. Then
a+a^{\prime}=n+m+1\geq m+2
. If both
a
and
a^{\prime}
were
<m/2+1
, then, since
a
and
a^{\prime}
are integers, we would have
a+a^{\prime}\leq m+1
, a contradiction. Thus at least one of
a
and
a^{\prime}
is
\geq m/2+1
. We may assume without loss of generality that
a\geq m/2+1
. Then we have

n=(a-1)+a^{\prime}-m\equiv(a-1)+a^{\prime}\pmod{m},

which means that
\sigma_{\mathcal{B}}(n)\geq 1
for any
1\leq n\leq m-1
. Hence
\mathcal{B}
is an additive basis of
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
.

Next, we will prove
\sigma_{\mathcal{B}}(n)\leq 4R_{m+1}
for any
0\leq n\leq m-1
. Let

\mathcal{A}_{2}=\mathcal{A}\setminus\mathcal{A}_{1}=\big\{a\in\mathcal{A}:a<m/2+1\big\}.

By this symbol, we have

\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{A}_{1}\cup\mathcal{A}_{2}\cup\mathcal{A}_{-}.

It would be convenient to consider
\sigma_{\mathcal{B}}(0)
separately. Suppose that

b_{1}+b_{2}\equiv 0\pmod{m},

where
b_{1},b_{2}\in\mathcal{B}
. Then
b_{1}+b_{2}=0
or
m
or
2m
. The cases
b_{1}+b_{2}=0
and

contribute at most two pairs in total. If
b_{1}+b_{2}=m
, then the corresponding elements of
\mathcal{A}
have sum

,

, or
m+2
, according as neither, exactly one, or both of the two summands come from
\mathcal{A}_{-}
. Therefore

\sigma_{\mathcal{B}}(0)\leq\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(-1)+\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(0)+\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(1)+2\leq 3R_{m+1}+2\leq 4R_{m+1},

where we used
R_{m+1}\geq 2
.

In what follows, let
n
be an integer satisfying
1\leq n\leq m-1
. Suppose that

\displaystyle n\equiv b_{1}+b_{2}\pmod{m},

where
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
are two elements of
\mathcal{B}
.
Then
n=b_{1}+b_{2}
or
n=b_{1}+b_{2}-m
since
0\leq b_{1}+b_{2}\leq 2m
. In the case

we have

\displaystyle\#\big\{(b_{1},b_{2})

\displaystyle\in\mathcal{B}^{2}:n=b_{1}+b_{2}\big\}\leq f_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1}}(n)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}}(n)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathcal{A}_{1}}(n)

\displaystyle+f_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{-}}(n)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{-},\mathcal{A}_{1}}(n)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathcal{A}_{2}}(n)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathcal{A}_{-}}(n)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{-},\mathcal{A}_{2}}(n)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{-},\mathcal{A}_{-}}(n).

Note that

f_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{-}}(n)=f_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1}}(n+1)=0,\quad f_{\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathcal{A}_{-}}(n)=f_{\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathcal{A}_{1}}(n+1),

f_{\mathcal{A}_{-},\mathcal{A}_{1}}(n)=f_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1}}(n+1)=0,\quad f_{\mathcal{A}_{-},\mathcal{A}_{2}}(n)=f_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}}(n+1),

and

f_{\mathcal{A}_{-},\mathcal{A}_{-}}(n)=f_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1}}(n+2)=0

since
a+a^{\prime}\geq m+2>n+2
for any
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
,
from which we obtain

\displaystyle\#\big\{(b_{1},b_{2})\in\mathcal{B}^{2}

\displaystyle:n=b_{1}+b_{2}\big\}\leq f_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1}}(n)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}}(n)

\displaystyle+f_{\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathcal{A}_{1}}(n)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathcal{A}_{2}}(n)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathcal{A}_{1}}(n+1)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}}(n+1).

(3.4)

Similarly, in the case

we have

\displaystyle\#\big\{(b_{1},b_{2}

\displaystyle)\in\mathcal{B}^{2}:n+m=b_{1}+b_{2}\big\}

\displaystyle\leq f_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1}}(n+m)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}}(n+m)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathcal{A}_{1}}(n+m)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+f_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{-}}(n+m)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{-},\mathcal{A}_{1}}(n+m)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathcal{A}_{2}}(n+m)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+f_{\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathcal{A}_{-}}(n+m)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{-},\mathcal{A}_{2}}(n+m)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{-},\mathcal{A}_{-}}(n+m)

\displaystyle=f_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1}}(n+m)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}}(n+m)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathcal{A}_{1}}(n+m)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+2f_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1}}(n+m+1)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathcal{A}_{2}}(n+m)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+f_{\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathcal{A}_{1}}(n+m+1)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}}(n+m+1)+f_{\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{1}}(n+2+m).

(3.5)

Hence, we conclude from (

),

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathcal{B}}(n)

\displaystyle\leq f_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}}(n)+2f_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}}(n+1+m)+f_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}}(n+1)+f_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}}(n+2+m)+f_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}}(n+m)

\displaystyle\leq 2\#\big\{(a_{1},a_{2})\in\mathcal{A}^{2}:n\equiv a_{1}+a_{2}\pmod{m+1}\big\}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\#\big\{(a_{1},a_{2})\in\mathcal{A}^{2}:n+1\equiv a_{1}+a_{2}\pmod{m+1}\big\}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\#\big\{(a_{1},a_{2})\in\mathcal{A}^{2}:n-1\equiv a_{1}+a_{2}\pmod{m+1}\big\}

Therefore, we have

3.2
we have

\displaystyle\big|R_{m}-R_{m+1}\big|=R_{m+1}-R_{m}\leq R_{m+1}-\frac{1}{4}R_{m+1}=\frac{3}{4}R_{m+1}.

Using the upper bound (
1.1
), we have

\big|R_{m}-R_{m+1}\big|\leq\frac{3}{4}\cdot 192=144.

Case II.

R_{m+1}\leq R_{m}
. In this case, again by Lemma
3.2
we have

\displaystyle\big|R_{m}-R_{m+1}\big|=R_{m}-R_{m+1}\leq R_{m}-\frac{1}{4}R_{m}=\frac{3}{4}R_{m}\leq\frac{3}{4}\cdot 192=144,

completing the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Following the argument of Sándor and Yang
[
SY17
, Theorem 1.2]
, we will make use of the Lev-Sárközy inequality.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Suppose that
|\mathcal{A}|=k
. Let
\alpha=k^{2}/m
. Then for any
c
,

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}\big(\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)-c\big)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}\big(\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)-\alpha+\alpha-c\big)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}\big(\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)-\alpha\big)^{2}+2(\alpha-c)\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}\big(\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)-\alpha\big)+m(\alpha-c)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}\big(\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)-\alpha\big)^{2}+m(\alpha-c)^{2}

(4.1)

By Lemma
2.1
and (
4
) we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}\big(\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)-c\big)^{2}\geq\frac{k^{2}(m-k)^{2}}{m(m-1)}+m\left(\frac{k^{2}}{m}-c\right)^{2}.

(4.2)

On the other hand, let
s=\left\lfloor R_{m}/2\right\rfloor
and
c=s+1
. Since
R_{m}\leq 192
, we have
s\leq 96
; the error terms below are therefore uniform in
s
. Then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}\big(\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)-c\big)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}\\
2|\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)\end{subarray}}\big(\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)-c\big)^{2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}\\
2\nmid\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)\end{subarray}}\big(\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)-c\big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(s-1)^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}\\
2|\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)\end{subarray}}1+s^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}\\
2\nmid\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)\end{subarray}}1.

Note that the number of odd values of
\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)
is at most
k
since
|\mathcal{A}|=k
. Hence, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}\big(\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)-c\big)^{2}\leq ks^{2}+(m-k)(s-1)^{2}=m(s-1)^{2}+k(2s-1).

(4.3)

Combining (
4.2
) with (
4.3
) we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{k^{2}(m-k)^{2}}{m(m-1)}+m\left(\frac{k^{2}}{m}-c\right)^{2}\leq m(s-1)^{2}+k(2s-1).

Dividing both sides by
m
, we get

\displaystyle\alpha\cdot\frac{(m-k)^{2}}{m(m-1)}+\left(\alpha-c\right)^{2}\leq(s-1)^{2}+\frac{k}{m}(2s-1).

(4.4)

Since

k^{2}=|\mathcal{A}|^{2}\leq mR_{m}\leq 192m,

we know
k\leq\sqrt{192m}
, and hence

k/m\to 0
as
m\to\infty
.
Then

\frac{(m-k)^{2}}{m(m-1)}=1+o(1).

From (
4.4
) and above inequalities we obtain

\displaystyle\alpha+(\alpha-s-1)^{2}\leq(s-1)^{2}+o(1).

(4.5)

Expanding (
4.5
) and rearranging it gives

\displaystyle\alpha^{2}-(2s+1)\alpha+4s\leq o(1),

from which we deduce

\displaystyle s+\frac{1}{2}-\sqrt{\left(s+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}-4s}-\varepsilon\leq\alpha\leq s+\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{\left(s+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}-4s}+\varepsilon,

where
\varepsilon
is arbitrarily small providing that
m
is sufficiently large (in terms of
\varepsilon
).
∎

5.
Related problems and results

We conclude the paper by posing several unsolved problems involving Ruzsa’s numbers. Recall that
\mathscr{A}_{m}
denotes the set of all subsets
\mathcal{A}\subset[0,m-1]
such that for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
we have

1\leq\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)\leq R_{m}.

Problem 1
.

Let
m
be a sufficiently large integer. Is it true that there are two subsets
\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}\in\mathscr{A}_{m}
such that

|\mathcal{A}_{1}|\neq|\mathcal{A}_{2}|?

For
\mathcal{A}=\{a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{t}\}\subset[0,m-1]
, put
a_{t+1}=a_{1}+m
and define the largest cyclic gap of
\mathcal{A}
by

G(\mathcal{A})=\max_{1\leq i\leq t}\{a_{i+1}-a_{i}\}.

Problem 2
.

Is it true that

\lim_{m\to\infty}\sup_{\mathcal{A}\in\mathscr{A}_{m}}\frac{G(\mathcal{A})}{m}=0?

For Problem
2
, we point out the following result.

Proposition 5.4
.

We have

\limsup_{m\to\infty}\sup_{\mathcal{A}\in\mathscr{A}_{m}}\frac{G(\mathcal{A})}{m}\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Proof.

Assume the contrary, and suppose that

\limsup_{m\to\infty}\sup_{\mathcal{A}\in\mathscr{A}_{m}}\frac{G(\mathcal{A})}{m}=\eta>\frac{1}{2}.

Then there exist arbitrarily large
m
and some
\mathcal{A}=\{a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{t}\}\in\mathscr{A}_{m}
such that

G(\mathcal{A})>\eta^{*}m,

where

\eta^{*}=\frac{\eta+1/2}{2}>\frac{1}{2}.

Choose
j
such that
a_{j+1}-a_{j}=G(\mathcal{A})
, where
a_{t+1}=a_{1}+m
. Let

\mathcal{A}^{*}=\big\{b_{i}:b_{i}\equiv a_{i}-a_{j}\pmod{m},\ 0\leq b_{i}\leq m-1,\ 1\leq i\leq t\big\}.

Clearly,
\mathcal{A}^{*}\in\mathscr{A}_{m}
. By the choice of the largest gap,
\mathcal{A}^{*}\cap(0,G(\mathcal{A}))=\emptyset
; since
G(\mathcal{A})>\eta^{*}m
, it follows that
\mathcal{A}^{*}\cap(0,\eta^{*}m]=\emptyset
. We claim that

\sigma_{\mathcal{A}^{*}}(n)=0

for any integer
n
satisfying

0<n<\min\big\{(2\eta^{*}-1)m,\eta^{*}m\big\}.

Indeed, suppose that
n\equiv x+y\pmod{m}
with
x,y\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
. If one of
x
and
y
is
0
, then the other one would have to lie in
(0,\eta^{*}m)
, which is impossible. If both
x
and
y
are nonzero, then
x,y>\eta^{*}m
. Hence
x+y>2\eta^{*}m>m
, and after reduction modulo
m
the corresponding residue is larger than
(2\eta^{*}-1)m
. This again is impossible. Thus no such
n
is represented, contradicting
\mathcal{A}^{*}\in\mathscr{A}_{m}
. This proves the proposition.
∎

For even
m
and
\mathcal{A}\in\mathscr{A}_{m}
, put

p_{m}(\mathcal{A})=\frac{\#\big\{a\in\mathcal{A}:2\mid a\big\}}{|\mathcal{A}|}.

Problem 3
.

Is it true that

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\to\infty\\
2\mid m\end{subarray}}\sup_{\mathcal{A}\in\mathscr{A}_{m}}\left|p_{m}(\mathcal{A})-\frac{1}{2}\right|=0?

For Problem
3
, we can prove the following result.

Proposition 5.5
.

For every even
m
and every
\mathcal{A}\in\mathscr{A}_{m}
, let

E_{\mathcal{A}}=\big\{a\in\mathcal{A}:2\mid a\big\},\qquad O_{\mathcal{A}}=\big\{a\in\mathcal{A}:2\nmid a\big\}.

Then

\frac{m}{2}\leq|E_{\mathcal{A}}||O_{\mathcal{A}}|\leq\frac{\left\lfloor R_{m}/2\right\rfloor}{2}m.

Moreover, for any fixed
\varepsilon>0
, if
m
is sufficiently large and even, then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}-\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{2(R_{m}^{*}+\varepsilon)}}\leq p_{m}(\mathcal{A})\leq\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{2(R_{m}^{*}+\varepsilon)}},

where

R_{m}^{*}=\left\lfloor\frac{R_{m}}{2}\right\rfloor+\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{\left\lfloor\frac{R_{m}}{2}\right\rfloor^{2}-3\left\lfloor\frac{R_{m}}{2}\right\rfloor+\frac{1}{4}}.

Proof.

Since
m
is even, every odd residue class can only be written as the sum of an element of
E_{\mathcal{A}}
and an element of
O_{\mathcal{A}}
. Moreover, for any odd residue class, all representations occur in off-diagonal pairs. Since
\mathcal{A}\in\mathscr{A}_{m}
, each odd residue class has at least two representations: if
n=e+o
with
e\in E_{\mathcal{A}}
and
o\in O_{\mathcal{A}}
, then
(e,o)
and
(o,e)
are two distinct ordered representations. Hence

2|E_{\mathcal{A}}||O_{\mathcal{A}}|=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}\\
2\nmid n\end{subarray}}\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)\geq 2\cdot\frac{m}{2}=m,

which gives the lower bound

|E_{\mathcal{A}}||O_{\mathcal{A}}|\geq\frac{m}{2}.

On the other hand, each odd residue class has an even number of representations, and this number is at most
R_{m}
. Hence it is at most
2\left\lfloor R_{m}/2\right\rfloor
. Therefore

2|E_{\mathcal{A}}||O_{\mathcal{A}}|=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}\\
2\nmid n\end{subarray}}\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)\leq 2\left\lfloor\frac{R_{m}}{2}\right\rfloor\cdot\frac{m}{2},

which gives

|E_{\mathcal{A}}||O_{\mathcal{A}}|\leq\frac{\left\lfloor R_{m}/2\right\rfloor}{2}m.

Now suppose that
m
is sufficiently large and even, and put
p=p_{m}(\mathcal{A})=|E_{\mathcal{A}}|/|\mathcal{A}|
. Then
|O_{\mathcal{A}}|=(1-p)|\mathcal{A}|
. From the lower bound above we obtain

\displaystyle p(1-p)|\mathcal{A}|^{2}\geq\frac{m}{2}.

(5.1)

By Theorem
1.2
we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}|^{2}\leq\big(R_{m}^{*}+\varepsilon\big)m,

(5.2)

provided that
m
is large enough in terms of
\varepsilon
. Combining (
5.1
) with (
5.2
) gives

p(1-p)\geq\frac{1}{2(R_{m}^{*}+\varepsilon)}.

Equivalently,

p^{2}-p+\frac{1}{2(R_{m}^{*}+\varepsilon)}\leq 0,

from which the desired inequality follows.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

Suppose that
R_{m}=6
for all sufficiently large
m
. Then

\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\to\infty\\
2\mid m\end{subarray}}\sup_{\mathcal{A}\in\mathscr{A}_{m}}\left|p_{m}(\mathcal{A})-\frac{1}{2}\right|\leq\sqrt{\frac{1}{8}}.

Proof.

If
R_{m}=6
, then

R_{m}^{*}=3+\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{9-9+\frac{1}{4}}=4.

The result follows from Proposition
5.5
by letting
\varepsilon\to 0
.
∎

Suppose
\mathcal{A}\in\mathscr{A}_{m}
. Let
L_{\mathcal{A}}
denote the largest integer
\ell
for which there exist
x,d\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
such that

x,\ x+d,\ \ldots,\ x+(\ell-1)d

are distinct elements of
\mathcal{A}
. Let

L_{m}=\max_{\mathcal{A}\in\mathscr{A}_{m}}L_{\mathcal{A}}.

Assuming Conjecture
2.3
, we have

L_{m}\leq 6
for
m\geq 40
. Generally,
L_{m}\leq R_{m}
for any
m
.
In fact, if

x,\ x+d,\ \cdots,\ x+(\ell-1)d\in\mathcal{A}

are distinct, then the
\ell
ordered pairs

\big(x+id,\ x+(\ell-1-i)d\big),\qquad 0\leq i\leq\ell-1,

all give representations of the same residue class
2x+(\ell-1)d
modulo
m
. Thus

\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}\big(2x+(\ell-1)d\big)\geq\ell,

which means
\ell\leq R_{m}
.
Naturally, determining the values of
L_{m}
would be another open question. It may also be useful to record
L_{\mathcal{A}}
for the certificates in the table above. This leads to the following natural problem.

Problem 4
.

Is it true that
L_{m}=R_{m}
for infinitely many
m
?

Problem 5
.

Does
\log|\mathscr{A}_{m}|
admit an asymptotic formula?
Is it true that

|\mathscr{A}_{m}|\leq|\mathscr{A}_{m+1}|

for all sufficiently large
m
?

As a partial result toward the first part of Problem
5
, we prove the following.

Proposition 5.6
.

For all sufficiently large integers
m
, we have

\frac{1}{2}\log m-\frac{1}{2}\log 191\leq\log|\mathscr{A}_{m}|\leq\sqrt{191m}\log\!\left(e\sqrt{\frac{m}{191}}\right)+O(\log m).

In particular, we have

|\mathscr{A}_{m}|<|\mathscr{A}_{m+1}|

infinitely often.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathscr{A}_{m}
, and write
k=|\mathcal{A}|
.
By Corollary
1.1
we have

k\leq\sqrt{191m}

for all sufficiently large
m
. Therefore,

\big|\mathscr{A}_{m}\big|\leq\sum_{j\leq\sqrt{191m}}\binom{m}{j}.

Let
K=\lceil\sqrt{191m}\rceil
. Using the standard estimate

\sum_{j\leq K}\binom{m}{j}\leq\left(\frac{em}{K}\right)^{K}\quad(K\leq m/2),

we obtain

\log|\mathscr{A}_{m}|\leq K\log\!\left(\frac{em}{K}\right)\leq\sqrt{191m}\log\!\left(e\sqrt{\frac{m}{191}}\right)+O(\log m).

It remains to give a lower bound. Take any
\mathcal{A}\in\mathscr{A}_{m}
.
For every
h\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
, the translate

\mathcal{A}+h=\big\{a+h:a\in\mathcal{A}\big\}

also belongs to
\mathscr{A}_{m}
. Let

\operatorname{Stab}(\mathcal{A})=\{h\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}:\mathcal{A}+h=\mathcal{A}\}.

The number of distinct translates of
\mathcal{A}
is

\frac{m}{|\operatorname{Stab}(\mathcal{A})|}.

Moreover, for any fixed
a\in\mathcal{A}
we have

a+\operatorname{Stab}(\mathcal{A})\subset\mathcal{A},

and hence

|\operatorname{Stab}(\mathcal{A})|\leq|\mathcal{A}|=k.

Consequently,

|\mathscr{A}_{m}|\geq\frac{m}{k}.

Using
k\leq\sqrt{191m}
, we get

|\mathscr{A}_{m}|\geq\sqrt{\frac{m}{191}}.

Taking logarithms gives

\log|\mathscr{A}_{m}|\geq\frac{1}{2}\log m-\frac{1}{2}\log 191.

Finally, the lower bound shows that
|\mathscr{A}_{m}|
is unbounded. If

|\mathscr{A}_{m}|<|\mathscr{A}_{m+1}|

held only finitely often, then the sequence
|\mathscr{A}_{m}|
would be eventually non-increasing, and hence bounded, a contradiction. This proves the proposition.
∎

Remark 5.1
.

The same lower-bound argument can also be applied to affine images. Indeed, for every
u\in(\mathbb{Z}_{m})^{\times}
and
h\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
, the set

u\mathcal{A}+h=\{ua+h:a\in\mathcal{A}\}

also belongs to
\mathscr{A}_{m}
. If

\operatorname{AffStab}(\mathcal{A})=\{(u,h)\in(\mathbb{Z}_{m})^{\times}\times\mathbb{Z}_{m}:u\mathcal{A}+h=\mathcal{A}\},

then the affine orbit of
\mathcal{A}
gives

|\mathscr{A}_{m}|\geq\frac{m\varphi(m)}{|\operatorname{AffStab}(\mathcal{A})|}.

In particular, if there are a constant
C>0
and sets
\mathcal{A}_{m}\in\mathscr{A}_{m}
such that

|\operatorname{AffStab}(\mathcal{A}_{m})|\leq C|\mathcal{A}_{m}|^{2}

for infinitely many
m
, then Corollary
1.1
gives, along those
m
,

|\mathscr{A}_{m}|\geq\frac{\varphi(m)}{191C}.

This would substantially improve the general lower bound in Proposition
5.6
.

The following problem is in the same flavor as Problem
5
.

Problem 6
.

Is it true that for infinitely many
m
we have

\min_{\mathcal{A}\in\mathscr{A}_{m}}|\mathcal{A}|>\min_{\mathcal{A}\in\mathscr{A}_{m+1}}|\mathcal{A}|?

Problem 7
.

Let
m
be sufficiently large and
\mathcal{A}\in\mathscr{A}_{m}
. Is it true that for any
2\leq v\leq R_{m}
there exists some
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
such that
\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)=v
?

Remark 5.2
.

The endpoint
v=R_{m}
in Problem
7
is automatic. Indeed, if
\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)<R_{m}
for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
, then
\mathcal{A}
would satisfy

1\leq\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)\leq R_{m}-1\qquad(n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}),

contradicting the definition of
R_{m}
.

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathscr{A}_{m}
. If no residue class has exactly two representations, then

|\mathcal{A}|^{2}\geq 4m-3|\mathcal{A}|.

Consequently, for any sequence
m_{j}\to\infty
and
\mathcal{A}_{j}\in\mathscr{A}_{m_{j}}
, if

\limsup_{j\to\infty}\frac{|\mathcal{A}_{j}|^{2}}{m_{j}}<4,

then
2
is attained by
\sigma_{\mathcal{A}_{j}}
for all sufficiently large
j
.

Proof.

Let

\Omega=\{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}:\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)\ \text{is odd}\}.

The number of residues in
\Omega
is at most
|\mathcal{A}|
, since an odd value of
\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)
can occur only if
n=2a
for some
a\in\mathcal{A}
. If no residue class has exactly two representations, then every residue outside
\Omega
has an even positive number of representations different from
2
, and hence has at least
4
representations. Every residue in
\Omega
has at least one representation. Therefore

|\mathcal{A}|^{2}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}(n)\geq 4(m-|\Omega|)+|\Omega|=4m-3|\Omega|\geq 4m-3|\mathcal{A}|.

The final assertion follows from this inequality and Corollary
1.1
, since
|\mathcal{A}_{j}|/m_{j}\to 0
.
∎
