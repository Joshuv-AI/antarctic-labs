1. ABSTRACT

Implementation of High Pressure Die Casting (HPDC) Aluminum (Al) body structures for high volume
electrified vehicles (EV) to improve electric efficiency remains a key strategy within many original
equipment manufacturers (OEM)s. In addition to high strength for safety requirements, superior Self-
Piercing Riveting (SPR) performance is demanded for HPDC Al alloys to be compatible with high volume
SPR joining. In this work, it is proposed to extend and validate an existing Contractor finite element
multiscale macro-micro modeling approach to quantify the influence of the microstructure of HPDC alloys
on the fracture strain/displacement under 3-point bend and clinch testing. The success of this work will
allow to replace solution treatment stage with low energy consumption heat treatment (HT) processes, or
to design new non heat treatable (NHT) HPDC Al alloys to eliminate HT requirements. Ultimately, this
project will facilitate the application of HPDC Al alloys for super-large vehicle structures to significantly
reduce vehicle weight and thus improving energy efficiency.
The purpose of this project is to extend and validate an existing finite element code, which is based on the
Contractor developed macro-micro multi-scale modeling approach, to numerically simulate the three-point
bending and clinch test and study the influences of material microstructural characteristics and phase
properties on rivetability. The macro-micro modeling approach begins with a sample scale model to identify
the location mostly prone to failure and obtain the deformation history of the boundaries of that location.
The calculated deformation history will then be used to drive a microstructure-based macro-models where
the material microstructure and microscale properties are considered.
The project began with phase I effort of building a framework of macro-micro three point (3-P) bending
test and clinch test of Aural-5 HPDC Al alloy (Al7SiMgMn) in both as-cast and e-coated conditions. In the
phase I work which has just been completed, the macro-micro modeling frameworks have been constructed
for both the 3-P bending and clinch tests and applied to the Aural-5 HPDC alloy plates with 2.9mm
thickness in both conditions. The tensile tests of the alloys in both conditions were obtained and flow
behavior extracted were then used in the macro and micro model. The microstructures of the as-cast alloy
were characterized and used in the micro-model construction. The microstructures consist of primary Al-
dendrites and Al/Si eutectics. The e-coating process will not influence the microstructures but promotes the
nano-precipitates formation in the Al-dendrites. The 3-P bending tests of the alloys in both conditions were
performed to be used to calibrate the fracture strains of both the Al-dendrites and Al/Si eutectics in the
aural-5 alloys in both as-cast and e-coated conditions. The clinch test of only the e-coated aural-5 was
performed. The fracture strains determined from the 3-P bending tests and modeling for the e-coated alloys
is applied to the micro-model of the clinch test and simulation calculated critical displacement is within the
range of data of experimental measurements.
The phase II project effort involved the utilization of the knowledge learned in phase I to establish the
quantitative correlation between the microstructure characteristics and the riveting performance, which will
be further used to guide the optimization of HPDC Al alloy microstructure with different casting conditions
and e-coating process to achieve sufficient rivetability to join large thin-wall HPDC alloys.

4.1 Alloy selection and microstructure characterizations
The high pressure diecast (HPDC) Al alloy (Aural-5: Al7SiMgMn) is selected for this work, with Si as the
major alloy element with around 7 wt% concentration, Mg and Si as minor alloy elements with less than 1
wt% concentration. This alloy is highly castable and possess good combination of strength and ductility
and is the prime focus at Ford Motor company for use in super-large HPDC of car front-end, back-end and
underbody structures, providing potentials for significant weight and cost reduction for electric vehicles.
In this study, the HPDC aural-5 plate with a thickness of 2.85 mm is used, both in as-cast (F temper) and
e-coated condition (H81 temper). The microstructure characterization of the as-cast condition is mainly
performed through the optical microscope with the magnification of 200x and 500x. Five images were taken
for both magnifications for each of the two areas: near the edge or in the center. The typical recorded
microstructures are shown in Fig. 1. As shown in the figure, there are two major features of the
microstructures: (a) the primary Al dendrites and (b) the Al+Si eutectics. While small amount of porosity
is in the microstructure near the edge, there are more porosity shown in the center. The light-colored phase
is the primary Al dendrites and the grey region represent the Al+Si eutectics. The dark region represents
pores in the microstructure.
The automotive HPDC aluminum alloys will normally go through an e-coating process to deposit paint on
the surface before joining. The e-coating involves heating to temperatures which will only cause aging
which leads to fine precipitation from a supersaturated matrix phase, such as the Al-dendrite. The e-coating
process will generally not cause meso-scale microstructural change.

4.2.2 3-P bending test
The geometry of the 3-point bending test is as shown in Fig. 3. During the test, the sharp punch plate with
a tip radius of 0.2 mm is pushing upwards against the rollers which are kept stationary. The gap between
the two rollers is the sum of two times the aural-5 plate thickness and the punch blade thickness.
The load (F)-displacement (d) curve of two As-Cast alloy samples and two E-coated samples are as shown
in Fig. 4. As can be seen from this figure, the load for the E-coated alloy is about 25% higher than that of
as-cast alloy when d is the same. This is consistent with the stress-strain curves of the two alloys, where the
E-coated alloy show higher engineering stresses than the as-cast alloy, when the engineering strains are the
same. The higher strength of the E-coated alloy should be due to the fine precipitation during the E-coating
heating process.
The maximum loads of the alloys in the two conditions (as-cast or e-coated) are quite similar and are around
1400N. The critical displacements (dc) at which the load starts to drop, however, are different. The critical
displacements for the two as-cast samples are 4.49 and 4.81 mm, while those for the two e-coated alloy are
3.46 and 3.69 mm. In overall, the e-coated alloys show smaller critical displacement than those of the as-
cast alloys, indicating the former has less ductility consistent with the tensile test.

4.3 Hierarchical macro-micro model development of three-point bending and clinch test

 12

As described by Hu et al. [1, 2], the macro-micro modeling approach starts with a finite element
macroscopic sample scale model, e.g. the macro-model. After the macro-model simulations, the most
critical region will be identified which will be used for the micro-model.
4.3.1 The macro-models
The macro-models for 3-P bending and clinch test are two dimensional and they are shown exactly as in
Fig. 3 and Fig. 5.
As for the 3-P bending model, plane strain is assumed. The rollers and punch blade are represented by rigid
body surfaces. The aural-5 are modeled as isotropic elastic-plastic deformable body and is represented with
4-noded plane strain rectangular meshes with reduced integration (CPE4R). Fine meshes were utilized at
the center region with a mesh size of 0.02×0.02 mm2 (see Fig. 8(a))
For the clinch test, axis-symmetry is assumed. The holders and the punch are represented by rigid body
surfaces, while 4-noded axisymmetric mesh is used to mesh aural-5 plate. Fine meshed were used at the
region of punch edge with a mesh size the same as that of the 3-P bending model (see Fig. 8(b)).

 (a) (b)
Fig. 8. The fine mesh regions of the macro-models of 3-P bending (a) and clinch test (b).

The isotropic elastic properties of the plate include the Young’s modulus of 70GPa and Poisson’s ration
of 0.33, which are typical for Al alloys.
The tension test results are used to provide the flow behavior of aural-5 as-cast and e-coated alloy in the
macroscopic model. For this purpose, one of these curves each for the as-cast and e-coated alloy was used.
Firstly, the engineering stress-strains is converted into a true (logarithm) stress-strain curve [3]. Then the
elastic part of the strain is removed, and plastic part of the curve is used. It was found that a Holloman type
power-law fitting is suitable for both alloys,

The Holloman parameter k and n obtained for the as-cast alloy are 478.65 MPa and 0.2225, and those for
the e-coated alloy are 431.49 MPa and 0.1451, respectively. No damage criterion is used in the macro-
model.

 6. CONCLUSIONS

In this project we built, calibrated, and validated a hierarchical macro–micro finite-element framework that
links HPDC Aural-5 (Al7SiMgMn) microstructure to fracture response under three-point bending and
clinch loading—the two tests most relevant to SPR performance. Phase I established the end-to-end
workflow: macro models to localize failure-prone regions and extract deformation histories; micro-models
that explicitly represent Al-dendrites and Al/Si eutectics; and data-driven calibration of phase properties
and fracture strains using tensile data, inverse nano-indentation, RVEs, and 3-P bending. The calibrated e-
coat micro-model reproduced the measured clinch critical displacement and was further checked against
VDA bending, providing strong validation. Phase II leveraged these foundations to quantify how casting-
condition–controlled features (e.g., dendrite/eutectic morphology and e-coat–induced nano-precipitates)
govern rivetability, and to develop a reduced-order model for rapid microstructure screening. Collectively,
the results provide a predictive route to specify casting and e-coating windows that achieve target SPR
performance, enable substitution of energy-intensive solution treatments with low-energy HT or the design
of NHT alloys, and support the adoption of super-large, thin-wall HPDC body structures for lighter, more
efficient EVs. The development in this project enable us to deploy the macro-micro approach to formulate
practical design rules and extend the approach to additional alloys and joint geometries in future work.
