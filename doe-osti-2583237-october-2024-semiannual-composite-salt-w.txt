 EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The aqueous waste from the Salt Waste Processing Facility (SWPF) is sampled semiannually for transfers
to the Saltstone Production Facility (SPF). Salt solution is treated at SPF and disposed of in the Saltstone
Disposal Facility (SDF). Per request of customer, X-TTR-Z-00027, Revision 0, one SDF waste form
(saltstone) sample was prepared in the Savannah River National Laboratory (SRNL) from the SWPF
Decontaminated Salt Solution (DSS) Waste Acceptance Criteria (WAC) sample and Z-area premix material
for the October 2024 semiannual Toxicity Characteristic Leaching Procedure (TCLP) sample. The sample
contained 60:40 (by weight) of slag and fly ash (referred to as the “Cement-Free grout sample”). Results
from the technical report support Task 2: ‘Grout Leaching Analyses’ of the Task Technical Request (TTR)
prepared by Savannah River Mission Completion (SRMC). At 64 days cured, a sample of the SDF waste
form was collected and shipped to a certified laboratory for analysis using the Toxicity Characteristic
Leaching Procedure (TCLP). The October 2024 semiannual Cement-Free grout sample met the South
Carolina (SC) Code of Regulations for Hazardous Waste Management Regulations (HWMR) 61-79.261.24
and 61-79.268.48 requirements for a non-hazardous waste form with respect to the Resource Conservation
and Recovery Act (RCRA) metals and Underlying Hazardous Constituents (UHCs), and met the SPF WAC
that was in effect at the time of the sample collection at SWPF.

1.0 Introduction
A composite sample from the Salt Waste Processing Facility (SWPF) Decontaminated Salt Solution (DSS)
was used to prepare one Saltstone Disposal Facility (SDF) waste form sample, referred to as a Cement-Free
grout sample1 per request of SWPF & End Stream Delivery (ESD) Engineering (S&E-E). The Cement-Free
sample is representative for 6 months of saltstone processing and is the sample used to determine that
saltstone produced by the Saltstone Production Facility (SPF) is a non-hazardous waste form. The October
2024 semiannual Cement-Free saltstone sample was prepared on November 25, 2024. The sample cured
for 64 days, it was crushed, sieved, packaged, and deemed “collected”2, 3. The sample was then shipped to
Southwest Research Institute (SwRI®) to analyze for toxicity per the Toxicity Characteristic Leaching
Procedure (TCLP) method1, 4. The Cement-free saltstone sample determined whether the non-hazardous
nature of the grout meets the requirements of the South Carolina (SC) Code of Regulations 61-79.261.245
for Resource Conservation and Recovery Act (RCRA) metals and 61-79.268.486 for inorganic/organic
Underlying Hazardous Constituents (UHCs) (for informational purposes only)1.

2.0 Experimental Procedure

2.1 Technical
The October 2024 Semiannual sample of the SWPF DSS is a composite from the six months of SWPF
processing during the Third Quarter Fiscal Year 2024 (3QFY2024) and the Fourth Quarter Fiscal Year
2024 (4QFY2024)7. Saltstone preparation was performed at Savannah River National (SRNL). S&E-E
provided SRNL with the saltstone grout recipe as well as the premix components1. Table 2-1 shows the
premix components obtained for the last semiannual TCLP sample including specific LOT numbers.
Savannah River Mission Completion (SRMC) provided SRNL personnel with the premix formulation for
the October 2024 semiannual TCLP sample.

The saltstone sample was prepared with a water-to-premix ratio of 0.59 using the mixing method outlined
in SRNL work instructions8 and the October 2024 semiannual premix components in Table 2-1. The sample
contained 60:40 (by weight) of slag and fly ash only referred to as the “Cement-Free grout sample”. The
sample cured in a Ziploc® sealed plastic bag for 64 days. After curing, the sample was crushed and sieved
using the method outlined in SRNL work instructions9. Material that passed through the 3/8-inch (0.375”)
sieve (9.252 mm) was subsequently screened through a No. 4 sieve (4.76 mm). The material retained on
the No. 4 sieve was packaged in a primary container (250 mL High Density Polyethylene (HDPE) bottle)
and shipped on the same day that it was prepared to SwRI® by the SRNL shipping group.

3.0 Results
Table 3-1 summarizes the analytical results provided by the vendor, SwRI®13. The first eight rows show
data for the RCRA metals, and the next four rows show data for the UHC metals from the TCLP leachates.
The last four rows show results from solids analyses of the saltstone for benzene, phenol, total and amenable
cyanide. The entire vendor report is documented and included as a reference13. Some of the data values are
flagged with qualifier letters (U, D, J, B) that are shown as footnotes to the table. Further explanation for
these qualifiers can be found in the vendor report. For comparison to the current Cement-Free results, the
previous quarter and four previous quarter average results for the Cement-Free sample are shown in the
final two columns. The four-quarter average values contain a qualifier (*, +, ^) if past values have been
reported as a non-detectable analyte (‘U’). The Regulatory Toxicity5 values and the Waste Acceptance
Criteria (WAC) Limits are from Table 7 of the WAC14 and reflect the requirements in the applicable version
of the document. Note that the vendor used a “modified” Method 1311 where sample mass was restricted
due to the elevated activity of the sample. This methodology is consistent with the joint guidance from the
Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) and Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) for mixed
radioactive and hazardous waste. For the October 2024 semiannual saltstone sample, the TCLP extraction
was performed on the Cement-Free sample using extraction fluid #2. Extraction fluid #1 is an aqueous
acetic acid solution with pH= 4.93 ± 0.05 prepared by diluting 5.7 mL of glacial acetic acid into 500 mL of
reagent water, then adding 64.3 mL of sodium hydroxide and diluting with reagent water for a total volume
of 1 Liter4. Extraction fluid #2 is an aqueous acetic acid solution with pH= 2.88 ± 0.05 prepared by diluting
5.7 mL of glacial acetic acid into reagent water for a total volume of 1 Liter. Determination of the extract
fluid used in the TCLP uses Section 7.1.4 ‘Determination of Appropriate Extraction Fluid’ of the TCLP
method4. A size reduced portion of the solid is mixed with reagent water and stirred for 5 minutes. If the
resulting pH of the contacted water is below pH 5.0, then extraction fluid #1 is used. However, if the
resulting pH is above 5.0, then an additional step using HCl and heating to 50 °C for 10 minutes is
performed. If the pH of the cooled solution is below 5.0, then extraction fluid #1 is used and if the pH is
above 5.0, then extraction fluid #2 is used. The sample resulted in an initial pH above 5.0 of the contacted
water and a second pH above 5.0. Therefore, extraction fluid #2 was used. The final extract pH value
resulting from the TCLP extraction using fluid #2 showed a pH of 2.57 for the Cement-Free Sample.

cyanide is lower than the average for the previous four Cement-Free samples and lower than the value of
22.1 mg/L for the previous semiannual sample (April 202415).

 4.0 Conclusions
 Analyses of the SDF Cement-Free waste form prepared from the October 2024 semiannual SWPF DSS
 sample and premix material resulted in the following findings.
  The RCRA metal TCLP result concentrations met the SC Code of Regulations 61-79.261.24
 requirements for a nonhazardous waste form5.
  The measured concentrations of the TCLP RCRA metals and additional inorganic/organic UHCs
 met the SC Code of Regulations 61-79.268.48 non-wastewater standards6.
  The measured concentrations of the TCLP RCRA metals met the SPF WAC14.
