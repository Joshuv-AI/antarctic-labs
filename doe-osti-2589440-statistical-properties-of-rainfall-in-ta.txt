1.0 Summary
 The objective of this project was to complement the U.S. Department of Energy Atmospheric Radiation
 Measurement (ARM) User Facility second ARM Mobile Facility (AMF2) deployment at
 kennaook-Cape Grim (CAPE-k), Tasmania with a micro-rain radar (MRR-PRO) mounted on top of one
 of the ARM containers to locate the MRR-PRO close to the ARM cloud radars (Ka-band ARM Zenith
 Radar [KAZR] and Marine W-band ARM Cloud Radar [MWACR]). This was to allow for a continuous
 calibration check of the ARM cloud radars against the MRR-PRO and Parsivel-2 disdrometers.
 Scientifically, it added one radar frequency to the analysis of vertical profiles of cloud and precipitation
 properties (K-band).

2.0 Results
 The main piece of work conducted in this project was to provide a calibration for the KAZR and WACR
 radar data, using calibrated ODM disdrometer observations and collocated MRR-PRO radar observations.
 The MRR-PRO radar was successfully installed on top of one of the AMF2 containers and operated
 almost continuously between 20240903 and 20251014.

 The calibration technique can be summarized as follows:
  • Calibrate ODM disdrometer with metal spheres
  • Calibrate MRR-PRO using ODM disdrometer T-matrix calculations of 24 GHz reflectivity
  • Work out a wet radome attenuation correction for KAZR and WACR by computing
 Z_ODM_TMATRIX – Z_WACR (orKAZR) = f(ODM_rainrate)
  • Calibrate KAZR and WACR by comparing with ODM disdrometer and MRR-PRO with two data
 sets: a selection of non or weakly drizzling cases without the wet radome attenuation correction and
 all cases including big precipitation cases but with the wet radome attenuation correction. I also apply
 a W-band and a Ka-band attenuation correction between the radar height and the range bin that I use
 for comparisons.
 1. Calibration of ODM disdrometer

 We used metal spheres of diameter ranging from 0.4mm to 8mm. From 8mm down to 2mm we dropped
 10 spheres, then for smaller diameters, we dropped more and it was harder to count them.

Our conclusion is that the ODM disdrometer accurately measure diameters and does not miss particles.
The experiment with spheres below 1mm will have to be redone due to high wind conditions (causing
some drops to partly fall outside of the sampling volume; see 0.8mm) and with a better way to control the
drop of the very small spheres (spheres falling at the same time, making the diameters artificially bigger;
see 0.6mm). The ODM disdrometer does detect 0.4mm as claimed.
2. Calibration of MRR-PRO using ODM disdrometer

At Cape Grim, the MRR-PRO was installed at the ARM site on top of one of the containers, pointing
vertically. Note that we have done K-band T-matrix simulations to produce these comparisons. This
comparison indicated that the MRR-PRO was 3.4 dB off, which is consistent with earlier estimates from
previous campaigns.

3. Wet radome attenuation correction for KAZR and WACR

The objective was to develop a wet radome attenuation correction by estimating the difference between
ODM T-matrix estimates of reflectivity and WACR or KAZR reflectivity as a function of rainfall rate
measured by the ODM disdrometer. This relationship can then be used to correct KAZR reflectivities for
wet radome attenuation using the ODM rainfall rates for the cases where precipitation has reached the
ground. From the interim report, the plot has been updated with the whole database.

4. KAZR calibration

Here we compare KAZR reflectivity and Doppler with MRR-PRO. It is important to note that we are
using the a1 files for this exercise. Two subsets of cases are considered for these comparisons : all data,
including heavier precipitation (where wet radome attenuation needs to be corrected for), and a selection
of non-precipitation or very weakly drizzling cases (where wet radome attenuation can be neglected). The
database has been updated since the interim report to include all the available data. The comparison with
non-precipitating clouds is expected to be the reference but includes less samples (because of the limited
sensitivity of the MRR-PRO), while the comparison with all cases is expected to be less accurate due to
uncertainties in wet radome attenuation but includes a lot more samples.

The first period from 20240401 to 20240430 has been processed separately, since there was clearly
something different about the KAZR calibration for this period. Results are indeed showing that the
KAZR radar was in good agreement (within 1 dB with the MRR-PRO over that short period at the

Our main conclusions : the KAZR is under-calibrated by 4 dBZ. Doppler velocities are in great
agreement, indicating that all instruments are most likely pointing vertically as required. The wet radome
attenuation correction seems to do a good job statistically because the two estimates in non-precipitating
clouds and precipitation agree reasonably well.
5. WACR calibration using collocated KAZR

Discussions with Roger Marchand (University of Washington) highlighted that the KAZR calibration
remained constant during the field experiment after 20240501, while there has been a drift noted for
WACR. As a result, we have decided to derive the calibration of WACR (a1 files) against the calibrated
KAZR data for selected periods of the experiment. We also provide below comparisons between Doppler
observations.

 5

These periods are:
 • 20240401–20240430 (KAZR calibration = 0 dBZ)

 For this period, the MWACR calibration error is estimated as 3.4 dBZ (needs to be added to the
 reflectivities in the a1 files) and the Doppler seems to be in very good agreement, with no noticeable
 statistical difference.
 • 20240503–20240903 (KAZR calibration = 4 dBZ)

 For this period, the estimated MWACR calibration error is 4.5 dBZ and the Doppler measurements are
 in reasonably good agreement, but there seems to be a slight shift at low Doppler velocities, where
 non-Rayleigh scattering effects are expected to be negligible.
 • 20240903–20250330 (KAZR calibration = 4 dBZ)

 For this period, the estimated MWACR calibration error is 5 dBZ. The slight shift in Doppler is still
 visible.
  • 20250330–20250728 (KAZR calibration = 4 dBZ)

 For this period, the estimated MWACR calibration error is 6 dBZ. The slight shift in Doppler is not
 visible for that period.

 We note that this increase in estimated WACR calibration error from 3.4 to 6 dB is consistent with results
 presented by R. Marchand (principal investigator of CAPE-k), who found an increasing statistical
 difference in dual wavelength ratio of about 2.5 dB between the KAZR and WACR.
