1 Introduction
We discuss the heat load inside a neutron spallation target made of tungsten hit
by an 800 MeV proton beam. This problem is important for the Neutron Target
project, where a spallation target will be surrounded by an extended graphite or
beryllium cube to create a neutron target. The idea of a free-neutron target enables
the measurement of neutron-induced reactions in inverse kinematics [1, 2, 3].
This idea is part of the LANSCE strategy to stay a worldwide leader for neutron-
induced research [4].
Figs. 1 and 2 show a graphite cube built and simulated from pieces available at
LANSCE. First activation experiments have already been performed at lower neu-
tron energies utilizing (p,n) reactions on Li and Be [5] and with 800 MeV protons
in the Blue Room at LANSCE with beam currents below 80 nA [6].

Figure 1: The graphite full-cube as set up in the Blue Room (Target 4) using 54
pieces of graphite (10.7 cm x 10.7 cm x 76.3 cm, [6]). See Fig. 2 for the simulated
version.

The next step within this project is an in-flight activation experiment in the Blue
Room (target 4) at LANSCE with 800 MeV protons as the driver of the spallation
source [8]. A beam of 78 Kr ions will be sent through the neutron target (see ion
pipe in Figure 2) and the neutron capture products, 79 Kr, as well as the unreacted
ions will be implanted into a capture foil. In order to maximize the 79 Kr/78 Kr
ratio, a beam structure with a very small duty cycle is preferred. We consider here
proton pulses of 5 µC every 5 s. This corresponds to an average current of 1 µA at
0.2 Hz. Such pulses are routinely delivered from by the LANSCE accelerator to
the proton storage ring (PSR) during the operation of the Lujan Center and have a

length of about 600 µs [9].

2 Equilibrium temperature
The melting point of tungsten is Tm = 3694 K (3422◦ C). If we assume a cylindrical
target of R = 3.5 cm radius, L = 25 cm length, the surface area is:

                         A = 2πR2 + 2πRL = 627 cm2                              (1)
The projected range of 800 MeV protons in tungsten (PSTAR, [10]) is rA = 436 g/cm2
or (density ρ = 19.3 g/cm3 ) r = 22.6 cm. Therefore a length of 25 cm ensures that
the 800 MeV beam is completely stopped (same as during LSDS experiments,
[11]).

Epulse = 4000 J.

4 Summary
We discussed the heat load inside a tungsten target hit by an 800 MeV beam
consisting of proton pulses of 5 µC every 5 s. The cylindrical target had 3.5 cm

 7

radius and 25 cm length. We found:

 • The impact of the pulse structure on temperature can be safely neglected for
 pulse up to a 10 µC. The local temperature changes upon impact are below
 1K/µC.

 • No cooling is required for average currents up to 1 µA. The black-body
 radiation alone is sufficient.
