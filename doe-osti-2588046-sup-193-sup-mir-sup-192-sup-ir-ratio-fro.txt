 Eight irradiations of K2 IrCl6 pellets were carried out with three critical assemblies: Flattop-Oy,
 Flattop-Pu and Godiva IV. One of the irradiations was performed with three pellets at different
 locations of the critical assembly, while the other irradiations with pellets at the center, but operating
 the critical assembly at different power and, respectively, different neutron flux. Counting samples
 were prepared from the irradiated pellets and X-ray spectra measured with silicone drift detectors.
 The 193m Ir/192 Ir ratio is reported for each irradiation.

 I. MOTIVATION

 Iridium is one of the rare neutron-flux monitors [1, 2] that can scan the neutron spectrum in three spectral groups:
below 1 MeV, between 2 and 8 MeV, and above 8 MeV. 193 Ir exhibits an isomeric state at 80.2 keV with T1/2 = 10.5 d,
which is populated by the 193 Ir(n, n′ )193m Ir reaction and measures the neutron fluence between 2 and 8 MeV. The first
and the third spectral groups are monitored by the Ir(n, γ) and Ir(n, 2n) reactions, respectively. Discrepancies between
iridium irradiations with critical assemblies and MCNP simulations using ENDF data regarding the 193 Ir(n, n′ )193m Ir
cross section [2] motivated us to repeat some of the measurements, but using a different detection system and analysis
of the data [3]. The method described in [3] relies on measurements with modern high-resolution X-ray detectors and
removes the dependence from the X-ray attenuation in the sample and X-ray detection efficiency.

 II. EXPERIMENTAL DETAILS AND RESULTS

 We carried out eight irradiations of pressed K2 IrCl6 pellets with three of the critical assemblies at the National
Criticality Experiments Research Center (NCERC): Flattop-Oy, Flattop-Pu, and Godiva IV. Flattop consists of a
spherical core of Highly Enriched Uranium (HEU) with diameter of 4.77” (Flattop-Oy) or highly enriched plutonium
with diameter 3.57” (Flattop-Pu) surrounded by a reflector shell of natural uranium with outer diameter of 19”.
Godiva IV is constructed of six HEU disks with diameter of 7” and thickness of about 1”. Each of these critical
assemblies has a hole through the core and reflector, called Glory Hole, to insert samples for neutron activation. To
keep the reactivity of the assembly high during the course of the irradiation, the space between the activation samples
on the Flattop critical assemblies is filled with plugs of HEU or plutonium. Detailed descriptions of the current
NCERC critical assemblies can be found in Ref. [4].
 We placed the K2 IrCl6 pellets in the center of the critical assemblies. In only one of the irradiations we placed three
pellets at different locations of Flattop-Pu, one near the center, the second near the core-reflector boundary and the
third one in the reflector. All eight irradiations are summarized in Table I. We operated the critical assemblies in two
modes - low power and high power. The power was set by the position of the control rods for adjusting the criticality.
In five experiments we irradiated the K2 IrCl6 pellets at low power and in additional three we did second irradiations
at high power. The configurations of the HEU plugs in the Glory Hole remained the same, when irradiating the
pellets at low and high power, and do not change the shape of the neutron spectrum.
 The irradiated pellets were transported to LANL and processed to convert K2 IrCl6 to a precipitate of Cs2 IrCl6
using a chemistry procedure described in Ref. [5]. The Cs2 IrCl6 precipitate was filtered onto a substrate of filter
paper creating a “flat” counting sample with diameter of 7/16”. The mass of each counting sample was between 10
and 15 mg. The counting samples produced from each irradiation are listed in Table I.
 We measured the X-rays from the counting samples with three Silicone Drift Detectors (SDDs) from Amptek [6].
SDD1 and SDD2 are standalone detectors with beryllium windows, while SDD3 is windowless and installed in a
vacuum chamber. The detectors have a thickness of 0.5 mm and area of 17 mm2 (SDD1) and 50 mm2 (SDD2 and
SDD3), respectively. Absorbers of 2-mm thick acrylic were used to remove the electrons emitted from the β decay.
Each sample was counted a few times with one or two SDDs over the span of three months or longer. The measured
spectra with binning of 15 eV/channel and zoomed at the 192 Ir and 193m Ir L→M X-ray peaks are shown in Appendix
A. The SDD used and the start and end days of the measurement are shown in each figure of Appendix A.
 In earlier work [3], we demonstrated that the 193m Ir/192 Ir ratio can be obtained in a model independent way when
measuring a high-resolution X-ray spectrum and fitting the 193m Ir and 192 Ir peaks of 9.18 and 9.44 keV, respectively,
with Voigt functions. The fitting function and its decomposition to background and three Voigt functions are shown
in each figure in Appendix A. The ratio of 193m Ir to 192 Ir atoms, N0193m and N0192 , respectively, is calculated according

 III. RESULTS

  Flattop-Oy April, 2021: We did two irradiations with Flattop-Oy at the same conditions, one at low power and
the other at high power. The K2 IrCl6 pellets were placed at the center of the critical assembly. We prepared two
counting samples with different masses from each pellet. Every sample was counted several times with SDD1 and
SDD2. This experiment was our testbed for determining if the 193m Ir/192 Ir ratio shows any systematic uncertainty
by looking at all eight permutations (two irradiations × two counting samples × two detectors). The results from
this experiment are reported in Ref. [3]. We include in this report only the final results in Fig. 1.

  Flattop-Pu May, 2021: This experiment consists of only one irradiation of three K2 IrCl6 pellets at three positions
of Flattop-Pu: center at 1.4 cm, core-reflector boundary at 3.6 cm, and reflector at 8.1 cm. Because the heat of
the plutonium core can damage the nickel cladding, the irradiation was at low power. We prepared one counting
sample from each K2 IrCl6 pellet. The X-ray activity of the samples was low, therefore we ignored the correction
of the 9.18 keV X-rays from 192m Ir, because of the small statistics. Attempts to correct this peak area could
not lead to noticeable change of the value for the 193m Ir/192 Ir ratio, but rather increased its uncertainty. The re-
sults for the 193m Ir/192 Ir ratios from all measurements are listed in Table II and the average values are shown in Fig. 2.

 Flattop-Oy July, 2021: This experiment is a repetition of the Flattop-Oy April, 2021 one. We did again two irradia-
tions, one at low power and the other at high power, but only prepared one counting sample from each K2 IrCl6 pellet.
The results for the 193m Ir/192 Ir ratio are listed in Table III. We used the last X-ray measurements to obtain the area
of the 9.18 keV and assumed it originated from 192m Ir (T1/2 = 240(9) y), because 193m Ir (T1/2 = 10.54(4) d) would
be completely decayed after three months. The results for the average ratio are shown in Fig. 3. It is noticeable that
the irradiation at low power has lower values than the irradiation at high power. We could attribute this discrepancy
only to misalignment of the pellets in the Glory Hole for the low-power irradiation. Placing the K2 IrCl6 pellet

 IV. SUMMARY

 We reported the results for the 193m Ir/192 Ir ratio from eight irradiations of K2 IrCl6 pellets with three critical
assemblies. We produced counting samples from each irradiated pellet and measured the 192 Ir and 193m Ir with
high-resolution SDDs. The 193m Ir/192 Ir ratios were obtained from a method described in our earlier report [3].
 We observed good agreement between the results from the Flattop-Oy irradiations with small discrepancies due to
different pellet loading.
