A parity Erdős–Hajnal theorem for t t -intersecting curves
Andrew Suk, Su Zhou

Abstract
For every fixed t ≥ 1 t\geq 1 , we prove a parity analogue of the mighty Erdős–Hajnal property for t t -intersecting curves in the plane. Let ℬ \mathcal{B} be a set of blue curves and 𝒢 \mathcal{G} a set of green curves in the plane such that ℬ ∪ 𝒢 \mathcal{B}\cup\mathcal{G} is a collection of t t -intersecting curves in general position. We show that there exist subfamilies ℬ ′ ⊆ ℬ \mathcal{B}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{B} and 𝒢 ′ ⊆ 𝒢 \mathcal{G}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{G} such that | ℬ ′ | ≥ ε ​ | ℬ | and | 𝒢 ′ | ≥ ε ​ | 𝒢 | , |\mathcal{B}^{\prime}|\geq\varepsilon|\mathcal{B}|\qquad\text{and}\qquad|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}|\geq\varepsilon|\mathcal{G}|, where ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 depends only on t t , such that either every pair in ℬ ′ × 𝒢 ′ \mathcal{B}^{\prime}\times\mathcal{G}^{\prime} intersects an even number of times or every such pair intersects an odd number of times. For t = 1 t=1 , this recovers the theorem of Fox, Pach, and Suk for pseudo-segments. Introduction

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a finite family of simple Jordan arcs in the plane.
Throughout the paper, we assume that
\mathcal{C}
is in
general
position
, that is, every two curves intersect in finitely many points, no two
curves are tangent, no three curves pass through the same point, and no
endpoint of one curve lies on another curve. In particular, every intersection
between two curves is a proper crossing. For an integer
t\geq 1
, we say
that
\mathcal{C}
is
t
-intersecting
if every pair of curves in

\mathcal{C}
intersect in at most
t
points. In the case
t=1
, we
refer to
\mathcal{C}
as a collection of
pseudo-segments
. The

intersection graph
of
\mathcal{C}
is the graph whose vertex set is

\mathcal{C}
, where two vertices are adjacent if and only if the
corresponding curves intersect. A graph is called a
string graph
if it
arises as the intersection graph of curves in the plane.

The celebrated Erdős–Hajnal conjecture states that every hereditary
graph class defined by forbidding a fixed induced subgraph contains a clique
or an independent set of polynomial size. We say that a hereditary graph class has the
Erdős–Hajnal
property
if there is a constant
\delta>0
such that every
n
-vertex
graph in the class contains a clique or an independent set of size at least

n^{\delta}
. A breakthrough result of Tomon
[
19
]
shows that the class of string graphs has this property.

One way to prove that a hereditary graph class has the
Erdős–Hajnal property is to establish a stronger bipartite statement.
A hereditary graph class is said to have the
strong
Erdős–Hajnal property
if there exists a constant
\delta>0
such
that every graph
G
in the class contains two
disjoint subsets
A,B\subseteq V(G)
, each of size at least

\delta|V(G)|
, such that the bipartite graph between
A
and
B
is
either complete or empty. It is known that string graphs do not have the
strong Erdős–Hajnal property
[
3
,
17
]
. On the other hand, Fox,
Pach, and Tóth
[
4
]
proved that, for every fixed
t\geq 1
,
intersection graphs of
t
-intersecting curves have the strong
Erdős–Hajnal property.

For some applications, including density and strong regularity-type results, even this bipartite strengthening is not enough. A
hereditary graph class is said to have the
mighty
Erdős–Hajnal property

For every integer

For every integer

\mathcal{G}
be a set of green curves in the plane such that

\delta|\mathcal{B}||\mathcal{G}|

\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{B}

\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{G}

such that every curve in
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}

\mathcal{G}^{\prime}

Likewise, an analogue of Theorem
1.4
holds for even crossings.

Quasi-planar graphs and odd crossings.
A
topological graph
is a graph drawn in the plane with points as vertices and simple Jordan arcs as edges connecting the corresponding endpoints. All topological graphs considered in this paper have no loops or multiple edges. The edges are allowed to cross, but may not pass through vertices other than their endpoints. We always assume the drawing is in general position: no two edges are tangent,
no three edges pass through the same interior point, and every intersection
between the interiors of two edges is a proper crossing. We say that the edges of a topological graph are
t
-intersecting
if no two edges cross more than
t
times.

Euler’s formula implies that every planar graph on
n
vertices has at most

3n-6
edges. A longstanding open problem in topological graph theory asks
whether a similar linear bound holds under the much weaker assumption that no

k
edges pairwise cross. A topological graph is called

k
-quasi-planar
if it contains no
k
pairwise crossing edges.

Conjecture 1.5
.

For every integer
k\geq 2
, every
k
-quasi-planar topological graph on

n
vertices has
O_{k}(n)
edges.

Pach, Radoičić, and Tóth
[
14
]
proved the conjecture for

k=3
, and Ackerman and Tardos
[
2
]
later improved the constant.
Ackerman
[
1
]
proved the conjecture for
k=4
. The conjecture remains open for
every
k\geq 5
. For general
k
, the best known bound is due to Fox,
Pach, and Suk
[
6
]
, who proved that every
k
-quasi-planar
topological graph on
n
vertices has at most

n(\log n)^{O(\log k)}
edges.

By the celebrated Hanani–Tutte theorem (see
[
18
]
), if an
n
-vertex topological graph has no two edges cross an odd number of times, then the graph is planar and has
O(n)
edges. In
[
16
]
, Pach and Tóth established the following parity analogue of the quasi-planar graph problem.

Theorem 1.6

(Pach–Tóth
[
16
]
)
.

For every
k\geq 2
, there exists a constant
C=C(k)
such that the following holds. If
G
is an
n
-vertex topological graph with no
k
edges that pairwise cross an odd number of times, then

|E(G)|\leq Cn(\log n)^{5k-10}.

It is possible that, for every fixed
k
, every
n
-vertex topological graph with no
k
edges that pairwise cross an odd number of times has only
O_{k}(n)
edges. While this remains open for all
k\geq 3
, our next result gives a substantially stronger bound for large
k
, under the additional assumption that the edges of
G
form a
t
-intersecting family.

Theorem 1.7
.

For all integers
k\geq 2
and
t\geq 1
, there exists a constant
C=C(t)>0
such that the following holds. Let
G
be an
n
-vertex topological graph whose edges form a
t
-intersecting family. If
G
contains no
k
edges that pairwise cross an odd number of times, then

|E(G)|\leq n(\log n)^{C\log k}.

2
An
r
-division with vertex constraints

In this section we prove a variant of the
r
-division theorem that will be used later. The notion of an
r
-division was introduced by Frederickson
[
7
]
as a refinement of the planar separator method of Lipton and Tarjan
[
11
]
. A key ingredient in the construction is the following simple-cycle separator theorem of Miller.

Theorem 2.1

(Miller
[
13
]
)
.

Let
G
be a triangulated planar graph with
N
vertices embedded in the plane, and let
w:V(G)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a weight function with total weight
W
. Then there exists a simple cycle
C
in
G
such that
|C|=O(\sqrt{N})
, and the total weight of the vertices strictly inside
C
and strictly outside
C
are both at most
2W/3
.

Let
G=(V,E,F)
be a planar graph embedded in the plane. A
region
of
G
is a connected edge-induced subgraph of
G
. A
division
of
G
is a collection of regions
\mathcal{R}=\{R_{1},\ldots,R_{k}\}
such that every edge of
G
lies in at least one region. Given a division
\mathcal{R}
of
G
, a vertex
v\in V(G)
is a
boundary vertex
if it belongs to more than one region in
\mathcal{R}
. For a region
R\in\mathcal{R}
, we denote by
b(R)
the set of vertices of
R
that are boundary vertices. A
hole
of a region
R
is a face of
R
that is not a face of
G
.

Theorem 2.2
.

There exists an absolute constant
r_{0}>0
such that the following holds. Let
G
be a triangulated planar graph on
N
vertices embedded in the plane, and let
Q\subseteq V(G)
. Then for every
r\geq r_{0}
and every
\rho\geq 1
, there exists a division
\mathcal{R}
of
G
such that

|\mathcal{R}|=O\!\left(\frac{N}{r}+\frac{|Q|}{\rho}\right),

and every region
R\in\mathcal{R}
satisfies

|V(R)|=O(r),\qquad|b(R)|=O(\sqrt{r}),\qquad|(V(R)\setminus b(R))\cap Q|=O(\rho),

and has at most
24
holes.

The proof of Theorem
2.2
is a simple modification of the recursive

r
-division construction of Klein, Mozes, and Sommer
[
9
]
. The only
change is that we add one more balancing step, in order to control the set

.
For the reader’s convenience, we include the details.

We shall use the following recurrence estimate from Klein, Mozes, and Sommer
[
9
, Section 2.7]
. We state the form needed here, which follows from their proof with only minor changes in the constants.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
1/2\leq\lambda<1
, and let
c,c^{\prime}
be positive constants. Suppose that, for every
\tau\geq 1
, a function
T_{\tau}
satisfies

T_{\tau}(m)\leq\begin{cases}c^{\prime}m^{\lambda}+\displaystyle\max_{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{16}}\sum_{i=1}^{16}T_{\tau}(\alpha_{i}m),&m>\tau,\\[4.30554pt]
0,&m\leq\tau,\end{cases}

where the maximum is over all
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{16}\geq 0
such that

\alpha_{i}\leq{2\over 3}+{c\over\sqrt{m}}\quad\text{for all }i,\qquad 1\leq\sum_{i=1}^{16}\alpha_{i}\leq 1+{c\over\sqrt{m}}.

Then, for
\tau
sufficiently large,

T_{\tau}(m)=O\!\left({m\over\tau^{1-\lambda}}\right),

where the implicit constant depends only on
c,c^{\prime}
and
\lambda
.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

Let
G
be a triangulated planar graph on
N

Q\subseteq V(G)
. We construct a rooted binary tree
\mathcal{T}
as follows. Each node

corresponds to a connected embedded region

, with the root
\hat{x}\in\mathcal{T}
corresponding to

. For each node

and let

denote the number of holes of

.

Fix a sufficiently large absolute constant
c_{0}
. The leaves of the final decomposition will be the rootmost nodes
x
satisfying

n(x)\leq c_{0}r,\qquad\beta(x)\leq c_{0}\sqrt{r},\qquad q(x)\leq c_{0}\rho.

(1)

Moreover, we shall show that every region created in the recursion has at most
24

Suppose that
x\in\mathcal{T}
is not a leaf, and let
R_{x}
be the corresponding region. We construct the two children of
R_{x}
as follows. Since
G
is triangulated, every natural face of
R_{x}
is already a triangle. We triangulate each hole of
R_{x}
by placing one
artificial vertex
in the hole and joining it to all vertices on the boundary of that hole with
artificial edges
. This gives a triangulated planar graph
R^{\prime}_{x}
. Since the number of holes will be bounded by an absolute constant,
R^{\prime}_{x}
has

Let

be the depth of

in
\mathcal{T}
. We choose the weight function in

2.1

d(x)\text{ }(\bmod\text{ }4)

. If
d(x)\equiv 1\pmod{4}
, then precisely the vertices in

have weight

. If
d(x)\equiv 2\pmod{4}
, then precisely the vertices in

(V(R_{x})\setminus b(R_{x}))\cap Q

. Finally, if

have weight

has weight

. Thus the separator balances, in turn, the number of vertices, the number of boundary vertices, the number of non-boundary vertices of

, and the number of holes.

Let
C_{x}
be the separator cycle. Then
|C_{x}|\leq c_{1}\sqrt{n(x)}
, for an absolute constant
c_{1}
. Let
R_{x,0}
and

.

(2)

\beta(x_{0})+\beta(x_{1})\leq\beta(x)+c_{1}\sqrt{n(x)},

(3)

and

q(x_{0})+q(x_{1})\leq q(x).

(4)

Indeed, the only vertices that can be duplicated are vertices on
C_{x}
. If a vertex of
Q
lies on
C_{x}
, then it becomes a boundary vertex in the children, and so it is not counted by
q
in either child.

The balancing step gives the following additional estimates. If
d(x)\equiv 0\pmod{4}
, then

\max\{n(x_{0}),n(x_{1})\}\leq{2\over 3}n(x)+c_{1}\sqrt{n(x)}.

(5)

If
d(x)\equiv 1\pmod{4}
, then

\max\{\beta(x_{0}),\beta(x_{1})\}\leq{2\over 3}\beta(x)+c_{1}\sqrt{n(x)}.

(6)

If

(7)

We next bound the number of holes. Say that a hole of
R_{x}
is fully contained on one side of

if all triangles obtained from its artificial triangulation lie on that side. Suppose that
k

. Then the corresponding child has at most

h(y)\leq{2\over 3}h(x)+4.

Thus, if
h(x)\leq 24
, then after one full four-level cycle

h(y)\leq{2\over 3}\cdot 24+4=20,

and during the next three intermediate levels the number of holes is at most
23

24

\mathcal{T}
. Since the leaves are precisely the regions in

c_{3}
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\beta(y_{i})\leq\beta(x)+c_{3}\sqrt{c_{0}r},\qquad\sum_{i=1}^{k}q(y_{i})\leq q(x).

Moreover, for every
y_{i}
with
|S^{\prime}_{r}(y_{i})|>1
, we have
\beta(y_{i})\leq{2\over 3}\beta(x)+c_{3}\sqrt{c_{0}r}
and
q(y_{i})\leq{2\over 3}q(x).
Choose
c_{0}
sufficiently large so that
c_{0}\geq 50
and
{\sqrt{c_{0}}\over c_{3}}\geq 50.
We prove by induction that

|S^{\prime}_{r}(x)|\leq\max\left\{1,\,{\beta(x)\over c_{3}\sqrt{c_{0}r}}+{q(x)\over\rho}-15\right\}.

If
x
satisfies the two stopping inequalities, then
S^{\prime}_{r}(x)=\{x\}
. Otherwise,
{\beta(x)\over c_{3}\sqrt{c_{0}r}}+{q(x)\over\rho}>50.
Let
s
be the cardinality of
\{i:|S^{\prime}_{r}(y_{i})|>1\}.
We consider the following cases.

Case 0.
If
s=0
, then

|S^{\prime}_{r}(x)|\leq 16\leq{\beta(x)\over c_{3}\sqrt{c_{0}r}}+{q(x)\over\rho}-15.

Case 1.
If
s=1
, say
|S^{\prime}_{r}(y_{1})|>1
, then

\displaystyle|S^{\prime}_{r}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq|S^{\prime}_{r}(y_{1})|+k-1

\displaystyle\leq{\beta(y_{1})\over c_{3}\sqrt{c_{0}r}}+{q(y_{1})\over\rho}

\displaystyle\leq{2\over 3}\left({\beta(x)\over c_{3}\sqrt{c_{0}r}}+{q(x)\over\rho}\right)+1

\displaystyle\leq{\beta(x)\over c_{3}\sqrt{c_{0}r}}+{q(x)\over\rho}-15.

Case 2.
If
s\geq 2
, let
I=\{i:|S^{\prime}_{r}(y_{i})|>1\}.
Then

\displaystyle|S^{\prime}_{r}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in I}\left({\beta(y_{i})\over c_{3}\sqrt{c_{0}r}}+{q(y_{i})\over\rho}-15\right)+(k-s)

\displaystyle\leq{\beta(x)\over c_{3}\sqrt{c_{0}r}}+{q(x)\over\rho}+1-15s+16-s

\displaystyle\leq{\beta(x)\over c_{3}\sqrt{c_{0}r}}+{q(x)\over\rho}-15.

Hence

|S^{\prime}_{r}(x)|=O\!\left(1+{\beta(x)\over\sqrt{r}}+{q(x)\over\rho}\right).

The leaves of the final decomposition are precisely the nodes in
S^{\prime}_{r}(x)
, over all
x\in S_{r}(\hat{x})
. Therefore, by (
9
), (
10
), and the fact that non-boundary vertices of
Q
are counted in at most one region of any antichain,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{R}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{x\in S_{r}(\hat{x})}|S^{\prime}_{r}(x)|

\displaystyle=O\!\left(|S_{r}(\hat{x})|+{1\over\sqrt{r}}\sum_{x\in S_{r}(\hat{x})}\beta(x)+{1\over\rho}\sum_{x\in S_{r}(\hat{x})}q(x)\right)

\displaystyle=O\!\left({N\over r}+{|Q|\over\rho}\right).

Each leaf region satisfies (
1
), and every region has at most
24
holes. This proves the theorem.

3
A structure theorem for
t
-intersecting curves

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem
1.2
. Recall that a set
\Delta\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is
path-connected
if any two
points of
\Delta
can be joined by a continuous curve contained in

\Delta
. A
domain
is an open path-connected subset of

\mathbb{R}^{2}
. We write
\partial\Delta
for the boundary of

\Delta
,
\operatorname{int}(\Delta)
for its interior, and
\operatorname{cl}(\Delta)
for its closure.

All path-connected sets
\Delta\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
considered in this
paper have boundary contained in a finite arrangement of Jordan arcs and
Jordan curves, where any two arcs or curves meet in only finitely many
points. Thus the arrangement has finitely many vertices, edges, and faces,
and contains
\partial\Delta
. Hence every connected component of

\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Delta
is a union of cells of this finite
arrangement, and is therefore path-connected. We call these components the

complementary components
of
\Delta
.

We now establish the following technical result.

Theorem 3.1
.

For every
t\geq 1
, the following holds. Let
\mathcal{B}
be a set of

m
blue curves, and let
\mathcal{G}
be a set of
n
green curves, all
contained in a domain
\Delta
, such that
\mathcal{B}\cup\mathcal{G}
is

t
-intersecting and in general position.

Then, for every
\delta\in(0,1)
and every nonempty point set
P

obtained by selecting at most one endpoint from each curve in
\mathcal{B}
,
there is a domain
\Delta^{\prime}
with
\operatorname{cl}(\Delta^{\prime})\subset\Delta
such that the following hold.

1.

|P\cap\Delta^{\prime}|\geq\Omega\left({\delta\over t}|P|\right).

2.

At most
O(\lceil\sqrt{\delta}(m+n)\rceil)
curves in
\mathcal{G}
intersect

\operatorname{cl}(\Delta^{\prime})
.

3.

The total number of crossing points between the curves in

\mathcal{B}\cup\mathcal{G}
and
\partial\Delta^{\prime}
is

O(\lceil\sqrt{\delta}(m+n)\rceil)
.

4.

\Delta\setminus\operatorname{cl}(\Delta^{\prime})
has at most
24

path-connected components.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{B}=\{\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m}\},\mathcal{G}=\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n}\}
. We may assume that
\delta(m+n)^{2}\geq r_{0}
, where
r_{0}
is the absolute
constant from Theorem
2.2
. Otherwise the statement follows by taking
\Delta^{\prime}
to be a sufficiently
small disk around one point of
P
, chosen in general position.

We construct a plane graph
G
as follows. Place a vertex at each
intersection point of two curves in
\mathcal{B}\cup\mathcal{G}
, and also at
the two endpoints of every curve. For each green curve
\gamma_{i}
, choose
one additional point
q_{i}\in\gamma_{i}
that is neither an endpoint nor an
intersection point, and add
q_{i}
as a vertex. Let

Q=\{q_{1},\ldots,q_{n}\}.

The edges of
G
are the arcs between consecutive vertices along the curves
in
\mathcal{B}\cup\mathcal{G}
. Thus
G
is a plane multigraph contained
in
\Delta
.

Since
\mathcal{B}\cup\mathcal{G}
is
t
-intersecting, the number of
intersection points determined by its curves is at most
t(m+n)^{2}
. We
refine the plane graph
G
inside
\Delta
as follows. Faces and their
sizes are understood in the usual plane embedding of
G
, with the size of a
face equal to the length of its boundary walk. We eliminate
faces of size
2
by, inside
\Delta
, placing one new vertex inside each such face and
joining it to the two boundary vertices. This adds
O(t(m+n)^{2})
vertices.
We then triangulate all faces of size at least
4
, again inside

\Delta
, and let
G^{\prime}
be the resulting triangulated plane graph. Hence

|V(G^{\prime})|=O(t(m+n)^{2}).

Apply Theorem
2.2
to
G^{\prime}
, with the set
Q
, and with
parameters

r=\delta(m+n)^{2},\qquad\rho=\sqrt{\delta}(m+n).

As a result, we obtain a division
\mathcal{R}
of
G^{\prime}
such that every region

R\in\mathcal{R}
satisfies

|V(R)|=O(\delta(m+n)^{2}),\qquad|b(R)|=O(\sqrt{\delta}(m+n)),\qquad|(V(R)\setminus b(R))\cap Q|=O(\sqrt{\delta}(m+n)),

and has at most
24
holes. Moreover,

|\mathcal{R}|=O\left({|V(G^{\prime})|\over r}+{|Q|\over\rho}\right)=O\left({t\over\delta}\right).

For each
R\in\mathcal{R}
, let
\Delta_{R}\subset\Delta
be obtained from
the embedded edges of
R
, together with
f\cap\Delta
for every face

f
of
R
that is also a face of
G^{\prime}
. Since
R
is connected,

\Delta_{R}
is path-connected. Since the regions cover all edges of
G^{\prime}
,
the sets
\Delta_{R}
with
R\in\mathcal{R}
, cover all vertices of
G^{\prime}
, and
hence cover
P
.

We next bound the number of path-connected components of

\Delta\setminus\Delta_{R}
. Since
\Delta_{R}
contains the embedded graph

R
, each such component is contained in a single face of
R
. The faces
of
R
that are also faces of
G^{\prime}
contribute nothing, by the definition of

\Delta_{R}
. Hence every component of
\Delta\setminus\Delta_{R}
is contained
in
\Delta\cap h
for some hole
h
of
R
. Since
\Delta
is a domain
and
\partial h\subset\Delta
, each set
\Delta\cap h
is path-connected.
Thus each hole of
R
contributes at most one component of

\Delta\setminus\Delta_{R}
. As
R
has at most
24
holes,

\Delta\setminus\Delta_{R}
has at most
24
path-connected components.

By the pigeonhole principle, there is a region
R\in\mathcal{R}
such that

|P\cap\Delta_{R}|\geq{|P|\over|\mathcal{R}|}=\Omega\left({\delta\over t}|P|\right).

Fix this region

.

We next bound the number of green curves that meet
\Delta_{R}
. A curve in

\mathcal{B}\cup\mathcal{G}
can enter or leave
\Delta_{R}
only through a
boundary vertex of
R
. By general position, each such boundary vertex lies
on at most two curves. Hence the total number of entry and exit points of all
curves through
\partial\Delta_{R}
is

O(|b(R)|)=O(\sqrt{\delta}(m+n)).

We claim that

\bigl|\{\gamma_{i}\in\mathcal{G}:\gamma_{i}\cap\Delta_{R}\neq\emptyset\}\bigr|\leq|(V(R)\setminus b(R))\cap Q|+3|b(R)|.

Indeed, if

, then
\gamma_{i}
is counted by the
first term. If
q_{i}\in b(R)
, then it is counted by the boundary term.
Otherwise

. If
\gamma_{i}
meets
\Delta_{R}
, then,

from

to its first point of entry into

, we meet
\partial\Delta_{R}
. Thus
\gamma_{i}
enters

\Delta_{R}
through a boundary vertex of

. By general position, each

, at most

green curves meet
\Delta_{R}
.

We may choose a sufficiently small open neighborhood
\Delta^{\prime}
of

\Delta_{R}
, with path-connected closure and

, chosen so close to
\Delta_{R}

that

has at most
24

path-connected components. We also choose it small enough so that every green curve

is also disjoint from
\operatorname{cl}(\Delta^{\prime})
.
Finally, since

is in general position, we can choose

so that the number of crossing points
between
\partial\Delta^{\prime}
and the curves in

is at most four times the

. Therefore, the number of crossing points between the curves in
\mathcal{B}\cup\mathcal{G}
and
\partial\Delta^{\prime}
is at most
O(\sqrt{\delta}(m+n))
. Moreover,

|P\cap\Delta^{\prime}|\geq|P\cap\Delta_{R}|\geq\Omega\left({\delta\over t}|P|\right).

Also, by the choice of
\Delta^{\prime}
, at most
O(\sqrt{\delta}(m+n))
green

.

∎

subarcs in
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{3}
meets

\operatorname{cl}(\Delta_{g_{2}})
. Again, taking
\delta_{2}
sufficiently small compared with
c_{3}
, we have

|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}|\geq c_{5}n,\qquad|\mathcal{B}^{\prime}|\geq c_{6}n

for some
c_{5}=c_{5}(t),c_{6}=c_{6}(t)>0
.

Now every curve in
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{2}
has its first
endpoint in
\Delta_{b_{1}}
and its second endpoint in
\Delta_{b_{2}}
.
Every curve in
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
has its first endpoint in
\Delta_{g_{1}}

and its second endpoint in
\Delta_{g_{2}}
. Since

\Delta_{g_{1}},\Delta_{g_{2}}\subseteq\Delta^{(2)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Delta^{(2)}\cap(\Delta_{b_{1}}\cup\Delta_{b_{2}})=\emptyset,

we have

(\Delta_{b_{1}}\cup\Delta_{b_{2}})\cap(\Delta_{g_{1}}\cup\Delta_{g_{2}})=\emptyset.

Also, every curve in
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{2}
is contained in

\Delta^{(2)}
, and hence avoids
\Delta_{b_{1}}\cup\Delta_{b_{2}}
. By
construction, every curve in
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
avoids both

\Delta_{g_{1}}
and
\Delta_{g_{2}}
. Setting
\varepsilon_{1}=\min\{c_{5},c_{6}\},
we get
|\mathcal{B}^{\prime}|,|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}|\geq\varepsilon_{1}n.

∎

We now prove
Theorem

1.2
when the two families have the same size.

Theorem 3.3
.

For every integer
t\geq 1
, there exists a constant
\varepsilon_{2}>0
with
the following property. Let
\mathcal{B}
be a set of
n
blue curves, and
let
\mathcal{G}
be a set of
n
green curves in the plane such that

\mathcal{B}\cup\mathcal{G}
is a collection of
t
-intersecting curves in
general position. Then there exist subsets

\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{B}
and

\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
, with

|\mathcal{B}^{\prime}|,|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}|\geq\varepsilon_{2}n,

such that either every curve in
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
intersects every curve in

\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
an odd number of times, or every such pair intersects an even
number of times.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{B}=\{\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n}\},\mathcal{G}=\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n}\}.
We start by applying Theorem
3.2
to
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{G}
, and obtain subsets
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
, together with domains

\Delta_{b_{1}},\Delta_{b_{2}},\Delta_{g_{1}},\Delta_{g_{2}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2},

such that

|\mathcal{B}^{\prime}|\geq\varepsilon_{1}|\mathcal{B}|\qquad\text{and}\qquad|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}|\geq\varepsilon_{1}|\mathcal{G}|,

where
\varepsilon_{1}=\varepsilon_{1}(t).
Moreover, each curve in
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
has its first endpoint in
\Delta_{b_{1}}
and its second endpoint in
\Delta_{b_{2}}
, while each curve in
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
has its first endpoint in
\Delta_{g_{1}}
and its second endpoint in
\Delta_{g_{2}}
. Furthermore, every blue curve in
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
avoids
\Delta_{g_{1}}\cup\Delta_{g_{2}}
, every green curve in
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
avoids
\Delta_{b_{1}}\cup\Delta_{b_{2}}
, and
(\Delta_{b_{1}}\cup\Delta_{b_{2}})\cap(\Delta_{g_{1}}\cup\Delta_{g_{2}})=\emptyset
.

For
\beta\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
and
\gamma\in\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
, let
\chi(\beta,\gamma)
be the number of intersections between
\beta
and
\gamma
. Clearly, all such intersection points lie outside of
\Delta_{b_{1}}\cup\Delta_{b_{2}}\cup\Delta_{g_{1}}\cup\Delta_{g_{2}}
.

Claim 3.4
.

For any
\beta,\beta^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
and
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
,

\chi(\beta,\gamma)+\chi(\beta^{\prime},\gamma)+\chi(\beta,\gamma^{\prime})+\chi(\beta^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})\equiv 0\pmod{2}.

Proof.

In the following proof, we may assume that
\beta\neq\beta^{\prime}
and
\gamma\neq\gamma^{\prime}
since otherwise the claim is immediate. Given
\beta
and
\beta^{\prime}
, we create a closed curve
\beta^{\prime\prime}
as follows. Since both first endpoints of
\beta
and
\beta^{\prime}
lie in
\Delta_{b_{1}}
, and
\Delta_{b_{1}}
is a domain, there is an arc that connects these endpoints that lies in
\Delta_{b_{1}}
. Likewise, since both second endpoints of
\beta
and
\beta^{\prime}
lie in
\Delta_{b_{2}}
, and
\Delta_{b_{2}}
is a domain, there is an arc joining these endpoints inside
\Delta_{b_{2}}
. As a result, we obtain a closed curve
\beta^{\prime\prime}
in the plane, not necessarily simple. Since
\Delta_{b_{1}}
and
\Delta_{b_{2}}
are domains, we can assume that
\beta^{\prime\prime}
has a finite number of self-intersections, and no self tangencies.

We repeat the same procedure above for
\gamma
and
\gamma^{\prime}
, to obtain a
closed curve
\gamma^{\prime\prime}
. Again, we can assume that
\gamma^{\prime\prime}
has a finite
number of self-intersections, and no self tangencies. Since every curve in

\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
avoids
\Delta_{g_{1}}\cup\Delta_{g_{2}}
, every curve in

\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
avoids
\Delta_{b_{1}}\cup\Delta_{b_{2}}
, and

(\Delta_{b_{1}}\cup\Delta_{b_{2}})\cap(\Delta_{g_{1}}\cup\Delta_{g_{2}})=\emptyset,

the crossings between
\beta^{\prime\prime}
and
\gamma^{\prime\prime}
are exactly the crossings
between one of
\beta,\beta^{\prime}
and one of
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}
. Therefore

|\beta^{\prime\prime}\cap\gamma^{\prime\prime}|=\chi(\beta,\gamma)+\chi(\beta^{\prime},\gamma)+\chi(\beta,\gamma^{\prime})+\chi(\beta^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}).

Since two closed curves in the plane properly cross an even number of times,
the summation above is even.∎

Now fix
\beta_{0}\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
and
\gamma_{0}\in\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
. Define functions
f_{1}:\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\to\{0,1\}
and
f_{2}:\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\to\{0,1\}
by

f_{1}(\beta)\equiv\chi(\beta,\gamma_{0})\pmod{2}

and

f_{2}(\gamma)\equiv\chi(\beta_{0},\gamma_{0})+\chi(\beta_{0},\gamma)\pmod{2}.

By the claim, we know that

\chi(\beta,\gamma)\equiv f_{1}(\beta)+f_{2}(\gamma)\pmod{2}.

Partition

\mathcal{B}^{\prime}=\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{0}\cup\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{G}^{\prime}=\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{0}\cup\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{1}

where

\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{i}:=\{\beta\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}:f_{1}(\beta)\equiv i(\text{mod }2)\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{j}:=\{\gamma\in\mathcal{G}^{\prime}:f_{2}(\gamma)\equiv j(\text{mod }2)\}.

Thus, for
\beta\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{i}
and
\gamma\in\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{j}
, the parity of
\chi(\beta,\gamma)
is
i+j\pmod{2}
. By the pigeonhole principle, for some pair
i,j\in\{0,1\}
, we have
|\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{i}|=\Omega(\varepsilon_{1}|\mathcal{B}|)
and
|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{j}|=\Omega(\varepsilon_{1}|\mathcal{G}|)
, and either every blue curve in
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{i}
crosses every green curve in
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{j}
an odd number of times, or every blue curve in
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{i}
crosses every green curve in
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{j}
an even number of times. By setting
\varepsilon_{2}=\Theta(\varepsilon_{1})
sufficiently small, the statement follows.∎

We now prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
|\mathcal{B}|\leq|\mathcal{G}|
. Let
\mathcal{B}
be a family of
m
blue curves and let

\mathcal{G}
be a family of
n
green curves, where
m\leq n
, such that

\mathcal{B}\cup\mathcal{G}
is a collection of
t
-intersecting curves in
general position. We show that the conclusion holds with

\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{2}/2
.

For each
\beta\in\mathcal{B}
, replace
\beta
by either
\left\lfloor\frac{n}{m}\right\rfloor
or
\left\lceil\frac{n}{m}\right\rceil
copies drawn in a sufficiently small neighborhood of
\beta
, choosing the numbers of copies
so that the resulting blue family
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
has exactly

n
members. The copies may be chosen so that

\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}\cup\mathcal{G}
is still a collection of

t
-intersecting curves in general position and every copy of
\beta

intersects each
\gamma\in\mathcal{G}
with the same number of times as
\beta
.

Apply Theorem
3.3
to

\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
and
\mathcal{G}
. We obtain subfamilies

\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
and

\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
such that

|\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}|,|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}|\geq\varepsilon_{2}n,

and all pairs in

\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}\times\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
have the same intersection
parity.

Let
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{B}
be the set of original blue curves
having at least one copy in
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
. Since each original
blue curve has at most
\lceil n/m\rceil
copies,

|\mathcal{B}^{\prime}|\geq\frac{|\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}|}{\lceil n/m\rceil}\geq\frac{\varepsilon_{2}n}{\lceil n/m\rceil}\geq\frac{\varepsilon_{2}}{2}m.

Moreover,
|\mathcal{G}^{\prime}|\geq\frac{\varepsilon_{2}}{2}n.
By the construction of the copies, either every pair in

\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\times\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
intersects an even number of times, or every
such pair intersects an odd number of times.
∎

4
Topological graphs with no
k
-pairwise odd crossing edges

In this section, we use Theorem
1.4
to prove Theorem
1.7
. For a graph
G=(V,E)
, the
bisection width

b(G)
is the minimum number of edges whose removal partitions
V
into two disjoint sets
V_{1}\cup V_{2}
such that

\frac{1}{3}|V|\leq|V_{1}|,|V_{2}|\leq\frac{2}{3}|V|.

Equivalently,
b(G)
is the minimum number of edges crossing between two parts of such a balanced partition.

The
odd crossing number
of a graph
G
, denoted by
\operatorname{odd\text{-}cr}(G)
, is the minimum number of pairs of edges that cross an odd number of times, taken over all drawings of
G
in the plane. We will need the following result due to Pach and Tóth.

Lemma 4.1

(
[
16
]
)
.

There is an absolute constant
c_{1}>0
such that every graph
G
with
n
vertices satisfies

b(G)\leq c_{1}\log n\sqrt{\operatorname{odd\text{-}cr}(G)+\sum_{v\in V(G)}d(v)^{2}},

where
d(v)
denotes the degree of the vertex
v
.

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

Let
f(n,k)
denote the maximum number of edges in an
n
-vertex topological graph with no
k
edges that pairwise cross an odd number of times, and whose edge set is
t
-intersecting. We prove by double induction on
n
and
k
that

f(n,k)\leq n(\log n)^{C\log k},

(12)

where
C=C(t)
is a sufficiently large constant.

Let
n_{0}
be a large constant that will be determined later. The base case
n\leq n_{0}
is trivial by setting
C
sufficiently large. The base case
k=2
and
n\geq 3
follows from the Hanani–Tutte theorem by setting
C
sufficiently large. Now assume that (
12
) holds for
n^{\prime}<n
or
k^{\prime}<k
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex topological graph with no
k
edges that
pairwise cross an odd number of times, and whose edges are
t
-intersecting.
After shortening each edge in a sufficiently small neighborhood of its
endpoints, we regard the edges as a family
E
of Jordan arcs in general
position. Clearly, this can be done without changing the proper crossings
between edges. We consider two cases.

Case 1.
Suppose that

\operatorname{odd\text{-}cr}(G)\leq\frac{|E|^{2}}{\log^{6}n}.

By Lemma
4.1
, there is a constant
c_{1}>0
such that

b(G)\leq c_{1}\log n\sqrt{\operatorname{odd\text{-}cr}(G)+\sum_{v\in V(G)}d(v)^{2}}.

Since we always have

\sum_{v\in V(G)}d(v)^{2}\leq n\sum_{v\in V(G)}d(v)=2n|E|,

it follows that

b(G)\leq c_{1}\log n\sqrt{\frac{|E|^{2}}{\log^{6}n}+2n|E|}.

If
|E|^{2}/\log^{6}n<2n|E|
, then we have
|E|<2n\log^{6}n
and we are done since
C
is large. Hence we may assume that
|E|^{2}/\log^{6}n\geq 2n|E|
, which implies

b(G)\leq\sqrt{2}c_{1}\frac{|E|}{\log^{2}n}.

Let
V(G)=V_{1}\cup V_{2}
be a partition with at most
b(G)
edges between
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
, where
|V_{1}|,|V_{2}|\leq 2n/3
. Since each induced subgraph
G[V_{i}]
also contains no
k
edges that pairwise cross an odd number of times, the induction hypothesis on
n
gives

\displaystyle|E|

\displaystyle\leq f(|V_{1}|,k)+f(|V_{2}|,k)+b(G)

\displaystyle\leq|V_{1}|(\log|V_{1}|)^{C\log k}+|V_{2}|(\log|V_{2}|)^{C\log k}+b(G)

\displaystyle\leq|V_{1}|(\log(2n/3))^{C\log k}+|V_{2}|(\log(2n/3))^{C\log k}+b(G)

\displaystyle=n\bigl(\log n-\log(3/2)\bigr)^{C\log k}+b(G)

\displaystyle\leq n(\log n)^{C\log k}-n(\log n)^{C\log k-1}+b(G)

\displaystyle\leq n(\log n)^{C\log k}-n(\log n)^{C\log k-1}+\sqrt{2}c_{1}\frac{|E|}{\log^{2}n}.

Rearranging the last inequality gives

\left(1-\frac{\sqrt{2}c_{1}}{\log^{2}n}\right)|E|\leq n(\log n)^{C\log k}-n(\log n)^{C\log k-1}.

For
n_{0}
sufficiently large,

\left(1-\frac{\sqrt{2}c_{1}}{\log^{2}n}\right)^{-1}\leq 1+\frac{4c_{1}}{\log^{2}n}.

Hence

\displaystyle|E|

\displaystyle\leq\left(n(\log n)^{C\log k}-n(\log n)^{C\log k-1}\right)\left(1+\frac{4c_{1}}{\log^{2}n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq n(\log n)^{C\log k}-n(\log n)^{C\log k-1}+4c_{1}\,n(\log n)^{C\log k-2}

\displaystyle\leq n(\log n)^{C\log k},

where the last inequality holds for
n_{0}
and
C
sufficiently large.

Case 2.
Suppose that

\operatorname{odd\text{-}cr}(G)>\frac{|E|^{2}}{\log^{6}n}.

Let
E=E_{1}\cup E_{2}
be a uniformly random equipartition, so that

|E_{1}|=\lfloor|E|/2\rfloor,\qquad|E_{2}|=\lceil|E|/2\rceil.

For every odd-crossing pair of edges, the probability that the two edges lie
in different parts is at least
1/2
. Hence the expected number of
odd-crossing pairs with one edge in
E_{1}
and the other in
E_{2}
is at least

\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{odd\text{-}cr}(G)>\frac{|E|^{2}}{2\log^{6}n}.

Therefore there is an equipartition
E=E_{1}\cup E_{2}
for which the number of
such pairs is at least
|E|^{2}/(2\log^{6}n)
. Since
|E_{1}||E_{2}|\leq|E|^{2}/4
, the density of odd-crossing pairs between
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
is at least

\frac{|E|^{2}/(2\log^{6}n)}{|E_{1}||E_{2}|}\geq\frac{1}{\log^{6}n}.

By Theorem
1.4
, there is a constant
c=c(t)
and subsets

E_{1}^{\prime}\subseteq E_{1},\qquad E_{2}^{\prime}\subseteq E_{2}

such that every edge in
E_{1}^{\prime}
crosses every edge in
E_{2}^{\prime}
an odd number
of times, and

|E_{i}^{\prime}|\geq\frac{|E_{i}|}{(\log^{6}n)^{c}}\qquad\text{for }i=1,2.

In particular, since
E_{1},E_{2}
are an equipartition,

|E_{1}^{\prime}|,|E_{2}^{\prime}|\geq\frac{|E|}{3(\log^{6}n)^{c}}.

If both
E_{1}^{\prime}
and
E_{2}^{\prime}
contain
\lceil k/2\rceil
edges that pairwise
cross an odd number of times, then, together with the fact that every edge in

E_{1}^{\prime}
crosses every edge in
E_{2}^{\prime}
an odd number of times, we obtain at
least
k
edges that pairwise cross an odd number of times, a contradiction.
Hence one of
E_{1}^{\prime}
or
E_{2}^{\prime}
contains no
\lceil k/2\rceil
edges that
pairwise cross an odd number of times. Without loss of generality, assume this
holds for
E_{1}^{\prime}
.

The topological graph with vertex set
V(G)
and edge set
E_{1}^{\prime}
has

t
-intersecting edge set and contains no
\lceil k/2\rceil
edges that
pairwise cross an odd number of times. By the induction hypothesis on
k
,

|E_{1}^{\prime}|\leq f(n,\lceil k/2\rceil)\leq n(\log n)^{C\log\lceil k/2\rceil}.

Therefore

\frac{|E|}{3(\log^{6}n)^{c}}\leq n(\log n)^{C\log\lceil k/2\rceil}.

Since
k\geq 3
, we have
\lceil k/2\rceil\leq 2k/3
. Hence

\log\lceil k/2\rceil\leq\log k-\log(3/2),

and so

|E|\leq 3n(\log n)^{C\log k-C\log(3/2)+6c}.

Choosing
C
sufficiently large compared to
c
, and then choosing

n_{0}
sufficiently large, we obtain

|E|\leq n(\log n)^{C\log k}.

∎
